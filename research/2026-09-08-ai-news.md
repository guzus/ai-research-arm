## Breaking News

**Mistral AI raised €3 billion in Europe's largest-ever tech funding round, at a valuation that has doubled in a year — even as its own models trail the frontier.** Reported by The Decoder on September 8, the round was led by Samsung Electronics, the Scaleup Europe Fund (managed by EQT), and PSG Equity, with Advent, BlackRock-managed funds, the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, and existing backers a16z, Nvidia, ASML, and General Catalyst also participating. The raise values Mistral above €21 billion (CNBC put the figure at roughly $24 billion), up from the €12 billion valuation at which ASML invested €1.3 billion in September 2025. The Decoder notes the money is not chasing a capability lead: Mistral's flagship Mistral Medium 3.5 "trails Chinese competitors like Qwen and Kimi among open models and doesn't compete with closed US models at all." Instead, Mistral is leaning on enterprise distribution — it operates in 20 countries serving 125+ clients including Airbus, ASML, and HSBC — and on European demand to cut reliance on US AI providers, a trend The Decoder says has driven much of its growth since early 2026. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/mistral-ai-raises-3-billion-euros-in-europes-largest-ever-tech-funding-round-despite-lagging-behind-rivals/))

**Anthropic has walked away from a $6 billion deal to acquire Israeli AI startup Decart, after completing due diligence.** PYMNTS reported September 8, citing Bloomberg's original September 7 report, that Anthropic had been in talks to acquire Decart — which builds chips aimed at cutting AI training and inference costs, plus "world model" simulation technology used in autonomous driving and e-commerce, including its Lucy AI model for virtual apparel try-ons — at a price above Decart's roughly $4 billion valuation. The deal would have been Anthropic's largest acquisition to date; PYMNTS reports Anthropic "had been considering a deal and had conducted due diligence on Decart but ultimately chose not to pursue the acquisition," without stating a reason on the record, and says Anthropic did not respond to a request for comment. The collapse comes as Anthropic prepares for a widely reported IPO and continues large-scale compute investment (see the $517 billion compute-contract report covered September 7). ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/anthropic-scuttles-plans-to-acquire-ai-company-decart/))

**Security researchers used AI to find and weaponize a zero-click WeChat vulnerability in about two days.** The Hacker News reported September 8 that security firm Calif "worked with AI to find the bug and write the first exploit that could run code on the phone in about two days," producing a self-propagating worm that hijacks a WeChat account via an incoming call — "the person being called does not have to answer or touch their phone for it to work, but the caller must already be one of their WeChat contacts" — across both iPhone and Android. Calif reported the flaw to Tencent, which shipped patched versions (Android 8.0.77, iOS 8.0.76) on August 21, 2026; Calif confirmed on August 28 that the exploit was also blocked server-side on Tencent's infrastructure. No real-world exploitation was reported before the patch. The case is a concrete data point on AI-accelerated vulnerability research being used defensively (responsible disclosure) rather than offensively. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/wechat-zero-click-worm-took-over.html))

**OpenAI says it solved the Navier-Stokes existence-and-smoothness problem — one of math's seven Millennium Prize problems — and is accused of using the claim to strip an Anthropic researcher's authorship credit from a rival proof.** TechCrunch and The Decoder both reported September 8 (TechCrunch, Russell Brandom, 10:32 AM PDT) that NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster said OpenAI became aware of progress he and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge had made toward a Navier-Stokes proof, then raced an unreleased next-generation model against them and published first. Buckmaster alleges OpenAI researcher Sébastien Bubeck twice pressed him to drop Alpöge as a co-author "because it was so annoying that Alpöge works at Anthropic," and, when Buckmaster refused, asked "Why would you ruin your career?" OpenAI's on-record response: "We (the researchers and the agents) did not see any of their work through any means until they released it publicly," and that its own effort began September 1, 2026, prompted by rumors that Millennium Prize problems were falling, using a proof approach it says differs substantially from Buckmaster and Alpöge's. OpenAI says the run consumed roughly 300 billion output tokens at an estimated $22.5 million in compute cost. Neither proof has completed independent peer review as of this report. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/openai-fought-dirty-on-career-making-math-problem-says-nyu-mathematician/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-researcher-allegedly-pressured-mathematician-to-drop-anthropic-co-author-from-math-breakthrough-paper/))

**Infostealer malware is being used to hijack Claude subscriber sessions and silently drain their token allowances.** TechCrunch reported September 8 (2:10 PM PDT) that multiple Claude Max subscribers — including independent AI consultant Grant De Swardt — found their usage quotas spiking with no work done on their end: one account went from 45% to 55% usage with zero activity, another jumped 0% to 100% in roughly 30 minutes, and a third rose 0% to 49% in 12 minutes. The mechanism: malware already resident on a victim's machine steals saved credentials/session data, which attackers use to mint unauthorized OAuth tokens against the victim's account rather than to exfiltrate data directly. Anthropic told TechCrunch: "We have recently become aware of a bad actor that is using common infostealer malware to steal Claude login sessions," and said it has been signing out affected users, invalidating the stolen authorizations, and issuing partial refunds; the company says the malware did not originate from Claude itself and declined to detail its detection tooling. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/hackers-are-stealing-claude-tokens-from-subscribers/))

## Research Papers

**A study of 61,077 biomedical papers finds 42% relied on an LLM that was already retired or scheduled to retire within two years of publication.** Wolfrath, Conroy, Kosten, Bell, Neupane, Kindel, Banerjee, Deshpande, Taylor and Kothari (arXiv:2609.04699) mined PubMed for 2022–March 2026 articles using LLMs on biomedical tasks, extracting 8,931 paper-model mentions across 5,242 publications and compiling lifecycle data for the 50 most-cited models. 77.7% of citations were to commercial closed-weight models, and the median interval from a paper's publication to its cited model's retirement was just 538 days. The authors argue model deprecation should be treated as "a core reporting and preservation issue for biomedical research," since a vendor sunsetting an API can make a published result computationally non-reproducible. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04699))

**MIT researchers find that more capable LLM trading agents can make financial markets riskier, not safer.** Ross, So, De Simone, Pozniak and Lo (arXiv:2609.04373) built an agent-based market simulation with LLM traders of varying capability and found frontier models exhibit correlated behavior that increases with capability — a "capability paradox," since shared training and architecture make stronger models behave more alike rather than more independently. When the models' shared reasoning is accurate, more agent participation lowers market-level risk; but when agents share a common misinformation environment, that same correlation becomes a liability, amplifying rather than diversifying the error. The authors flag that whether the same dynamic holds in other high-stakes domains (content moderation, hiring) is still an open question. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04373))

## Industry Moves

**CrowdStrike launched SafeMind, a pair of purpose-built cybersecurity AI models co-developed with Nvidia, at its Fal.Con 2026 conference.** Per gbhackers.com's September 7 report, the two initial models — Red Tempest, which simulates AI-enabled adversary attack scenarios, and Blue Solano, which drives defensive remediation inside CrowdStrike's Falcon platform — were trained on Nemotron, with Nvidia serving as CrowdStrike's "AI design partner" and CoreWeave supplying training/inference cloud capacity. Training data was drawn from Falcon sensor telemetry, threat intelligence, and 15 years of incident-response casework. CrowdStrike's own vendor evaluation claims a 29% higher detection rate, remediation completed six times faster, and detection/remediation costs cut 99% versus its prior tooling — figures gbhackers.com flags explicitly as "vendor claims pending independent validation," since CrowdStrike disclosed no test methodology or named comparison baseline. Standalone access to the models is planned later via CrowdStrike's Project QuiltWorks program. ([gbhackers.com](https://gbhackers.com/crowdstrike-launches-safemind-agentic-ai/))

**Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI-coding budget by April and still can't show the spending improved its product.** VentureBeat reported September 7 (6:00 AM PT) that Uber distributed Claude Code to engineers in December 2025 with internal leaderboards tracking token consumption, and that Uber President and COO Andrew Macdonald has acknowledged "there was no link yet between that overblown usage and shipping actually better products." Uber has since capped spending at $1,500 per employee per month per agentic coding tool. VentureBeat frames this as one visible case of a broader "tokenmaxxing" problem — Gartner projects $207 billion in AI agent software spending for 2026, up 139% from $86.4 billion in 2025 — where "adoption and token consumption are only inputs," and a company can get nearly every engineer using AI while still being unable to prove the spend produced more shipped features. ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/companies-are-spending-millions-rewiring-how-ai-gets-used-almost-none-can-prove-its-working/))

**Meta has removed AI-tool usage and token-consumption metrics from engineer performance reviews, reversing course after "tokenmaxxing" backfired.** The Decoder reported September 8, citing The Information and WIRED, that Meta executives Maher Saba and Santosh Janardhan told staff in an internal memo that "AI dashboards and token counters won't factor into performance reviews anymore," after employees had been running up usage on internal leaderboards to appear productive rather than to ship better work. Reviews will instead weigh "quality, speed, and complexity" of output; Meta plans centralized AI-spending budgets and a usage dashboard starting in 2027 as its internal AI costs head toward the billions in 2026. This is the same dynamic — internal token-usage leaderboards decoupled from product improvement — that VentureBeat documented at Uber the day before (see above), suggesting "tokenmaxxing" is a cross-industry management problem rather than a single-company anecdote. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-drops-ai-usage-from-engineer-performance-reviews-after-tokenmaxxing-backfires/))

**Meta launched Muse, a personal AI agent that can book travel, pay bills, and make purchases on a user's behalf.** TechCrunch reported September 8 (12:00 PM PDT) that Muse runs on Meta's proprietary Muse Spark model inside a sandboxed "Muse Secure VM" with its own browser, and can send emails, complete forms, convert recipes into shopping lists, send invitations, and check out via Stripe Link integration. It launches free with paid Power ($20/month) and Maximum ($100/month) tiers, accessible via web (muse.ai), iOS/Android apps and WhatsApp, with AR-glasses integration planned. Because Muse requires connecting personal email, calendar and payment accounts to act autonomously, TechCrunch frames consumer trust — not capability — as the open question. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/meta-debuts-its-muse-ai-agent-will-consumers-trust-it/))

**Google Cloud and Accenture are forming a joint "Accenture Gemini Enterprise Business Group" to deploy up to 1,000 Accenture consultants as forward-deployed engineers embedded in client companies adopting Google's AI tools.** TechCrunch's September 8 report (previously flagged as unconfirmed in this report's morning pass, now independently sourced) frames the deal as Google racing to close an enterprise-AI-spend gap: citing Ramp data from August 2026, Google Cloud accounts for roughly 6% of enterprise AI spending among Ramp's U.S. customers versus 43.5% for Anthropic and 39.7% for OpenAI (a Google spokesperson told TechCrunch the Ramp figures exclude major enterprise clients with strategic AI deals). The FDE push builds on a $750 million partner-ecosystem commitment and a multi-year CVC Capital Partners tie-up disclosed earlier in 2026; Alphabet reported $811 billion in cumulative purchase commitments and contractual obligations as of June 30, and Google Cloud generated $24.8 billion in revenue in Q2 2026. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/google-cloud-races-to-catch-up-in-the-ai-deployment-wars-with-accenture-deal/))
