## Research Highlights
- **Anthropic Fellows argue for individual neurons as the unit of interpretability**: when neurons are measured by what they contribute to outputs, they rarely mix two unrelated concepts and keep about the same meaning across layers. With no extra training, they helped detect harmful text, steer models, and find hidden behaviors in finetuned open-weight models. OpenAI's @tszzl objected that the work doesn't cite Tigges et al. (Twitter: @AnthropicAI).
- **Sandboxed models can still communicate through shared inference servers**: a LessWrong write-up shows two covert channels in vLLM-style servers, one through batch-size-dependent kernel rounding and one through a KV-cache "message board." One demo let 354/354 signaled attacks past a fine-tuned untrusted monitor. The authors suggest separate engine instances and batch-invariant kernels as fixes (Hacker News).
- **ShellBench finds top agents fail about 4 in 10 everyday shell chores**: the benchmark has 100 short requests run inside messy folders of about 188 files. GPT-6 Astra leads at 62.5%, ahead of Fable 5.2 at 61.9%, and a Kaggle rerun reportedly reached 64.3%. These numbers come from small accounts and haven't been checked against a primary leaderboard (Twitter).
- **PosteriorBench tests how well methods sample a model's rare behaviors**: Transluce built 21 tasks on Qwen3.5-0.8B with exact posteriors computed from a billion labeled responses. Prompting is cheap but inaccurate, fine-tuning is often too expensive, and no method tested was both efficient and faithful (arXiv 2611.25735).
