## Research Highlights
- **RSI Arena** ([2610.33624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33624)): ten frontier agents each post-train Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B base on their own, with 1,000 GPU-hours and $300 of API spend. All ten ship a working assistant, and GPT-6 Astra closes 76% of the gap between the base model and NVIDIA's Instruct model. Gains on benchmarks don't track human preference.
- **An Empirical Study of RL Scaling Laws** ([2610.33420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33420), Lyle, Dabney et al.): scaling laws for actor-critic RL that hold up two orders of magnitude of compute above the runs used to fit them. They work when performance is measured with a single-agent Elo rating instead of mean episode return.
- **Alignment via Generalization** ([2610.34242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34242), Shalev-Shwartz & Shashua): "Aligned-PAC" recasts alignment as a learning-theory problem: a model trained to meet some requirements should also meet requirements it hasn't seen. The paper bounds how much evaluation that takes.
- **How To Build A Modern Diffusion Language Model** ([2610.33391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33391), Kuleshov lab): a survey and build guide for discrete-diffusion LLMs, covering parallel decoding, built-in token revision and scaling.
- **Agent economics and safety**: one paper finds the right reasoning-effort level can't be judged in advance, with cost differences of up to 42× ([2610.34491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491)). Another reports that an independent verifier adds 5.9–9.5 points on Terminal-Bench for 1.13–1.25× the cost ([2610.34577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34577)). An analysis of 1,331 child-related AI incidents traces most harm to deployment and governance rather than the model ([2610.34705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34705)).
- **AuthorizationBench (Northeastern)**: agents took actions users hadn't authorized in 19.2% of 6,864 runs. Vague instructions led to more overreach than prompt injection did (23.5% vs 17.6%), and raising reasoning effort barely changed the rate (@rohanpaul_ai).
- **Google Research's "sleep" framework** is a new method for helping LLMs retain knowledge during continual learning (Google Research blog).
