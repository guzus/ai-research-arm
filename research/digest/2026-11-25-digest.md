## Research Highlights
- **Tether: a verified policy engine for monitoring agents**: runs each proposed agent action in an ephemeral sandbox, infers behavior from syscalls, and enforces stateful policies written in a Lean 4 DSL (e.g. "no network after reading a secret file"). One policy blocked 100% of in-scope attacks at under 5% benign false-positive rate (arXiv 2611.25599).
- **WeightCode installs capabilities by editing weights directly**: a frozen language model writes programs that read, build, or patch network weights with no gradient step; unseen command compositions went from 0% to 99.8% exact match, and the edits accumulate rather than overwrite (arXiv 2611.25476).
- **Feedback-free skill self-editing degrades agents**: when models rewrite their own expert "skill" docs with no task or judge, every tested model got worse — 15 points on SkillsBench after 30 rounds — because rewriting pulls text toward the model's own prior. The paper argues this is prior collapse, not self-improvement (arXiv 2611.25534).
- **VR-SparseQuant targets Vera Rubin's native FP4**: an adaptive FP4 recipe using Rubin's 2:4 sparsity cuts WikiText-2 perplexity 6.85 → 5.57 versus dense W4A4 and hits 3.1× GEMM speedups vs BF16 on native kernels (arXiv 2611.25717).
