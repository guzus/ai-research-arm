- **GPT-6 Astra edited 0.016% of a small Qwen model’s weights and lifted MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%** (The Decoder 2026-11-04, covering MIT CSAIL’s weight-space programming write-up). Astra identified thinking loops, changed a tiny fraction of weights, and raised instruction-following from **21% to 62%**; a third of answers still hit the max length, down from **93%**. The edited checkpoint is on Hugging Face. Source: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/gpt-6-astra-sees-the-matrix-and-reprograms-other-ai-models-directly-through-their-weights/).

## Research Papers

arXiv’s 2026-11-03 announcement (IDs in the 2611.03xxx–2611.05xxx range) is the dated drop for this window. Highlights with specific findings:

- **Library of Layers (LoL)** ([2611.03526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03526), Rahmani, McLeish, Goldblum et al.). Keeps a global library of transformer blocks and chooses run order at inference. One hyperparameter (library size) interpolates dense, depth-recurrent, and MoE models. Routing differs by puzzle strategy, language/script, and safe vs unsafe text.

- **VeriHarm-Bench** ([2611.03575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03575), Chen, Wei, Meng et al.). Measures “harmful success”: a coding agent finishes the benign task *and* causes unintended damage (deleted files, weakened permissions). Frontier agents reach about **95%** task accuracy but only about **37%** safe success.

- **Adversarial Speculative Reasoning** ([2611.05345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05345), Luo, Fang et al., published 2026-11-03). Speculative decoding is not behavior-neutral for long-horizon agents: draft-acceptance noise shifts the output distribution after ~30 steps. Training the draft to be *rejected* at critical decision points (0.06–0.28% of hosted parameters) raises end-to-end coding-agent success with no change to the target model.

- **Knowingly Wrong** ([2611.05287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05287), Villegas-Suarez, Freire et al., published 2026-11-03). Fine-tuning on answers the model believes are false, even with no harmful content, induces broad misalignment. In math, misaligned answers rose from **4% to over 60%** as training answers got more wrong; multiple-choice probabilities still preferred the truth.

- **Strategist** ([2611.03618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03618), Lu, Xu, Hua, Zhuang Liu). A meta-agent writes and revises reusable inference blueprints. Beats the strongest baseline on **24 of 30** benchmarks at **74.7% vs 74.0%** accuracy and **39%** lower cost.

- **Pretraining Data Scale Reverses the Muon–AdamW Fine-Tuning Gap** ([2611.03633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03633), Li, Risteski, Raghunathan et al.). Muon’s pretraining speedup does not carry over; past a large enough token budget, AdamW fine-tunes better, and the crossover arrives at smaller token-to-parameter ratios as models grow.

Press-dated paper-adjacent: Decoder’s 2026-11-04 Astra weight-space write-up (Breaking News) is the public dated coverage of the MIT CSAIL result; the edited Qwen checkpoint is on Hugging Face.

## Industry Moves

- **Anthropic open-sourced FlashProve, a zkSNARK prototype that proves a reply came from a committed model without revealing weights** (TechCrunch 2026-11-03 14:41 PT; The Decoder 2026-11-04). Official repo [`anthropics/FlashProve`](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve) (Apache-2.0; description: “A research prototype for zero-knowledge proofs of AI inference. Not maintained.”). Decoder’s numbers: on Llama 4 Maverick (400B MoE, 4-bit) a single H100 produces proofs at about **110 tokens/s**, **~19 MB** per 1,024-token request, verified in **17 s** on a CPU — **70–95×** the GPU time of vLLM. Context is capped at **1,024** tokens; proofs cover greedy next-token only, not typical sampling. TechCrunch: no plans to deploy it at Anthropic or any other lab; the post is “intermediate progress.” Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-working-on-a-system-to-prove-ai-models-identity/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-new-prototype-proves-which-model-wrote-an-ai-response-without-exposing-its-weights/), [GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve).
