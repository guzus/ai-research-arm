  <h3 class="twitter-story-title">Anthropic: Claude Mythos Preview finds algorithm-level cryptographic flaws — halving HAWK's key strength and sharpening a reduced-round AES attack 200–800×</h3>
  <p class="twitter-story-lead">Anthropic published primary research on July 28 in which Claude Mythos Preview — the unsafeguarded twin of Fable 5 — discovered genuine mathematical weaknesses in two cryptographic algorithms, not implementation bugs but flaws in the algorithms themselves: an improved attack on HAWK (a NIST third-round post-quantum signature candidate) that roughly halves its effective key size, and a 200–800× faster attack on reduced-round AES-128. Both were found largely autonomously in about 60 hours at ~$100K API cost each, and neither affects any deployed system.</p>

      <p><strong>Evidence:</strong> The HAWK attack works by finding a previously unexploited lattice symmetry — a "nontrivial automorphism" in the Lattice Isomorphism Problem HAWK relies on. Prior work had proved such an automorphism would permit an attack but could not show it was accessible; Mythos found it, enabling a faster (still exponential, HAWK-specific) enumeration attack that forces a doubling of HAWK key sizes to hold the same security — which, the post notes, "eliminates many of the reasons making the scheme attractive" as a PQC candidate. HAWK had survived two rounds of expert human review over two years. The AES result improves the best known meet-in-the-middle attack on 7-of-10-round AES-128 by 200–800× using a novel "Möbius Bridge" fingerprinting transform, after ~1 billion output tokens over three days; it still requires ~2^105 chosen plaintexts and does not break full AES. The post also reports further attacks: a 13-round LEA key recovery running in under an hour on a desktop, a 6-round Serpent-128 attack, and smaller (&lt;10×) gains on Salsa20, Poseidon and SHA-1. Mythos is Fable 5's same weights with safeguards removed (karpathy, June 9).</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> Three honest caveats survive scrutiny. (1) Timing: Microsoft announced MAI-Cyber-1-Flash beating "Mythos" by 12 points on the CyberGym benchmark at half the cost the day before (July 27), so this reasserts Mythos's distinct value as research-level mathematical reasoning rather than vulnerability scanning — a competitive reframe, but not a contradiction of the technical result. (2) Hedging: Anthropic says Mythos "helped find" the weaknesses, and the HAWK run was semi-autonomous with a single non-lattice-expert researcher steering; the AES run was more autonomous. (3) Significance ceiling: both attacks are "expected results" (NIST's process exists to surface such flaws; the AES attack extends a long cryptanalysis literature) and have <em>zero</em> production impact — no deployed software must change. Falsification check: I fetched the primary post to test whether the claims were substantive or marketing; the HAWK attack ships reproducible demonstration code and was independently accepted by the scheme's authors and the NIST community, so the technical claim holds — but the "no production impact" framing is the honest ceiling on how big this is today.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> This is the first concrete evidence an LLM can do expert-level cryptanalysis on real, heavily-scrutinized schemes that eluded years of human review. It lands exactly as the open-weights safety debate peaks (Anthropic's own open-weights stance and Dario Amodei's rejection of a "ban," July 27), making Mythos Anthropic's living argument that frontier models pose novel risks worth pre-deployment scrutiny even as they harden security — the dual-use frame the company is centering.</p>
