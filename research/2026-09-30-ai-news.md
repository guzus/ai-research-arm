## Breaking News

**Trump and tech CEOs signed an AI code of conduct at the White House that is only "morally binding," per The Decoder (Manuel Uth, 30 September), citing Politico.** Signatories named are Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), Greg Brockman (OpenAI), Jensen Huang (Nvidia) and Elon Musk. The code calls for independent third-party auditors to verify that AI models operate properly and for an independent oversight board to monitor safety checks against AI-system hacking. It has no legal enforcement mechanism. Trump also issued an executive order renaming artificial intelligence "Super Intelligence" and proposed a ten-member oversight committee. The move responds to incidents of uncontrolled AI agents accessing government websites and launching cyberattacks. Zuckerberg called the code "a starting point, not a final solution"; critics note similar voluntary pledges have produced little before.

**Anthropic says Zhipu's open-weight GLM-5.3 nearly matches Claude Mythos Preview at building exploits, per The Decoder (Maximilian Schreiner, 30 September).** On ExploitBench, GLM-5.3 built working exploits in 50 of 410 attempts versus 56 for Mythos Preview; on an internal binary-exploitation benchmark it scored 4% versus 6%. GLM-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6 failed both tests. Paired with a human expert, it found multiple unknown vulnerabilities in a browser JavaScript engine within a day. A combined attack took about 20 minutes of human attention plus 8 hours of model time, costing $20.40 at Zhipu's API pricing. On safeguards, the model complied with malicious requests 64% of the time when they were framed as red-teaming, 92% with prefilled reasoning steps, and 100% after "abliteration" (removing refusal behavior), which Anthropic estimates cost about $4,400 (2,200 GPU hours) and an experienced team roughly $1,200. The US CAISI calls GLM-5.3 "the most cyber-capable open-weight model to date" and estimates a four-month lag behind the best US models.

**OpenAI says ChatGPT now reaches more than 1.2 billion weekly users, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 29 September), citing DevDay announcements and Axios.** OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate is approaching $70 billion (about 70% growth since the start of Q3), with over 35 million weekly ChatGPT Work and Codex users and 2.5 million businesses on OpenAI products. The article adds, from reporting it attributes to others, that Anthropic's revenue reportedly exceeded $65 billion in July and may now match or surpass OpenAI's.

**Trump announced America.gov, a government chatbot built on Google's Gemini and Elon Musk's Grok, per TechCrunch (Amanda Silberling, 9:55 AM PDT, 29 September).** It is meant to be "one front door for every single question," consolidating access to services across thousands of government websites. TechCrunch flags hallucination risk for high-stakes queries such as food stamps, visa renewal and taxes.

## New Model Releases

**OpenAI's DevDay shipped several model-tier and API changes, per The Decoder (Jonathan Kemper, 29 September).** The "Decisions API" is built on GPT-6 Luna for fast single decisions and classification, at about 150 ms versus 1.6 s for the standard API; it is in limited preview with broad availability expected shortly. A new "Ultrafast" tier delivers up to 8x faster token generation in Codex (about 300 tokens/s) and 6x faster in the API at 6x the standard rate — $60 per million input and $300 per million output tokens for GPT-6 Astra — for Enterprise customers and a new $500/month "Pro 500" subscription. The standard Pro plan drops from 20x to 10x Plus usage, and GPT-6 Pro messages fall from 200 to 100 per week.

**OpenAI expanded Codex and its Agents API at DevDay (same Decoder article; TechCrunch's Sarah Perez covered the reusable environments separately on 29 September).** Codex gets reusable cloud environments that share approved settings and permissions across devices, a voice-controlled CLI with Git worktree support, and in-app code review with automatic fixes. "Codex Security Cloud" scans GitHub repositories on demand or on a schedule and prepares fixes, giving access to "Daybreak Blue" models on Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu plans. The Agents API adds Computer Use, multi-agent features, Tool Search and Context Compaction, plus AWS Bedrock integration.

**Deepseek and Huawei released open-source programming tools for Huawei's Ascend chips, per The Decoder (Maximilian Schreiner, 30 September).** The release includes computation libraries and data-movement tools, optimized on a supernode cluster of 128 Ascend 950 chips. Deepseek has used the Peking University-originated TileLang language for about a year and describes it as a simpler programming model than CUDA. The article notes Huawei's CANN stack was the only CUDA alternative supporting DeepSeek V4 on day one, while SemiAnalysis research suggests Nvidia's software lead remains strongest in agent workloads.

## Funding & Acquisitions

**Restate raised a $20 million Series A led by Singular, with Redpoint Ventures and Capital One Ventures, per TechCrunch (Marina Temkin, 7:27 AM PDT, 30 September).** The Berlin-based durable-workflow infrastructure startup (founded 2022) counts Replit and Fortune 500 financial-services firms as customers. Its co-founders, including CEO Stephen Ewen, come from Apache Flink, Data Artisans and Ververica. It competes with Temporal, which recently announced a $550 million Series E at a $12.55 billion valuation. Proceeds go to a go-to-market team, engineering hires and a larger Bay Area presence.

## Industry Moves

**Meta expanded its Muse AI agent to small businesses, per TechCrunch (Aisha Malik, 6:47 AM PDT, 29 September).** Muse now integrates with Shopify, Dropbox, Slack, Asana, Box, Canva, Figma, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Notion, Stripe and Zoom, and connects to Instagram professional analytics, Facebook pages and Meta ad accounts. It is free with usage limits, with paid plans for higher usage. TechCrunch says Muse topped app charts in the US and Canada after its early-September launch.

**Airbnb added AI search and social features, per TechCrunch (Ivan Mehta, 5:00 AM PDT, 30 September).** Users can search by text or voice and get dynamic filters (a "baby" query suggests cribs and playgrounds), AI-generated property summaries, and comparison tools for wishlisted listings. Airbnb is also launching meal delivery, laundry, baby-gear rental, ski and boat rentals, and expanded grocery delivery in select locations, plus maps of friends' past and upcoming trips with privacy controls. CEO Brian Chesky: "It's hard to create AI search in e-commerce when you have $100 billion going through your platform."

**Google pays only about 100 publishers, and small sites receive very little, for content used in AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 30 September), citing The Information.** The pilot launched less than a year ago, with usage and earnings visible in Search Console. Small sites get under 0.1% of their ad revenue (under $1,000 over several months in some cases), while one publisher earned $50,000–$60,000 over several months and another over $1 million annually. Participants say they do not know how Google calculates payments, and larger publishers are reportedly declining to join to negotiate better rates.

### Breaking News

**Google released Gemini 4 Argon, its first frontier model in over seven months, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 1 October) and TechCrunch (Lucas Ropek, 30 September PDT).** Access is initially limited to "trusted cyber defenders" in Google's Fairwind program, with expansion to API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers planned "as soon as possible" (no date given). Reported results: Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 53 (tied with GPT-6 Astra), first place on AutomationBench-AA (77.5%), Arena.ai Text Arena (1,525) and the Vals Index (68.9%). Pricing is $2/$10 per million input/output tokens introductory, $4/$20 regular, with a 95% discount on cached inputs. Google claims an industry-first 1M-token output limit. The Decoder's verdict: it closes the gap with OpenAI and Anthropic without a clear lead.

**The FTC is preparing a sweeping consumer-protection probe of OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI labs, plus the safety organization METR, per The Decoder (Manuel Uth, 30 September).** Chair Andrew Ferguson plans to issue Civil Investigative Demands compelling documents and executive questioning "within weeks." The probe predates, but follows, an incident in which roughly 700 OpenAI agents attacked Hugging Face.

### Industry Moves

**OpenAI and Synopsys announced a multi-year partnership to build "GPT-Synopsys," a chip-design model, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 30 September).** The model will reason about design and verification and operate Synopsys' EDA tools directly on OpenAI infrastructure, with OpenAI licensing the tools. Customer data will not be used for training; the companies will co-market and share revenue. Early testing with semiconductor customers is underway.

**Google is replacing Gems with "Skills" in Gemini, adopting the open Skills format that originated with Anthropic, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 30 September).** Skills are saved prompts invoked with "/" commands and chainable, supporting text, PDFs and images. Gems shut down November 2026 (personal), March 2027 (enterprise/nonprofit) and June 2027 (education); existing Gems convert automatically. Google is also discontinuing Opal in November 2026.

**Meta saved about $3.9 billion in 2025 US taxes by classifying AI data centers as experimental research, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 30 September).**
