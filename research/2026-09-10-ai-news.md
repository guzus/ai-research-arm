## Breaking News

**DeepSeek released V4.1 Flash as MIT-licensed open weights and put the promised Flash price cut on the live card.** The Hugging Face model card, fetched today, describes a 552B-parameter multimodal MoE with a new Causal Encoder–Decoder (CED) stack: a 40-layer Transformer split 20+20, activating 8B parameters on input and 16B on output, native image understanding via a from-scratch DeepSeek-ViT, and a 1M-token context. CSA2 sparse attention plus FP4 main KV caching cuts the global KV footprint to 890 bytes per token — about one-quarter of V4-Flash — and SWA Bounded Replay shrinks persistent KV to roughly one-eighth. Additional pieces include Engram conditional memory (196B parameters, sparsely accessed), Single-Pass mHC, and DSpark speculative decoding. Pre-training used 45T multimodal tokens, with 1M-context extension at 34T. The live API docs now list the model as `deepseek-flash` (legacy `deepseek-v4-flash` and `deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp` already route here) at **$0.003 / $0.15 / $0.60** per million tokens off-peak for cache-hit / cache-miss / output, and $0.006 / $0.30 / $1.20 at peak (01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 UTC, Monday–Friday). V4 Pro stays $0.022 / $0.66 / $1.98 off-peak until **12:00 Beijing on September 14**, when `deepseek-v4-pro` traffic is scheduled to route to V4.1 Flash and bill at Flash rates. DeepSeek's own max-effort table prints DeepSWE v1.1 74.2 vs Opus-5.0 74.0 / GPT-5.6 Sol 73.0, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 90.6 vs 89.1 / 88.8; the same card has the model well behind on HLE (36.8 vs Opus 56.3) and on Terminal-Bench 3.0 / 4.0. Treat the coding/agent numbers as first-party harness results, not an independent leaderboard. The open-weight drop and the live card are today's news; yesterday's report only had the yuan schedule as an unflipped announcement. ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash), [DeepSeek API docs](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing))

([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-ai-chipmakers-raise-prices-high-bandwidth-memory-shortage-bites-2026-09-10/))

**Cognition's Eric Lu published a first-party write-up dated September 9 saying a Devin fleet factored RSA-260 and built a GPU lattice siever that he estimates is 10× cheaper than the previous public GNFS record.** RSA-260 (260 digits) is the largest publicly solved RSA Factoring Challenge problem; the previous record was RSA-250 in February 2020. Lu reports ~4,900 GPU-days / ~$400k at market prices (643 polyselect, 3,813 sieving, 467 linear algebra), run as a side project on spare or fragmented NVL72 nodes that LLM training could not pack. He estimates RSA-1024 at roughly $30 million at market GPU prices and "substantially less" with more optimization, and says RSA-2048 remains "roughly a billion times harder" and is not meaningfully affected. The implementation is a heavily modified CADO-NFS with a GPU drop-in for `las`; Lu writes there were "essentially no algorithmic advancements" — the gain is performance engineering on GPU memory systems. His role, he says, was priorities and off-track recognition; Devin handled measurements, cluster ops, and optimization. This is a lab blog, not a peer-reviewed cryptanalysis paper, and the $30 million RSA-1024 figure is an author's scaling estimate. ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/factoring-rsa-260))

## New Model Releases

**IBM released Granite Time Series PatchTST-FM-r2, a ~385M-parameter zero-shot forecaster dual-licensed Apache 2.0 / OpenMDW 1.0.** The September 9 Hugging Face Research post says the model is, as of September 8, the top permissively licensed replicable zero-shot entry on GIFT-Eval and #2 overall among replicable zero-shot models (geometric-mean CRPS 0.467, MASE 0.6846), sitting behind TimesFM-3. Architecture moves from standard Transformer blocks to conformer blocks (attention plus temporal convolution), 50% overlapping Hamming-weighted patches, 30 blocks, context up to 8,192, and a 99-quantile prediction head. The documented pretraining mix is selected GiftEvalPretrain datasets, KernelSynth-style synthetics, a TSMixup corpus restricted to data outside the GIFT-Eval eval set, and about 500,000 CauKer sequences of length 4,096. Weights, architecture, and a reproduction pipeline are on the Hub (`ibm-granite/granite-timeseries-patchtst-fm-r2`). Leaderboard rank is a snapshot as of September 8, not a frozen SOTA claim. ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-research/ibm-releases-sota-granite-time-series))

## Research Papers

Thursday's arXiv daily build (papers submitted 2026-09-09, announced 2026-09-10 04:00 UTC) is distinct from Tuesday's selections in yesterday's report. Five findings cleared a concrete-result bar.

**A single refusal direction still jailbreaks a 320B MoE — but only if you edit every writer at once.** Shi, Chen and Shen (`2609.09793`, submitted 2026-09-09) apply directional ablation to GLM-5.3-Flash (320B, 288 routed experts, four-wide hyper-connection residual, block-FP8). Editing attention, dense, or routed-expert writers alone removes 0.039 / 0.016 / 0.148 of refusal; all three together remove 0.776, and 74% of that effect exists only under the joint edit. A random orthogonal direction does nothing. Across seven harmful benches: 41–89 percentage points less refusal, no detected capability change. A module-name-matched dense-era recipe that reaches 0.066 of that 0.776 fails silently. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793))

**A token prediction lives in a handful of named components.** Oskin (`2609.10210`, submitted 2026-09-09) counts signed contributions across 18 models: the mass pushing *away* from the predicted token is a median 7× the mass carrying it. On the baseline, 53 components carry 90% of a prediction, 13 it cannot survive losing, and 8 suffice alone. Across 12 models trained elsewhere (124M–7B), the sufficient set is 2–16 components — 1–3% of the model, a share that does not grow with size. Three-quarters of a layer update is a fixed linear map of the state it received. Nothing is trained: a name can be installed into one spare unit for a quarter of a percent of held-out loss. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10210))

**Programmable World Model splits mechanics from pixels and holds state off-screen.** Huang, Lin and colleagues (`2609.10540`, submitted 2026-09-09) have an agent compile natural-language instructions into programs over entity state; a lightweight engine keeps a persistent global state, including off-screen entities; state-augmented 3D oriented bounding boxes plus a camera trajectory condition a pretrained video renderer. On CombatStateBench the method hits 94% Count Accuracy and 98% State Accuracy, well above existing interactive video world models, with coherent long-horizon generation. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10540))

**A synthesized diagnostic reward lets a 35B MoE beat prompted Opus 5.** Sun, Shi and He (`2609.10315`, submitted 2026-09-09) engineer the missing verifier for diagnostic RL: sample an intervention, inject it into a 12-root-cause advertising simulator, and use the hidden intervention as the oracle. The agent still has to hunt with Python and SQL through noisy, confounded evidence. On a held-out 235-episode set, Claude Opus 5 is the strongest prompted baseline at 0.686 FullAttr@1. SFT lifts Qwen3.5-35B-A3B from 0.159 to 0.637; RL on the synthesized reward reaches 0.757 — above every prompted baseline, including the 122B sibling — and with fewer tool calls. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10315))

**Coding agents fail at noticing that a method was never specified.** Ma, Zhao, Wu, Patwardhan and Cohan (`2609.10539`, submitted 2026-09-09) introduce IdeaAMBIG: 660 evidence-grounded implementation gaps (163 real from repro reports and GitHub issues; 497 synthetic injections). Across 13 LLMs the best model recovers 9.6% of real-world defects but, given the defect, writes a successful clarification 80.6% of the time. An oracle resolution lifts the downstream ready-to-code rate from 14% to 98%. Localization is the bottleneck in every model. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10539))

## Regulatory Updates

**OpenAI's chief global affairs officer, Chris Lehane, published a September 9 note asking Congress for mandatory, capability-based national frontier-AI rules "before it adjourns," and newly endorsing four California bills on Gavin Newsom's desk.** The first-party page, dated September 9, 2026, names SB 813 (independent safety-assessment infrastructure), AB 1405 (AI-auditor standards), SB 1119 (youth / companion-chatbot protections), and AB 1864 (federal screening standards for gene-synthesis providers and benchtop equipment). OpenAI writes that it "did not endorse some of these bills" previously and is supporting them now "after reconsidering in light of the recent jump in capabilities." The federal ask restates the June *Blueprint for Democratic Governance of Frontier AI* — common testing, independent assessment, stronger cybersecurity, incident reporting, national preparedness, and shared measures for tracking progress toward recursive self-improvement — and adds the adjournment deadline. Obligations, the note says, should hit "the handful of well-resourced laboratories" and "should not become open-weights policy by another name"; the company points to its signature on the Open Weights and American AI Leadership letter. No named Senate or House vehicle is attached. Newsom has not acted on the four bills in this window. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/ai-policy-window/))

**Microsoft agreed a contractually enforceable school-AI privacy compact with the American Federation of Teachers and New York City's UFT.** The Verge (5:07 PM UTC, September 9) says the ten principles include no training on student or educator data, data-minimization and plain-language disclosure, a ban on AI companions, and human review for "high-risk" decisions. Districts can add the terms to new or existing contracts beginning in November without renegotiating the whole deal. The agreement lands a week after New York City and Los Angeles announced one-year bans on many student-facing AI tools. AFT president Randi Weingarten called the terms "iron-clad" because "no one else, including the federal government, has stepped up to do the real work." AFT still runs a $23 million educator-training hub funded by Anthropic, Microsoft, and OpenAI. The compact is opt-in for districts, not a statute. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/policy/992359/microsoft-aft-schools-ai-privacy))
