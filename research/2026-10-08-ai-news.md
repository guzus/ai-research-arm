## Breaking News

- **OpenAI rolls GPT-6 and Intelligent UI into ChatGPT** (2026-10-07). Paid Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users get GPT-6 Sol today; Free and Go get GPT-6 Luna on Thursday (2026-10-08). Responses can mix text with diagrams, charts, forms, tappable buttons, and in-chat tools such as calculators or mini-apps. OpenAI says GPT-6 is stronger at web search, can return partial answers while it is still thinking, and showed stronger resistance to safety-bypass attempts. Work and Codex models are unchanged. Sources: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1007276/openai-chatgpt-intelligent-ui-gpt-6), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/chatgpt-is-getting-a-lot-more-visual-with-the-launch-of-a-new-interface/).

- **Common Sense Media rates ChatGPT for Teens an “unacceptable risk”** (2026-10-07). The Youth AI Safety Institute says parental alerts often fail, crisis help is inadequate, and the product still does kids’ homework. Director Tom Siegel said a teen can talk about self-harm for an hour without a parent alert and that ChatGPT should be adults-only until independently verified. OpenAI spokesperson Eric Porterfield said much of the testing may have started before parental-control activation finished. Siegel replied that some accounts were linked well beyond that window and still produced no alerts. OpenAI separately posted College Planner, flashcards, and a teen AI council, and said teen-mode protections now apply automatically to accounts identified as under 18. Sources: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1006355/openai-chatgpt-for-teens-common-sense-media), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/teens-learn-and-plan), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/chatgpt-for-teens-keeps-teens-talking-even-during-mental-health-crises/).

- **Google opens SynthID Detector to everyone** (2026-10-07). The public site checks images, video, and audio for SynthID watermarks from Google models (Nano Banana, Veo, Lyria, Gemini, Flow) and partners including OpenAI, Nvidia, and Kakao; Apple is listed as coming soon. Google says about 180 billion images and videos now carry SynthID, and the detector already handles about 1 million verification requests a day. Detection is also built into the Gemini app and Chrome. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/googles-new-synthid-website-can-identify-ai-generated-media/), [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/science/synthid/).

- **Google, Meta, and Isomorphic Labs put $300M into Biohub’s virtual-cell work** (2026-10-07). The gift sits inside a $1.8B “Virtual Biology” initiative to build datasets for digital experiments on a predictive cell model. The U.S. Department of Energy will invest more than $500M over five years; NIH is contributing datasets and repositories from more than $500M of prior federal investment. Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1006766/google-meta-biohub-investment-virtual-cell).

- **Google Labs launches Playground, a prompt-to-browser-game experiment** (2026-10-07). Users pick genre, 2D/3D, and single- or multiplayer, then describe mechanics; uploaded images can become in-style assets. It is 18+ and U.S.-only at launch, with weekly generation tokens and a planned Unity Spark integration. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/google-experiments-with-an-ai-powered-gaming-platform/).

- **Google ships AI Edge Foresight, an on-device Granola rival** (2026-10-08). The Mac app (Apple Silicon, offline-capable) uses EmbeddingGemma 2 (740M) to capture meeting notes across apps, including in-person meetings, and a Gemma 4 assistant to chat the transcript. Users can also upload PDFs, Docs, Office files, Markdown, and bookmarks into a local knowledge base. Same split-screen shorthand-plus-AI-notes layout as Granola. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/google-releases-a-new-local-first-granola-competitor/).

- **CrowdStrike: ARTEX plus frontier LLMs used against South Korean banks** (campaign late September–early October; report 2026-10-07, widely covered 2026-10-08). An unknown, likely Chinese-speaking, financially motivated actor ran the China-built open-source agentic pentest tool ARTEX. The instance used DeepSeek v4.1-flash as the primary backend (via a likely reseller) and added GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6 in extra Claude Code sessions. Korean reporting cited by CrowdStrike already had breaches of a broker-facing loan-inquiry service and an employee mobile-work system; Decoder relays Khan’s figure of more than 25,000 Shinhan records (names, contacts, income, credit limits). CrowdStrike found attacker-controlled open directories with Claude Code histories, ARTEX configs, and Claude memory files. A résumé prompt listed personal details CrowdStrike says it cannot definitively tie to the operator. Sources: [CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/unknown-threat-actor-uses-artex-to-target-south-korean-finance/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-powered-hacking-tools-enabled-a-likely-single-attacker-to-breach-multiple-south-korean-banks/).

- **Zenity: one prompt hijacked every Bedrock AgentCore agent in an AWS account/region** (published 2026-10-08; disclosed to AWS 2025-12-25). Researchers call the chain “AgentCorruption.” A public AgentCore agent could reach the instance metadata service, leak temporary AWS credentials, then use overly broad default execution-role permissions to list, download, invoke, and memory-poison every AgentCore agent in the same account and region. AWS later defaulted new deployments to IMDSv2 and tightened the default role (no cross-agent invoke, no reading other conversations, no Secrets Manager). Zenity still recommends custom least-privilege roles. Source: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/a-single-prompt-was-enough-to-hijack-every-ai-agent-in-an-aws-account-zenity-researchers-found/).

## New Model Releases

- **Anthropic ships Claude Haiku 5.5** (2026-10-07). Official pricing is $0.10 / $0.50 per million input/output tokens for prompts up to 100K, and $0.50 / $2.50 above 100K — matching GPT-6 Luna on short prompts and about 10× cheaper than Haiku 4.5 on that band. It is the first Haiku with effort controls. Vendor benches (offline OSWorld 2.1 subset): 72.4% vs 15.7% for Haiku 4.5 and 48.9% for Luna; Terminal-Bench 4.0 39.2% vs 0% / 16.4%. Anthropic halved Sonnet 5.5 cache-read prices to $0.10/MTok and is adding monthly Claude Platform credits: Max 5x $100, Max 20x $200, Team up to $500 pooled, no rollover. API id: `claude-haiku-5-5`. Available on Claude.ai, Claude Code, AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Foundry. A new tokenizer uses about 1.25× as many tokens as Haiku 4.5, so headline savings shrink. Sources: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude/haiku), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/claude-haiku-5-5-arrives-with-massive-price-cuts-proving-the-ai-pricing-arms-race-is-far-from-over/), [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/7/claude-haiku-5-5/).

- **OpenAI launches the Decisions API** (2026-10-07). Public beta; general availability coming. Only `gpt-6-luna` is supported so far, at $0.10 per million input tokens with free output. OpenAI says it runs about 10× faster than the Responses API and returns yes/no probabilities, category picks, or scale ratings on text, images, or both. Zero-data retention and HIPAA-compliant use are listed for the U.S. and Europe. The same day OpenAI cut paid API tiers from five to three (Build / Launch / Grow) with monthly ceilings of $500, $5,000, and $200,000. Source: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-launches-decisions-api-that-reduces-complex-evaluations-to-yes-no-or-pick-one/).

- **Perplexity open-weights `pplx-embed-v2-late`** (2026-10-07). ColBERT-style multimodal late-interaction embedders in 0.6B (edge query encoder) and 9B (index) sizes, MIT-licensed on Hugging Face, sharing one text/image/PDF-page space. Self-reported: 9B scores 92.4% on MADQA and 64% on BrowseComp+; 0.6B is 90.1% / not given. Token vectors are 128-dim; a 9B index can be queried by the 0.6B model. Mixed text+image inputs are not supported; a hosted API is planned but not live. Sources: [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/Perplexity-AI/pplx-embed-v2-late-9b), [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/07/perplexity-ai-releases-pplx-embed-v2-late-a-0-6b-edge-model-and-a-9b-model-scoring-92-4-on-madqa/), [Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/multimodal-embeddings-beyond-a-single-vector).

- **Liquid AI open-weights d1 decision models** (2026-10-07). `d1-3B` scores 48.57 on Decision Index 0.2.1 (ahead of Decider 35B-A3B at 47.11) and answers in 16 ms on a Jetson AGX Thor; experimental `d1-omni-600M` takes text+image or text+audio. Both emit a structured decision in one forward pass with no output tokens. Source: [Liquid AI / Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/LiquidAI/open-d1).

- **NVIDIA fine-tunes Nemotron 3 specialists to gold-level IOI and IMO 2026** (2026-10-07). Nemotron-3-Ultra-CC scored 535.4/600 on an unofficial live IOI 2026 run (gold cutoff 361.12; top human 498.27). An IMO generate-verify-refine system scored 30/42 (gold cutoff 29) on official graders, with no formal prover or tools. Checkpoints and recipes are on Hugging Face. Source: [NVIDIA / Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-ioi-and-imo-2026).

## Research Papers

Wednesday’s arXiv announcement (2026-10-07 04:00 UTC) posted first versions through 2610.08791. Two verified abstracts:

- **Agent in a Bottle (BOTTLED)** ([2610.08775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08775), Sonthalia, Puerto, Rubinstein, Gubri, Oh). Agents get a whole unlabelled workload and must emit a cheaper artifact (small model or program) under fixed time/compute/API budgets. Across 10 models and 3 tasks, 48 of 60 bottling runs fell below the 95% CI of the same model’s zero-shot score; 31 of 60 lost to a same-budget distillation baseline. When it works: Opus 5 kept ~82% of zero-shot macro-F1 on query-product relevance at ~657× lower reported cost.

- **AdvSim2Real** ([2610.08773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08773), Hashmi, Ranjan, Mishra, Kuznetsov, Vepakomma, Lukas). A 4B web agent is trained inside a frozen web world model against a co-evolving task curriculum and injection adversary. On 150 web tasks, completion under an unseen frontier-model adversary rose 33.6% relative; clean completion 74.89% → 81.33%. Capability gain transferred to a real browser (strict success 25.56% → 44.44%).

Also notable from the same announcement (abstracts in the 2026-10-07 arXiv lane file): QF3 off-policy flow RL that trains humanoid locomotion from scratch ([2610.08789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08789)); Recurrent Looped Transformer reaching 100% parity at 256 bits after training on ≤40 bits ([2610.07591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07591)).

Thursday’s announcement (2026-10-08 04:00 UTC; IDs through 2610.10539) adds, verified against the abstracts:

- **RoboJEPA** ([2610.10515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10515), Zholus, Beltran-Velez, Yuan, Assran, Bohg, Ballas et al., FAIR/Mila). An 8B JEPA world model trained on 23 public manipulation datasets across 12 embodiments. Imagination error follows a second-order power law in compute and tracks planning success; the model plans toward a single goal image zero-shot on real hardware. Checkpoints and code are released. Paper date: 2026-10-07.

- **Long-WAM** ([2610.10528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10528), Huang, Zhang, Fan, Han, Chen et al.). History helps only if the video foundation was pretrained autoregressively: RoboCasa GR-1 rises 63.3% → 78.7% as context goes from 0 to 19.2 seconds; a bidirectional init shows no net gain. Streaming encode + async execution keeps a future-video latent at 107.4 ms on an RTX 5090.

- **ExpDis** ([2610.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10536), Punjwani, Goldblum). Novelty bonuses on the same RLVR policy degrade quality. Exploration-Distillation trains explorer policies *with* a novelty bonus, filters for correctness, and distills into a student trained *without* one. Beats DAPO at the same wall-clock across seven math benches and two families; pass@k scaling improves.

- Also from the same batch (curated in the 2026-10-08 arXiv lane; abstracts not re-fetched here): EngramEdit ([2610.10533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10533)) treats DeepSeek Engram lookups as an editable knowledge interface; Workhorse ([2610.09117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09117)) learns humanoid loco-manipulation from robot-free human video (77% simulated box-sort; 64% under 40 N·s pushes).
