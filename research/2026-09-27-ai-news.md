## Breaking News

**Google is testing Flipkart checkout inside Gemini and AI Mode in India, per TechCrunch (Jagmeet Singh, 6:30 PM PDT, 26 September).** Users in the test see a "Buy" button on selected Flipkart listings that opens a Flipkart-branded checkout without leaving the AI interface. The trial is limited to some users and a small catalog — smartphones, electronics, and mobile accessories — with a broader India rollout planned later in October, ahead of the festive shopping season. A Google spokesperson said the company is "always testing new features and experiences" and had no further details. The flow TechCrunch saw is not the Google-hosted Universal Commerce Protocol checkout Google demonstrated earlier this year: tapping Buy brings up Flipkart's own checkout. Amazon listings appeared alongside Flipkart products in the same session without a Buy button. Google already holds a minority stake after investing about $350 million in Flipkart in 2024. Flipkart did not comment before publication. What does not survive: no official product name, no user or GMV target, and no confirmation of which checkout stack (UCP vs retailer-hosted) will ship in October. **Goldman Sachs expects Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Oracle, and Meta to spend a combined $1.2 trillion on AI infrastructure in 2027, per The Decoder (Manuel Uth, 27 September, 08:17 UTC, citing Bloomberg strategist Ryan Hammond).** That is more than 50 percent above the roughly $800 billion projected for this year and above Wall Street's $1.1 trillion consensus. ## New Model Releases

**Naive AI released Naive-N0.5-Flash, an open-weight 309-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 15.5 billion active weights, per the company's technical blog (NaiveAI Team, 27 September) and the matching Hugging Face card.** The stack is a hybrid of sliding-window attention (128-token window) and DeepSeek Sparse Attention (top-2,048 tokens, GQA with four KV groups) across 48 layers — 39 SWA plus 9 DSA — with no full-attention layers and a native 1-million-token context. Weights and inference code are MIT-licensed; the FP8 checkpoint is about 315 GB. A forthcoming API is priced at $0.10 input / $0.40 output / $0.01 cache-read per million tokens. The lab's NaiveRT runtime, built in six days across 151 documented optimization trials, is claimed at 50 tokens/s per user in Standard mode and a 2,122 tokens/s peak one-second window on 8 GPUs with thinking off; source is promised by 12 October. Training after the attention swap was 3.25 trillion tokens (50 billion indexer warmup, 3 trillion sparse-attention continued pretraining, 200 billion decay). A separate 400-hour research run tasked the model with building a world model (AutoWM) that scored 77.43 on WorldArena-1 Track 1 against a previous published high of 73.64. What does not survive: this is continued pretraining on Xiaomi's open MiMo-V2.5, not a from-scratch 309B; the coding and research charts are the lab's own comparisons against mixed vendor blogs and leaderboards, not an independent bakeoff; and the API row is a posted price card, not a confirmed live endpoint. ([Naive AI](https://naive.ai/en/research/), [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/NaiveAI/Naive-N0.5-Flash))

**Stanford and Caltech researchers' HomeBody system put GPT-6 Astra on a Unitree G1 and had it tidy an unfamiliar kitchen, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 27 September, 10:59 UTC).** HomeBody skips a trained low-level control layer: a swappable vision-language model — here Astra — calls an extensible skill library for grasping, navigating, and opening drawers. The robot first explores the room, builds a digital twin in Nvidia Isaac Sim, and logs objects in spatial memory so it can fetch items after they leave the camera frame. For a goal such as "clean up the kitchen," the language model plans steps and self-corrects on errors. Decoder flags Astra latency, overheating finger servos, and high compute cost as limits, and says the code is on GitHub. What does not survive: no paper ID, GitHub URL, success rate, or kitchen-task count was on the fetched page, so this is Decoder's dated short recap, not a verified arXiv listing. **A Fudan-led team logged 769 tasks from 56 people building Atria Dawn Preview and found agents doing more of the work while humans still made the calls, per The Decoder (Jonathan Kemper, 27 September, 15:18 UTC) and the fetched preprint.** AI was used in 96.5 percent of reviewed tasks; the median ratio of agent actions to human inputs rose from 11 to 28.5 over four weeks. Of 455 completed AI-assisted tasks, 151 — about a third, spread across 27 participants — were rated infeasible at the same scope without AI. On methods and parameters the most common pattern was "AI proposes, human selects" (55.4 percent); humans made 85.5 percent of those final decisions and 93.4 percent of goal-and-scope calls. When work stalled, humans intervened in 76 percent of recorded-difficulty cases, almost always with context or a diagnosis rather than taking over (full takeovers 0.7 percent). The model itself is a 744-billion-parameter MoE agentic system trained through a Verifiable Experience Pipeline; the paper's table gives it the highest reported score on five of 16 benches, including AutomationBench 53.8 and CyberGym 86.5, while it trails Claude Opus 5 on GDPval (1583 vs 1768) and SWE-bench Pro (59.6 vs 74.7). What does not survive: the preprint ([arXiv:2609.15818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15818)) is dated 14 September, so today's item is Decoder's first fetched write-up, not a Sunday arXiv listing; the "highest on five" ranking is the team's table against the six comparison models they listed. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-do-more-of-the-work-in-model-development-but-humans-still-make-the-decisions/))

## Industry Moves

**OpenAI Head of Applied Research Boris Power said 80 to 90 percent of the company's research already targets GPT-7, GPT-8, and beyond, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 27 September, 10:36 UTC).** Power's case is that most of the value arrives at a new generation, after which the company has to relearn where the short-term wins are. In-generation bumps such as GPT-5.1 to 5.2 come from specialized training data and are "seen as extremely shortsighted" internally, but they let OpenAI iterate faster today. He locates today's assistant bottleneck in onboarding, not raw model quality: most ChatGPT users still do not know what they can ask for. In his telling, GPT-4 needed careful prompting, GPT-5 still needed a lot of feedback, and GPT-6 already behaves more like a colleague you can hand a goal. What does not survive: Decoder's 209-word short does not name the interview, podcast, or event, and no first-party OpenAI page was fetched. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-says-80-to-90-percent-of-its-research-already-targets-gpt-7-and-beyond/))
