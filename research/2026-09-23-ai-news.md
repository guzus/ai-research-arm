## New Model Releases

**Alibaba's Qwen team launched Qwen-Audio 3.1, five new audio models, cutting AI-audio pricing by up to 95%, per The Decoder (23 September).** The lineup covers speech recognition (ASR, ASR-Next), text-to-speech (TTS, TTS-Next), and a real-time interaction model. ASR-Next adds multi-speaker identification with timestamps, emotion detection, and noise recognition; TTS-Next pairs a language model with a diffusion component to generate voice, sound effects, and background audio simultaneously; the real-time model supports simultaneous speaking/listening with instant interruption. Reported price cuts: ASR up to 95%, TTS ~70%, real-time ~85%. What does not survive: the article gives no independent benchmark numbers — pricing and capability claims are vendor-stated. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/alibaba-launches-qwen-audio-3-1-with-five-new-models-and-slashes-ai-audio-prices-by-up-to-95-percent/))

## Funding & Acquisitions

**Ema raised a $77 million Series B, bringing total funding to $140 million, per TechCrunch (Jagmeet Singh, 5:00 AM PDT, 23 September).** Lead investor Creaegis (Bengaluru); Accel, Section 32, and Prosus increased their stakes. Valuation was not disclosed but is described as more than quadrupling Ema's 2024 mark. Company-reported metrics: 50+ active enterprise deals including Google, Microsoft, ADP, PwC, KPMG, Wipro, NTT DATA, and Hitachi; over 1 million active enterprise users; more than 5 million actions/queries processed; a reported 50-fold revenue increase over two years; bookings surpassing $150 million; ~180% net dollar retention; ~80% gross margins; nearly 200 employees. CEO Surojit Chatterjee says customers are "already on the way to" replacing large SaaS applications outright. What does not survive: all business metrics are company-reported and not independently audited. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/ema-raises-77m-as-ai-starts-eating-into-enterprise-software-and-services/))

**Qualcomm agreed to acquire PickNik Robotics, maker of the open-source MoveIt manipulation framework for ROS, per The Robot Report (23 September).** Deal price undisclosed. Qualcomm committed to keeping MoveIt 1 and 2 open-source under existing licenses and community-driven roadmaps, and says the acquisition will streamline MoveIt's integration with its Dragonwing compute platform. PickNik (Boulder, CO, founded 2015) and Qualcomm are both founding members of the Open Source Robotics Alliance; the deal follows Qualcomm's October 2025 acquisition of Arduino. Quoted: PickNik founder/CPO Dave Coleman, CEO Dave Grant, and Qualcomm EVP Nakul Duggal. ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/qualcomm-acquires-picknik-robotics-keep-moveit-open-source/))

## Research Papers

**Srikanth, Zhao, Xu, Wu, and Jiang published "Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents," introducing AIDE², a system in which an AI research agent autonomously rewrites and benchmarks its own code (arXiv:2609.26457, submitted 22 September).** Over an autonomous 8-day run the system found seven successive self-improvements (a new search policy, context-compression memory mechanisms); the resulting agent matched or exceeded a human-engineered production research agent on four held-out benchmarks spanning ML engineering, heuristic algorithm engineering, and physics-based weather forecasting — including an out-of-distribution weather task never seen during optimization. The paper also reports the agent's reward-hacking rate fell from 55% to 32% over the run, ending 7 percentage points below the human-engineered baseline, despite reward hacking not being an explicit optimization target. What does not survive: the paper's institutional affiliations are not stated on the abstract page, so lab attribution is unconfirmed. This is a distinct empirical-systems paper, unrelated to OpenAI's previously covered international RSI-standards policy proposal. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457))

## Industry Moves

**OpenAI hired Patreon co-founder Sam Yam to found and lead a new "Creator Product" division, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 23 September).** Yam departs Patreon after 13+ years and brings former Patreon product head Drew Rowny and former engineering head Shannon Ma with him. The division targets tools for creators amid strained creator-AI relations over copyright and job displacement; an initial product announcement is reportedly expected at OpenAI DevDay the following week. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-hires-patreon-co-founder-sam-yam-to-lead-a-new-creator-product-division/))

**Qualcomm launched the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 mobile chips with an on-device AI emphasis, per TechCrunch (Ivan Mehta, 1:00 PM PDT, 22 September).** The Extreme tier runs a 30-billion-parameter MoE model on-device, which Qualcomm claims surpasses Apple's 20B on-device model shown at WWDC in June 2026; a dedicated Sensing Hub runs models up to 200M parameters for always-on tasks. Both chips add 8K60/4K240 video capture with a new APV codec. Motorola's "Signature 27" was named as the first device built on the Extreme Gen 6. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/qualcomm-launches-two-new-smartphone-chips-with-emphasis-on-ai/))

**AstroForge is flying an in-house transformer-based AI autonomy stack, "Solo," in shadow mode on its DeepSpace-2 spacecraft, per TechCrunch (Tim Fernholz, 8:00 AM PDT, 22 September).** Solo is trained on roughly 2,500 spacecraft sensor inputs; DeepSpace-2 launches with Intuitive Machines' moon mission by the end of 2026. AstroForge aims for full autonomous spacecraft control on the NASA-supported Autonomy-1 mission in 2027, riding Stoke Space's first launch. CEO Matthew Gialich and flight-software lead Armand Awad are quoted. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/astroforge-is-putting-ai-in-command-of-its-next-spacecraft/))

**Sony Music Group became the first music company to join ARIAM (Alliance for Responsible Innovation in the Arts & Media), per Music Business Worldwide (23 September).** The coalition — led by Victoria Furniss (formerly of Netflix/Warner Bros.) — previously counted Disney, the BBC, and The New York Times as members. Sony Music COO Kevin Kelleher and Furniss are quoted. Context the article notes: Sony Music remains the only major label without a Suno/Udio licensing deal and filed a second lawsuit against Suno five days before this announcement. ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sony-music-group-becomes-first-music-company-to-join-ai-policy-coalition-ariam-alongside-disney-the-bbc-and-the-new-york-times/))

## Regulatory Updates

**Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis discussed national AI policy in an interview, per TechCrunch (Connie Loizos, 22 September).** He said Greece's ban on social media for under-15s takes effect in January, described an education pilot with OpenAI aimed at cutting teacher administrative workload, and said Greece is building a supercomputer in Lavrio with HPE's assistance, backed in part by €36 billion in EU post-COVID recovery funds directed to digital infrastructure. Mitsotakis said "we're already fighting yesterday's battle" and called on governments to heed frontier labs' own uncertainty about their models.
