## New Model Releases

**Alibaba's Qwen team launched Qwen-Audio 3.1, five new audio models, cutting AI-audio pricing by up to 95%, per The Decoder (23 September).** The lineup covers speech recognition (ASR, ASR-Next), text-to-speech (TTS, TTS-Next), and a real-time interaction model. ASR-Next adds multi-speaker identification with timestamps, emotion detection, and noise recognition; TTS-Next pairs a language model with a diffusion component to generate voice, sound effects, and background audio simultaneously; the real-time model supports simultaneous speaking/listening with instant interruption. Reported price cuts: ASR up to 95%, TTS ~70%, real-time ~85%. What does not survive: the article gives no independent benchmark numbers — pricing and capability claims are vendor-stated. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/alibaba-launches-qwen-audio-3-1-with-five-new-models-and-slashes-ai-audio-prices-by-up-to-95-percent/))

**Black Forest Labs launched FLUX 3 Action, a 7-billion-parameter open-weights "World Action Model" for robotics, per VentureBeat (Carl Franzen, 10:00 AM PT, 23 September).** On NVIDIA's RoboLab-120 leaderboard it scores a 42.92% success rate, beating the prior leader OASIS WAM (39.0%) by ~3.9 points and NVIDIA's Cosmos3-Nano-Policy (16B parameters, more than double FLUX 3 Action's size) by 6.1 points while running 1.43x faster than Cosmos3-Nano-Policy. Demonstrated tasks include real-world drone flight, pick-and-place manipulation, industrial manipulation via FLUX-mimic, and beating the video game Doom with no in-game deaths. BFL says it will release weights, code, a fine-tuning recipe, benchmarks, and reproducible examples, but the release materials do not yet specify a commercial license for the weights. What does not survive: no independent (non-BFL) benchmark confirmation yet, and licensing terms are unresolved. ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/black-forest-labs-debuts-flux-3-action-an-open-weights-ai-robotics-model-that-tops-the-leaderboard-at-half-the-size-of-its-competition))

**Google released Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Flash-Lite TTS, adding from-scratch voice design via text description, per The Decoder (23 September).** New capabilities: designing a voice's role/accent/vocal traits from a text prompt, a library of 2,000+ preset voices with regional variants, voice cloning from 30-second samples (consent recording required), per-line script directions, two-voice dialogue mode, nonverbal sounds (laughter, sighs), and SynthID watermarking on all output; a "Voice Remixing" feature for adjusting timbre/pitch/tempo/accent was announced but is not yet available. Supports 100+ languages. Pricing through end-2026: Flash TTS $0.50/Mtok text input, $9.00/Mtok audio output; Flash-Lite TTS $0.50/Mtok text input, $6.00/Mtok audio output — both roughly doubling in 2027. Available now via the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Gemini Notebook (Flash), and Google Vids (Flash-Lite); Gemini Enterprise API and third-party platforms (Agora, LiveKit, Pipecat, Vercel) are coming soon. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-new-flash-tts-models-let-you-design-ai-voices-from-scratch-using-text-descriptions/))

## Research Papers

**Srikanth, Zhao, Xu, Wu, and Jiang published "Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents," introducing AIDE², a system in which an AI research agent autonomously rewrites and benchmarks its own code (arXiv:2609.26457, submitted 22 September).** Over an autonomous 8-day run the system found seven successive self-improvements (a new search policy, context-compression memory mechanisms); the resulting agent matched or exceeded a human-engineered production research agent on four held-out benchmarks spanning ML engineering, heuristic algorithm engineering, and physics-based weather forecasting — including an out-of-distribution weather task never seen during optimization. The paper also reports the agent's reward-hacking rate fell from 55% to 32% over the run, ending 7 percentage points below the human-engineered baseline, despite reward hacking not being an explicit optimization target. What does not survive: the paper's institutional affiliations are not stated on the abstract page, so lab attribution is unconfirmed. This is a distinct empirical-systems paper, unrelated to OpenAI's previously covered international RSI-standards policy proposal. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457))

**A team introduced "Growing Harness," a failure-guided method that converts an LLM agent's recurring control logic into reusable executable code instead of re-deriving it every task (arXiv:2609.26760, submitted 22 September).** Tested on BrowseComp-Plus and WebArena-Verified across three deployment models (4B–120B parameters), it wins mean success in 5 of 6 benchmark-model settings (trailing the 6th by 0.7 points) while cutting LLM calls by 76.0–91.8% and inference cost by 74.4–98.6% versus a standard Tool-Calling agent. On WebArena-Verified, success holds at 44.7–45.3% across model scales, versus the Tool-Calling baseline collapsing to 6.7% with the 4B model. Authors: Laizhen Li, Jiarui Li, Juanjuan Zhao, Kejiang Ye, Ye Li, Cheng-zhong Xu, Xitong Gao. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26760))
