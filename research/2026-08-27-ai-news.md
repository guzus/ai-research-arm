## Breaking News

- **Nvidia reportedly agrees to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion.** According to The Information, corroborated by CNBC, TechCrunch, Fortune, Bloomberg, and Yahoo Finance on August 26–27, Nvidia has struck a deal to acquire Hugging Face, the widely used open-source AI model and dataset hub often described as "the GitHub of AI." A signed agreement had reportedly not yet been finalized as of the reporting and TechCrunch cautioned the deal could still fall apart; neither company has confirmed it publicly. The price is nearly triple Hugging Face's last disclosed valuation of $4.5 billion (a 2023 round that included Nvidia participation) and would be Nvidia's largest completed acquisition ever, surpassing the $7 billion Mellanox deal in 2020. Analysts frame it as Nvidia moving to control both the chip layer and the dominant open-model distribution layer of the AI stack. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/27/nvidia-hugging-face-acquisition.html), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/26/nvidia-closes-in-on-hugging-face-acquisition/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/27/nvidia-hugging-face-billion-dollar-deal-open-source-ai/), [Bloomberg Opinion](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-27/-13-billion-hugging-face-deal-would-make-nvidia-the-king-of-ai))
- **Nvidia shares jump 7.6% as market digests blockbuster Q2 results.** Following the August 26 earnings beat reported in yesterday's coverage (revenue $96.2B, +106% Y/Y, with Q3 guidance of ~$108B ±2% and CEO Jensen Huang's ~70% FY2028 growth forecast), Nvidia stock rose 7.63% overnight into August 27 trading — a sharper positive reaction than the muted-to-negative moves that followed several of its prior quarters. Analysts described the market response as unusually strong given expectations coming in. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/27/nvidia-stock-q2-earnings-call-i-think-the-whole-market-was-like-whoa-analyst-says/))

## New Model Releases

- **Z.ai releases GLM-5.3-Flash, its first natively multimodal GLM-5 model, under MIT license.** Announced August 26, GLM-5.3-Flash (previously previewed anonymously on OpenRouter as "Ox Alpha") is a 320B-total/18B-active-parameter mixture-of-experts model handling text, image, and video with a 1,048,576-token (1M) context window. Z.ai says it beats GLM-5.2 on coding and agentic benchmarks at roughly one-tenth the price and approaches Claude Opus 4.8 on Z.ai's internal coding benchmark. List pricing is $0.15 input / $0.03 cached / $0.50 output per 1M tokens, with a 50% promotional discount through September 9, 2026. Weights are open (MIT license). ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/26/z-ai-open-sources-ox-alpha-model-as-glm-5-3-flash/), [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/26/z-ai-releases-glm-5-3-flash-a-320b-a18b-natively-multimodal-moe-with-a-1m-token-context/), [testingcatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/z-ai-launches-glm-5-3-flash-under-mit-license/))

- **Stability AI closes $76 million Series B backed by all three major music labels.** Announced August 25, the round included Universal Music Group, Sony Music Group, Warner Music Group, and Electronic Arts as investors — making Stability AI the first AI company to receive direct equity investment from all three major music labels in a single round. ## Industry Moves

- **Anthropic adds a built-in browser to Claude Cowork on desktop.** Announced August 26, the feature lets Claude open a sandboxed browser in a side panel to navigate, read, click, and type on live webpages when a Cowork task requires it — extending the built-in-browser capability Anthropic first shipped for Claude Code desktop in July. Rolling out this week to Pro, Max, and Team plans, with built-in safety controls and an option to import existing browser logins. ([Claude/Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-built-in-browser), [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/claude-built-in-browser-cowork/))
- **US Labor Department confirms OpenAI, Google, Meta, and Amazon are sharing data for a new AI workforce hub.** Reported August 26 by Axios, the Department of Labor — working with the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Census Bureau, and Bureau of Economic Analysis — has signed data-sharing memorandums of understanding with major AI and tech companies to build a centralized platform assessing AI's effect on jobs, industries, and skills demand. The initiative stems from the Trump administration's AI Action Plan; officials say the private-sector data (previously unreleased) is meant to ground workforce policy in measurable evidence rather than speculation, with a public launch expected "in the coming months." ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/26/labor-department-tech-giants-ai-jobs-data), [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/labor-department-ai-workforce-hub-launch/))
