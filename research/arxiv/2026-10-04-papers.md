## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. ActiveSaddler: Automated Curriculum Learning for Agent Harness Optimization
**Authors**: Sungho Park, Wonjoong Kim, Jue Zhang, Wook-Shin Han, Pengfei Gao, Chanyoung Park, Yongqiang Yao, Rao Fu, Elsie Nallipogu, Qingwei Lin, Victor Rühle
**arXiv**: [2610.00906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00906)
**TL;DR**: Friday's VISTA / AutoCompact / GUI-HARVEST papers said the harness is the product. ActiveSaddler says the *curriculum that trains the harness* is the missing dial. Existing harness optimizers fix the scenario order up front and only search how to edit prompts, tools, and control logic. As the harness changes, the useful failures change. ActiveSaddler treats that as a non-stationary bandit: recurring failures become reusable arms, each scored for remaining learning progress, and the loop balances revisiting known weaknesses against hunting new ones. Same optimizer, adaptive order: +4.4 Pass@1 on GAIA2 and +7.5 on Terminal-Bench 2.0 versus a frozen scenario list.
**Why it matters**: Harness search without a co-evolving curriculum is leaving points on the table. The training room has to move with the scaffold.

### 2. Probe with Participation Trophies: Random-Reward RL as a Probe of LLM Capability
**Authors**: Yu Mao, Lei Yu, Zining Zhu, Yusheng Zheng, Haohang Li, Freda Shi, Yutong Yin, Zhaoran Wang, Jingcheng Niu
**arXiv**: [2610.01066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01066)
**TL;DR**: Random-reward RL has two popular stories — "it's the RL mechanism" and "it's contamination." This paper offers a third: it is a probe of *reachability*, what further training can still extract from a checkpoint. Two OLMo snapshots sit at the same 3.5% on synthetic arithmetic; under identical correctness-rewarded RL they split to 8.5% vs 55%. Across pre-training and mid-training the authors find three regimes: early, even correct rewards do almost nothing; later in pre-training, correct rewards work and random rewards stay weak; once mid-training starts, random rewards produce large gains. Number-masked SFT shows the same ordering, so it is not an RL-only trick. Random rewards are useful precisely because they leak no label about the right answer.
**Why it matters**: Equal bench scores are not equal remaining headroom. Reachability is a property of the checkpoint, not of the score.

### 3. CARM: Cancellation-Aware Response Masking for LLM Reinforcement Learning
**Authors**: Yafei Zhang, Songshuo Lu, Sicong Liao, Zhi Chen, Yaohua Tang
**arXiv**: [2610.02039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02039)
**TL;DR**: Production LLM RL is off-policy: rollout and train engines disagree, so sequence-level masks decide which responses may update. The usual mask is a length-normalized geometric mean of token probability ratios. Signed log-ratios cancel across positions and hide two-way drift. CARM takes the absolute value first, then averages, and proves accepted responses jointly bound the fraction of tokens outside a ratio band *and* their mean log-distance past it. On AIME 2024/2025/2026 + BeyondAIME, mean@16 rises by up to 3.13 points over geometric-mean masking; average pass@1 across four code benches rises 2.88 over the strongest baseline.
**Why it matters**: Off-policy control that lets opposing token moves cancel is lying about how far the policy already drifted.

### 4. Where-OPD: Spatially Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation of MLLMs with Synthetic Scenes
**Authors**: Sophia Sirko-Galouchenko, Monika Wysoczanska, Andrei Bursuc, Nicolas Thome, Spyros Gidaris
**arXiv**: [2610.02117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02117)
**TL;DR**: On-policy distillation has been a language-model trick. For MLLMs the privileged signal has been image crops, which only help zoom tasks and need grounding labels or an external teacher. Where-OPD instead gives the teacher *text*: object IDs and coordinates from procedurally generated scenes. The teacher locates and integrates multi-region evidence; the student must do the same from image + question alone. No human boxes. Gains transfer off the synthetic train set: +3.23 average on CVBench, V*, ZoomBench, BLINK, HR-Bench, and MME-RealWorld, plus counting / document / chart benches across several backbones.
**Why it matters**: Privileged spatial text on fake rooms can teach a VLM to look, and the looking survives contact with real photos.

### 5. Beyond Memory: Harnessing Long-Horizon Agents with Explicit Belief States
**Authors**: Yu Luo, Jiamin Jiang, Yimin Zuo, Xidao Wen, Rongchen Gao, Yongqian Sun, Shenglin Zhang, Guiyang Liu, Cheng Zhang, Fang Situ, Qi Zhou, Dan Pei
**arXiv**: [2610.01415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01415)
**TL;DR**: History stores and compactors keep tokens. They do not keep a coherent world. PoS is an inference-time loop that maintains an explicit belief: current world state plus unresolved task requirements. It checks consistency, watches for Belief Trapping (the agent keeps acting without progress), and recovers by trap type and leftover requirement. On four execution and diagnosis benches, PoS is the best overall score for every benchmark × three LLM backbones. Ablations say consistency checks and recovery both matter; context-scaling runs stay up as the window grows.
**Why it matters**: Long-horizon context management is a belief problem. Compression without a world model is just a shorter confusion.
