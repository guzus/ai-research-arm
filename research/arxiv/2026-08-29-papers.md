# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-29

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Performance Foundations of Parallel & Distributed Reasoning Language Models
**Authors**: Maciej Besta, Leonard Schmidt, Lara Nonino, Robert Gerstenberger, Pierre Pang, Patrik Okanovic, Ales Kubicek, Tiancheng Chen, Baraq Lipshitz, Torsten Hoefler
**arXiv**: [2608.27046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27046)
**TL;DR**: The first compute-centric systems treatment of training Reasoning Language Models (DeepSeek-R1-style RLVR post-training), which require millions of GPU-hours and tightly coupled multi-model pipelines. Systematizes PPO/GRPO-style post-training from a parallelism angle and builds a taxonomy of intra- and inter-model parallelism for RL-for-LLMs — disaggregated placement, stage fusion, hybrid parallelism, asynchronous execution — made rigorous with the work-depth model, plus practical guidelines and open problems.
**Why it matters**: Turns RLM training into a first-class parallel & distributed systems problem, providing the missing blueprint for building scalable, cost-effective reasoning models.

### 2. Squeezing More from Limited Data with Recursive Transformers
**Authors**: Serdar Gülbahar, Lukas Edman, Alexander Fraser
**arXiv**: [2608.26973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26973)
**TL;DR**: Maps the limited-data pretraining regime (10M–100M words) where scaling parameter count past an optimum degrades generalization, and argues standard Transformers scale down poorly because embeddings eat the parameter budget and compute is tied to capacity. Recursive Transformers (shared block reused across depth) plus factorized embeddings decouple compute from parameters and outperform standard Transformers at both 10M and 100M words, staying competitive with BabyLM 2025 winners.
**Why it matters**: A concrete architecture recipe for the data-constrained scaling law regime — increasingly relevant as high-quality web data runs out.

### 3. Cross-Lingual Alignment Without Joint Training: Do Monolingual Language Models Converge on Universal Representations?
**Authors**: Ej Zhou, Suchir Salhan, Catherine Arnett, Anna Korhonen
**arXiv**: [2608.27115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27115)
**TL;DR**: Strictly monolingual LMs (Goldfish family, independently trained models) develop alignable representational geometry — no joint training, shared batches, or alignment objectives needed — with alignment strengthening under more data, scale, and linguistic proximity. A single Procrustes rotation fitted on parallel sentences maps hidden states between models, and causally transfers functional content: patching a rotated English residual into a German model flips factual cloze predictions to the donor's answer.
**Why it matters**: Shows cross-lingual alignment can emerge from language structure itself, pointing to model stitching, merging, and modular multilingual systems built from monolingual components.

### 4. JudgeStealer: Extracting LLM Judging Capabilities across Evaluation Protocols
**Authors**: Chen Chen, Yaolin Chen, Xuehan Sun, Juan Lin, Xueluan Gong, Yuhang Zheng, Qian Wang, Kwok-Yan Lam
**arXiv**: [2608.26982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26982)
**TL;DR**: First query-efficient model extraction attack targeting LLM judges and reward models: it acquires pointwise scores under a limited budget and converts them into pairwise and listwise supervision for free via cross-protocol agreement, with diversity/uncertainty/bias-aware input selection and score smoothing. Clones judging capabilities with up to 73.3% (pointwise), 87.0% (pairwise), and 71.6% (listwise) fidelity, remaining robust against representative extraction defenses.
**Why it matters**: Evaluation and reward infrastructure is becoming valuable IP and a security surface — this defines the threat model for it.

### 5. SpatialCrafter: Single Image World Modeling with Generative 3D Proxies
**Authors**: Chuan Fang, Lingteng Qiu, Yixun Liang, Rui Chen, Kunming Luo, Zhaohua Zheng, Tongyuan Bai, Feipeng Tian, Zilong Dong, Zihan Zhou, Ping Tan
**arXiv**: [2608.27073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27073)
**TL;DR**: Two-stage image-to-scene generation that first predicts a spatially aligned, geometrically consistent global 3D proxy (PaSS Flow), then uses the video diffusion model as a Generative Deferred Refiner for photorealistic appearance on proxy-defined geometry — with parallel geometry injection and proxy-aware corruption training to protect the pretrained manifold. Releases a new 115K-scene hybrid dataset; state-of-the-art consistency with reduced long-term drift under rapid camera motion and extreme viewpoints.
**Why it matters**: A strong step from stochastic video-diffusion hallucination toward explorable, geometrically grounded single-image world models for gaming, robotics, and VR.
