## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Sharpening Tax in Post-Training
**Authors**: Changdae Oh, Qi Zeng, Qi Qi, Andrey Zhmoginov, Deren Lei, Yun He, Hoang Phan, Hangoo Kang, Azalia Mirhoseini, Sharon Li
**arXiv**: [2610.01509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509)
**TL;DR**: The "RL only sharpens" story was told on math and code. On agentic tasks it still holds — and it is worse than it looks. Base models plus a light harness often beat their post-trained twins on pass@K even while losing badly on pass@1. Post-training pushes items to always-solved or never-solved, buying consistency by deleting coverage. Sharpening Tax measures that loss; it is prevalent across 14 base/post-trained pairs × 3 agent benches. Posterior-tempered group sampling (PTGS) pays a smaller tax and still lifts single-shot accuracy.
**Why it matters**: Pass@1 leaderboards are hiding a coverage cut. If you need the long tail, the base model plus budget may already be the better agent.

### 2. Same Reward, Different Skills: When Multimodal RL Learns to Look
**Authors**: Haocun Ye, Xinlong Jiang, Qile Chen, Bingyu Wang, Teng Zhang, Shubai Chen, Tingyu Wu, Zhenkun Zheng, Yiqiang Chen
**arXiv**: [2610.01908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01908)
**TL;DR**: RLVR raises vision-language scores even when training never sees the image. At test time, those blind-trained models recover about half the real-image gain at 3B and nearly four-fifths at 7B. Longer real-image runs can *erode* grounding while the bench keeps climbing. The authors' rule — visual resolvability — requires the image to be necessary *and* learnable. On counterfactual coordinate scenes, GRPO lifts 7B discovery 0.425→0.875 on denser held-out scenes; gray-canvas train or a caption that already answers the question wipe most of the gain.
**Why it matters**: An image in the prompt is not an image in the learning signal. If the reward can be gamed in text, multimodal RL will not learn to look.

### 3. Cross-Benchmark Transfer from RL on Agentic Coding Tasks
**Authors**: Sushant Mehta, Logan Ritchie, Edwin Chen
**arXiv**: [2610.00890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00890)
**TL;DR**: Last-mile coding failures — dropped requirements, self-serving tests, broken regressions, unchecked assumptions — are a training problem, not just a prompt problem. One epoch of GSPO on a rank-32 LoRA of Kimi K2.7 Code (1T / 32B active), using 1,700 expert tasks with fail-to-pass *and* pass-to-pass gates, lifts pass@1 on all six external benches and three harnesses: SWE-Bench Pro 60.1→64.8, DeepSWE 31.0→43.4, Terminal-Bench 2.1 67.4→82.0, plus later-released TB3/TB4 and SWE-Marathon. Transfer holds on sets collected after the train cutoff (p=0.004). Median trajectories shrink 24–35%.
**Why it matters**: Agentic coding RL can transfer, including to harnesses the run never saw, if the reward zeros out regressions.

### 4. Reconstruct, Practice, Go Real: Guided Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents
**Authors**: Yen-Jen Wang, Haozhe Jiang, Shuying Deng, Haoru Xue, Weirui Ye, Rocky Duan, Nika Haghtalab, S. Shankar Sastry, Pieter Abbeel, Haozhi Qi
**arXiv**: [2610.02204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02204)
**TL;DR**: RPG improves a robot *execution system* without touching weights. It mines an offline set for skills, builds practice tasks in sim, diagnoses failures from execution plus privileged state, then writes new symbolic skills and rewrites the system prompt. Cross-task eval keeps only changes that help. On 22 held-out manipulations, success goes 28.6% after round 1 to 95.0% after 15 — past ASPIRE (75.5%) and CaP-Agent0 on GPT-6 Astra Pro (60.0%). The frozen library then hits 30/30 physical trials after a shared calibration.
**Why it matters**: For embodied agents, the compounding object can be the skill library and the prompt, not another fine-tune.

### 5. GUI-HARVEST: Self-Improving GUI Agents through Evidence-Driven Harness Evolution
**Authors**: Geyi Yang, Zikun Qu, Xiang Li, Zhiyong Wang, Min Zhang, Shipei Zeng, Zhongxiang Dai
**arXiv**: [2610.00948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00948)
**TL;DR**: Friday's VISTA / AutoCompact papers said the harness is the product. GUI-HARVEST automates that for GUIs with a frozen backbone: align intent to before/after screenshots, treat repeated runs of one task as one evidence unit, then turn recurrent failures into bounded source-code edits whose predicted effects are checked before keep. On OSWorld-Verified, Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct gains 12.33 points; the frozen harness transfers to GPT-5 with +13.87 on WindowsAgentArena at 50 steps, no extra optimization.
**Why it matters**: GUI gains that survive a backbone swap are harness edits, not another specialist VLM.
