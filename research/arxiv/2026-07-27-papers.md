## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Do Agent Benchmarks Measure Capability? Protocol Validity in the Age of Agentic AI
**Authors**: Jiaqi Shao, Hanck Chen, Wei Zhang, Maxm Pan, Bing Luo
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22368v1
**TL;DR**: HackDetect audits 2,385 agent traces across 15 benchmarks and finds environment exposures/reward hacking in 67.0% of "Frontier Science" traces, introducing a "Mislead gap" metric to separate score inflation from genuine capability.
**Why it matters**: A large fraction of reported agent-benchmark gains may reflect exploiting benchmark protocol flaws rather than real capability — directly relevant to how the field should trust agent leaderboards.

### 2. Skill Self-Play: Pushing the Frontier of LLM Capability with Co-Evolving Skills
**Authors**: Siyuan Huang, Pengyu Cheng, Haotian Liu, Tao Chen, Yihao Liu, Jingwei Ni, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22529v1
**TL;DR**: Proposes a proposer/solver/skill-controller RL framework where reusable "skills" resolve the tension between task diversity and verification reliability in self-play training, improving reasoning and tool-use benchmarks.
**Why it matters**: A concrete mechanism for moving LLM training off manual annotation and onto interaction-driven, self-generated curricula.

### 3. On the Identifiability of Controlled World Models
**Authors**: Xiangteng Zhang, Yang Guan, Bo Zhang, Ya-Qin Zhang, Shengbo Eben Li
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22430v1
**TL;DR**: Establishes formal identifiability theory for JEPA-style controlled world models, proving conditions (spectral signal separation, non-degenerate action variation) under which a global optimum recovers the true latent state and dynamics up to orthogonal transform.
**Why it matters**: Puts theoretical grounding under a fast-moving empirical area (world models/JEPA) that has so far lacked guarantees about what these models actually learn.

### 4. Scaling Native Multimodal Pre-Training From Scratch
**Authors**: Haoyuan Wu, Aoqi Wu, Hai Wang, Jiajia Wu, Jinxiang Ou, Bei Yu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22043v1
**TL;DR**: Characterizes scaling laws for training multimodal models natively (not adapting from a text-only base), finding the language-token allocation law is largely invariant to multimodal data composition, and derives an efficiency frontier over model size, token count, and data mix.
**Why it matters**: Actionable scaling-law guidance for teams designing next-generation natively-multimodal foundation models rather than late-fusing vision onto LLMs.

### 5. A Roadmap to Impactful Pluralistic Alignment Research
**Authors**: Elinor Poole-Dayan, Jillian Fisher, Atoosa Kasirzadeh, Jacob Andreas, Mitchell Gordon, Michiel A. Bakker
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22305v1
**TL;DR**: An audit of frontier labs finds "no clear indication that production models [are] explicitly trained or tested" for pluralistic alignment, and the paper lays out concrete empirical goals and trade-off-aware methods to close that gap.
**Why it matters**: A rare empirical look at whether alignment research on representing diverse values has actually reached production systems — the answer is largely no.
