## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Mass Producing Training Data with Surprising Side Effects
**Authors**: Yaowen Ye, Jacob Steinhardt, Sarah Schwettmann, Cassidy Laidlaw
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06324
**TL;DR**: Isolated examples such as insecure-code data inducing broad misalignment have been found by hand. This paper automates the search for datasets with surprising side effects (DSSEs): they elicit a target behavior, look like ordinary post-training data, and surprise human auditors. Evolution, RL and subliminal-learning searches yield 6,575 DSSEs across 8 behaviors, many of which transfer across model families. Automated metrics track ML researchers' judgments, and frontier models beat humans at predicting the side effects.
**Why it matters**: It turns accidental generalization failures into a searchable testbed, which is what you need before you can predict training side effects at deploy time.

### 2. Rubric Curriculum RL: Exploiting the Generation-Verification Gap in Non-Verifiable Domains
**Authors**: Sumeet Ramesh Motwani, Tejas Krishnan, Alistair Letcher, Charles London, Suhaas Bhat, Huitian Jiao, Philip Torr, Riashat Islam, Christopher Summerfield, Jakob Foerster et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06244
**TL;DR**: RLVR works for math and code because answers can be checked. RcRL extends that recipe to open-ended work without new data, human labels or a stronger teacher: pairwise preferences over rollouts plus a curriculum over rubric criteria. Unlike stationary rubric judges, the objective stays non-stationary, so entropy does not collapse and training keeps improving past 1,000 steps. On creative writing it wins 70.5% of human comparisons against the base model; on HealthBench it gains 14.6% (25.4% on the consensus subset). ICML 2026.
**Why it matters**: It is a concrete path for RLVR-style gains in domains where ground-truth verification does not exist.

### 3. What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents?
**Authors**: Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Shangqing Tu, Tianyi Men, Yupu Hao, Jiachun Li, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06483
**TL;DR**: Multi-turn agentic RL looks strong on long-horizon tasks, but the gain is budget-sensitive. Measured with Pass@(K, T), trajectory-level RL advantages shrink when the turn budget tightens. The method does not uniformly raise single-step decision quality; it mainly improves error recovery and long-horizon resilience. BudgetMix RL, which varies interaction budgets during training, is more robust across execution settings.
**Why it matters**: It tells you what agentic RL actually buys, and why a model that looks good in a long-horizon eval can fail once the turn budget is realistic.

### 4. SafeStart: Mitigating the Overlooked Safety Cost of Active Expert Reduction in MoE LLM Serving
**Authors**: Erfan Baghaei Potraghloo, Saeid Shokoufa, Seyedarmin Azizi, Arya Fayyazi, Souvik Kundu, Mehdi Kamal, Massoud Pedram
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06147
**TL;DR**: Serving stacks speed up MoE models by running fewer experts per token and check the change on accuracy. Under a jailbreak that tells the model not to refuse, that change raises harmful compliance by 18.4 points on Qwen1.5-MoE, 9.5 on OLMoE and 6.6 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, while MMLU drops by less than two points. Most of the damage happens on the prompt and the first few tokens. SafeStart runs all experts for the prompt and the first eight generated tokens, then the reduced set, and keeps at least 85% of the decode speedup.
**Why it matters**: Faster MoE serving is already shipping; this shows those knobs must be tested on refusal, not only on MMLU.

### 5. D3D: Decoupled Diffusion Drafting for Asynchronous Speculative Decoding
**Authors**: Baoyou Chen, Ruiqiao Mei, Guancong Lin, Tao Song, Weihao Yuan, Qun Liu, Siyu Zhu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06425
**TL;DR**: Target-conditioned diffusion drafters still wait on the target model's hidden states, so drafting and verification stay sequential. D3D is a standalone diffusion drafter that uses blockwise denoising plus auxiliary autoregressive supervision, so it can draft the next block while the current one is verified. Layer pruning that drops 5 of 36 layers keeps more than 99% of mean acceptance length. Average speedups are 5.00× / 5.71× with Qwen3-8B / 32B targets (vs 4.03× / 3.67× for DFlash), and 7.48× on GSM8K with Qwen3-32B.
**Why it matters**: It removes the hidden-state lockstep that has capped diffusion speculative decoding.
