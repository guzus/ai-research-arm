## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Thinking Before Thinking: Scaling Agentic Inference Through Meta-Reasoning
**Authors**: Paras Dahal, Anton Bakhtin, Taco Cohen, Zhengxing Chen, Carole-Jean Wu, Rob Fergus, Scott Yih, Gabriel Synnaeve, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Sanjeev Arora, Jason Weston, Anirudh Goyal
**arXiv**: [2609.38147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38147)
**TL;DR**: Once an agent run is long enough, *control* — what to keep, whether to restart, when to stop — is its own reasoning problem. A controller consolidates a compact account of the run, scores next options against the remaining budget, and dispatches workers from persistent memory. On ProgramBench, GPT-5.5 hits 71.5% versus Codex at 58.0%; Opus 4.8 hits 67.2% versus Claude Code at 65.5%. Across abstract, long-horizon, and proof benches the same harness adds 3.6–4.2 points over a compute-matched Direct Control Agent, and keeps climbing after direct control plateaus.
**Why it matters**: Extra tokens on structured control beat extra tokens on more of the same work. The Meta FAIR + Arora + Weston lineup is treating execution policy as the new scaling axis.

### 2. Hidden Reasoning Must Leak, but Need Not Be Readable: Fundamental Opportunities and Limits for Chain-of-Thought Monitoring
**Authors**: Mohammadali Mohammadkhani, Madhava Krishna, Yash Sarrof, Michael Hahn
**arXiv**: [2609.37312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37312)
**TL;DR**: Simple covert computations can stay hidden in a thinking trace. Past a model-size threshold, solving the hidden task *must* leak a near-linear amount of information about its input into the CoT — an information-theoretic footprint, not a style cue. Under standard crypto assumptions a one-layer Transformer can encrypt that footprint online so no polynomial-time monitor can read it. Theory plus experiments map both sides: monitors get a guarantee on complexity, not on readability.
**Why it matters**: CoT monitoring is not a free lunch. Hard hidden work always leaves a mark; a capable model can still make that mark unreadable.

### 3. Pretraining Latent Information Feedback Transformers with Teacher Supervision
**Authors**: Dor Tirosh, Ido Amos, Mor Geva
**arXiv**: [2609.38149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38149)
**TL;DR**: Standard Transformers are feed-forward: the only deep-to-shallow path across steps is the decoded token. LIFT pairs each token with an information-dense state taken from an off-the-shelf LM's next-token distribution and trains the student to predict both the next token *and* the next state. States are precomputed, so pretraining stays fully parallel. At 135M–1B, LIFT beats token-matched Transformers on LM, reasoning, and procedural tasks; a tiny LIFT beats same-size Transformers trained on 8× the data on state tracking, even when the teacher itself fails the task.
**Why it matters**: Recurrent state does not require recurrent training. Teacher-forced latent feedback is a pretraining objective, not an inference hack.

### 4. When do data mixtures improve scaling laws? Insights from high-dimensional regression
**Authors**: Diyuan Wu, Lehan Chen, Theodor Misiakiewicz, Marco Mondelli
**arXiv**: [2609.38011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38011)
**TL;DR**: Mixing domains is not automatically more data. In a high-dimensional mixed-data regression model with shared target, heterogeneous covariances, and datasets that may grow at different rates, the theory yields explicit scaling laws in spectral decay, target regularity, and relative sample growth. Auxiliary data raises the *exponent* — not just the prefactor — only when spectra and relative sizes interlock. Language-model experiments show the same qualitative split: the right mix drops target-domain loss faster than either domain alone.
**Why it matters**: Data mixing has a phase diagram. Most blends add samples; only some blends change the law.

### 5. \(S^3\): Spectral Null-Space Swap Makes Reasoning Models Efficient
**Authors**: Hongbo Ma, Sansheng Cao, Jiajun Fan, Bangji Yang, Ge Liu
**arXiv**: [2609.37976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37976)
**TL;DR**: The extra reasoning capacity of a Thinking checkpoint lives in the null space of a projection defined by its Non-thinking twin's dominant singular directions. \(S^3\) is a training-free swap: keep the Non-thinking model inside its own dominant subspace and take the Thinking checkpoint outside it. Across 2B–30B dense and MoE models and 28 math / multimodal / audio benches, it cuts inference tokens 27.4% versus full Thinking and *raises* accuracy 1.0 point (HMMT25: +8.3% accuracy, 33% fewer tokens). Attention entropy drops in the retained component.
**Why it matters**: Thinking-mode bloat is a subspace you can leave behind. Composition of two checkpoints beats another length penalty.
