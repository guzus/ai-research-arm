## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Kolibri: A Sovereign European Model on the Pareto Frontier
**Authors**: Aleph Alpha (Moritz Althaus, Artur Baranowski, Michael Barlow, Fabien C. Y. Benureau et al.)
**arXiv**: [2610.31945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31945)
**TL;DR**: Friday's IQuest-Q1 was a 320B-A15B open CLI MoE. Kolibri is the other kind of open foundation: 78.1B total / 3.46B active (4.4%), English–German, Apache 2.0. Full stack controlled — data, architecture, infra, post-train, eval. 24T tokens across pre / mid / long-context; German is >20% by bytes, including >2T tokens they curated or synthesized. Post-train is SFT plus RL on >1.2M internal tasks (reasoning, tools, IF, code, retrieval). Among the open models they evaluate, it sits on the quality-vs-serving-cost Pareto in both languages. Kolibri Origin was the pipeline rehearsal.
**Why it matters**: The week's other open drop was built for the CLI. This one is built so a government or a plant can actually serve it.

### 2. Ψ-0.6: Adaptive Streams of Thought in a Unified Vision-Language World Model
**Authors**: Khai Loong Aw, Klemen Kotar, Baihan Zhang, Yifan Liu, Lilian Chen, Atlas Kazemian, Rahul Venkatesh, Daniel Yamins
**arXiv**: [2610.32177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32177)
**TL;DR**: Friday's video-as-world-ops survey said reading a clip is not keeping a world state. Ψ-0.6 puts perception, language, geometry, and simulation in one model and lets them take turns as *streams of thought* — images, text, and explicit geometry/motion tokens on a shared interface. Zero-shot it beats leading open-weight models on multi-view spatial reasoning, with extra lift from geometric intermediates. It can generate a counterfactual scene and ask whether moving an object blocks a path. RL on task feedback jointly trains *which* intermediate to emit; VQA gains transfer to unseen benches.
**Why it matters**: The missing piece was not another specialist. It was a model that chooses the representation it reasons through.

### 3. HyScale: Scaling Long-Context Training for Learned Sparse Attention
**Authors**: Yangsheng Deng, Xinjing Huang, Yulong Wang, Yunzhuo Liu, Jun Hu, Shuyong Tang, Junchi Zeng, Mingji Han, Ray Ying, Key Zhang, Luo Mai
**arXiv**: [2610.32057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32057)
**TL;DR**: Saturday's OpenAttention asked what to do with the unread tail at 1% read. This is how you *train* that architecture at a million tokens. Selection — score every prior token, then read *k* — is the bottleneck, and current stacks materialize a full score table and redo it on recompute. HyScale's claim: selection output is indices, not activations. HyTopK finds exact top-*k* without the table; selection replay reuses the forward indices in recompute. GLM-5.1 at 1M tokens: 2.07× vs Megatron-LM on 512 H20. CSA layers of DeepSeek-V4-Flash 2.01× and QSA layers of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 1.57× at 1M on 32 H800.
**Why it matters**: Learned sparse attention has been a production architecture since DeepSeek-V3.2. Training it at 1M tokens was still a Megatron tax.

### 4. Rewriting Scaling Laws for Synthetic Data with Compute and Data Constraints
**Authors**: Nicholas Roberts, Ahmed Ahmed, Suhas Kotha, Percy Liang, Yu Wang, Alex Renda, Nitya Mani
**arXiv**: [2610.31867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31867)
**TL;DR**: Friday's safety-probe and GenRM laws asked whether to buy labels or a bigger model, and whether a better judge is a better rewarder. This one is the Chinchilla sheet for *rewrites*: loss as a function of model size, training tokens, rewrite fraction, epochs over the natural data, and the compute spent generating the rewrites. Compute-limited (natural data plentiful, rewriter bigger than the run): the fitted optimal rewrite fraction *rises* with compute, from under 10%. The other setting asks whether a model trained on rewrites can beat the rewriter that made them.
**Why it matters**: "Rewrite the corpus" is now a line item. This is the first law that prices the rewriter against the pretrain.

### 5. When Agents Fail: Model or Harness--Who's to Blame?
**Authors**: Shao Zhang, Hangfan Zhang, Yuanyuan Shi, Hao Li, Min-Le Su, Kangcong Li et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.32483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32483)
**TL;DR**: Friday's own-harness ARC-AGI-3 paper asked what happens when the model writes the scaffold. This one asks who to blame when the cell is red. Five measures: Valid Completion Rate, Model-Harness Fit, Cross-Model Fit, Interaction Residuals, Token Load (including failed attempts). Four general harnesses — OpenCode, Codex CLI, Pi CLI, DeepAgents — plus two native model-harness systems, four core models, six benches (context, tools, long-horizon planning, multimodal, artifact handoff). 111 settings, 52,168 trajectories. Rankings swap across models and benches; similar task scores hide different execution profiles; task score and valid completion can pick different pairings.
**Why it matters**: An agent benchmark is a pairing. Publishing the model name and burying the harness is how we have been mis-ranking both.
