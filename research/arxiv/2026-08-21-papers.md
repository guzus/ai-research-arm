## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. EchoCoT: Extracting Hidden Chain-of-Thought from Large Reasoning Models
**Authors**: Yiting Qu, Ziqing Yang, Chi Cui, Ye Leng, Junjie Chu, Yang Zhang
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20055](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20055)
**TL;DR**: Identifies a previously overlooked "reasoning replay" surface between tool calls in black-box reasoning models and develops EchoCoT, a multi-step attack framework that iteratively extracts hidden chain-of-thought traces near-verbatim via API-returned fidelity signals, with an automated search for universal injection trajectories.
**Why it matters**: This is a serious proprietary-model disclosure risk — hidden CoT is a defended model asset, and this shows it can be extracted from frontrunners through black-box APIs, with direct safety/alignment implications.

### 2. Manifold Drift in Flow Preference Optimization: A Root Cause of Reward Hacking
**Authors**: Yansen Han, Shengyi Liao, Yuanxing Zhang, Pengfei Wan, Tao Lin
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20011)
**TL;DR**: Formalizes "manifold drift" — preference-optimization updates to flow-matching transport trajectories move terminal samples off the pretrained data manifold — and proves the mechanism mathematically, then proposes ThermoDPO, a temperature-controlled objective that anchors pairwise updates to the data manifold.
**Why it matters**: Offers a principled root-cause account and fix for reward hacking in flow-matching generators, one of the main barriers to reliable alignment.

### 3. Learning When to Think: Adaptive Reasoning for Test-Time Compute Allocation
**Authors**: Gijs Kassenaar, Zhao Yang, Vincent François-Lavet
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20256](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20256)
**TL;DR**: Trains a reasoning model to emit a grammatical first token choosing among NoThink/Short/Long reasoning modes, learned end-to-end inside GRPO with no separate router, so the model allocates compute adaptively per problem.
**Why it matters**: Directly targets the fixed-token-budget inefficiency of RL-trained reasoning models (over-thinking easy, under-thinking hard problems) — a clean test-time-compute efficiency result.

### 4. Let's Scale Step by Step: Compute-Efficient Hyperparameter Transfer for Large-Scale Mixture-of-Experts
**Authors**: Nayeon Kim, Hojin Lee, Yunju Bak, Jaesun Park, Boseop Kim
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20061](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20061)
**TL;DR**: Proposes a two-step framework that estimates optimal learning rates for large MoE models by transferring hyperparameters across scaling model widths (a μP-style adaptation for MoE with latent-attention) then extrapolating to trillion-token horizons, avoiding prohibitive sweep costs.
**Why it matters**: Tackles the open problem of compute-efficient hyperparameter transfer at extreme model scale in one of the most cost-effective architectures (MoE).

### 5. Orthogonal JEPA: Factorized Predictive States for Latent World Models
**Authors**: Taoyong Cui, Pheng Ann Heng, Wanli Ouyang
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20065](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20065)
**TL;DR**: Introduces orthogonal predictive factorization for latent world models, splitting the monolithic JEPA target/prediction pathway into orthogonal predictive subspaces so that dominant signals no longer drain capacity and gradients from less salient predictive structure.
**Why it matters**: A new architecture-level principle for world models that could improve representational quality and sample efficiency in model-based RL and planning.
