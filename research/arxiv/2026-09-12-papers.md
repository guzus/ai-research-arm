# arXiv AI Research - 2026-09-12

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Musec: MomentUm SpEctral Clipping for Stable Muon-type Training
**Authors**: Zhuanghua Liu, Menglian Wang, Luo Luo
**arXiv**: [2609.11655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11655)
**TL;DR**: Muon beats AdamW on LLM training, then spikes: spectral flattening sets every momentum singular value ≈1 and weights grow without bound. Architecture-level clips (weights, attention logits) miss whole components. Musec *clips* singular values above a threshold and keeps the rest of the spectrum; Soft Musec approximates that with coupled Newton–Schulz. First convergence proof for Muon-type methods in nonconvex nonsmooth stochastic opt. Soft Musec stays up at learning rates and sizes where other Muon variants diverge, and matches them when those variants are well-tuned.
**Why it matters**: The optimizer that is eating pretrain budgets now has an architecture-agnostic stabilizer — and a proof, not just a clip.

### 2. Vidu S2: Real-Time Interactive, Editable, and Spatial Video Generation
**Authors**: Jintao Zhang, Kai Jiang, … Jianfei Chen, Jun Zhu
**arXiv**: [2609.11638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11638)
**TL;DR**: Vidu S2-Avatar does real-time 720p digital-character video with references that can be swapped mid-stream and stronger instruction following (dance). Vidu S2-Editing restyles, re-clothes, re-casts, and re-backgrounds a live video stream. Both stacks get a first cut at real-time spatial video. The paper reports wins over all listed baselines; a playable demo is up.
**Why it matters**: Interactive video is no longer a batch job — 720p, editable, and spatially aware in one serving path.

### 3. Learnware and AI Model Management System
**Authors**: Zhi-Hua Zhou
**arXiv**: [2609.11656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11656)
**TL;DR**: Hugging Face-style pools are *model storage*. What is missing is a *management system* that identifies, reuses, and assembles models trained by different people, on different data, for different tasks — without the developer’s training set or the user’s raw data. Learnware = model + specification; the spec is produced by a published-reference ML process with a data-preservation property, so specs are comparable across independently trained models and can act as a collaboration protocol. The Learnware Dock System is the proposed runtime.
**Why it matters**: Zhou’s unit of reuse is not the checkpoint — it is a comparable spec. That is the DBMS move for models.

### 4. ORCH: Organizational Principles Enable Collective Intelligence in Embodied AI
**Authors**: Zhengran Ji, Jonathan Hyun, Boyuan Chen
**arXiv**: [2609.11737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11737)
**TL;DR**: Embodied multi-agent stacks usually freeze an org chart. ORCH builds a task-specific hierarchy: pooled interdependence where work can run concurrently, sequential interdependence where prerequisites rule. Across 25 wildfire missions (recon, rescue, transport, resources, containment, suppression) and teams of up to 50 heterogeneous agents on eight LLMs, human-designed ORCH beats four prior embodied MAS frameworks by +63.97% final score and +74.29% execution efficiency; LM-generated orgs still take +43.63% / +52.53%. Scale of the underlying LLM is not monotonic. Hierarchy keeps concurrency inside specialist groups while sequencing phase transitions.
**Why it matters**: Collective intelligence is an org-design problem. The org chart is the algorithm.

### 5. Characterizing Job Power Elasticity for Power-Flexible AI Training
**Authors**: Philip Colangelo, Charles Dawson, Shayan Sengupta, Ayse Coskun, Varun Sivaram
**arXiv**: [2609.11542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11542)
**TL;DR**: Power, not FLOPs, is the expansion constraint. Across 131 H200 LLM training runs (plus 24 H200 validation and 34 matched H100), dense and MoE, pretrain and finetune, up to 32 GPUs, jobs show large but *variable* throughput sensitivity to GPU power caps. The Power Flexibility Index (PFI) is a normalized, SLA-usable control primitive; runtime telemetry predicts it. Under a 30% power cut, PFI-aware allocation recovers ~1.5k tokens/s per job — 63% of the gap between equal-weight and an oracle.
**Why it matters**: Grid-responsive training needs a per-job elasticity number, not a cluster-wide cap.
