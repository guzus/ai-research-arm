## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm
**Authors**: Yuda Song, Alvin Zhang, Fahim Tajwar, Daman Arora, Jeff Schneider, Daniel Khashabi, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, J. Andrew Bagnell, Aarti Singh, Andrea Zanette
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564v1
**TL;DR**: Derives RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions. This puts pretraining, midtraining, SFT and RL under one likelihood-based algorithm, with staged training as a special case. A unified trainer that mixes static data and RL generations improves the accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage while preserving broader capabilities.
**Why it matters**: It could replace the staged LLM training pipeline with one principled recipe.

### 2. Algebraic World Model: From Exploration to Proofs
**Authors**: Guancheng Pan, Qixin Tan, Yang Yuan, Andrew Chi-Chih Yao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40503v1
**TL;DR**: An agent explores an unknown environment, writes executable hypotheses, simplifies them with category-theory-guided transformations, and formalizes them in Lean 4 to prove reachability and impossibility. It reports clearing all 183 levels of the 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games, with certified trajectories using 43% of the human baseline's actions.
**Why it matters**: It is a concrete step toward agents whose world models come with machine-checked guarantees.

### 3. How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference
**Authors**: Chanakya Ekbote, Artidoro Pagnoni, Devendra Singh Sachan, Shruti Bhosale, Mike Lewis, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325v1
**TL;DR**: A large study (up to 1B parameters, 155B tokens, 32 loops) of looped transformers. Looping the full model is best at a fixed parameter budget, and looping a middle block is best at a fixed FLOPs budget. Loop-specific low-rank adapters help, and a fix to Parallel Loop Transformers recovers quality while removing the decoding slowdown.
**Why it matters**: It gives practical scaling guidance for weight-shared depth under different compute constraints.

### 4. Efficient Scalable Oversight via Rewinding
**Authors**: Ziyi Guan, Yael Tauman Kalai, Ron Rothblum, Zoe Xi
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40535v1
**TL;DR**: Introduces rewindable interactive proofs, where a weak verifier can restore a prover AI's earlier state. This gives a relativizing protocol that needs no truthful-prover assumption and no cryptography. The prover runs in linear time and the verifier in nearly linear time after preprocessing, and a preliminary MNIST implementation is reported.
**Why it matters**: It offers a theoretically grounded and cheaper route to scalable oversight.

### 5. Understanding and Mitigating Role Spoofing in LLMs
**Authors**: Ezra Edelman, Benjamin Edelman, Surbhi Goel, Eran Malach
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40396v1
**TL;DR**: Shows that models track "who said what" from both content cues and structural cues (control tokens, formatting). Attacks that fake both are highly effective. Adversarial training against content-only injections leaves models open to structure-based ones, and linear probes on role representations detect spoofing.
**Why it matters**: It explains why prompt-injection defenses still fail against agents that read untrusted tool output.
