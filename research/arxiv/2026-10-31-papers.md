## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm
**Authors**: Yuda Song, Alvin Zhang, Fahim Tajwar, Daman Arora, Jeff Schneider, Daniel Khashabi, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, J. Andrew Bagnell, Aarti Singh, Andrea Zanette
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564
**TL;DR**: Writes RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions. Pretraining, midtraining, SFT and RL then become one likelihood-based "unified training" algorithm that mixes static data and RL generations in one trainer. It improves the accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage while keeping broader capabilities.
**Why it matters**: It challenges the staged pretrain/SFT/RL pipeline and recasts it as a data-mixture scheduling problem.

### 2. Algebraic World Model: From Exploration to Proofs
**Authors**: Guancheng Pan, Qixin Tan, Yang Yuan, Andrew Chi-Chih Yao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40503
**TL;DR**: An agent explores an unknown environment and writes executable Python hypotheses. It restructures them with category-theory-guided transformations and formalizes them in Lean 4. This lets it prove reachability and impossibility, including that a goal is unreachable. It replays winning trajectories for all 183 levels of the 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games.
**Why it matters**: It is a concrete route from interactive exploration to machine-checked guarantees about an unfamiliar world.

### 3. How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference
**Authors**: Chanakya Ekbote, Artidoro Pagnoni, Devendra Singh Sachan, Shruti Bhosale, Mike Lewis, Maryam Fazel-Zarandi, Shashank Rajput, Sujan Kumar Gonugondla
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325
**TL;DR**: A large study of looped transformers (up to 1B parameters, 155B tokens, 32 loops). Looping the full model is best at a fixed parameter budget, and looping a middle block is best at fixed FLOPs. Gains saturate or reverse at high loop counts. Loop-specific low-rank adapters help, and a fix to Parallel Loop Transformers recovers quality while removing the decoding slowdown.
**Why it matters**: It gives practical scaling guidance for depth reuse as a parameter-efficiency lever.

### 4. ShallowDefend: Harnessing Shallow Safety Alignment for Multi-Turn Defender
**Authors**: Qiyao Ma, Xiaodong Liu, Weiwei Yang, ..., Jianfeng Gao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40627
**TL;DR**: Multi-turn jailbreaks work because alignment is concentrated in the first few output tokens. The proposed Assess → Decide → Respond framework makes the model commit to an explicit, parseable safety decision first. That commitment is trained with RL and a rule-based self-verified reward. It gives the strongest multi-turn safety in a controlled comparison across 14 benchmarks while preserving utility.
**Why it matters**: It turns the known shallow-alignment weakness into a defense mechanism.

### 5. Gym-Anything for Robotics: Scaling physical simulations with digital agents
**Authors**: Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40565
**TL;DR**: Coding agents build robotics simulations through an adversarial creation-audit loop, producing RoboWorld (25 simulations, 200 RL environments). Frontier models average a reward of 0.06 on the test tasks. Privileged-teacher and self-assisted learning, plus code policies that generate demonstrations for VLAs such as π0.5, improve results substantially.
**Why it matters**: It makes simulation creation a coding-agent task, so progress in coding agents carries over to embodied AI, and it supplies a hard benchmark.
