# arXiv AI Research - 2026-09-10

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. How Fragile Is Safety Alignment at Frontier Scale? A Single-Direction Attack on a 320B MoE
**Authors**: Yi Shi, Tanyu Chen, Kai Shen
**arXiv**: [2609.09793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793)
**TL;DR**: Directional ablation of a single refusal direction was a dense-model result (to ~70B). It still works on GLM-5.3-Flash (320B, 288 routed experts, four-wide hyper-connection residual, block-FP8) — but not where the original recipe looks. Editing attention, dense, or routed-expert writers alone removes 0.039 / 0.016 / 0.148 of refusal; all three together remove 0.776. 74% of the effect exists only under the joint edit, so a module-name-matched recipe that reaches 0.066 of that 0.776 fails silently. A random orthogonal direction does nothing. Violence / sexual / hate subspaces leave a residue at every rank 1–12. Across seven harmful benches: 41–89 pp less refusal, no detected capability change.
**Why it matters**: Open-weight MoE alignment is not a new refusal geometry — it is a split write that a dense-era attack misses unless you edit every writer at once.

### 2. Through the Looking Glass: Directly Reading and Writing Transformers
**Authors**: Mark Oskin
**arXiv**: [2609.10210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10210)
**TL;DR**: Counted by absolute contribution, a token rides on thousands to hundreds of thousands of units and channels. Contributions are signed: across 18 models the mass pushing *away* from the predicted token is a median 7× the mass carrying it. Divide by the net and the count is dozens — 53 components carry 90% of a prediction, 13 it cannot survive losing, 8 suffice alone. On 12 models trained elsewhere (124M–7B) the sufficient set is 2–16 components, 1–3% of the model, a share that does not grow with size. Three quarters of a layer update is a fixed linear map of the state it received. Nothing is trained: a name can be installed into one spare unit for a quarter of a percent of held-out loss, a fortieth of a rank-one update.
**Why it matters**: A prediction is a small, nameable circuit — and because it is nameable, it can be written, not just observed.

### 3. Programmable World Model
**Authors**: Zheng-Hui Huang, Guixu Lin, … Kaipeng Zhang, Zhixiang Wang
**arXiv**: [2609.10540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10540)
**TL;DR**: Interactive video world models look real and forget the rules. PWM splits the job: an agent compiles natural-language instructions into programs over entity state and transitions; a lightweight engine keeps a persistent global state, including off-screen entities and non-visual attributes; state-augmented 3D oriented bounding boxes plus a camera trajectory compile into pixel-aligned conditioning for a pretrained video renderer. CombatStateBench: 94% Count Accuracy and 98% State Accuracy, well above existing interactive video world models, with coherent long-horizon generation.
**Why it matters**: Persistence and mechanics are a program, not a hope that the pixels will remember.

### 4. TRACE: Training Reasoning Agents for Causal Exploration with Synthesized Rewards
**Authors**: Rui Sun, Zhan Shi, Bing He
**arXiv**: [2609.10315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10315)
**TL;DR**: RLVR needs a cheap, sound check. Diagnosis does not have one. TRACE engineers the missing verifier: sample an intervention, inject it into a controlled advertising simulator (12 root causes + segment attribution), and use the hidden intervention as the oracle. The agent still has to hunt with Python and SQL through noisy, confounded, distributed evidence. On a held-out 235-episode set, Claude Opus 5 is the strongest prompted baseline at 0.686 FullAttr@1. SFT lifts Qwen3.5-35B-A3B from 0.159 to 0.637; RL on the synthesized reward reaches 0.757 — above every prompted baseline, including the 122B sibling — and with fewer tool calls.
**Why it matters**: For diagnostic reasoning, a scalable objective reward can matter more than model scale.

### 5. IdeaAMBIG: Benchmarking Implementation-Critical Gaps in Research-Idea Specifications
**Authors**: Yiling Ma, Yilun Zhao, … Manasi Patwardhan, Arman Cohan
**arXiv**: [2609.10539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10539)
**TL;DR**: A research idea can be novel and still unimplementable. IdeaAMBIG scores *codification readiness*: 660 evidence-grounded gaps (163 real from repro reports and GitHub issues; 497 synthetic injections into ready specs). Three tasks — readiness, defect localization, clarification. Across 13 LLMs the best model recovers 9.6% of real-world defects but, given the defect, writes a successful clarification 80.6% of the time. An oracle resolution lifts downstream ready-to-code rate from 14% to 98%. Localization is the bottleneck in every model.
**Why it matters**: Coding agents do not fail at writing the method. They fail at noticing the method was never specified.
