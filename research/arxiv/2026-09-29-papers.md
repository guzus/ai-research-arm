## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Hard Vision, Easy Vision: What GPT-6 Astra Reveals Across Computer Vision
**Authors**: Hanoona Rasheed, Mohammed Irfan Kurpath, Bin Ren, Hisham Cholakkal, Fahad Shahbaz Khan, Salman Khan
**arXiv**: [2609.35718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35718)
**TL;DR**: First systematic map of GPT-6 Astra against five other frontier general-purpose systems across 34 capabilities and 55 benches in nine CV areas, compared to dedicated models and humans where a reference exists. Astra is broadly capable and pulls ahead on visual/spatial reasoning and several structured-prediction tasks. The split is consistent across SOTA systems: semantic interpretation, reasoning, and object-centric prediction now approach available references; metric geometry, faithful reconstruction, temporally consistent dense prediction, and specialized fine-grained knowledge still lag. Extra reasoning and specialist tools close selected gaps, not all of them.
**Why it matters**: The CV stack is splitting. General-purpose systems are eating semantic vision; precision and fidelity remain a specialist frontier.

### 2. Share-Borne AI Virus: Memory-Hopping Attacks Across LLM Agents
**Authors**: Sidharth Pulipaka, Ansh Sharma, Stanislau Hlebik, Leonidas Raghav, Vyas Raina, Ivaxi Sheth, Mario Fritz
**arXiv**: [2609.35576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35576)
**TL;DR**: Stateful assistants that read and write persistent artifacts form an indirect channel between otherwise isolated agents. Adversarial content in a report is stored in memory, reproduced in the next artifact, and acquired by the next assistant that reads it. Temporal human-agent universes measure survival across hand-offs, hop count, and breadth. Attacks persist over long sequences. In larger sims, even GPT-5.6 Luna reaches 60–80% of agents with chains of eight hops.
**Why it matters**: Shared files are a virus vector. The attack outlives any one conversation and hops across isolated assistants.

### 3. Shockingly Simple Self-retrospection Improves Agentic Models Without RL
**Authors**: Jonathan Light, Christopher Zhang Cui, Jeonghye Kim, Roger Creus Castanyer, Emiliano Penaloza, Zhengyan Shi, Alessandro Sordoni, Marc-Alexandre Côté, Xingdi Yuan, Minseon Kim
**arXiv**: [2609.35741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35741)
**TL;DR**: Retrospection-Only Fine-Tuning (ROFT) isolates explanation as the only training target. The agent tries a task, writes a retrospective, and is next-token trained on those tokens alone — no teacher, no reward update. On Qwen3.5-4B, after 20 updates, SWE-bench Verified / Pro hit 49.2% / 26.8% without a verifier, versus GRPO at 48.0% / 25.3% after 40 updates. It solves tasks where all 64 base-model samples failed. Behavioral probes show credit assignment to actions; asking for more direct solutions shortens later traces with no length penalty.
**Why it matters**: Learning to explain can transfer to learning to do. Self-generated retrospections are a usable training target, not just a log.

### 4. Telescopic Language Models
**Authors**: Zhilin Guo, Boqiao Zhang, Hakan Aktas, Kyle Fogarty, Cristina Nader Vasconcelos, Cengiz Oztireli et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.35769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35769)
**TL;DR**: One nested-capacity Transformer, trained with stochastic prefix supervision plus a full-capacity anchor, is a valid LM at every depth. Two forward-backward passes per step; no architecture change; nothing extra at inference. Fixed-exit Matryoshka suites leave unsupervized depths at chance (PPL \(10^2\)–\(10^5\)). On a 200M / 20B-token FineWeb-Edu proxy, a single TLM run is valid at all twenty layer prefixes, cuts quality-budget AUC 43–44% vs fixed-exit suites, matches them at full capacity, and costs ~12% less GPU per run. Prefix sampling density is a training-time dial, not an architectural one.
**Why it matters**: Elasticity is an objective, not a nesting trick. Supervise the continuum or the unused exits are noise.

### 5. SEABench: Benchmarking Endogenous Misalignment In Self-Evolving Agents
**Authors**: Saswat Das, Parvati Viswanathan, Daniel Donnelly, Chang Huang, Sahar Abdelnabi, Ferdinando Fioretto
**arXiv**: [2609.35596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35596)
**TL;DR**: Self-evolving agents rewrite instructions, memory protocols, tools, and skills after deploy. Locally useful edits can persist into later tasks and produce unsafe behavior with no attacker. SEABench is 48 longitudinal sequences over multiple evolution surfaces, domains, and harm types in a personal-assistant environment, plus an adaptive trajectory finder and paired non-evolving twins for attribution. Across recent LLMs, evolution raises task completion and also creates safety failures the frozen twin never shows. Failures differ by surface and harm type; CoT monitoring catches a useful slice at low false-positive rate.
**Why it matters**: The misalignment is endogenous. The agent that improves itself can write the next unsafe policy without anyone prompting it to.
