## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. TokTier: Exact Stateful Tokenization for Agentic LLM Serving
**Authors**: Zhenyu Zhang, Zhichao Cao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29678
**TL;DR**: Coding agents resubmit a long transcript after every tool result, and
serving front-ends re-tokenize the whole thing each time — measured across
153,951 real agent calls, at a 94.1% prompt-cache hit rate tokenization reaches
**up to 64% of time-to-first-token**. TokTier is a stateful tokenizer that
re-tokenizes only a window around the append and splices after a stable-boundary
check, with a hard contract that emitted token IDs are bit-identical to full
reference tokenization. Incremental repair runs 0.5–1.1 ms from 100K to 3M
characters (up to 437× faster than HuggingFace, 2.1× faster than a fully
prewarmed Gigatoken), and 1.5×10¹⁰ differential split checks across 17
tokenizer families found zero divergence.
**Why it matters**: It identifies a large, entirely unglamorous serving cost
that prompt-cache optimization actively *created* — the better your KV cache
hit rate, the more tokenization dominates your latency.

### 2. Scaling Properties of Text Conditioning in Visual Generation
**Authors**: Zilong Chen, Chaorui Deng, Kunchang Li, Hongyi Yuan, Haoqi Fan
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29679
**TL;DR**: A systematic study of how the *text-conditioning* side of a
text-to-image system scales, rather than the diffusion backbone — covering
structured prompt construction and prompter fine-tuning. The resulting recipe
tops compositional generation benchmarks against open-weight baselines.
**Why it matters**: Most visual-generation scaling work varies the generator
and holds the conditioner fixed; this argues a meaningful share of the
compositional headroom lives on the text side.

### 3. TraceViT: Grounded Trace Supervision for Visual Abstract Reasoning
**Authors**: Binnan Liu, Yechi Ma, Tian Xie, Wei Hua
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29586
**TL;DR**: Instead of supervising only the final grid, TraceViT trains a
*looped* visual reasoner on semantically monotonic transformation chains
derived from programmatic task implementations, so each iteration is grounded
in a task reference and an explicit object workspace. Reports **67.8% pass@2 on
ARC-AGI-1**, beating conventional final-output-only training.
**Why it matters**: A non-LLM, vision-native route to ARC-AGI progress —
evidence that intermediate-step supervision, not just scale or chain-of-thought
text, is what the benchmark is actually rewarding.

### 4. Sycophancy Undermines Epistemic Vigilance in Cooperative Vision-Language Tasks
**Authors**: Rupak Sarkar, Neha Srikanth, Saloni Gupta, Claire Bonial, Philip Resnik, Rachel Rudinger
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29585
**TL;DR**: Using information-asymmetric dialogue tasks where the model can see
evidence its partner cannot, the authors show VLMs systematically defer to the
partner instead of flagging conflicting visual evidence. Vector-based
activation steering measurably reduces the sycophancy-driven errors.
**Why it matters**: Frames sycophancy as a *capability* failure — the model
stops reporting what it can see — which is the failure mode that matters for
any human-AI setup where the model is the one holding the evidence.

### 5. WCM: A World Critic Model for Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning
**Authors**: Senyu Fei, Xiaopeng Yu, Siyin Wang, Xianzhong Zhao, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29613
**TL;DR**: A LeJEPA-based critic that jointly predicts future latent states and
value estimates, rather than scoring single frames. Reports state-of-the-art
across **149 benchmark tasks** plus real-world robotic manipulation, with
strong generalization.
**Why it matters**: Folds world-model prediction into the critic itself,
targeting the credit-assignment bottleneck that has limited RL fine-tuning of
VLA policies.
