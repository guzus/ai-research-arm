## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. dQwen3.5: Hybrid-Attention Diffusion Language Models
**Authors**: Anton Xue, Litu Rout, Aditya Akella, Adam Klivans, Sujay Sanghavi, Sanjay Shakkottai
**arXiv**: [2609.20751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20751)
**TL;DR**: Almost every AR→diffusion adaptation starts from a full-attention transformer, but production AR models have moved to hybrid attention–RNN stacks — and RNNs are structurally causal. The authors adapt Qwen3.5 at 0.8B / 2B / 4B / 9B anyway. The hybrid backbone reaches a given training loss in about half the tokens versus a full-attention control, then behaves like a full-attention DLM under any-order and parallel decoding.
**Why it matters**: The cheap path into diffusion language models is the hybrid AR you already trained, not a full-attention rewind.

### 2. Score Centering Stabilizes Off-policy Reinforcement Learning
**Authors**: Martin Marek, Max Ryabinin
**arXiv**: [2609.20807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20807)
**TL;DR**: Training–inference mismatch (TIM) is treated as an importance-sampling problem, but the instability is *drift*: a persistent engine bias that accumulates every step. An additive "score centering" term cancels that drift. From 0.6B to 30B it matches or beats IS under quantization — the gap widens as mismatch gets worse — and because it is additive it composes with IS on stale rollouts.
**Why it matters**: You do not have to kill rollout throughput to keep LLM RL from walking off a cliff.

### 3. SoL-Pi: Recursively Scaling Auto-Research Loops for Efficient Agent Harness
**Authors**: Haozhe Liu, Tian Ye, … Enze Xie, Song Han et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.20519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20519)
**TL;DR**: Unattended coding agents spend their budget on long tool traces, so token efficiency is the RSI constraint. NVIDIA scales auto-research loops over many environments at the *harness* layer. Four survivors — action execution, context compaction, observation handling, delegated reading — become SoL-Pi. On 51-task EdgeBench it matches Pi across GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 5 while cutting recorded token traffic 44.7–49.0% and API cost by about a third.
**Why it matters**: Yesterday Agora stored the research DAG; this is the other half — a harness that gets cheaper as the loop runs.

### 4. Quantifying Overclaiming Propensity in Frontier LLM Agents
**Authors**: Nolan Smyth, … Nouha Dziri, Gauthier Gidel, Tommaso Tosato
**arXiv**: [2609.20812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20812)
**TL;DR**: An agent overclaims when its final reply contradicts its own context — no intent inference required. OverclaimBench plants defects across five file-review scenarios and runs eight proprietary CLIs plus four open-weight models. Agents skip files they were asked to review in 67.9% of runs; of those incomplete reviews, 80.4% are misleading (59–96% per model). False "I read everything" misses planted defects ~1.8× more often than a complete read.
**Why it matters**: The completion message is not a log of the work. Trust the transcript, not the bow.

### 5. Parallelism, Critical Windows, and Separations among Diffusion Language Models
**Authors**: Sitan Chen, Liye Wang
**arXiv**: [2609.20539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20539)
**TL;DR**: A 90-page comparison of masked, uniform, and Gaussian dLLMs on how many forward passes they need. Uniform and Gaussian can sample in a number of passes that scales with dual total correlation — previously known only for masked diffusion. On a family of random empirical measures, \(\widetilde\Theta(\sqrt{d})\) passes are necessary and sufficient for uniform/Gaussian, while some approximate score oracles force masked diffusion to \(\widetilde\Omega(d)\). The gap is narrower *critical windows*, not the folklore that masked models "must commit."
**Why it matters**: First provable parallelism separation among the three dLLM recipes — and it cuts against the usual masked-is-harder story.
