## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Scaling Trajectories for Complex Tasks through Recursive Self-Rewrite
**Authors**: Zongxia Li, Yucheng Shi, Zhongzhi Li, Junyao Yang, Ruhan Wang, Chengsong Huang, Fuxiao Liu, Haitao Mi, Jordan Boyd-Graber, Leowei Liang
**arXiv**: [2610.02826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02826)
**TL;DR**: Friday's papers said the harness is the product. RSR says the harness is a *discovery* tool, not a deployment crutch. One 27B base (Qwen-3.8-27B) solves hard terminal tasks under three specialist harnesses — jointly 34.3% more tasks than the best single harness — then a planner, critic, and executor rewrite those wins as trajectories a *general* harness can run. Leakage is screened; the student never sees the privileged scaffold at test. 2,001 source traces become 11,094 rewritten ones. Pass@3: Terminal-Bench 2 57.0→74.2, Terminal-Bench 4 1.5→9.1, a self-curated Hard split 39.0→63.0. Direct trajectory SFT loses to the rewrite.
**Why it matters**: Harness-assisted success is not a capability until you can replay it without the harness.

### 2. All Work And No Play Makes Jack a Dull Boy: Understanding and Preventing Catastrophic Strategy Collapse in RLVR
**Authors**: Qiyuan Huang, Tianshi Xu, Meng Li
**arXiv**: [2610.02835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02835)
**TL;DR**: Late GRPO collapse is not "the model got picky." Prompt probes show distinct reasoning strategies becoming *inaccessible* — a contraction of strategy capacity, not a prune of bad ones. The authors prove standard RLVR objectives concentrate mass onto a single strategy, while nontrivial accuracy needs a minimum capacity; the collision is the collapse. Mirrored Entanglement Index (MEI) is the cheap online warning. Mesh Learning keeps several strategies in the update and stops any one from dominating. Across AIME 25/26, MATH-500, GPQA, and LiveCodeBench: up to +13.4 on Qwen and +11.5 on Phi.
**Why it matters**: If RLVR is allowed to pick a favorite strategy, it will, and then it cannot reach the rest of the bench.

### 3. Large Language Continuous Diffusion Models
**Authors**: Zhihan Yang, Wei Guo, Jean-Marie Lemercier, Simon Welker, Yonggan Fu, Mohammad Mahdi Kamani, Sajad Norouzi, Julius Berner, Tomas Geffner, Karsten Kreis, Yongxin Chen, Molei Tao, John Thickstun, Pavlo Molchanov, Ante Jukić, Arash Vahdat, Morteza Mardani
**arXiv**: [2610.02665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02665)
**TL;DR**: Discrete diffusion LMs decode fast and steer poorly: the token simplex is not a space you can push around. Sigma is the first large continuous dLM (3B/8B). It lives on low-dimensional ODE/SDE latent trajectories, warm-starts from AR weights, and jointly denoises Gaussian-corrupted embeddings while learning the embedding geometry. Classifier-free guidance and score temperature turn out to be load-bearing for math and code. After pre-training it matches discrete dLMs and AR baselines on GSM8K, Minerva, HumanEval, MBPP; after SFT it is competitive on MATH-500 and AIME. Continuous trajectories give a real quality–diversity knob (pass@k) and degrade gracefully at low NFE.
**Why it matters**: If you want to *steer* a diffusion language model, the latent has to be continuous.

### 4. Recursive Harness Self-Improvement for Frontier Reasoning Data Synthesis
**Authors**: Wenlong Zhang, Zhengbo Jiao, Chenxu Zhang, Lekang Jiang, SiYuan Ma, Qituan Zhang, Guo Chen, Linfeng Zhang
**arXiv**: [2610.03548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03548)
**TL;DR**: Recursing on *tasks* while leaving the synthesis harness fixed is how you generate more of the same difficulty. Task-harness co-evolution (RSI) updates skills, prompts, and workflows from solver failures — online during generation and after each batch — and keeps an edit only when the next batch is harder at bounded extra cost. Weights and verifiers stay frozen. Mean solver accuracy falls 100.0→54.8 over fourteen rounds (that is the point). A 27B student fine-tuned on 10K of the resulting math items hits 62.5% mean-16 on APEX. Both update schedules together beat fixed-harness recursion and either schedule alone.
**Why it matters**: Harder reasoning data is a property of the generator's scaffold, not of the seed problems.

### 5. Text-Centric Post-Training for Omni-Modal Reasoning
**Authors**: Ziyang Cheng, Yuhao Wang, Hongcheng Liu, Qimin Wu, Jingru Fan, Chen Qian, Yanfeng Wang, Yu Wang
**arXiv**: [2610.02819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02819)
**TL;DR**: Omni models fail multi-hop AV questions they can answer hop-by-hop. Perception and reasoning are being optimized in different places. The cheap fix is almost embarrassing: text-only reasoning SFT + RL lifts Qwen2.5-Omni-7B's geometric mean of nine reasoning scores by 25.83% and uses 56.6% fewer GPU-hours than the full native audio-visual route. Data synthesized by a *text-only* LLM still gains 21.01%. Text-only training does hurt perception; a short native AV RL pass with ~90% fewer input tokens puts perception back above the base and keeps 93.5% of the reasoning gain.
**Why it matters**: Most of "omni reasoning" is still a language-model problem. Spend the AV compute on the last mile.
