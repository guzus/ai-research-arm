# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-10

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Skaling: Chinchilla's Exponents Meet Kaplan's Coupling
**Authors**: Mathurin Videau, Badr Youbi-Idrissi, David Lopez-Paz, Kartik Ahuja
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07222
**TL;DR**: Proposes the Skaling law, a generalized scaling-law functional form that couples model capacity and training data through a single interaction exponent instead of treating them as independent. It cuts loss-prediction MAPE by 1.5-3x in both interpolation and extrapolation regimes, and a sparse-grid strategy gives accurate full-grid extrapolation from low-compute runs using ~10x less compute than uniform sweeps.
**Why it matters**: Fixes a systematic blind spot in both canonical scaling-law families and makes small-scale training far more predictive of large-model loss.

### 2. Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits
**Authors**: Elena Dumitrescu, Gert Lek, Lydia Y. Chen, Jérémie Decouchant
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07430
**TL;DR**: Shows safety alignment in diffusion LLMs (DLLMs) is sparse and transferable across architectures: self-pruning or transfer-pruning of safety neurons inherited from autoregressive predecessors raises attack success rates from ~2% to 73.8% on LLaDA, 86.6% on Dream, and 86.3% on Fast-dLLM. Introduces SN-Guided Diffusion, a neuron-guided attack exploiting these mechanisms.
**Why it matters**: Reveals an architecture-level alignment vulnerability unique to diffusion-based LLMs that standard red-teaming and prior autoregressive safety analysis miss.

### 3. UniJEPA: A Unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Task-Agnostic Visual World Modeling
**Authors**: An Lanji, Dawei Liu, Jin Li, Haoran Xu, Mei Chen, Yu Tian
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07409
**TL;DR**: Unifies photometric (image-level) and temporal (video-level) next-state prediction in a single shared latent space and one end-to-end JEPA objective, consolidating the fragmented I-JEPA / Image-World-Models / V-JEPA family of recipes into one encoder-predictor with a shared anti-collapse regularizer.
**Why it matters**: A step toward one task-agnostic visual world model instead of separate image- and video-scale models with duplicated components.

### 4. SimWAM: A Simple World Action Model for End-to-End Autonomous Driving
**Authors**: Zongchuang Zhao, Xin Zhou, Tianyang Xu, Zhengyang Sun, Kaixuan Zhou, Honglin Li, Dingkang Liang, Xiang Bai
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07468
**TL;DR**: Co-trains a pretrained video-expert and a lightweight action-expert with joint flow matching, but an isolated attention mask keeps action prediction independent of future frames, so the video branch is discarded after training. The result is a self-contained planner that directly predicts trajectories with no costly future-frame generation at inference.
**Why it matters**: Decouples video-dynamics prior transfer from inference cost, turning video generation into a pure training signal for driving planners.

### 5. CoBa: Cost-Effective Test-Time Scaling via Compute-Balanced Routing
**Authors**: Yan Zhou, Yue Ouyang, Kaiyang Zheng, Suncheng Xiang
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07424
**TL;DR**: Frames test-time reasoning as a compute-allocation problem over generation, verification, and stopping, and learns a routing policy that verifies cheaply and broadly while escalating uncertain or high-value candidates to stronger verification. On MATH-500, AIME 2024/2025, and AMC 2023 it reaches 85.13% macro accuracy, statistically matching a self-evaluation weighted-voting proxy at 49.1% fewer parameters.
**Why it matters**: One of the first principled treatments of how to spend a fixed test-time budget across competing scaling axes.
