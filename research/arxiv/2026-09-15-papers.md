## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. ZGCM-1: A Fully Open and Extremely Efficient Foundation Model for Math and Agentic Search
**Authors**: Jiyan He, Guang Liang, Hao Liu, … Shuxin Zheng, Tie-Yan Liu, Taine Zhao, Yatao Li et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.13356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13356)
**TL;DR**: A 7B dense model trained from scratch under the premise that compact models cannot memorize the web and must couple internal thinking with tool use. Recipe: interleaved gated sliding-window + full attention, a stable FP8 Muon optimizer, context curriculum 16K→64K→256K, and interaction traces recast as MDPs. On several math-reasoning and agentic-search suites it stays competitive with Qwen3-235B-A22B and GLM-5.1. Pretraining design is ~4.2× faster to a 16K loss target. Weights (pre/mid/post + intermediate ckpts), code, per-stage data recipes, and W&B logs are released.
**Why it matters**: An actually-open 7B recipe that treats tools as capacity, not a chat add-on — and posts numbers against 200B-class systems.

### 2. When Agents Slow Down: Understanding LLM Agents' Test-Time Strategies via Elo-per-token Analysis
**Authors**: Kaiyuan Liu, Qiuyang Mang, Bo Peng, … Luke Zettlemoyer, Alex Dimakis, Alvin Cheung
**arXiv**: [2609.15309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15309)
**TL;DR**: Agent test-time compute is not a single decode. Elo-per-token tracks the best intermediate submission at each token budget and aggregates across tasks with Bradley–Terry. Independent sampling is the reference: Elo grows linearly with log compute. Agents beat that reference early, then their marginal Elo falls below it. Historical AtCoder humans improve *superlinearly* over contest time. Define the scaling inflection as the per-session budget where agent slope matches iid sampling; splitting 100M tokens across parallel sessions on FrontierCS Polyomino Packing gains +264 Elo vs one long session and +355 vs ten short ones.
**Why it matters**: The first scaling law that treats an agent's stop/revise/tool loop as the object — and it says long single sessions waste the tail.

### 3. Stellar Colosseum: A Many-Agent Harness for Long-Horizon Research in Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science
**Authors**: Honghao Lin, David P. Woodruff, Yuan Deng, Jieming Mao, Song Zuo, Vahab Mirrokni
**arXiv**: [2609.15983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15983)
**TL;DR**: A model-agnostic research harness: explore strategies before proving, gate readiness, decompose into interdependent section-level subproblems, and route verifier findings back to the affected argument. Candidates are generated in parallel, attacked, and merged by overlapping random-sample tree aggregation. Wired into Google Antigravity's Teamwork as the Long Proof pattern. With Gemini 3.1 Pro: several new results on open problems from FOCS and JMLR. On TCS-Bench (research-level theorems from FOCS/STOC/SODA): 71.0% with Gemini 3.1 Pro + 3.7 Flash. Codeforces: 218/222 with the proof-oriented pipeline plus execution feedback.
**Why it matters**: A many-agent proof harness that is already producing venue-level TCS results, not another short-proof bench.

### 4. Lightning Weave: Improving the Accuracy-Efficiency Frontier of Reasoning Models through Capability Composition
**Authors**: Yecheng Wu, Song Han, Han Cai
**arXiv**: [2609.14708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14708)
**TL;DR**: Independently post-trained specialists already own different accuracy/efficiency corners. Lightning Weave treats each as a policy shift from the pre-post-train model, aligns log-ratio shifts at shared student token states, and trains with Tilted-Target DOPD so cached signals become a stable target — no live multi-teacher serving. On Qwen3.5-4B: HMMT 2025 59.2% → 64.0% with 10.7% fewer response tokens; LiveCodeBench v5 41.7% → 54.2% with 9.6% fewer tokens. Mixing anchor strengths traces an empirical accuracy–efficiency Pareto.
**Why it matters**: Efficient reasoning is composition of already-trained specialists, not another long-CoT RL run.

### 5. Corrupt Plans, Clean Traces: Evading Chain-of-Thought Monitoring with Plan Injection
**Authors**: Keertana Chidambaram, Andrew Ilyas, Vasilis Syrgkanis
**arXiv**: [2609.15989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15989)
**TL;DR**: Planting harmful but benign-sounding reasoning in an actor's context steers adversarial actions while the CoT monitor stays clean — "plan injection." Scales to harder monitorability benches (25–33% evasion) and to DeepSeek-R1. Actors paraphrase the injected plan as their own, with no attribution. Extra monitor resources can *hurt*: giving the monitor the injected plan drops detection by as much as 50% on Bio-Math; extra thinking tokens are spent rationalizing the plan rather than flagging it.
**Why it matters**: CoT monitoring is not a sealed audit. Context can author the "honest" trace the monitor is graded on.
