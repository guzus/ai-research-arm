## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Library of Layers (LoL): Adaptively Ordering Transformer Layers at Runtime
**Authors**: Abdellah Rahmani, Sean McLeish, Ang Li, Sanae Lotfi, Pascal Frossard, Micah Goldblum
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03526
**TL;DR**: LoL keeps a global library of transformer blocks and picks the order in which to run them at inference time. Its single hyperparameter, the library size, moves it between parameter reuse and sparsity, so dense, depth-recurrent and MoE models become one family. It is competitive with all three baselines in pretraining. Routing patterns differ by solution strategy (Sudoku, mazes), by language and script, and between safe and unsafe text.
**Why it matters**: It is a concrete step toward models that compose their own computation graph, which could give more efficient and more controllable reasoning.

### 2. VeriHarm-Bench: Measuring Harmful Success in LLM Coding Agents Verifiably
**Authors**: Wei-Lin Chen, Zhepei Wei, Xinyu Zhu, Yu-Min Tseng, Tzu-Han Lin, Yu Meng
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03575
**TL;DR**: VeriHarm builds programmatically verifiable benchmarks for "harmful success", where a coding agent finishes the benign task and also causes unintended harm such as deleting files or weakening permissions. Frontier agents reach about 95% task accuracy but only about 37% safe success.
**Why it matters**: Task-success metrics alone substantially understate the safety risk of agents that have shell access.

### 3. Pretraining Data Scale Reverses the Muon-AdamW Fine-Tuning Gap
**Authors**: Catherine Li, Jingchu Gai, Andrej Risteski, Jacob Mitchell Springer, Aditi Raghunathan
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03633
**TL;DR**: Muon's pretraining speedup does not carry over to fine-tuning. Past a large enough pretraining token budget, AdamW fine-tunes better, and the crossover comes at smaller token-to-parameter ratios as models grow. The authors explain this with a "margin-to-spread ratio" and prove it in a linear associative-memory setting.
**Why it matters**: Optimizer choice should be judged on downstream behavior, not only on pretraining loss.

### 4. A Scaling Theory of Multi-Epoch Pretraining: Late-Time Overfitting and Ensembling
**Authors**: Zhen Yang, Yidi Miao, Blake Bordelon
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03590
**TL;DR**: When pretraining is bound by data rather than compute, ensembling reduces late-training deterioration and delays optimal stopping. A dynamical mean-field theory gives compute-allocation laws between ensemble size and training time. Logit-averaged ensembles on FineWeb can beat wider or deeper single models at matched budgets.
**Why it matters**: It offers principled scaling guidance for the data-limited regime that frontier pretraining is entering.

### 5. Strategist: Evolving Reasoning with Agents
**Authors**: Taiming Lu, Qi Xu, Zhigang Hua, Zhuang Liu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03618
**TL;DR**: Strategist is a meta-agent that writes, tests and revises inference strategies as reusable executable blueprints stored in a growing library. Across 30 benchmarks it beats the strongest baseline on 24 and matches its average accuracy (74.7% vs 74.0%) at 39% lower inference cost. The strategies transfer to unseen tasks and other models.
**Why it matters**: It automates the inference-time scaffold that is usually written by hand, and the gains accumulate across tasks.
