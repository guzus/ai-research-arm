## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. LimiX-2: A Contextual Mechanism Network Towards General Structured-Data Intelligence
**Authors**: Xingxuan Zhang, Gang Ren, … Yue He, Peng Cui et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.17488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17488)
**TL;DR**: A new tabular foundation model trained under previously published scaling laws. Contextual Mechanism Networks shift in-context learning from target-centric \(p(y \mid x, D_{\mathrm{context}})\) to mechanism-oriented joint modeling \(p(x, y \mid D_{\mathrm{context}})\), pretrained with Context-Conditional Masked Modeling on synthetic SCM datasets that span graph structures, functional mechanisms, and observation processes. On TabArena, TALENT, and BCCO it beats current dataset-specific models and tabular FMs. Feature attention encodes direct causal relationships and supports accurate causal-skeleton recovery.
**Why it matters**: The tabular-FM bet that ICL should learn the *mechanism*, not just the conditional — and the attention map is already a causal skeleton.

### 2. ScienceBuddy: Recursive-in-Recursive Self-Improvement for Interactive Scientific Agents
**Authors**: Shuhan Xue, Jianyuan Zhong, … Zhenfei Yin, Yingcheng Wu, Ling Yang et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.17523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17523)
**TL;DR**: An interactive scientific workspace that turns researcher requests, feedback, and execution traces into tasks and rubrics for continual learning. Recursive-in-recursive self-improvement couples two loops: the inner recursion evolves the harness with the model fixed; the outer trains the model under the improved harness. Harness evolution shapes training experience; model learning opens new harness adaptations. Case studies span four scientific task families. Workspace, code, and the RSI recipe are released.
**Why it matters**: RSI as a *product* in the researcher's loop, not another isolated self-play paper — harness and weights improve each other.

### 3. Coding Agents Have Converged: Why the SWE-bench Leaderboard Can No Longer Order Its Top Entries
**Authors**: Fengshuo Liu, Ying Liu, Ruize Sun, Lie Luo, Siyuan Guo
**arXiv**: [2609.17394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394)
**TL;DR**: An audit of 254 SWE-bench submissions across four splits, without running models. On Verified the leading two entries each resolve 396 of 500. The top ten share 285 successes and 51 failures; only 164 instances distinguish them. Frontier solution-set nesting is 0.935 vs a score-implied 0.774. Within-model scaffold ranges hit 29.8 pp against an 8.8-point top-thirty spread. Exact paired McNemar tests separate *none* of 29 adjacent Verified top-thirty pairs at \(\alpha=0.05\) (Test separates 14 of 23). A five-step audit protocol is released.
**Why it matters**: Small SWE-bench gaps are not a ranking. Report comparison-set resolution and model–scaffold provenance, or stop treating the leaderboard as an ordering.

### 4. Large Language Models Develop Belief State Geometry In-Context
**Authors**: Daniel Balcells, Andrew Jun Lee, Chirag Rastogi, Paul M. Riechers, Adam Shai, Xavier Poncini
**arXiv**: [2609.17376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17376)
**TL;DR**: Prompt six open-source LLMs with sequences from 40 HMMs chosen for non-trivial belief structure and probe for the Bayesian posterior over hidden states. Belief states are linearly decodable from the residual stream (peak probe \(R^2\) 0.83–0.99, early to late layers). Patching and steering on the probe subspace leave downstream prediction on the order of the untampered model; controls collapse it. 87-page computational-mechanics paper.
**Why it matters**: Production-scale ICL is not a bag of heuristics — the residual stream is carrying an approximately Bayesian belief state.

### 5. Verifiable Social Reasoning for LLM Assistants
**Authors**: Amir Taubenfeld, Zorik Gekhman, … Marian Croak, Avinatan Hassidim, Yossi Matias, Amir Feder
**arXiv**: [2609.17496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17496)
**TL;DR**: Social advice is hard to grade: the assistant only hears a subjective user narrative, and other people's motives have no ground truth. Fuse is a multi-agent sim: a target with a hidden motive interacts with others (including a user-agent), who then consults the evaluated assistant. Motive is verifiable by construction. Human study: 24k annotations. Across 12 LLMs: user mediation compounds the difficulty; models are systematically sensitive to biased user framing; they can need more details than humans; longer chats do not always help. 21k examples released.
**Why it matters**: The first setup where "what does this person want?" has a certificate — and user mediation is the thing that breaks the model.
