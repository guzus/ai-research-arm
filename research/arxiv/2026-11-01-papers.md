## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm
**Authors**: Yuda Song, Alvin Zhang, Fahim Tajwar, Daman Arora, Jeff Schneider, Daniel Khashabi, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, J. Andrew Bagnell, Aarti Singh, Andrea Zanette
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564
**TL;DR**: Derives RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions. This puts pretraining, midtraining, SFT and RL under one likelihood-based "unified training" algorithm that mixes static data and RL generations in a single trainer. It improves the accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage while preserving broader capabilities.
**Why it matters**: Treating the training lifecycle as a data-mixing and scheduling problem could simplify how frontier models are built.

### 2. How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference
**Authors**: Chanakya Ekbote, Artidoro Pagnoni, Devendra Singh Sachan, Shruti Bhosale, Mike Lewis, Maryam Fazel-Zarandi, Shashank Rajput, Sujan Kumar Gonugondla
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325
**TL;DR**: Large-scale study (up to 1B params, 155B tokens, 32 loops) of looped transformers. Looping the full model is best at a fixed parameter budget, while looping a middle block is better when training FLOPs are constrained. Loop-specific low-rank adapters help, and a fix to Parallel Loop Transformers removes the decoding slowdown.
**Why it matters**: It gives practical, compute-aware guidance on weight-reuse depth scaling.

### 3. Algebraic World Model: From Exploration to Proofs
**Authors**: Guancheng Pan, Qixin Tan, Yang Yuan, Andrew Chi-Chih Yao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40503
**TL;DR**: An agent explores an unknown environment, writes executable Python hypotheses, simplifies them with category-theory-guided transformations, and formalizes them in Lean 4 to prove reachability and impossibility. It produced kernel-checked impossibility proofs on all six runs of two impossible game levels, where tool-equipped frontier-model baselines produced none in twelve runs. It also replays winning trajectories on all 183 levels of the 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games.
**Why it matters**: It links agentic exploration with formal verification, so an agent can prove a goal is unreachable and not only search for a way to reach it.

### 4. Gym-Anything for Robotics: Scaling physical simulations with digital agents
**Authors**: Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40565
**TL;DR**: Coding agents build robotics simulations through an adversarial creation-audit loop, yielding RoboWorld (25 simulations, 25 test and 175 training RL environments). Best frontier models average a reward of only 0.06 on the test tasks. In-context learning, privileged-teacher learning and code-policy demonstrations for VLAs each help substantially.
**Why it matters**: Simulation creation becomes a coding-agent task, so progress in coding agents directly scales embodied-AI training environments.

### 5. Efficient Scalable Oversight via Rewinding
**Authors**: Ziyi Guan, Yael Tauman Kalai, Ron Rothblum, Zoe Xi
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40535
**TL;DR**: Introduces rewindable interactive proofs, where a weak verifier can restore an AI prover to an earlier state. The construction needs no truthful-prover assumption and no cryptography, and the prover runs in time linear in the original computation. A preliminary MNIST experiment is reported.
**Why it matters**: It offers a theoretically grounded, cheaper route to verifying powerful AI systems.
