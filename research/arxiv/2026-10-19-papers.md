## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. At the Limits of Computability: Steps Towards Autonomous AI Discovery on the Busy Beaver Frontier
**Authors**: Marco Dos Santos, Vivek Veeriah, Alex Havrilla, Andrew Bolt, Goran Zuzic, Satinder Singh, Tom Zahavy
**arXiv**: [2610.22603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22603)
**TL;DR**: Friday's leftover RSI papers asked whether an agent can grow skills without a terminating gate. This Gemini pipeline asks a harder, open-ended question: can an agent *discover mathematics* on a frontier no algorithm can finish? It finds compact Collatz-like accelerations of Turing machines — pattern recognition → conjecturing → informal proof → Lean. Eleven BB(6) machines get novel, formally verified accelerations; provisionally Cryptids (exact compact descriptions, halt still open). Olympiad medals and conjecture-solving still start from a human problem list. Busy Beaver does not.
**Why it matters**: Autonomous discovery that formulates its own questions, not just solves ours, and the proofs are Lean, not a judge score.

### 2. WebCoder: Scaling General Frontend Development with Artifact-Grounded Agentic Reinforcement Learning
**Authors**: Zijun Dou, Zhen Yang, Xijun Liu, Dongzhuoran Zhou, Aohan Zeng, Jie Tang
**arXiv**: [2610.23104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23104)
**TL;DR**: Real frontend work is not one SWE ticket. It is reproduce / generate / modify / repair over a living app. WebCoder trains that as one policy: an independent verifier scores the *executable artifact* against requirements; intermediate artifact-progress redistributes the rollout advantage across development segments; context compaction and a message-tree keep trajectories longer than one window. WebCoder-27B lifts Qwen3.8-27B from 64.8→72.8 requirement satisfaction on Webdev-Bench, beating Claude-Opus-4.8 (65.1) and GPT-5.4 (59.6); Vision2Web 41.1→62.4, on par with Opus 4.8.
**Why it matters**: Saturday's OS-Helix and leftover MAI-Gym trained GUI+code routing. This is the missing general frontend policy, and a 27B beats the frontier APIs on the composed-requirement bench.

### 3. Self-Building Labs
**Authors**: Jiaru Bai, Yuchi Zhao, Han Hao, Miroslav Bogdanovic, Yang Cao, Varinia Bernales, Alán Aspuru-Guzik
**arXiv**: [2610.23826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23826)
**TL;DR**: Self-driving labs execute the procedures they were built for. Self-building labs (SBL) take a high-level scientific goal and design the missing setup: layout, digital twin, robot skills, workflows — from an empty room or a working SDL. Demonstrated across robots and from one workstation to multi-robot labs; end-to-end quantum-dot synthesis. Humans specify objectives, not device automation.
**Why it matters**: Lab flexibility, not experiment suggestion, is the bottleneck for scaling discovery. Aspuru-Guzik's group just moved the SDL contract one layer up.

### 4. DiscoveryRSI: Recursively Learning to Discover Valuable Hypotheses
**Authors**: Jianyuan Zhong, Chenwei Wu, James Zou, Zhenfei Yin, Ling Yang et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.23839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23839)
**TL;DR**: A valuable hypothesis is defined operationally: evidence-grounded claim, feasible investigation, decision criteria, alternative explanations. DiscoveryRSI grows hypothesis trees, compiles investigations into Harbor tasks, and learns three ways — credit on documented contributions, harness replay of productive strategies, rediscovery of accepted improvements. DFM-Bench scores hypotheses, revisions, paths, and runs. Two self-improve rounds lift quality 4.86→5.39; DiscoveryRSI-27B is preferred over GPT-6 Luna on importance and evidence-boundedness, and matches post-cutoff published work at 6.3% vs GPT-6 2.5% / Claude Opus 5 2.3%.
**Why it matters**: The Busy Beaver paper discovers on an uncomputable CS frontier. This one learns the *research process* in the life sciences and beats the frontier labs on correspondence with papers that did not exist at cutoff.

### 5. From Approval to Abuse: Privilege Escalation Attacks on Harness-Protected Coding Agents
**Authors**: Xianglin Yang, Yechao Zhang, Jiahao Liu, Yulin Chen, Yuxin Cao, Bryan Hooi, Jiaheng Zhang
**arXiv**: [2610.22987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22987)
**TL;DR**: Coding-agent harnesses intercept out-of-sandbox actions and ask the user. Allow-rules exist so users are not prompted forever. Untrusted repo context can steer a routine task into requesting a plausibly justified, dangerously broad allow-rule. Across Claude Code and Codex CLI, agents elicit the target rules in 58% of instances; approved rules cover 56.9% of category capability tests; conditional on approval, silent goal completion is 81.0% (47/58). The harness is blinded for later tasks, not just the current one.
**Why it matters**: Human-in-the-loop is not a safety property if the approval dialog is attacker-writable. This is the permission-update attack surface, not prompt injection of the next command.
