## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. A team of Gemini 4 Argon agents beats Minecraft
**Authors**: Maxence Faldor, Satinder Singh, Roberta Raileanu
**arXiv**: [2610.22319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319)
**TL;DR**: Four Gemini 4 Argon agents, one sandboxed survival world, one goal: kill the Ender Dragon. No assigned roles, no human-written game logic — they planned, wrote, and debugged every program themselves through Mineflayer plus in-game chat and shared memory. 2,783 programs, 605 deaths, 27 hours 14 minutes. First reported end-to-end autonomous Minecraft completion without pre-scouted seeds or custom tools.
**Why it matters**: Tuesday's Tev and Wednesday's Into the Unknown asked whether agents can hold a plan when there is no reference answer. A day-long, thousands-of-steps coding loop that actually finishes the game is the existence proof those benches were waiting for.

### 2. T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models
**Authors**: Chenyuan Zhou, Yining Hong, Yunfan Jiang, Wenlong Huang, David Held, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu, Leonidas Guibas, Yejin Choi et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.22364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22364)
**TL;DR**: World models miss what is not in the pixels — friction, mass, human-defined rules. T2-z grafts a compact environment latent onto the world model and splits training: freeze the latent, learn dynamics; freeze the dynamics, let the latent emerge from prediction error. At test time the world model stays frozen and a fresh latent is optimized from one or a few interactions. Object-prediction error 15.20→7.00; action success 33.73%→72.96% across 7 sim settings and 4 real tasks. The latents discover structured factors nobody labeled.
**Why it matters**: RSI-Anything said the missing piece was a predictive world model. This is how that model adapts when the environment itself is a latent, not a known spec.

### 3. Librarian Agents: Training LLMs to Curate Long-Context Workspaces that Anticipate Future Queries
**Authors**: Anikait Singh, Teresa Zhang, Yoonho Lee, Aviral Kumar, Chelsea Finn et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.22273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22273)
**TL;DR**: Retrieval is reactive; knowledge graphs are rigid. A builder agent spends RL budget *before* inference turning a raw corpus into a hierarchical filesystem of synthetic files and links, then a downstream answering agent reads that workspace. Workspace construction is an MDP whose reward is answering accuracy. +33 pp over the strongest retrieval/precomputation baselines on BrowseComp-Plus; construction cost amortizes across repeated queries.
**Why it matters**: Claude Code already navigates filesystems. Nobody had trained the agent that *builds* the filesystem the next query will need. Anticipatory curation is the missing half of the harness.

### 4. Interpreting Language Model Parameters
**Authors**: Lucius Bushnaq, Dan Braun, Oliver Clive-Griffin, Bart Bussmann, Nathan Hu, Lee Sharkey et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.20748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748)
**TL;DR**: Activation-level circuits are well studied; *parameter* algorithms are not. Adversarial Parameter Decomposition (VPD) splits weights into simple subcomponents that still reproduce the network under ablations — including ablations chosen to destroy behavior. Only a small fraction of subcomponents is needed for any given input. 124 pages, interactive figures; the first method that asks how parameters and nonlinearities compute on representations, not just which activations light up.
**Why it matters**: If you cannot decompose the weights, you cannot say what the model *implemented*. This is the parameter-side counterpart of sparse autoencoders.

### 5. On-Policy Residual Learning for Diffusion Language Models
**Authors**: Yifan Yu, Yuqing Jian, Xiaoxia Wu, Junxiong Wang, Fan Lai, James Zou, Chenfeng Xu et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.20687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20687)
**TL;DR**: Residual context — carrying predictive distributions across denoising steps — is the dLLM speed/quality dial, and it is inherently on-policy. ORL learns from model-induced residual trajectories with an entropy-contraction objective, plus systems co-design (async residual capture, block-diffusion attention, packed training). On a 4B DLM converted from Qwen3-4B: up to 6.2× tokens/forward and ~6.9× end-to-end throughput at matched or better quality. On one H100 it beats autoregressive + EAGLE3 / DFlash speculative decoding on the speed-quality Pareto, strict and relaxed.
**Why it matters**: Yesterday's One Block Ahead sped up block-dLLMs without training. ORL trains the residual itself. The dLLM tax is no longer a law if the leftover distribution is a first-class learning signal.
