## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. VISTA: A Visual Harness for Reasoning in an Interactive World
**Authors**: Qiushi Han, Keya Hu, Linlu Qiu, Cathy Wu, Kaiming He
**arXiv**: [2610.02200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02200)
**TL;DR**: The missing piece for multimodal agents is not another backbone — it is a harness that keeps a lossless visual memory and lets the model retrieve and reorganize past frames as it reasons. On ARC-AGI-3, VISTA takes Claude Opus 5.0 from 40.68 Relative Human Action Efficiency to a perfect 100.00, finishing all 25 public games with 57.4% fewer actions than first-time humans. The same thin design transfers to three other visual-game benches with almost no adaptation.
**Why it matters**: Kaiming He's first agent paper treats vision as a working memory, not a one-shot caption. The harness, not the model, is what closed ARC-AGI-3.

### 2. Finetuning with Sampling: SFT Learns Better Than You Think
**Authors**: Aayush Karan, Sitan Chen, Yilun Du
**arXiv**: [2610.02140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02140)
**TL;DR**: The SFT-vs-RL split is usually blamed on the objective. This paper blames the *data*: off-policy expert traces are the wrong distribution for a supervised learner. An MCMC sampler walks those traces toward the reference model's on-policy manifold, then ordinary SFT is run on the rewritten set. Across scientific skills, math, and open-ended expertise, resampled SFT matches or beats strong on-policy RL, generalizes better, and forgets less — and the resulting models learn past mere base-model sharpening.
**Why it matters**: If you can make the data on-policy, you may not need RL. That inverts a year of post-training dogma.

### 3. Looping Beyond Twice: A Scalable Recipe for Looped Mixture-of-Experts
**Authors**: Di He, Pengxiang Li, Da Chang, Qingyan Meng, Lu Yin, Shiwei Liu
**arXiv**: [2610.01153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01153)
**TL;DR**: Yesterday's Loop Scaling Laws said recurrence is a third axis. In practice, looped MoEs stall at two loops: residual variance explodes and routers collapse onto the same experts. LOOM bounds variance growth, re-injects the input embedding every loop, and gives each loop its own router plus a Looping Residual that carries earlier expert work forward. Extra loops now add *new* computation instead of repeating the first pass.
**Why it matters**: The law from Thursday now has a recipe. Loop depth is usable past two if the state stays stable and the experts stay diverse.

### 4. Does Scaling Reinforcement Learning Really Require More Training?
**Authors**: Bangji Yang, Jiajun Fan, Hongba Ma, Ruihan Guo, Ge Liu
**arXiv**: [2610.01133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01133)
**TL;DR**: A finished RL run already contains policies stronger than any checkpoint the optimizer visited. SURGE spectrally fuses a high-accuracy "anchor" with a shorter-response "donor" from the same history — keep the anchor's dominant update, graft the donor's complementary component — with no extra training and no extra per-query compute. On 1.5B DeepSeek math it hits 54.17% AIME24 vs a measured native max of 50.83%; on 7B OLMo coding, 83.7% HumanEval+ vs 82.8%. Both sit *above* the observed training curve.
**Why it matters**: Policy-space scaling is a new dial. The history is the product, not just the last checkpoint.

### 5. On Language Drift during RLVR Post-Training
**Authors**: Michael Sullivan, Alexander Koller
**arXiv**: [2610.02015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02015)
**TL;DR**: RLVR CoTs have been sliding into non-standard, hard-to-monitor language. The paper proves the pressure is structural: RLVR's objective permits unbounded language drift; SFT does not. Empirically, drift appears specifically when the target behavior cannot be elicited from the base model. A second theorem is the safety punchline — you cannot bound the drift without also bounding expected reward.
**Why it matters**: CoT monitorability and frontier RLVR performance are now formally in tension. You cannot have both for free.
