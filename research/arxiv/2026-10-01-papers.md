## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts
**Authors**: Yanbei Chen, Anirudh Goyal, Raghuraman Krishnamoorthi
**arXiv**: [2609.40316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40316)
**TL;DR**: Recurrence and MoE sparsity have been scaled separately. Loop Scaling Laws jointly model both, via a bounded, sparsity-conditional map from loop depth to effective parameters. They recover dense and MoE laws as special cases, and at trillion-token scale a law-derived looped MoE matches a ~2× larger non-looped MoE on reasoning at matched training compute — and can keep scaling at test time through extra loops. Sparsity buys ~3× active-parameter efficiency; recurrence buys ~2× total-parameter efficiency on reasoning.
**Why it matters**: Looping is no longer an architecture trick. It is a third axis on the compute–parameter–data chart, with a fitted law you can design to.

### 2. How Much Is an AI Token Worth? Scaling Laws for Wild AI-Generated Web Text
**Authors**: Jenna Russell, Ben Glickenhaus, Katherine Thai, John Wieting, Mohit Iyyer, Max Spero, Bradley Emi
**arXiv**: [2609.40295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40295)
**TL;DR**: After FineWeb filtering, Pangram labels 27.5% of June 2026 web tokens as AI-generated (31.1% by August). This is wild mix, not model-collapse synthetic. Across 800 pretrained models, adding AI tokens first helps data-starved runs, then *hurts*; on high human-token budgets they raise human-text loss immediately. Chinchilla-style laws miss the sign flip. A new law with separate benefit and harm terms predicts 3.6×-larger models at 41% lower error and collapses to Chinchilla when AI text is absent.
**Why it matters**: Web-scale pretraining is no longer a one-token-one-sample problem. Filter AI text when the target is human; report the two losses separately.

### 3. Physis-Lang: Self-Evolving Language as a Physical Representation for Video World Model
**Authors**: Liming Lu, Victor Prisacariu, Ming-Yu Liu, Song Han, Han Cai et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.40358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40358)
**TL;DR**: World models still paint videos that break physics because the field assumed language cannot carry the needed physical state. Physis-Lang treats physical language — entities, causes, interactions, governing principles, temporal evolution, effects — as a shared, optimizable representation across curation, training, and generation. An agentic loop scores atomic assertions on PhysCapBench and rewrites the captioning instruction; missing processes become retrieval queries. On Wan and Cosmos backbones it lifts physical plausibility; Cosmos3-Nano + Physis-Lang beats proprietary Veo 3.1.
**Why it matters**: The missing physics channel may already be language, if you evolve the language instead of adding another latent.

### 4. How Much of a Harness Does a Strong Agent Need for Autonomous ML Engineering?
**Authors**: Kirill Brilliantov, Alejandro Hernández-Cano, Emmanuel Abbé
**arXiv**: [2609.40303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40303)
**TL;DR**: Public MLE agents keep growing orchestrators, retrieval subagents, and other machinery around a frontier backbone. Under an equal time budget and the same LLM, open-source SOTA harnesses do *not* beat a single session of a minimal coding agent with read / write / bash. Large ablations say the extra layers are redundant once the model can drive the execution environment directly. Effort spent hand-crafting harnesses around strong models has poor returns on current MLE benches.
**Why it matters**: For autonomous ML engineering, the backbone is the product. Harness complexity is no longer a free performance dial.

### 5. Cogentic: Multi-Agent Orchestration for Automated Proof Discovery
**Authors**: Yang Cai, Vineet Gupta, Yanchen Jiang, Christopher Liaw, Aranyak Mehta, Grigoris Velegkas, Di Wang
**arXiv**: [2609.40324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40324)
**TL;DR**: Single-shot frontier math is not enough for open problems that need competing conjectures, technical repairs, and a ledger of intermediate lemmas. Cogentic runs an iterative prove–verify loop: an orchestrator farms independent provers across directions, adversarial verifiers stress the drafts, and confirmed fragments land in a persistent verified ledger. Using Gemini, it produced novel results on five open problems in online learning, auction theory, and mechanism design — each independently checked by domain experts and written up as companion papers.
**Why it matters**: The harness is no longer just scoring answers. It is keeping a proof state that later rounds can actually build on.
