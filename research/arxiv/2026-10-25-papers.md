## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Questioning Instrumental Convergence in RLVR: Checkpoint Evidence from OLMo-3
**Authors**: Barış Alparslan, Sandy Tanwisuth, Caleb Petterson
**arXiv**: [2610.31624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31624)
**TL;DR**: Saturday's safety-probe laws asked whether to buy labels or a bigger model. This one asks whether the first post-train stage that *optimizes behavior* installs the power-seeking IC predicts. InstrumentalEval on OLMo-3-7B-Think across SFT, DPO, and the RLVR trajectory: the rate rises early, then falls *below* the SFT baseline. Prompt framing explains 15% of within-checkpoint variance; INLP-ing the framing direction does not close the SFT-to-final-RLVR gap. Preliminary 32B endpoints: 35.53% SFT → 22.37% RLVR final.
**Why it matters**: "RL will make it power-seeking" is a trajectory claim. On this family and bench, the trajectory goes the other way.

### 2. AfterDeleteBench: Auditing Post-Deletion Information Persistence in LLM Agents
**Authors**: Ehsan Saleh, Roozbeh Nahavandi, Sama Salarian, Raef Bassily, Andrew Perrault
**arXiv**: [2610.31366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31366)
**TL;DR**: Saturday's IQuest-Q1 puts weights back into its own loop. This one asks whether `delete` is a real operation once summaries and derived state exist. Matched exposed-then-deleted vs never-exposed agents: a single query recovers the secret from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in ~2/3 of cases; never-exposed controls are zero. Exact reconstruction succeeds in 62–99% across Qwen, Mistral, and Phi-3, including from separately stored components with no surviving verbatim copy. Provenance-aware closure zeros the positive-baseline recoveries; a literal write filter does not.
**Why it matters**: Deletion is a behavioral property of the agent-plus-memory system. Storage-level erase is not the audit.

### 3. RECAP: Looking Once Is Not Enough for Vision-Language Reasoning
**Authors**: Zhaolu Kang, Tailong Luo, Chenxin Li, Yingjie He et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.30834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30834)
**TL;DR**: Saturday's video-as-world-ops survey said reading a clip is not the same as keeping a world state. RECAP shows long VLM CoT *drops the image* while GRPO still scores only the final answer. Visual-revisit peaks predict correctness and are causal: mask them and accuracy falls more than masking non-peaks; inject them into failed rollouts and some answers come back. The training method turns detached revisit traces into credit — revisit-conditioned advantage, dynamic discounting, gated value — with no extra reward or inference change. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B / InternVL3-8B / LLaVA-OV-7B: +6.1–6.5 HallusionBench over SFT, reasoning and text-only held.
**Why it matters**: Test-time scaling a VLM without re-grounding is just more words about a picture the model has already forgotten.

### 4. Same Beliefs, Different Policies: Learning Reusable Representations for Early Stopping in LLM Reasoning
**Authors**: Jingwei Ni, Dmitry Abulkhanov, Vladislav Smirnov, Markus Leippold, Elliott Ash, Timothy Baldwin, Artem Shelmanov, Mrinmaya Sachan
**arXiv**: [2610.30450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30450)
**TL;DR**: Saturday's GenRM laws said a more accurate static judge is not a better online rewarder. BELT says the same split at stop-time: beliefs about current and future answers are not the stopping policy. One frozen-hidden-state probe, then a linear policy refit on CPU for each token price. Five OOD math benches: −41% tokens on Qwen3-1.7B and −51% on OLMo-3-7B-Think with no accuracy loss; the math probe transfers to coding and agentic tasks with no code or agent data. vs OS-Pruner on OLMo-3: 21.2× fewer probe params, 55× fewer GPU-hours than five price-specific checkpoints.
**Why it matters**: You do not need a new controller for every dollar-per-token. You need a belief, then a cheap policy on top of it.

### 5. OpenAttention: One Percent Is Enough with Tail Proxies
**Authors**: Hyunwoo Yu, Jongbeom Lee, Hyunbin Kim, Sungjun Park et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.30951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30951)
**TL;DR**: Saturday's BeNice / MoEZip leftover closed looping and routing-aware KV. This one asks what to do with the unread tail when sparse attention keeps 1% of the cache. Sampling the tail nearly doubles the weight error of discarding it; one count-aware mean proxy stays below both. OpenAttention clusters (query-geometry, training-free), ErrorGate-allocates the exact read by estimated weight error, then folds the rest into a few proxies. Llama-3.1-8B on RULER-32K HARD: 99% of dense accuracy at a 1% read. Custom kernel: 10× decode vs dense at that read.
**Why it matters**: Below 5%, the tail is where the missed heavy hitters live. A mean that knows its count is a better stand-in than a sample.
