## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. IQuest-Q1: Advancing Foundation Capabilities for Agentic CLI Systems
**Authors**: IQuest Research (Jian Yang, An Huang, Bo Dan, Chen Mu et al.)
**arXiv**: [2610.30949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30949)
**TL;DR**: Wednesday's Sage-A1.5 was a 35B MoE that *composed* long-horizon work. IQuest-Q1 is a 320B-A15B open MoE built for the CLI itself: SplitNorm + Muon + μP, hybrid GQA/SWA, recursive MTP drafter, then SFT / multi-scaffold RL / multi-teacher on-policy distillation and a merge across harnesses. Same-data vs a Qwen3-MoE-style recipe: 1.93× scaling efficiency. Scores: 64.6 DeepSWE v1.1, 57.5 SWE-Bench Pro, 84.5 CyberGym, 55.7 JobBench, 39.2 / 55.7 HLE without / with tools. The lab then puts the weights back into its own training loop under a human-on-the-loop RSI setup.
**Why it matters**: The week's agent drops were 30–35B. This is the open CLI foundation those agents have been waiting to sit on, and the training recipe is the paper.

### 2. PLE: Physical-Engineering's Last Exam
**Authors**: Rujun Han, Yufan Zhuang, Arijit Ray, Tomas Pfister, Chen-Yu Lee et al. (Google Cloud AI Research)
**arXiv**: [2610.30537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30537)
**TL;DR**: HLE / GDPval / APEX grade knowledge work. None of them asks an agent to size a feeder, keep a wetland buffer, and issue a drawing a licensed reviewer would stamp. PLE is 85 long-horizon electrical / civil / structural / mechanical jobs — mean 61-file project corpus, production memos / workbooks / DXF-IFC — half expert-authored, half Creator–Solver co-authored until a frontier agent stops saturating them. 14 models × 7 public harnesses: Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code is the best cell at 50.6%, $17.18 / 27M tokens / 63 minutes per task. 29 tasks public; the rest held for a leaderboard.
**Why it matters**: Software agents have SWE-bench. Physical engineering now has a bench that is not a textbook problem and is not already solved.

### 3. Scaling Laws for Safety Probes
**Authors**: Yuchen Wu (University of Washington), Jerry Wei (Anthropic)
**arXiv**: [2610.30678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30678)
**TL;DR**: Public safety sets saturate at 32 labels or run out of data. Six new minimal-pair sets (truth, sycophancy, policy, harm, jailbreak, prompt injection) × 20 base models × two probe architectures × 32–10^6 labels. Probe error \(E = 1-\mathrm{AUROC}\) has a floor that is a power law in model size (Qwen2.5 truth / CC++: ×0.86 per doubling, 0.18 at 0.5B → 0.056 at 72B) and a label curve whose slope steepens with size. Fit on a family's small models: next-size error within 0.02 in 72% of cells; cheaper of "more labels vs larger model" in 73%; architecture pick costs 0.006 extra error when wrong.
**Why it matters**: Safety teams have been retraining probes by grid search. This is the Chinchilla sheet for whether to buy labels or a bigger model.

### 4. A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy
**Authors**: Jonathan T. Barron, Dan Gnanapragasam, Michael Tschannen
**arXiv**: [2610.31642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642)
**TL;DR**: Two bugs in the kernels everyone already ships. Softmax Collapse zeros the target-logit gradient when one logit dominates, so the remaining gradients no longer sum to zero. Softmax Cancellation leaves a nonzero gradient when student logits equal the teacher. The patched log-softmax / softmax / cross-entropy kill the Slingshot, speed grokking under weight decay, stop accuracy collapse without it, and keep a distillation student from drifting off the teacher. After memorization, logit growth is an equilibrium between weight decay and Adam's ε, not an unbounded race.
**Why it matters**: If your loss is the stock kernel, some of what you have been calling optimization dynamics is a rounding error.

### 5. Scaling Laws for Generative Reward Models
**Authors**: Angel Raychev, Nathan Lile, Rafael Rafailov
**arXiv**: [2610.30765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30765)
**TL;DR**: Wednesday's misspecification paper asked what a bad grader does to CoT. This one asks whether a *better* static judge is a better online rewarder. On LitBench creative writing, Thinking GenRMs (GRPO) beat Answer-Only (SFT) on Gold-label accuracy across Qwen3 0.6B–14B — and then lose as rewarders: Answer-Only wins peak and top-five policy Elo in 7/8 matched online-DPO cells under a frozen 32B Gold. Intermediate Thinking checkpoints beat later, more accurate ones. Extra judge capacity still helps above policy size.
**Why it matters**: "Our RM is more accurate" is not a claim about the policy it will train. The evaluator–rewarder gap is now a measured object.
