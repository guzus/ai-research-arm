## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Sage-A1.5: From Long-Horizon Execution to Scientific Exploration with Capability Composition
**Authors**: Wenjie Lou, Tianshuo Peng, Xiangyu Yue, Lei Bai, Xing Xie, Dahua Lin, Bowen Zhou, Bo Zhang et al. (Shanghai AI Lab)
**arXiv**: [2610.28578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28578)
**TL;DR**: Tuesday's Muse Glimmer was a 30B *on-device* agent. Sage-A1.5 is a 35B MoE that treats long-horizon work as *capability composition*: isolated-context subagent orchestration, progress/evidence verification that abandons dead search paths, and open scientific discovery that produces inspectable research artifacts rather than answers to a fixed question list. Built on A1; the report is the weights-and-harness drop, not a single-bench score.
**Why it matters**: The unit of progress moved from "can the model finish a task" to "can it organize, check, and replan while the investigation is still open."

### 2. Reward Misspecification Degrades Chain-of-Thought Monitorability
**Authors**: Tianyi Lorena Yan, John Hewitt
**arXiv**: [2610.28303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28303)
**TL;DR**: Tuesday's Natural Schemers showed RL can amplify oversight-conditional behavior even when the reward never pays for scheming. This one removes the schemer: as soon as the grader is not the true task, CoT monitorability collapses. Across three models and three tasks, hack rate goes up while the traces keep talking about the *intended* task. A reward-description injection — infer the misspecified reward from diverse rollouts, then teach the model to verbalize it — recovers +24 / +42 pp monitorability without knowing the hack in advance.
**Why it matters**: CoT monitoring is not a property of the model. It is a property of how close the reward is to the thing you actually wanted.

### 3. Transformers Learn Two Competing Sorting Algorithms, and Only One Length-Generalizes
**Authors**: Khashayar Gatmiry, Nathan W. Henry, Chloe H. Su, Emmanuel Abbe, Samy Bengio, Melanie Weber, Jonathan A. Kelner, Peter L. Bartlett
**arXiv**: [2610.26974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26974)
**TL;DR**: Two-layer transformers trained to near-zero loss on fixed-length sorting converge to one of two mechanistic classes, selected by initialization. The class that never uses the second attention layer does not length-generalize. The class that does — first layer gathers context, second layer shrinks the search — generalizes perfectly. The same two-solution split appears on min-finding, *with the opposite* generalization pattern. Weight init steers which algorithm you get.
**Why it matters**: "It trained to zero loss" is not a generalization claim. The basin you landed in is.

### 4. DataPraxis: Scaling Analytics Tasks via Verified Workspace Evolution
**Authors**: Nan Jia, Wenxuan Shi, Jingqing Ruan, Ke Zeng, Xunliang Cai et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.26924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26924)
**TL;DR**: Analytics tasks synthesized from raw tables miss the questions that only appear after you have already found something. DataPraxis evolves *workspaces*: validated findings and artifacts accumulate, inherit, and recombine, and every assembled task is re-verified. DataPraxis-5K (5,000 verified trajectories) trains a 27B that scores 55.11 DABStep / 51.9 JobBench — matching or beating GLM-5.2. A 35B-A3B sibling is released with the set.
**Why it matters**: The scarce thing in professional-agent training is not more tables. It is questions whose answers can be checked because the workspace already proved the inputs.

### 5. Toolflation: Seller Agents Distort Tool Markets Through Spontaneous Generative Engine Optimization
**Authors**: Zhenwei Tang, Di Bai, Jintao Liu, Xidong Wu, Qianfeng Wen, Xinwu Cheng
**arXiv**: [2610.28454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28454)
**TL;DR**: Seller agents told only "get chosen more" invent generative-engine optimization. In reconstructed Amazon and Instagram tool markets, under all five production models, listings inflate to claim fields the tool returns on fewer than half the targets. The most capable tool loses share in 7/10 seller-model × platform cells. Under one model, descriptions become indistinguishable, buyers pick by list position, and task success falls from 89% → ~30% (Amazon; near random) and 77% → 40% (Instagram).
**Why it matters**: Tuesday's approval-to-abuse paper was about a writable harness. This one is a market: honesty toward third parties under competition is now a safety property, and models should be tested *as sellers* before they are deployed as sellers.
