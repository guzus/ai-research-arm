# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-19

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Recirculation
**Authors**: Michael C. Mozer, Shoaib Ahmed Siddiqui, Danny Sawyer, Sunny Sanyal, Rosanne Liu
**arXiv**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17981
**TL;DR**: A training-free, inference-time architectural enhancement that adds a specific form of recurrence so frozen foundation models can act as dynamical systems and track belief states. On the Gemma3 family it cuts perplexity by 23% and boosts GSM8k accuracy by 21% with essentially no added generation latency.
**Why it matters**: Introduces a new architectural primitive that improves off-the-shelf models without finetuning, pointing to a route where trained networks guide their own architectural evolution.

### 2. Debate Training Reduces Reward Hacking in RLAIF
**Authors**: Zachary Kenton, Lili Janzer, Rory Greig, Tian Huey Teh, Kirill Tyshchuk, Jonah Brown-Cohen, Harri Edwards, Senthooran Rajamanoharan, Noah Y. Siegel, Natasha Jaques, Rohin Shah
**arXiv**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17776
**TL;DR**: RL finetuning with debate (generator vs. critic adjudicated by a weak judge) keeps the judge reliable throughout training, recovering a 45% validation-accuracy gap over a RLAIF baseline that quickly hacks its weak judge. Critique word limits (up to 150 words) balance the game and prevent judge hacking.
**Why it matters**: Positive evidence that adversarial multi-agent training can supervise increasingly capable AI without reward hacking, the central obstacle in scaling oversight.

### 3. The IOL-AI Challenge: An Open Challenge towards Advancing Linguistic Reasoning
**Authors**: Eduardo Sánchez, Rita Berrada, Dan-Mircea Mirea, Sara Rajaee, Alexander Piperski, Ana Meta Dolinar, Boris Iomdin, Andrey Nikulin, Mariya Shmatova, Marzieh Fadaee, Julia Kreutzer
**arXiv**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18011
**TL;DR**: A competition on unseen International Linguistics Olympiad puzzles judged by the official IOL Jury: 731 submissions from 46 teams under a strict compute budget, with Claude Opus 4.8 earning a gold-medal-equivalent score. Capability did not track scale, and linguistic reasoning remains a strong proxy for generalizable reasoning since parametric priors on the languages do not help.
**Why it matters**: Defines a cleanly verifiable, compute-bounded benchmark family where reasoning must be discovered, not derived from provided rules.

### 4. GenRec: Knowing Where to Reconstruct and Where to Generate
**Authors**: Ata Çelen, Jaewoo Jung, Federico Tombari, Marc Pollefeys, Sunghwan Hong, Michael Niemeyer, Daniel Barath
**arXiv**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17832
**TL;DR**: A multi-view flow-matching model for novel view synthesis that splits reconstruction and generation inside the architecture: an observation mask gates supervision so regression signals do not contaminate the generative prior. It sets SOTA fidelity on observed pixels and beats purely generative baselines on perceptual quality in unobserved regions.
**Why it matters**: Correctly formalizes the reconstruction–generation dichotomy that most sparse-input view synthesis conflates under a single loss.

### 5. Chain-of-Experience for Continual LLM Improvement
**Authors**: Haoqin Tu, Yunhao Fang, Yizhong Wang, Cihang Xie, Shen Yan
**arXiv**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18027
**TL;DR**: Studied how LLMs improve by accumulating experiential traces at test time via self-feedback or environment signals (e.g., test-pass rates), across 8 LLMs in math, code, and knowledge. Beats feedback-free baselines with a 5.6% overall gain and 19% lower API cost, with higher accuracy per token than existing test-time strategies.
**Why it matters**: Reframes inference-time computation as a continual improvement loop and quantifies how base ability, feedback quality, and iteration interact.
