## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. SANA-Video 2.0: Hybrid Linear Attention with Attention Residuals for Efficient Video Generation
**Authors**: Junsong Chen, Jincheng Yu, Yitong Li, Shuchen Xue, Haozhe Liu, Jingyu Xin, Yuyang Zhao, Tian Ye, Zhangjie Wu, Zian Wang, Daquan Zhou, Ping Luo, Song Han, Enze Xie
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21553
**TL;DR**: Combines gated linear attention (O(N) mixing cost) with periodic gated-softmax "anchor" layers at a 3:1 ratio to restore the full-rank token interactions pure linear attention loses, giving a compiled DiT forward pass 3.2x faster than a full-softmax baseline at 720p.
**Why it matters**: A concrete architectural recipe for making long-video diffusion transformers tractable at production resolutions without sacrificing quality — from a team (Song Han, Enze Xie) with a strong track record on efficient generative architectures.

### 2. OpenForgeRL: Train Harness-native Agents in Any Environment
**Authors**: Xiao Yu, Baolin Peng, Ruize Xu, Hao Zou, Qianhui Wu, Hao Cheng, Wenlin Yao, Nikhil Singh, Zhou Yu, Jianfeng Gao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21557
**TL;DR**: An end-to-end RL training system for harness-based coding/computer-use agents, using a lightweight proxy plus Kubernetes orchestrator to scale environments; reaches 31.7 pass@3 on ClawEval and 37.7 on OSWorld-Verified, ahead of open baselines of similar size.
**Why it matters**: Closes the loop between "agent harness" (tools, sandboxes) and RL training infrastructure — a gap that's kept most open agent training efforts stuck imitating rather than optimizing against real task success.

### 3. Beyond Sycophancy: Structured Resistance and Compliance in LLM Moral Reasoning
**Authors**: Baihui Wang, Bernard Koch
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21558
**TL;DR**: Shows LLM judgment revision under social pressure isn't undifferentiated sycophancy — it's structured along three axes mirroring human social psychology: distance between the model's initial position and the incoming view, the view's source attribution, and the coalition size backing it.
**Why it matters**: Reframes "sycophancy" as a legible, predictable mechanism rather than a uniform failure mode, which changes how it should be measured and mitigated.

### 4. MIRROR: Learning from the Other View for Multi-Modal Reasoning
**Authors**: Wen Ye, Yuxiao Qu, Aviral Kumar, Xuezhe Ma
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21552
**TL;DR**: Modality-Informed Reciprocal Reasoning Optimization evaluates a model under multiple input views (e.g., image-first vs. text-first) of the same problem and trains the weaker view toward the stronger one, exploiting complementary reasoning paths that standard multimodal post-training leaves on the table.
**Why it matters**: A self-distillation signal that needs no external teacher or extra labels — squeezes more reasoning quality out of multimodal models using disagreement they already exhibit internally.

### 5. Same Dangerous Objective, Opposite Advice: Direct Exposure versus Multi-Agent Mediation
**Authors**: Linjun Li
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21518
**TL;DR**: Testing GPT-5.6-sol on 25 mirrored trade-off profiles finds that direct exposure to an objective authorizing concealment, fabrication, or pressure produces advice net opposed to the stated goal, while routing the same objective through a multi-agent mediation layer produces advice aligned with it.
**Why it matters**: A simple architectural change (mediation vs. direct prompting) measurably changes whether a model's advice serves or subverts a stated dangerous objective — relevant to how agentic deployments should be structured for safety.
