# arXiv AI Research - 2026-07-28

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence
**Authors**: Kimi Team (Tongtong Bai, Yifan Bai, et al. — 250+ authors)
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653
**TL;DR**: Kimi K3 is a 2.8T-parameter mixture-of-experts model (104B activated) with native vision support and a 1M-token context window, built on a new "Delta Attention" mechanism and "Stable LatentMoE" architecture. It reports frontier-level results on coding, agentic, and reasoning benchmarks.
**Why it matters**: This is the model card for Kimi K3 — the same backend this pipeline routes generative-research and Twitter-summary lanes to via opencode; the architecture/training details here explain the capabilities the routing docs already depend on.

### 2. The Physics of Multi-Turn Long-Horizon Planning: From Pre-training to Post-training via Single- and Multi-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation
**Authors**: Tianyi Men, Zhuoran Jin, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24720
**TL;DR**: A systematic study of how long-horizon agentic planning ability is shaped across pre-training, GRPO/on-policy-distillation post-training, and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD), tracing how data quality and planning patterns affect compositional generalization in foundation-model agents.
**Why it matters**: Gives an empirical, stage-by-stage account of where long-horizon agent reliability actually comes from — relevant to anyone building or evaluating agentic pipelines rather than just single-turn chat models.

### 3. DataOrchestra: Learning to Orchestrate Per-Example Curation of Pretraining Data
**Authors**: Zhen Huang, Yikun Wang, Shijie Xia, Pengfei Liu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24717
**TL;DR**: DataOrchestra learns example-specific preprocessing decisions (drop, preserve, or clean) for pretraining data rather than applying a single fixed filter, showing stable gains across 11 benchmarks for models from 0.5B to 7B parameters.
**Why it matters**: Per-example data orchestration is a cheap, model-size-agnostic lever on pretraining quality — the kind of efficiency win that compounds across every downstream model trained on the curated corpus.

### 4. Eviction as Estimation: A Fixed-Lag Smoothing View of Test-Time Memory, and When Measuring Beats Accumulating
**Authors**: Maruthi Vemula, Neeraj Praneeth Gajula
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24667
**TL;DR**: Reframes bounded working-memory/context-eviction decisions in LLM agents as an estimation problem over hidden reuse signals, placing existing memory-management heuristics on a single "commit-lag" axis, and proposes RMM, which uses fixed-lag smoothing on demonstrated behavioral utility instead of naive accumulation.
**Why it matters**: A principled framing for test-time memory/context management — a problem every long-running agent (including multi-hour research and synthesis pipelines) has to solve ad hoc today.

### 5. D-Score: A Spectral Hidden-State Signal for Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models
**Authors**: Bianca Raimondi, Davide Evangelista, Maurizio Gabbrielli, Elena Loli Piccolomini
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24586
**TL;DR**: Introduces D-Score, a spectral statistic computed directly from hidden-state activations that flags likely hallucinations without needing an external verifier, retrieval, or multiple sampled generations.
**Why it matters**: A lightweight, inference-time hallucination signal is exactly the kind of cheap safety check that's practical to bolt onto production generation pipelines without added latency or cost.
