# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-28

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. LeVJEPA: Efficient & Scalable Video Pretraining without the Heuristics
**Authors**: Lukas Kuhn, Lucas Maes, Giuseppe Serra, Quentin Le Lidec, Yann LeCun, Randall Balestriero, Florian Buettner
**arXiv**: [2608.27395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27395)
**TL;DR**: Introduces the first video encoder trained under LeJEPA's collapse-free objective, dropping the EMA target encoder, stop-gradient, and capacity-limited predictor entirely. Pretraining reduces to a single encoder plus projector with one hyperparameter, using SIGReg-without-collapse regularization with a provable guarantee.
**Why it matters**: Could make video representation learning dramatically cheaper and simpler, removing the fragile heuristic machinery that dominates current self-supervised video pretraining.

### 2. CLAP: Cross-Embodiment Video World Models are Zero-Shot Physical Simulators
**Authors**: Kechen Liu, Ola Shorinwa
**arXiv**: [2608.27406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27406)
**TL;DR**: A cross-embodiment action-conditioned video generation framework that reconciles disparate robot action spaces (end-effector poses, language, etc.) so models can be trained on internet-scale heterogeneous human+robot video. The resulting world models act as zero-shot physical simulators across embodiments.
**Why it matters**: Shows universal physics can be learned from heterogeneous video data, pointing toward general-purpose world models that generalize across robots without per-embodiment retraining.

### 3. Puro-2B: Poor Lab's Qwen2-1.5B Trained on RTX 5090 within $5090
**Authors**: Kairong Luo, Jiarui Cui, Yaorui Yin, Shengqi Chen, Yiming Yang, Linxiang Gao, Yanmohan Wang, Mingzhe Zhang, Kaiyue Wen, Kaifeng Lyu, Wenguang Chen
**arXiv**: [2608.27370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27370)
**TL;DR**: An open, hardware-accessible pretraining recipe that trains 2B-class models from scratch on up to 1.4T tokens with FP8 precision on consumer RTX 5090 GPUs for under $6.9K, approaching Qwen2.5-1.5B performance. Contrasts with $1.5M+ costs for Llama-3.2-3B-scale training.
**Why it matters**: Attacks the cost barrier of LLM pretraining, making open pretraining viable for academic and small-budget labs.

### 4. SWE-Prime: Fewer Trajectories, Better Performance
**Authors**: Dewu Zheng, Ruizhe Ye, Yanlin Wang, Yang Ye, Hongyu Zhang, Ensheng Shi, Xilin Liu, Yuchi Ma, Jianxing Yu, Zibin Zheng
**arXiv**: [2608.27449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27449)
**TL;DR**: Multi-granularity two-stage SFT data selection that filters agent trajectories at trajectory and segment levels by process quality, result quality, and representativeness. Shows that "successful" trajectories contain noisy/risky steps and that filtering them out yields better SWE resolution from far fewer samples.
**Why it matters**: Challenges the "more agent trajectories is better" assumption and provides a principled recipe for high-quality agentic supervision.

### 5. CritICL: Inference-Time Weak-to-Strong Generalization from Small Language Model Failure Modes
**Authors**: Yufan Wu, Yinghui He, Zhengyi Hu, Lang Wei, Ruichen Li, Qifan Yang, Ting Zhu
**arXiv**: [2608.27455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27455)
**TL;DR**: Exploits the observation that LLM failure modes are structured across scales within a model family, using critiques derived from weak-model failures as in-context examples (static and dynamic variants) to guide stronger models at inference time without repeated generation or external verification.
**Why it matters**: Turns small-model errors into a free, efficient signal for inference-time reasoning improvement — a new weak-to-strong mechanism that avoids expensive verifier or sampling budgets.
