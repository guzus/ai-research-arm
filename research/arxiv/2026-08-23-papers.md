## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. HealMed: Multilingual Evaluation of Large Language Models in Medicine
**Authors**: Yingjian Chen, Fan Gao, Sherry T. Tong, Haoyu Zhang, Aosong Feng, et al.
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19981](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19981)
**TL;DR**: An expert-reviewed, 9-language (1,000 examples per language) medical benchmark spanning MCQA, NLI and open-ended QA, built over two years by 23 physicians across nine countries with two-expert revision per translation. Performance degrades most in low-resource languages, medical specialization alone does not ensure multilingual robustness, and expert revision can raise or lower scores — proving translation quality materially affects cross-lingual results.
**Why it matters**: A rigorous, expert-audited multilingual medical evaluation that exposes how much of model ranking is really an artifact of translation quality rather than capability.

### 2. V-REX: Efficient Specialist VLM Training for Veterinary X-Rays
**Authors**: Tim Elsner, Nicole McNally, Andre Dourson, Michael Fitzke
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20069](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20069)
**TL;DR**: Rethinks the whole VLM pipeline for a data-scarce domain — text tokenization, generative pre-training, grounding, and inference — to train a diagnostic-report-generating VLM for veterinary radiographs from scratch. With a fraction of the parameters, data, and compute of generalist foundation models, it beats open foundation models on this task by a significant margin.
**Why it matters**: Direct counter-evidence to the assumption that specialists must fine-tune ever-larger foundation models, with efficiency lessons for other narrow domains.

### 3. RuleMaze: Rule-Compliant Visual Spatial Planning for Multimodal Large Language Models
**Authors**: Yu Chen, Ting Lei, Yaoyi Li, Jia Cai, Zhecen Wu, Yang Liu
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20237](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20237)
**TL;DR**: Introduces RuleMaze, a controllable benchmark where MLLMs must navigate mazes under natural-language rules of varying complexity, built with a Language-Logic-Function hybridization that auto-generates rules and validators. A Disentangled Multimodal Planning (DMP) method — separating perception, execution and rule verification into interpretable primitives — substantially improves rule compliance and generalizes to unseen rules.
**Why it matters**: Targets the under-explored capability of rule-constrained reasoning in multimodal models, with a scalable, automatically-generated benchmark and a transparency-friendly planner.

### 4. DECOWAM: Decoupled Whole-Body World-Action Model for Legged Mobile Manipulation
**Authors**: Siyuan Ma, Boshi Zhang, Yutian Zhang, Qinglian Wu, Jiaqi Zhai, Dong Wei, Qiaojun Yu
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20114](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20114)
**TL;DR**: A whole-body world-action model that explicitly separates camera ego-motion from base and arm actions via dedicated conditional interfaces — residual adapters, an action-equivalent future bottleneck distilled from privileged observations, and adversarially separated base/arm latents. Trained on the new ARMDOG real-robot dataset, it cuts action MSE 21.7% vs a frozen FastWAM backbone with only 25.95M trainable parameters and is most robust in closed-loop trials.
**Why it matters**: A parameter-efficient factorization for predicting how locomotion and manipulation jointly change future observation and control under moving viewpoints.

### 5. Evidence-Gated Task and Motion Planning with Vision-Language Models
**Authors**: Tsunehiko Tanaka, Matthew Stephenson, Alistair Macvicar, Edgar Simo-Serra
**arXiv**: [http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20084](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20084)
**TL;DR**: EAFG adds an Evidence Acquisition and Feasibility Gating stage to VLM+TAMP pipelines: the robot generates exploratory subgoals that gather visual evidence before committing, then a feasibility gate decides whether to proceed, gather more evidence, or halt. In ambiguous cooking tasks it improves recipe completion by discovering task-relevant objects before planning, and for absent-object instructions it promotes correct halt decisions and reduces repeated futile manipulation attempts.
**Why it matters**: Directly attacks unsafe/wasteful planner behavior under partial observability — where VLMs hallucinate goals unsupported by the scene — by making evidence acquisition and halting first-class planning operations.
