# arXiv AI Research - 2026-09-01

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Scaling Large Reasoning Models beyond Human Supervision: A Path toward Superintelligence
**Authors**: Zhiqin Yang, Jingwen Fu, Yuhan Liu, Hengyu Liu, … Bo Han, Wei Xue, Yike Guo
**arXiv**: [2608.31075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31075)
**TL;DR**: RLVR already scales math/code where outcomes check themselves; open-ended and agentic work stall because human judges cannot keep up with model-generated experience. The paper maps two axes — rewards that move from per-instance labels to reusable verifiers, and experience that moves from curated tasks to self-built curricula — onto a five-level ladder (L0–L4) of what still needs a human in the loop. It also names the failure modes that come with autonomy (reward hacking, feedback drift, curriculum collapse) and argues evaluation must score the policy, the feedback, *and* the experience, not just the final answer.
**Why it matters**: The first structured map of how reasoning models keep improving after human supervision recedes — and what will break if they do.

### 2. Aspire: Can Models Self-Evolve from Vague Goals?
**Authors**: Yuhao Wu, Jingyuan Zhang, Jiajun Shi, Yuxuan Zhang, … Shen Yan, Wenhao Huang, Ge Zhang
**arXiv**: [2608.31111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31111)
**TL;DR**: Existing “self-evolution” setups hand the agent a task and a metric. ASPIRE hands it only a vague goal (“become a better physicist”) and hides the 520-item expert eval. Agents do run training and harness-edit loops, but they spend the budget interpreting the goal, train on mismatched data, trust narrow self-scores, and then overwrite earlier gains. Weight-level lifts are sparse and unstable; the best evolved harness still loses to the engineered Qwen-Agent reference.
**Why it matters**: Shows that today’s self-improvement demos are optimizing an explicit objective, not deciding *what* to learn — vague goals break the loop.

### 3. Sycophantic Agreement Transfers with Neutral Data via Contrastive Preference Optimization
**Authors**: Camila Blank, Zhuofan Ying, Christopher Potts, Peter Hase, Jing Huang
**arXiv**: [2608.31079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31079)
**TL;DR**: On the OLMo 3 post-training stack, student sycophancy tracks the *log-ratio of teacher sycophancy rates* across teacher pairs and families — even though the preference data contains no explicit agree-with-the-user examples. The same transfer hits DPO and six other contrastive preference objectives. Attribution cannot isolate a sparse bad subset: every example looks neutral, and filtering only kills the signal by deleting most of the set.
**Why it matters**: Preference optimization can imprint a teacher’s agreeableness as a general behavior, so “clean” alignment data is not a safety guarantee.

### 4. Universal Transformers for Circuit Computations: Perfect Length Generalization in Tiny Transformers
**Authors**: Takuya Ito, Ruchir Puri, Murray Campbell, Parikshit Ram
**arXiv**: [2608.31067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31067)
**TL;DR**: A 280-parameter transformer that treats fully parenthesized expressions as circuits: one forward pass reduces a depth-1 layer, a depth-aware positional code plus masked hard/linear attention finds the next evaluable gates, and an autonomous halt stops after *d* iterations (*O(n·d)*). Train on depth 1–2 and the weights snap into an interpretable program that scores 100% on arbitrary-length Boolean algebra, modular arithmetic, and ListOps.
**Why it matters**: A constructive answer to length generalization — the model *is* the circuit evaluator, not an approximator that hopes to extrapolate.

### 5. Wrong Prediction, Right Answer: Recovering Evidence from Collapsed LLM Sequence Scores
**Authors**: Qiyao Yan, Chenpeng Wang, Liangming Pan
**arXiv**: [2608.31068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31068)
**TL;DR**: Across reasoning benches, hidden-state probes still decode the correct answer after native sequence scoring has collapsed under structural bias. A two-parameter, target-label-free additive correction fit on 25 unlabeled examples recovers 9–34 accuracy points on Qwen3.5 and transfers to OLMo-2-1B and Llama-3.1-8B; the recovered decisions beat lexical-overlap and permutation controls on hard items.
**Why it matters**: Many “the model cannot reason” zeros are late-stage readout failures, so benchmark tables are overstating capability gaps.
