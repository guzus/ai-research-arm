## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. ELF-REG: Scaling Continuous Diffusion Language Models to Reasoning Tasks
**Authors**: Zeyu Michael Li, William Xingxu Chen, Bingshuo Qian, Jiayin Liu, Xiang Cheng
**arXiv**: [2609.29102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29102)
**TL;DR**: Fully continuous diffusion LMs denoise in representation space and decode every token in parallel at the last step — and have lagged AR and masked dLMs on hard reasoning. ELF-REG scales Embedded Language Flows with REPA+REG: a frozen AR teacher supervises intermediate denoiser features and a global representation that is jointly denoised with the response. ELF-REG-L hits 55.96% GSM8K pass@1 at 64 NFE, 13.39% MATH-500 and 22.56% HumanEval at 128 NFE, ahead of comparable-scale dLMs. No few-step training: early-stop decode at 16 NFE already gives 41.21% HumanEval pass@10.
**Why it matters**: Continuous diffusion LMs can be reasoning models, not just parallel decoders — and the cheap early-stop path is free.

### 2. Breaking the Environment Wall: Evolving LLM Agent Environments for Recursive Self-Improvement
**Authors**: Yukai Wu, Yuanjing Yang, Le Zhou, Shaokun Han, Haoyu Wang, Zirui Tang, Xuanhe Zhou, Fan Wu et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.29773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29773)
**TL;DR**: Real offices and labs are not agent-ready: evidence is scattered, mixed with conflicting versions, and the room keeps changing. That drop is measured — SOTA agents go 83.9% → 57.6%. Env-Rethink (27B, post-trained) builds Collection Maps and Event Logs, then uses offline trajectory learning to find the noise, then *evolves* the environment with virtual event histories that rewrite state and evidence so the next agent has a harder room. Downstream rubric pass rises more than 15.1 points across nine models on 30 tasks.
**Why it matters**: Recursive self-improvement is an environment-search problem. The wall is the room, not the policy.

### 3. Just Ask Jev: Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions as a Zero-Shot Detector of AI Alignment Failures
**Authors**: Ruoqi Guo, Yi Liu, Gelei Deng, Yuekang Li, Lida Zhao, Yutao Wu, Simin Chen, Ying Zhang, Leo Yu Zhang
**arXiv**: [2609.29429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29429)
**TL;DR**: Alignment detectors are usually a generative judge per criterion or a one-label classifier. Jev (RL for calibrated decisions) answers many typed questions about one input with probabilities in a single call. RLCDAlignBench covers ten failures — sycophancy, jailbreak, deception, injection, hallucination, privacy, bias, reward hacking, hidden uncertainty, power seeking — across 44 benches and five target models. One generic question: median AUROC 0.886 zero-shot, beats supervised baselines on most benches, matches human-scorer agreement, and is 63× cheaper than LLM-judge scorers. Wording barely matters; context fields that encode the label do.
**Why it matters**: Screening alignment failures does not need a decoding pass per rubric line.

### 4. Who Holds the Pen? Let Specifications, Not Agents, Sign Off
**Authors**: Haiqing Li, Xin Ma, Yinhao Wu, Wenliang Zhong, Feng Jiang, Thao M. Dang, Junzhou Huang et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.29921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29921)
**TL;DR**: Agents plan, act, and declare done against specs that live in the same context window as the actor. Two gaps follow: understood-but-unsatisfied requirements, and a completion claim that is not the required state. On SkillsBench they extract 509 source-grounded task directions; seven models satisfy 79.6–86.4% of them, while completion-claim rates exceed official pass rates by 28.7–37.9 points. SpecHarness compiles visible specs into source-linked obligations; only admissible evidence from qualified providers may establish specification-governed state. Verifiable requirements are mediated at runtime; subjective ones stay advisory.
**Why it matters**: The model that acts cannot also be the authority that says the spec is met.

### 5. Beyond Spatial Benchmarks: From Spatial Reasoning to Navigation
**Authors**: Xun Huang, Shijia Zhao, Rongsheng Qu, Jiayuan Li, Xin Lu, Weixin Li, Chenglu Wen, Cheng Wang
**arXiv**: [2609.29934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29934)
**TL;DR**: Spatial-reasoning benches test isolated inferences from images or video. They do not predict navigation. The authors measure that gap, then build Spatial-Nav-100K and train in two stages: a shared spatial-navigation foundation, then phase-specialized skills. Spatial-NPD lets a teacher with spatial priors write grounded action preferences and distill them so the student needs no explicit spatial reasoning at inference. With 45 A100 GPU-hours of policy training, an 8B model reaches SR/SPL 77.4/35.4 on HM3D-v0.2, 60.2/30.5 on v0.1, and 47.9/20.6 on held-out MP3D — ahead of several closed-source or thousand-GPU-hour systems — at 148 ms/action.
**Why it matters**: A higher spatial-bench score is not a better navigator. Align the supervision with the walk.
