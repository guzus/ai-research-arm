# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-06

*Curation window: the 2026-08-05 submission batch across cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.CL,
cs.CV, cs.NE (~180 entries screened; cs.NE reaches back further because the
category's daily volume is thin).*

The day's centre of gravity is **agent training infrastructure**: three
independent groups attack the same bottleneck — sparse, uninformative reward on
long-horizon rollouts — from credit assignment (ABSeeker), rollout economics
(RAIL, SpecRoll), and environment-derived supervision (State2State). Second
theme: **audit papers**, where the contribution is finding that an accepted
benchmark or evaluation protocol is broken (SciCode-Verified, NAVSIM scoring,
IRT over safety benchmarks).

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Argus: A General-Purpose Agentic Runtime for Long-Horizon Reasoning
**Authors**: Boxiu Li, Zimo Wen, Yijia Fan, Junxiang Lei
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05144
**TL;DR**: A self-evolving runtime that splits long-horizon work across Manager,
Planner and Engineer roles with persistent state carried between them, and gates
each self-modification behind a verification step. Reports 78% on SWE-Bench Pro.
**Why it matters**: The claimed gain comes from the harness rather than the
model, which is where most of this year's agentic headroom has actually been.

### 2. Towards Physics of Multimodal Pretraining
**Authors**: Junlin Han, Shengbang Tong, David Fan, Minghao Chen
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05000
**TL;DR**: A systematic sweep of multimodal pretraining that maps how knowledge
flows between modalities, when the modalities are actually synergistic rather
than merely co-present, and why unifying them early in training pays off.
**Why it matters**: Multimodal training recipes are still largely folklore; this
is the rare paper trying to give them scaling-law-style structure.

### 3. Reasoning Core: Designing Broad Procedural Data for Completion-Supervised Reasoning Training
**Authors**: Damien Sileo, Valentin Lacombe, Dimitri Kachler, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05148
**TL;DR**: Procedural generators emit verifiable reasoning problems across
mathematics, logic, planning and code, with quality audits on the generated set,
producing training data whose correctness is checkable by construction.
**Why it matters**: Verifier-backed synthetic data is the main escape route from
the human-annotation ceiling on reasoning training.

### 4. Gradient Immunity: Null-Space Resistance to Malicious Fine-Tuning
**Authors**: Yuxuan Huang, Xingyu Zeng, Tianhang Zheng, Chaochao Lu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05045
**TL;DR**: A "unidirectional safety gate" installed before release blocks harmful
gradient directions in representation space, so downstream fine-tuning cannot
easily strip the model's refusal behaviour.
**Why it matters**: Tamper-resistant open weights is the unsolved precondition
for open-weight releases being defensible at all.

### 5. ABSeeker: Training Long-Horizon Search Agents via Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment
**Authors**: Yijun Lu, Rui Ye, Jiajun Wang, Yuwen Du
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05102
**TL;DR**: Backtracks from the final answer to convert a single sparse trajectory
outcome into dense step-level rewards, reporting 55.3% on BrowseComp.
**Why it matters**: Sparse terminal reward is the binding constraint on
long-horizon agent RL; dense credit assignment without human step labels is the
lever everyone is reaching for.
