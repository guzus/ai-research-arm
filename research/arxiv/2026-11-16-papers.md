## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots
**Authors**: Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, Fangzhou Zhao, Yilong Zhao, Zekai Li, Xingyang Li, Yuyang Zhao, Song Bian, Zhijian Liu, Song Han
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562
**TL;DR**: Open-weight robot-use agents lag GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2. A 27B reasoning model is trained with robot-use RL entirely in simulation, with no demonstrations and no harness: it looks, thinks, and emits the next end-effector pose. Skills transfer to an unseen robot, to novel instructions, and to hardware. Real dual-arm success is 60% vs 0% for the base model; LIBERO rises 1.6% → 74.4%, approaching frontier models at max reasoning effort. On LIBERO-Pro a new task in a familiar scene yields 57.4% vs 0.75% for demonstration-tuned π₀.₅, with no drop on nine general benches.
**Why it matters**: It treats robot use as the same post-training problem as computer use, and shows sim-only RL is enough to put an open-weight reasoner on a real robot.

### 2. Soteria: Formally Verified Planning with Runtime Enforcement for Safe LLM Agents
**Authors**: Mike He, Ankush Desai, Sharad Malik, Aarti Gupta
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17880
**TL;DR**: Blocking an unsafe tool call protects the spec and strands the agent. Soteria verifies a structured plan against domain policies before any tool runs, then uses that plan both as a legal trajectory and as recovery guidance when a runtime check fires. Across tool-use benches it reaches 1.7× the average task success of the best baselines with zero policy violations. NeurIPS 2026 Oral.
**Why it matters**: It is a rare safety result that raises utility instead of trading it away, from the labs that own hardware verification.

### 3. V-JEPA-LM: Video-Language Models That Read Dynamics Beyond Static, Text-Aligned Representations
**Authors**: Keuntae Kim, Dongjun Kim, Yong Suk Choi
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17831
**TL;DR**: Frozen V-JEPA already encodes speed, order and state, but caption/QA alignment teaches the language head to retrieve named attributes instead of reading the clip: a linear probe gets speed at 87% while the VLM is at chance. Counterfactual pixel transforms turn public videos into answer-balanced pairs whose labels are set by the change. One-stage training on 4.53M public samples with 32 tokens/frame (open VLMs use several stages, 9–172M samples, 64–256 tokens) puts 1B–8B models first in every size class across eleven video benches; the 4B scores 72.1 vs 61.1 for the best open 4B and 64.8 for the best open 8B. Matched-budget counterfactual supervision adds 4.5 points on average and 38 on the speed question.
**Why it matters**: It separates what a video encoder can represent from what language-side supervision actually reads.

### 4. Learning Anisotropic Prediction Targets for Flow Models
**Authors**: Rayhan Zirvi, Hansheng Chen, Gordon Wetzstein, Leonidas Guibas
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17767
**TL;DR**: Flow models are trained on a hand-chosen target (velocity, noise, or clean data). AnisoFlow replaces that with a fully learnable one: a nonnegative weight per direction in a jointly learned basis, with a closed-form map back to velocity so architecture, loss and sampler stay put. Theory predicts that preferred noise weight scales near-linearly with data variance, matching the weights learned in pixel space. On ImageNet 256×256, FID falls 1.90 → 1.55 over the JiT baseline with no representation alignment and no architecture change; in pixel-space T2I it also cuts patch-seam artifacts under reduced-precision inference.
**Why it matters**: It treats the prediction target as a first-class learned object, not a hyperparameter.

### 5. ModHarness-RL: Recasting Harnesses as Learnable and Evolving Programs for Agentic RL
**Authors**: Zelin He, Siyu Zhu, Jacob Helwig, Yujian Liu, Li An, Rohit Jain, Shiyu Chang, Alborz Geramifard
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18533
**TL;DR**: Agentic RL usually freezes a hand-written harness while the model trains. ModHarness-RL records the interaction as typed State blocks and learns two programs over them: Compression (what the model sees of its history) and Steering (guidance when behavior drifts). Candidate programs are proposed from recent trajectories, verified by execution, and replayed on the past, so the harness tracks the policy. On CodeSearch, ALFWorld and BIRD SQL with 1.5B–8B models, learned Compression and Steering beat the fixed-harness baseline in most settings.
**Why it matters**: It makes the scaffold around an agent a trained, evolving artifact instead of a prompt that is assumed not to matter.
