## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Latent Sparse Attention: Towards Subquadratic Ultra-Long Context Attention
**Authors**: Ales Kubicek, Andrea Caciolai, Liangzu Peng, Sukrit Sumant, Benjamin Hoover, Fraser Greenlee, Ruairidh M. Battleday, Radim Urban, Torsten Hoefler, James C. R. Whittington
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42147
**TL;DR**: LSA hierarchically clusters keys in a learned latent space, so queries score clusters instead of every key. A distilled variant (D-LSA) retrofits pretrained models, and a native variant (N-LSA) trains from the next-token loss alone. At 10M tokens, D-LSA speeds up one attention layer by up to 72x in prefill over full attention with similar accuracy. N-LSA matches full-attention accuracy while training 1.6x faster per layer at 128k.
**Why it matters**: It targets the cost of attention beyond 1M tokens that long-horizon agentic work runs into.

### 2. Subquadratic Attention with a Hierarchical Tree-Structured Indexer (ZHA)
**Authors**: Leon Lufkin, Cutter Dawes, Alexander Speicher, Kamesh Krishnamurthy, Tomás Figliolia, Beren Millidge
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42112
**TL;DR**: Zyphra Hierarchical Attention builds a tree of recursively compressed key summaries over the KV cache. Queries traverse it top-down to fetch a sparse key subset, which makes total cost asymptotically subquadratic. A short KL-distillation stage over a few billion tokens retrofits existing models and keeps long-context retrieval, reasoning, math and agentic-coding performance. It is robust to trees up to 6 levels deep.
**Why it matters**: It is a second independent route to subquadratic sparse attention on pretrained models, and the authors project context windows of hundreds of millions of tokens.

### 3. SecPO: Principled Adversarial Training for Prompt Injection Security
**Authors**: Sizhe Chen, Yibo Peng, Jaewon Chang, Chawin Sitawarin, David Wagner
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41998
**TL;DR**: DPO anchors on an insecure reference model, so the authors approximate an idealized injection-invariant reference and derive the SecPO loss. They pair it with adversarial training on strong injections (best@240). On AgentDojo with Qwen3.6-27B, ASR@800 under AttackerMovesSecond falls to 8.8%, against 78.8% for Meta-SecAlign, with no noticeable benign-utility loss across nine benchmarks. Code, data and model are released.
**Why it matters**: It is a large, open, reproducible gain against adaptive prompt injection, a top threat to deployed agents.

### 4. Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space for Unified Multimodal Models (LARC)
**Authors**: Byungwoo Jeon, Yoonwoo Jeong, Hyunseok Lee, Minsu Cho, Jinwoo Shin
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42149
**TL;DR**: Discrete chain-of-thought tokens mismatch continuous image generation. LARC reasons over continuous hidden states, trained with curriculum SFT followed by information-gain RL with no ground-truth supervision. It sets state of the art on GenEval (+7 points over BAGEL) and shows test-time scaling under both parallel and sequential strategies.
**Why it matters**: It gets LLM-style test-time scaling to work for image generation in unified multimodal models.

### 5. MoETok: Sparse Expert Routing for 1D Visual Tokenizers
**Authors**: Jiahao Guo, Bao Tang, Haoxiang Cao, Jingfeng Yao, Bo Li, Wenyu Liu, Kun Gai, Changqian Yu, Xinggang Wang
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41992
**TL;DR**: Under semantic supervision, learnable queries in 1D tokenizers split into Content queries (semantics) and Detail queries (reconstruction). MoETok routes tokens through sparse experts matched to these roles. It beats dense tokenizers at matched active compute. In a unified autoregressive model it reports DPG-Bench 87.61, GenEval2 40.04 and WISE 0.49, matching or surpassing diffusion models such as BAGEL and Qwen-Image.
**Why it matters**: It is an architectural step toward discrete unified models that rival diffusion on generation.
