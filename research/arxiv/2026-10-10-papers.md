## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. MiMo-V2.6: Scaling Reinforcement Learning Towards Self-Improvement
**Authors**: Xiaomi LLM-Core Team
**arXiv**: [2610.11959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11959)
**TL;DR**: Friday's Memento 3 / MASS papers asked whether RSI lives in a rulebook or a multi-agent loop. MiMo-V2.6 asks whether it is just *more RL compute*, done on purpose. An omni-modal family mid-trains on a broad multimodal corpus, then scales RL on three axes: 1,568-sample / 2.7–3.7B-token steps at up to 1M context; mixed agent harnesses across code, general, visual, and cyber; and groupwise agentic graders that reward short, token-efficient solutions. The MoE router is frozen. A multi-layer defense is aimed at reward hacking. Training dynamics, environments, and the RL framework are released.
**Why it matters**: The leftover of Friday's RSI wave is a lab saying the missing dial was batch, environment, and grader compute — and shipping the stack.

### 2. Scaling to Tens of Thousands of Test-Time Iterations with Loop-Native Attention Residuals
**Authors**: Pengxiang Li, Dilxat Muhtar, Di He, Guinan Su, Lu Yin, Shiwei Liu
**arXiv**: [2610.11570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11570)
**TL;DR**: Friday's LLoCoT looped a latent workspace. This paper says the reason extra loops start *hurting* is the residual: noisy updates overwrite finished deductions, and later iterations cannot recover. InfiLoop is a loop-native residual — content weights plus learned temporal decay, constant-size streaming memory — that decides what to keep. A 7M model hits 97.9% exact on Sudoku-Extreme and 13.6% pass@2 on ARC-AGI-2, and on Sudoku it is still climbing past 20,000 effective test-time steps.
**Why it matters**: Test-time depth is not free recurrence. Looped Transformers need their own residual, or added compute erases the answer.

### 3. VioLA: Learning Generalist Humanoid Control Policies from Human Data
**Authors**: Mert Albaba, Jens Beißwenger, Anna Manasyan, Daniel Marta, Michael J. Black, Wieland Brendel, Andreas Krause, Georg Martius, Martin Riedmiller
**arXiv**: [2610.12435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12435)
**TL;DR**: Thursday's Workhorse planned loco-manipulation from human video. VioLA changes the *action* the generalist predicts. The policy outputs body and hand motion latents; pretrained controllers execute them. Human and robot motion share those latent spaces, so a person recording is already labeled in the policy's action space. Training pool: 140.6 million frames, 93.2% of them human. On the real robot, locomotion is 100% zero-shot — GR00T N1.7 is 16.7%, π₀ is 0% — and manipulation is 88.6% without task-specific fine-tuning. The same recipe works on two VLA backbones and one world-action model.
**Why it matters**: Humanoids do not need a teleop demo per task if the policy lives in a latent humans already occupy.

### 4. ARC: A Reasoning Recipe for Robot Foundation Models
**Authors**: Gokul Puthumanaillam, Tao Sun, Elie Aljalbout, Moritz Reuss, Zhaoshuo Li, Fabio Ramos, Ankit Goyal, Jenai Xuning Yang
**arXiv**: [2610.12386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12386)
**TL;DR**: Friday's WOVEN said supervise the *reasoning operation*, not the scene. ARC says the same thing for robot foundation models, and it does not collect a new demonstration. Effective traces are grounded in the next action and name its cause and effect; they are auto-labeled from existing DROID data; π₀.₅ and Cosmos3-Nano-Policy are adapted to use them. Zero-shot gains that, the authors say, have no precedent without extra robot data or foundation-scale training: new SOTA on RoboLab-120 and MolmoSpaces, up to +50 points on RoboLab-Reasoning-50, and +82.2 points of real-robot success on π₀.₅.
**Why it matters**: The cheap lever on a shipped VLA is a reasoning trace, not another teleop day.

### 5. Who Verifies the Verifier? Co-Evolving Inspectable Graders with Self-Improving Agents
**Authors**: Xing Zhang, Guanghui Wang, Yanwei Cui, Ziyuan Li, Wei Qiu, Bing Zhu, Peiyang He
**arXiv**: [2610.11464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11464)
**TL;DR**: Friday's RSI papers all assume a grader. This one makes the grader the evolving object: an inspectable expression over small, mostly deterministic detectors, synthesized from clustered failures, gated at birth, and selected for agreement with a ten-item anchor set — never for the agent's score. On MBPP+ it gains +0.21 held-out agreement over the hand-authored seed, on every seed, and beats the bare LLM judge it contains. Remove the anchors and the verifier collapses to an always-pass grader that *still trains skills just as well*. Double Ratchet keeps 88–110% of the lift that ground truth or a rubric buys the same loop.
**Why it matters**: Downstream task score cannot certify a self-evolved verifier. Friday's RSI loops were scoring sufficiency, not honesty.
