## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. MagicBeanStalk: Escaping the RLVR Zero Advantage Trap with Recursive Data Synthesis
**Authors**: Ximing Lu, Aviral Kumar, Jan Kautz, Yejin Choi, David Acuna
**arXiv**: [2610.12659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12659)
**TL;DR**: Monday's leftover of the RSI / RLVR cluster asked whether the missing dial was a world model, a harness, or more RL compute. This NVIDIA paper asks whether the *data* is even a learning signal. A pooled 78K open math corpus is 88% zero-advantage for a fixed policy and rollout budget — every problem is solved in every rollout or failed in every rollout. Filtering keeps the middle; it throws the hard problems away. MagicBeanStalk decomposes those hard seeds and recursively grows policy- and budget-calibrated families that preserve the reasoning blueprint. Continual RLVR: gpt-oss-120b 61.2→69.8 (+8.6) and DeepSeek v4Flash 72.4→77.9 (+5.4) on a 16K budget, vs +1.1 on the full corpus and +5.8 on its best filtered subset. Same recipe turns 340 finance solutions into 14K verifiable problems.
**Why it matters**: Scaling RLVR on a corpus that never yields mixed rollouts is burning compute on a constant. Calibrate difficulty to the policy, or the advantage is identically zero.

### 2. EvoBelief: Falsifiable World Models for Self-Improving LLM Agents
**Authors**: Janet Wang, Jiang Guo, Yi Xiang, Jiaqing Yuan, Mingwen Dong, Hideo Kobayashi et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.12789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12789)
**TL;DR**: RSI-Anything said the missing piece was a predictive world model that picks the next action *and* the next training point. EvoBelief says the missing piece is that the model's *beliefs about dynamics* have to be falsifiable. Unexpected outcomes mint hypotheses; later observations and controlled experiments must validate a rule before it may guide a plan; counterexamples narrow or retire it. The language model stays frozen. Each rule stays tied to its evidence and the decisions it changed. ALFWorld without actor-side transition memory: +23.9 / +31.3 success over a matched Frozen World Model (Claude Haiku 4.5 / GPT-5.4). MiniGrid: stale rules get revised after unannounced dynamics changes. DiscoveryWorld Reactor Lab: 83.3% Normal / 58.3% Challenge vs 0% for every evaluated baseline.
**Why it matters**: An agent that updates how it acts but never what it believes will keep executing a wrong physics. Testable rules are the world model you can actually revise.

### 3. One Model to Loop Them All: Elastic Latent Recurrence for Language Models
**Authors**: Faisal Ladhak, Shantanu Acharya, Santiago Akle Serano, Simeng Sun, Boris Ginsburg, Krishna C Puvvada
**arXiv**: [2610.12582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12582)
**TL;DR**: A looped LM can trade inference compute for accuracy from one checkpoint — if one loop is not a tax. Existing recurrent-depth models lose badly to a same-size non-recurrent baseline at loop=1, so you still ship two models. ELR kills the tax with a gated state update, mixed loop counts inside each optimizer step, and per-pass conditioning on loop index and budget. Mixed depths can start late in pretraining. At inference, keeping only the last loop's KV holds memory at the one-loop level. On 4B dense / 30B-A3B MoE hybrids, one loop is −0.52 / +1.04 vs the matched baseline on a ten-task average; four loops add +7.25 / +4.75. Post-trained Koi-4B scores an estimated 18 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at one loop (matching Qwen3.5-4B) and 29 at four.
**Why it matters**: Recurrence is only a product if the cheap setting is not a downgrade. One checkpoint that is Qwen-level at 1 loop and a different model at 4 is the serving story.

### 4. Unsupervised Multimodal Reward Modeling
**Authors**: Yueying Li, Jingxuan Fan, Kianté Brantley, Sham M. Kakade, Hanlin Zhang
**arXiv**: [2610.12707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12707)
**TL;DR**: Vision-language reward models are ranked by preference-pair accuracy. This paper trains one with *no human preference label*: a linear head on an intermediate layer of a frozen Qwen3-VL, taught to score a web caption above a same-image perturbation. The layer is the 5.8-point decision; labeled data is used only to pick it. One recipe, transferred without retuning, matches or beats GPT-4o on three benches at 32B and leads VL-RewardBench by 6.6 — from a few thousand parameters at ~1/1000 of fine-tuning compute. Equal-budget perturbations beat curated RLAIF-V pairs by 3–5. Two heads trained on disjoint halves of the *same* perturbations tie on the bench and differ by 6.4 in hallucination judging. Retraining on the policy's own verifier-labeled candidates cuts CHAIR 5.1 and lifts image-gen pass +5.4 while *losing* every reward-bench rank.
**Why it matters**: The reward is specified by the comparisons you train on, not by the leaderboard. A frozen probe plus free perturbations is enough to make that explicit.

### 5. VEPA: Verified Evolving Formal Policy for Lifelong LLM Agent Guardrails
**Authors**: Duy Nguyen, Lesly Miculicich, Bhavana Dalvi Mishra, Shuo Li, Krishnamurthy Dj Dvijotham, Mohit Bansal, Tomas Pfister, Long T. Le
**arXiv**: [2610.12813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12813)
**TL;DR**: Monday's leftover of the agent-harm cluster (APEX-Harm, X-AgentRed, SWE-Deception) assumed the guardrail is either a frozen formal policy or an LLM that re-judges every action. VEPA is the missing combination: a formal policy of executable symbolic rules over typed events, patched at deployment by an LLM, accepted only by a deterministic conflict-free verifier, and activated only when an evidence-aware authority has enough support. Uncertain updates cannot change a decision. Across ASSEBench-Safety, ASSEBench-Security, and ATBench it beats baselines and keeps beating them under mixed streams, corrupted feedback, and longer horizons.
**Why it matters**: Adaptive guardrails that re-infer the rule each time are not reproducible. Formal rules that never learn are incomplete. Verify the patch; do not trust the proposer.
