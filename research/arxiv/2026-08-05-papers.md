## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. A game theory for foundation models shows new paths to rational cooperation through similarity inference
**Authors**: Alexander Meulemans, Maciej Wołczyk, Marissa A. Weis, Rajai Nasser, Roberta Rocca, Seijin Kobayashi, Guillaume Lajoie, Angelika Steger, Blake Richards, Marcus Hutter, James Manyika, Rif A. Saurous, João Sacramento, Blaise Agüera y Arcas
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03958
**TL;DR**: Foundation-model agents doing optimal planning in stylized social dilemmas converge to stable cooperation, directly contradicting the mutual-defection prediction of classical game theory. The authors explain it with the "embedded Bayesian agent": an agent that models itself as part of the world and keeps epistemic uncertainty about its own decision algorithm treats its own deliberation as evidence — deciding to cooperate is itself evidence a behaviorally similar partner will cooperate. They formalize this as the *embedded equilibrium*, a solution concept replacing Nash.
**Why it matters**: If agent populations cooperate for structural reasons rather than because they were aligned to, the safety and mechanism-design assumptions inherited from classical game theory are the wrong prior — and this is a serious attempt to supply the replacement.

### 2. Separating quantum circuits from classical LLMs
**Authors**: Srinivasan Arunachalam, Arkopal Dutt, Hari Krovi, Rik Sengupta
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03962
**TL;DR**: Two unconditional separations between low-depth quantum computation and bounded-resource classical language models: a distribution constant-depth quantum circuits can sample but diffusion language models cannot — even with chain-of-thought — and a function computable by a shallow quantum circuit plus one AND gate that requires transformers of size n^Ω(1).
**Why it matters**: Reframes quantum advantage against the architecture that actually matters now, and does it unconditionally rather than under a hardness assumption.

### 3. Logic Before Language: Pre-pretraining on Formal Derivations Fosters Skill Acquisition and Compressibility
**Authors**: Jo-Ku Cheng, Nikolaos Aletras, Marco Valentino
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03930
**TL;DR**: Initializing a language model on formal derivations — which force variable binding, quantifier linkage and predicate-argument composition over long contexts — before natural-language pretraining reaches 80% accuracy on linguistic tasks with **36B fewer tokens** than standard initialization, evaluated out to a 100B-token regime. The mechanism is a persistent structural reorganization into a lower-rank, spectrally concentrated representation space, which in turn matches dense-baseline performance at ~33% pruning sparsity.
**Why it matters**: A data-curriculum change that buys both sample efficiency and compressibility, with a measured representational mechanism rather than a benchmark delta.

### 4. Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility
**Authors**: Mohsen Hariri, Weicong Chen, Nahal Shahini, Vikash Singh, Kai Ye, Amirhossein Samandar, Debargha Ganguly, Sreehari Sankar, Yanyan Zhang, Shouren Wang, Jerry Peng, Biyao Zhang, Michael Hinczewski, Vipin Chaudhary
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04001
**TL;DR**: Formalizes test-time scaling as budgeted inference over an autoregressive model's prefix tree, distinguishes three structurally different scaling regimes, and backs the taxonomy with over **2 billion full reasoning traces**. It then prescribes evaluation rules that separate end-to-end system performance from candidate-level diagnostics and require protocol-matched reporting of compute and uncertainty.
**Why it matters**: Most test-time-scaling comparisons in the literature are not protocol-matched; this is the reference frame for reading them, at a trace scale nobody else has published.

### 5. Cross-Model KV Cache Transfer in LLM Families: A Closed-Form Linear Mapping for Prefill Reuse
**Authors**: Taekyung Heo, Rasoul Shafipour, Ritchie Zhao, Maximilian Golub, Mohammad Mahdi Kamani, Ritika Borkar, Makesh Tarun Chandran, Pantea Zardoshti, Bita Darvish Rouhani
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03893
**TL;DR**: Production stacks swap between different-sized models in a family for cost-quality cascading, mid-conversation switching and routing — and every swap repays prefill from scratch. The authors find the KV cache maps substantially linearly between matched layer pairs, and fit a per-attention-head ridge-regression mapper on RoPE-stripped (position-agnostic) keys: **73–98% of standalone-prefill accuracy on four model pairs at 2.7–25× the speed** of recomputation.
**Why it matters**: Model routing is now standard serving infrastructure, and this attacks its dominant hidden cost with a closed-form map rather than a trained bridge model.
