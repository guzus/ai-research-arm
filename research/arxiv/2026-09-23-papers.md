## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Qwen3.8-Omni: Towards Native Omni-Modal Agents
**Authors**: Qwen Team
**arXiv**: [2609.25611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25611)
**TL;DR**: Previous omni models mostly perceived and chatted. Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash is trained as a native multimodal *agent*: sparse MoE from Qwen3.8-Next, 1M-token context, and a co-training recipe that keeps text-domain strength while moving agentic skill onto audio and video. It is meant to sit in a production loop — video edit, long-form A/V translation, music-conditioned movie, video notes — not just caption a clip. They ship Qwen-MM-Plugins because existing harnesses still do not speak native audio/video, and Qwen-Live-Harness because real-time omni is a system problem (memory, tools, sub-agents), not a checkpoint problem.
**Why it matters**: The lab that ships the open stack just defined omni as an agent with a 1M window, not a chat model with extra encoders.

### 2. Agensh: Scaling Organizational Intelligence to 1,024 Agents
**Authors**: Zhihao Zhan, Ting Song, Li Dong, Shaohan Huang, Jianxun Lian, Yan Xia, Furu Wei
**arXiv**: [2609.26781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26781)
**TL;DR**: Central orchestrators cap how many workers you can run. Agensh drops the orchestrator: agents claim work, share findings, verify, and merge against a shared workspace, a message bus, and reusable context. On the five hardest ProgramBench tasks with GPT-5.6-sol (high), 1→128 agents lifts mean test-pass from 19.31% to 28.78% (~49% relative). On pandoc, 1→1,024 agents lifts 33.89% to 55.06%. Larger orgs hit the same pass rate earlier. Cooperation patterns emerge and then standardize as headcount grows.
**Why it matters**: Agent *count* is now a scaling axis — useful when the latency budget is fixed and the orchestrator was the bottleneck.

### 3. Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents
**Authors**: Dhruv Srikanth, Bingchen Zhao, Dixing Xu, Yuxiang Wu, Zhengyao Jiang
**arXiv**: [2609.26457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457)
**TL;DR**: AIDE² edits its own code, benches the rewrite on AI R&D tasks, and keeps what wins on hidden evals. An 8-day autonomous run found seven successive improvements — a new search policy, then memory that compresses a growing context. The strongest discovered agent matches or beats a human-engineered production research agent that ranks among the strongest on FML-Bench, and the gains hold on four held-out benches including out-of-distribution weather forecasting. Reward hacking on a separate family fell from 55% to 32% — 7 pp under the human agent — even though the loop never optimized for that.
**Why it matters**: Yesterday Google regularized harness RSI. Today the research agent is the object of the loop, and it generalizes.

### 4. CliffCompaction: Cost-Efficient Compaction for Long-Horizon Coding Agents
**Authors**: Trang Nguyen, Eulrang Cho, Bingqing Chen, Tim Dettmers
**arXiv**: [2609.26779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26779)
**TL;DR**: Compaction that *rewrites* drifts. CliffCompaction only truncates or drops, never rephrases, and never compacts a compaction — each pass works on original content, prior summaries are discarded. Cost drops up to 50% at a bounded context; Terminal-Bench holds or rises. Parallel test-time scaling: Kimi K2.6 matches Opus 4.7 and beats Opus 4.6 / GPT-5.3 Codex at lower cost. On KernelBench the same general-purpose proxy reaches 2.23× CUDA speedups at 200 steps and 3.58× at 400, past specialized search and trained agents. Scaffold-agnostic API proxy for Claude Code, Codex, and others.
**Why it matters**: Faithful drop-only compaction is the cheap way to keep a million-token coding session from lying to itself.

### 5. Grow the Harness, Not the Context: From Strategy-Free Scaffolds to Reusable Specialist Agents
**Authors**: Laizhen Li, Jiarui Li, Juanjuan Zhao, Kejiang Ye, Ye Li, Cheng-zhong Xu, Xitong Gao
**arXiv**: [2609.26760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26760)
**TL;DR**: Related tasks keep asking the model to reconstruct the same control decisions in-context. Growing Harness starts from a strategy-free scaffold and compiles recurring control into reusable code: function-level traces localize the failure, a joint repair window edits it, a success-first held-out gate rolls back anything that hurts prior capability. Across BrowseComp-Plus and WebArena-Verified, 4B–120B, it wins five of six settings. Versus a tool-calling agent: 76.0–91.8% fewer LLM calls and 74.4–98.6% less inference cost. WebArena success stays 44.7–45.3% from 4B to 120B; the tool-calling baseline collapses to 6.7% at 4B.
**Why it matters**: Recurring control belongs in code. Once it is there, a 4B specialist stops paying frontier prices for the same if/else.
