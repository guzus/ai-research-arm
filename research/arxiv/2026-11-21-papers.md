## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Frontier models need only one text to unmask its human or AI author
**Authors**: Atrey Desai, Deniz Böloni-Turgut, Ty Brassington, Minjoon Choi, Jenna Russell, Rishanth Rajendhran, Bradley Emi, Hila Gonen, Hal Daumé, John Wieting, Marzena Karpinska, Mohit Iyyer
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26216
**TL;DR**: One text and a zero-shot prompt — no tools, candidates, or writing samples — is enough for frontier models to say whether a piece is human or AI, which lab wrote it, and often *who*. Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 4 Carbon match trained detectors like Pangram; Opus 5.5 names the correct lab on 98% of Anthropic/OpenAI/Google texts. Fable 5.2 names the author of 39% of public Oct–Nov 2026 peer reviews (78% on a Nature subset) and the username behind 69% of active Redditors. Familiarity with an author’s writing, asked of the model itself, predicts whether identification will work.
**Why it matters**: For anyone with a public paper trail, frontier models are a bigger anonymity threat than stylometry-as-usual, and the paper says the defenses are thin.

### 2. Budget Internalization: Fixed-Budget RLVR Trades Test-Time Scaling for In-Budget Accuracy
**Authors**: Ilia Zavidnyi, Alexander Kovrigin, Arina Puchkova, Kirill Gelvan, Sergey Titov (JetBrains Research)
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26172
**TL;DR**: RLVR’s rollout cap is not just a training-cost knob. After training, Qwen3.5-4B, Gemma4-E2B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B barely spend extra tokens past the cap: they beat the base model near that budget and *lose* to it when you let them think longer. The effect transfers from math training to science and coding. Forcing an answer on truncated rollouts largely removes internalization — and with it the in-budget gain. SFT on artificially truncated traces reproduces the same freeze; SFT on naturally short traces does not.
**Why it matters**: Length-censored post-training, not an RL-specific term, is what sells cheap in-budget accuracy for worse test-time scaling.

### 3. TwinLab-Bench: Evaluating AI Scientists in Agentic Twins of Real Labs
**Authors**: Yang Wan, Linchao Zhu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26248
**TL;DR**: Existing science-agent benches score workflow execution or choosing from a closed design space. TwinLab-Bench scores *self-designed* experiments: 52 tasks from physics, chemistry, wet-lab biology, materials and engineering, >100 action types, with a TwinLab environment agent that executes the tested agent’s protocol against real lab records. Closed-book probes control for memorization. Claude Fable 5.2 averages 45.0, Opus 5.5 40.2, GPT-6.1-sol 31.7; the rest of a 13-agent field (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, …) is lower.
**Why it matters**: It is the first bench that lets an AI scientist invent the experiment and still grades it against a hidden real-lab answer.

### 4. Streaming-AAA: Streaming AAA Game Video Generation at High Resolution, High Frame Rate, and Low Latency
**Authors**: Zhihao Hu, Jianqi Ma, Jun Zhang, Jiayang Bai, Haifeng Shi, Yanxiang Zhuang, Zhongqian Sun, Weidong Zhang, Wei Yang
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26040
**TL;DR**: Interactive game video has not jointly hit fidelity, frame rate and latency. Streaming-AAA generates 1080p gameplay at 43 FPS with 198 ms end-to-end interaction on A800s, without collapse on long play. Three pieces: a VAE rebuilt for real-time 1080p decode; diffusion-forcing pretrain with a much longer temporal horizon; then distillation plus memory compression. The claim is infinite, controllable AAA-looking play, not another short clip demo.
**Why it matters**: This is the first streaming game generator that looks like a playable loop rather than a research video.

### 5. Efficient and Robust Drafting with Multi-Token Prediction
**Authors**: Filippo Galgani, Ziwei Ji, Omri Homburger, Ganesh Jawahar, Michelle Ramanovich, Ivan Korotkov, Luciano Martins, Utku Evci, Edouard Yvinec, Pooja Consul, Vivek Kumar, Tal Schuster
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25813
**TL;DR**: G-MTP is a single shared drafting head on Gemma 4: target-activation injection, zero-copy KV-cache cross-attention (no prefill of the drafter), and clustered vocabulary projection. Out of the box it is up to 3.1× on large server setups and 2.3× / 2.2× on H100 and a smartphone GPU. Parameter-efficient drafter fine-tunes keep it aligned under domain shift with up to 1.83× fewer target forwards than tuning the backbone.
**Why it matters**: Speculative decoding’s bottleneck is the drafter; this is a production-shaped MTP recipe on a current open family, not another tiny extra head.
