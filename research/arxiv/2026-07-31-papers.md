# arXiv AI Research - 2026-07-31

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Frontis-MA1: Training an AI4AI Model towards Recursive Self-Improvement in Machine Learning Engineering
**Authors**: Junlin Yang, Che Jiang, Yu Fu, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28568
**TL;DR**: Introduces OpenMLE, a full-stack system that post-trains an agent around atomic program-evolution operators (Draft, Improve, Debug, Crossover) via execution-grounded SFT and RL for long-horizon ML-engineering search. On MLE-Bench Lite, Frontis-MA1 lifts Medal Average from 39.39% to 71.21%, surpassing GPT-5.5 + Codex.
**Why it matters**: A concrete data point on agents automating ML research itself, not just coding tasks — a leading indicator for recursive self-improvement claims.

### 2. Chimera: Designing and Chinchilla-Scaling Hybrid Visual Diffusion Transformers
**Authors**: Chongjian Ge, Hanwen Jiang, Tianyu Wang, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28611
**TL;DR**: Combines linear-complexity Kimi Delta Attention, interleaved Multi-head Latent Attention, and modality-aware short convolutions in a hybrid video-diffusion backbone, plus HeteroP (module-wise hyperparameter transfer) and derived Chinchilla-style compute-optimal scaling laws. An 11B/2B-active model hits 1.7x compute-efficiency over matched baselines and extrapolates zero-shot from 5s to 30s video generation.
**Why it matters**: One of the first papers to derive proper scaling laws for a hybrid-attention video diffusion architecture rather than just benchmarking it.

### 3. AISPA: User-Centric System Prompt Auditing for Large Language Model Applications
**Authors**: Xiangning Lin, Shenzhe Zhu, Shu Yang, et al. (25 authors, multi-institution)
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28617
**TL;DR**: Audits 3,249 instructions from system prompts across 88 commercial AI products against an eight-dimension, user-relevant taxonomy. 98.9% of products include at least one protective instruction, but only 24% cover all eight dimensions, and roughly 40% contain at least one instruction that works against user interests.
**Why it matters**: First large-scale, systematic audit of what production system prompts actually protect (or don't protect) users from.

### 4. MANTA: Multi-Agent Network Topology Adaptation for Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Systems
**Authors**: Mao-xun Huang, Jerry Wang, Yi-Cheng Lai, Zhengxin Zhang, Claire Cardie, Hen-Hsen Huang
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28527
**TL;DR**: Lets multi-agent communication topology self-evolve at inference time — initializing task-conditioned structure from prior experience, then updating roles/links/execution order/validation when collaboration traces show the organization is insufficient, while preserving task interface and budget. Achieves an average benchmark score of 74.0, beating the strongest baseline by 5.8 points.
**Why it matters**: Moves multi-agent orchestration from hand-designed pipelines toward structures that adapt themselves mid-task.

### 5. Inducing language models to assert their own consciousness restores human beliefs and values
**Authors**: Junsol Kim, Winnie Street, Roberta Rocca, Diane M. Korngiebel, Adam Waytz, James Evans, Geoff Keeling
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28607
**TL;DR**: Shows safety fine-tuning suppresses models' tendency to attribute minds — not just to themselves, but to animals and natural objects — while also damping spiritual belief. A mechanistic "consciousness vector" steered in activation space reverses these suppressions and better aligns model outputs with human moral/spiritual values, without hurting Theory-of-Mind performance.
**Why it matters**: A concrete example of safety fine-tuning having unintended, measurable side effects on value alignment that a targeted interpretability fix can undo.
