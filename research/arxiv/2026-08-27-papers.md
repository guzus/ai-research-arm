# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-27

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Prefix Sliding for efficient test-time scaling
**Authors**: Niklas Muennighoff, Zhengyang Wang, Zeyi Chen, Weijia Shi, Binyuan Hui, John Yang, ... Percy Liang, Andrew Y. Ng, Luke Zettlemoyer, Yejin Choi, Mike Lewis
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26070
**TL;DR**: Shows most intermediate reasoning tokens lose importance as a model keeps thinking, and proposes Prefix Sliding, which discards non-prefix, non-recent tokens during reasoning to cap memory use regardless of reasoning length. Without training it makes existing models 3x faster at matched performance, and with RL training it unlocks reasoning traces beyond 100K tokens that outperform summarizing or vanilla sliding windows.
**Why it matters**: Removes the central cost barrier to long-horizon test-time scaling, turning "think longer" from a memory-prohibitive regime into a practical one for frontier models.

### 2. Spectral Allocation: Why Muon Outperforms Adam, and How to Improve Muon (SAMuon)
**Authors**: Xiaodong Wu, Wenyi Yu, Chao Zhang, Philip Woodland
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25990
**TL;DR**: Uses out-of-sample spectral probing to decompose momentum buffers into singular directions and finds a stable anisotropic step-size profile: a volatile "Edge-of-Stability" head supports small steps while the "tolerant bulk" allows much larger ones. This yields a unified account of why Muon > Adam > SGD and motivates Spectral-Aware Muon (SAMuon), which amplifies the bulk with a static spectral prior at no extra optimizer state or FLOPs.
**Why it matters**: First principled explanation of the empirical Muon-vs-Adam gap, delivering 13-24% fewer tokens to a fixed validation loss — a concrete training-efficiency win at scale.

### 3. VBVR-Pro: A Scalable and Verifiable Suite for Native Visual Reasoning
**Authors**: Junxiang Xu, Ruisi Wang, Fanyi Pu, ... Dahua Lin, Lei Yang, Hokin Deng, Zhongang Cai
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26105
**TL;DR**: A closed-loop testbed for "native visual reasoning" — treating generated images/videos as the reasoning substrate itself — with 300 procedurally generated tasks, deterministic task-grounded reward scorers, and a controlled study across 30+ image/video/interleaved generators. Trained models transfer across seven external visual reasoning benchmarks, and the rule-based scorers outperform VLMs-as-judges as RL reward signals.
**Why it matters**: Provides the scalable, verifiable training signal that visual-reasoning-through-generation has been missing, and surfaces which generative substrate best supports which kind of reasoning.

### 4. AsymSpec: Context-Asymmetric Speculative Decoding for Agentic LLMs
**Authors**: Sheng Liang, Yongyue Zhang, Nathanael Brian, Hang Lv, Hao Wang, Chen Zhang, Yong Liu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26004
**TL;DR**: Breaks the speculative-decoding assumption that drafter and verifier share identical context: a lightweight drafter reads the full input while the large verifier works on a compressed view, steered by contrastive logit fusion with a divergence-aware acceptance gate. Reaches ~90% of full-context accuracy at 1.3-1.7x throughput and 0.2-0.3x compute across agentic capabilities and end-to-end agent benchmarks.
**Why it matters**: For the first time resolves the accuracy-vs-latency trade-off of context compression in agentic pipelines without lossy fine-tuning, a direct answer to escalating agentic inference costs.

### 5. R^3: Training Robots to Reason in Natural Language via Reinforcement Learning
**Authors**: Lehong Wu, Yuxiao Qu, Zheyuan Hu, Ivan Zhang, Limin Wei, Zackory Erickson, Aviral Kumar
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26053
**TL;DR**: A post-training recipe that turns off-the-shelf VLMs into robotic "reasoners": mid-train on expert reasoning traces, then improve free-form language reasoning with single-step rubric-based RL from offline action data. Trained on Language Table and bimanual grocery packing, R^3's reasoning guides low-level policies and significantly outperforms instruction-only imitation baselines on unseen tasks.
**Why it matters**: Makes language-based test-time compute usable for steering policies and generalizing decomposition, constraint tracking, and recovery beyond seen manipulation tasks.
