# arXiv AI Research - 2026-09-06

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Principia: Relational Physics Tests for Video Models
**Authors**: Varun Varma Thozhiyoor, Shivam Tripathi, Venkatesh Babu Radhakrishnan, Anand Bhattad
**arXiv**: [2609.04200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04200)
**TL;DR**: Absolute motion in generated video depends on frame rate, scale, and camera calibration — quantities the clip usually does not disclose. Principia instead scores *relational* Newtonian consistency between paired objects in the same scene (gravity, restitution, friction, rotational inertia, projectiles, momentum, pendulum, mass-spring), with a calibration-free image-space violation score. Across thousands of generations from six SOTA video models, none exceed 0.42 on Principia while all sit near 0.8 on VBench. The best VLM detects relational violations at 67%; most are near chance.
**Why it matters**: VBench-style visual quality is not a physics test — current video generators fail a calibration-independent relational check that a real camera would pass.

### 2. Clean Engineering, Unstable Measurement: A Preregistered Reliability Failure of Black-Box LLM Observers on Shared Endpoints
**Authors**: Haoyaun Zhu, Jie Zhang
**arXiv**: [2609.04198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04198)
**TL;DR**: LLM-as-judge pipelines assume the same request to the same model name reads the same tomorrow. Two preregistered campaigns (52,988 attempts) never cleared instrument validation: same-window rank Spearman 0.400 vs a required 0.90, next-day byte-identical replay 0.78 vs 0.99, with the execution record at ceiling. Waiting, switching providers (four share the floor, medians 0.74–0.88), and metric substitution did not repair it; self-hosting on batch-invariant kernels helped only while the server was quiet. A 2% pilot would have killed both gates.
**Why it matters**: On a shared endpoint a model name is not a frozen instrument — any preregistered eval has to measure the judge before it freezes a gate on it.

### 3. Legibility is Not Interpretability: Comparing Judged and Actual Importance in Chain-Of-Thought Reasoning
**Authors**: Kevin Du, Alexander Hoyle, Laura Ruis, Acyr Locatelli
**arXiv**: [2609.04194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04194)
**TL;DR**: Process reward models, generative critics, and faithfulness judges treat the text of a CoT step as encoding its functional role. Importance is operationalized as Monte Carlo advantage — the change in expected final-answer reward from including that step. Capable LLM judges beat a prevalence baseline but sit well below the noise ceiling; a fine-tuned step critic improves on *incorrect* traces and stays far from ceiling on *correct* ones. Step importance is only partly recoverable from the words. COLM 2026.
**Why it matters**: Readable traces are not interpretability — PRMs and CoT monitors are supervising a narrative that only loosely tracks which steps actually move the answer.

### 4. Efficient Test-Time Adaptation through Human-AI Interaction
**Authors**: Zora Zhiruo Wang, Apurva Gandhi, … Pang Wei Koh, Diyi Yang, Graham Neubig, Daniel Fried
**arXiv**: [2609.04141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04141)
**TL;DR**: Population-trained agents miss the personal bar professionals will stake a reputation on. TAHI treats cross-session edits as the missing signal: it writes them into context and weights and crystallizes each user’s criteria in an evolving rubric. On 600 writing and visual-creation tasks for 30 people, solo success rises 4.5–20.9% within tens of tasks; the rubric catches 16.0–22.3% more failures than LM-only or human-only labels. Personalized agents still transfer, lifting other users by up to 8.8%.
**Why it matters**: The expertise that matters is the *departure from the average* — and it is already sitting in the interaction log, unused.

### 5. Terminal-Universe: Turning Agent Trajectories into Scalable Terminal Environments
**Authors**: Jie Wu, Zhenru Zhang, … An Yang, Fei Huang, Dayiheng Liu
**arXiv**: [2609.04148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04148)
**TL;DR**: Post-training needs executable environments (re-queryable, with feedback); public traces are frozen demos. Terminal-Universe replays recorded file ops to restore a partial workspace, a completion agent fills missing files and deps, then scales along breadth (cross-repo queries from mined dependencies) and depth (multi-round user-agent refinement). From public terminal traces it yields 37.3k task-sufficient environments. SFT of Qwen3.5-27B: +11.9 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, +13.8 on EvoCode-Bench v2 MT@4.
**Why it matters**: The bottleneck was not more traces — it was turning the traces you already have back into live terminals.
