# arXiv AI Research - 2026-07-29

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. MODUS: Decoder-Only Any-to-Any Modeling of Diverse Modalities
**Authors**: Mingqiao Ye et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25948
**TL;DR**: A single decoder-only architecture that symmetrically generates any modality from any combination of input modalities, without modality-specific encoders or task heads.
**Why it matters**: Collapses the usual encoder-zoo multimodal design into one uniform generative backbone, a meaningful architectural simplification for any-to-any systems.

### 2. Wonder: Video World Model Done Better
**Authors**: Jiacong Xu et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26037
**TL;DR**: A real-time, camera-controllable video world model that supports interactive exploration and long-horizon video synthesis rather than short fixed clips.
**Why it matters**: Real-time interactive control is the key missing piece for using video world models as simulators for planning and embodied agents.

### 3. Falling Behind Drives Unsafe Development in an Idealised AI Race Experiment
**Authors**: Elias Fernández Domingos et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26034
**TL;DR**: A behavioral/game-theoretic experiment showing that competitive pressure and fear of falling behind causally push participants toward unsafe AI development choices, even when they know the risks.
**Why it matters**: Direct empirical evidence for the "race to the bottom" mechanism that underlies most AI-safety-governance arguments, rather than just theoretical modeling.

### 4. HANDBOOK.md: A Benchmark for Long-Context Agentic Instruction Following
**Authors**: Liudas Panavas et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25398
**TL;DR**: 65 agentic tasks paired with 20-124 page policy/handbook documents that agents must correctly follow; frontier models achieve only a 36.2% strict pass rate.
**Why it matters**: Exposes a large, measurable gap between "long context window" and actually operationalizing long-form instructions in agentic settings.

### 5. RSIBench-Data: Benchmarking Data-Centric Research for Recursive Self-Improvement
**Authors**: Fanqing Meng et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25886
**TL;DR**: A controlled benchmark testing whether LLM agents can iteratively revise their own training-data strategies given feedback, as a proxy for recursive self-improvement capability.
**Why it matters**: One of the first concrete, measurable benchmarks for a capability that is central to both capability-scaling and AI-safety timelines discussions.
