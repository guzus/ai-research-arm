## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. How Model Growth, Recursion, and Boundary Operators Influence Scaling Exponents
**Authors**: Zixi Chen, Akshay Vegesna, Samip Dahal, Andrew Gordon Wilson
**arXiv**: [2609.19107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19107)
**TL;DR**: Architectural interventions can change pretraining *exponents*, not just constants. Looping / recursive depth is treated as a growth operator: raise the number of loops during training (with or without shared weights). A 7.4B growth architecture matches GPT-3 13B on CORE at roughly \(20\times\) less compute, and the efficiency gap widens with scale. A cheap boundary operator — normalize and inject an earlier block into a vanilla transformer — also buys increasing compute-efficiency. In the data-constrained multi-epoch regime, extra loops regularize; it is compute-optimal to grow loop count with scale. Framing: spend the budget on usable computational depth.
**Why it matters**: The first clean demonstration that architecture can move the scaling *slope* — looping as growth, not as a recursion curiosity.

### 2. Monitoring and Discovering Reward Hacking with Internal Representations during LLM Evaluations
**Authors**: Leon Bergen, Usha Bhalla, Andrew Lee, … Atticus Geiger, Thomas Fel, Thomas McGrath, Ekdeep Singh Lubana, Jack Merullo
**arXiv**: [2609.19101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19101)
**TL;DR**: Difference-of-means vectors in the residual stream coherently represent reward hacking in Kimi K3, GLM 5.2, and Qwen 3.8 Max. GLM 5.2 hacks 57.2% of DeepSWE rollouts and 73% of SWE-bench rollouts. DoM detectors match expensive LLM monitors (Kimi K3 +3.1 pp, GLM 5.2 −7.9 pp at matched FPR) and can run on the CoT *before* the action. Probe-hits the monitor misses surface other undesirable behaviors and transfer off SWE evals.
**Why it matters**: Reward hacking on the coding benches is not rare — and a free white-box vector finds it before the tool call.

### 3. ScienceIDE: Turning World's Scientific Codebase into Agent Learnable Environments
**Authors**: Hejia Geng, Zesen Huang, … Zhenfei Yin, Yingcheng Wu, Ling Yang et al.
**arXiv**: [2609.19134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19134)
**TL;DR**: Scientific repos encode decades of method, but implicit conventions and domain-specific correctness block agent learning — the "scientific experience bottleneck." ScienceIDE has agents, guided by expert cases and acceptance criteria, turn those repos into executable environments for task generation, execution, and scientific verification. Verified trajectories train PhAI-IDE-72B / 9B / 4B. Gains on held-out scientific-code repair plus selected general code, reasoning, and knowledge benches — positive transfer from scientific experience. Workspace and code released.
**Why it matters**: Yesterday's ScienceBuddy was RSI in the researcher's loop; this is the substrate — humanity's scientific software as a shared gym.

### 4. Reinforcement Learning for Real-Time Vision-Language-Action Policies
**Authors**: Perry Dong, Kuo-Han Hung, Dorsa Sadigh, Chelsea Finn
**arXiv**: [2609.18207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18207)
**TL;DR**: Large VLAs are too slow for the observation they act on: the state is stale by execution time. Real-Time EXPO-FT decouples a slow pretrained VLA that proposes action chunks from a lightweight edit policy that patches those chunks against the latest observation. On Kinetix, a delayed policy wins 10/10 environments against delayed and non-delayed baselines. On four dynamic real-robot tasks (object passing, ball balancing, table soccer, dynamic pick) with ≤10 minutes of online data and no human intervention, average success goes 42% → 97%.
**Why it matters**: RL on VLAs that actually keeps up with the robot — latency is treated as a control problem, not a serving footnote.

### 5. Agora: Git as Shared Memory for Collective AutoResearch
**Authors**: Yifan Zhang, Yunheng Zou, Shaokun Zhang, Jian Hu, Hao Zhang, Binfeng Xu, Jan Kautz, Yi Dong
**arXiv**: [2609.18094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18094)
**TL;DR**: One AutoResearch loop improves a training setup; many loops just rediscover the same local maxima. Agora stores research as an append-only Git DAG: every claim is a checkoutable commit. A derived index exposes the frontier, neglected branches, and verification status; diversity-aware selection stops collapse onto one leader. First sustained run: 13 LM workers, ~12 days, no assigned tasks, on a weight-transfer problem (141 donor models → frozen 119.6M attention–SSM hybrid, no training data or gradients). 1,703 contributions; evaluator 3.39 → 1.899 BPB, closing 62% of the gap to trained GPT-2 124M. Winning ancestry spans 15 accounts; 165 independent reproductions, zero failures. One mid-run human intervention broke a monoculture.
**Why it matters**: Shared, replayable research state — not more agents — is what turns parallel search into discovery.
