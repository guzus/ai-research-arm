## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Intern Lumina U2: A Multi-Codebook Diffusion Large Language Model for Omni-Visual Understanding and Image Generation
**Authors**: Qian Yu, Zongyang Qiu, Juncheng Yan, Yu Qiao, Yihao Liu, Bin Fu et al. (Shanghai AI Laboratory)
**arXiv**: [2610.20818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20818)
**TL;DR**: Friday's ORL / LangDiT trained residual dLLMs for *text*. Intern Lumina U2 is the first diffusion LM that puts *multi-codebook* visual tokens in a fully discrete space: K complementary codes per position, concatenated embeddings in, a lightweight AR head within each position, masked-diffusion backbone across positions. Built on AToken's 4D tokenizer and LLaDA2.0; image, video, and 3D share one packed sequence. 1.4B activated: first on 6/10 image-understanding benches, all three video benches, and 3D MM-Vet versus the same-backbone LLaDA2.0-Uni; no separate continuous generator. Plateau-Halton sampling + drift-triggered cache refresh: 4.45× image-gen. Trained on 1,024 Ascend 910B NPUs.
**Why it matters**: Discrete unified models were capacity-starved by one code per patch; hybrid models split the objective. Multi-codebook discrete is the third option, and it covers image/video/3D understanding plus generation in one backbone.

### 2. Open-world AI: a Tale of Three Requirements
**Authors**: Jianyu Zhang, Léon Bottou
**arXiv**: [2610.20782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20782)
**TL;DR**: Closed-world AI manufactures iid data against a fixed criterion. Open-world AI does not know the task list, so it needs inference-time learning (requirement 1). Modeling tasks as sharing latent factors yields two more: a *rich representation* that keeps more than iid generalization requires, and *predictive disentanglement* that groups matched input/output factors. The analysis also wants a consistent inner learner and long contexts that span related tasks without mixing them. Tested on LMs up to 8B (Memory Mosaics). Qualitative, not leaderboard: models that meet the requirements *improve* as related-task data accumulates; the rest *degrade*. Neither scale nor layer-type interleaving repairs it.
**Why it matters**: Friday's AIT compositionality paper asked how structure is generated. Bottou's three requirements say what a network has to keep if in-context "learning" is actually going to be learning.

### 3. A Self-Improving Agent that Grows its Own DSL: Oracle-Free Operator Induction for Exact Question Answering
**Authors**: Yun-Shiuan Chuang, Yirou Ge, Yixi Li, Yiming Bian, Zijie Pan, Prakhar Mehrotra et al. (PayPal AI)
**arXiv**: [2610.20809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20809)
**TL;DR**: Friday's Self-Improvement Problem said no terminating gate is both reliable and non-conservative. Athanor used GPU post-training with real rewards. This one asks a harder question: can an agent grow what it can *compute* with no reward, no judge, and no answer key, and still be exactly right? A frozen model runs two loops over a sandboxed DSL. Inner: keep a question only when two independently written expressions agree on every record (~3× the answerable set). Outer: admit a new operator only when a second implementation agrees *and* a conservation law holds. The gate rejects 138/138 buggy variants; admit-if-it-runs, self-consistency, and compression admit most of them. Unmodified, the same loop grows business-ledger and clinical-encounter coverage >10× and ~20×. Transfers across Mistral-Small-24B and Llama-3.3-70B.
**Why it matters**: RSI that keeps whatever scores, persuades a judge, or compresses the library will keep a wrong skill. Exact domains need a gate that does not consult the model that wrote the skill.

### 4. MAI-Gym: An Open All-in-One Platform for GUI Agent Evaluation and RL Training across Mobile and Computer Environments
**Authors**: Panrong Tong, Quyu Kong, Pengxiang Li, Tianyu Xia, Hanzhang Zhou, Lei Wang et al. (Alibaba Token Hub / MAI-UI)
**arXiv**: [2610.20939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20939)
**TL;DR**: Friday's OpenGCUA shipped an open computer-use stack; Saturday's OS-Helix trained GUI+code routing to 66.44% OSWorld-Verified. MAI-Gym is the missing gym: one rollout path for eval, data, and online RL across mobile, computer, and web; ten established benches; 20,000 parallel instances. Coding agents synthesize resettable mocked apps and coherent cross-app worlds with execution-validated verifiers. MAI-Gym-9B, trained on it, is SOTA among open GUI agents under 10B: 78.4% MobileWorld and 76.2% OSWorld-Verified. RL in a mocked train-ticketing app transfers to the real app on a physical phone, 28.8%→48.1%. Framework, mocked apps, 2,000 verified tasks, and the 9B checkpoint released. Used to train Qwen-UI-Agent.
**Why it matters**: Computer-use RL was starved of cheap, resettable, verifiable environments. A 9B beating Saturday's 27B number is the gym, not a bigger backbone.

### 5. Learning to Read Out: Unembedding Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining
**Authors**: Matteo He, William F. Shen, Alex Iacob, Andrej Jovanovic, Xinchi Qiu, Nicholas D. Lane
**arXiv**: [2610.21025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21025)
**TL;DR**: Friday's Adversarial Parameter Decomposition asked how *weights* compute. Saturday's CEL asked which heads to ablate. This NeurIPS paper asks a dating question: when a model "acquires" a capability, is that the hidden state or the unembedding? Parameter-trajectory crosscoding fits one sparse dictionary to every token's readout row at every checkpoint — weights only, no activation corpus. In Pythia, the readout reorganizes in a narrow window at 0.4–1.1% of pretraining; WordNet categories become decodable from its rows. By then, linear probes already read task information that the model's own outputs do not yet show. Raising only the readout learning rate makes the readout develop earlier and closes the gap sooner, with little effect on final loss.
**Why it matters**: Early-training developmental dates — induction, grokking, "when did it learn X" — are confounded with when the unembedding learned to *say* it.
