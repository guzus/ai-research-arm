## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Latent Lattice Language Models
**Authors**: Shivam Duggal, Zongze Wu, William T. Freeman, Antonio Torralba, Richard Zhang, Eli Shechtman
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21608
**TL;DR**: Depth, looping and residual reroutes add per-token compute; this paper adds *capacity* — how many computational states stay active. A Lattice of Latents is a per-layer horizontal stream that runs along time beside the vertical residual stream and directly conditions attention, KV writes, the MLP and decoding. On 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens a 406M Lattice Transformer drops validation perplexity 15.41 → 14.40; stacked with hyper-connections it reaches 13.61. The same add-on improves Gated DeltaNet, Mamba-2 and hybrids at every native state size, beating baselines with 4× larger SSM states.
**Why it matters**: It names a third scaling axis — persistent per-layer state — that composes with Transformers, SSMs and residual-stream tricks instead of replacing them.

### 2. ViRA: Toward Ultra-High-Resolution Visual Agents via Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization
**Authors**: Huawei Lin, Jinting Liu, Tongyan Wang, Xuyang Chen, Ruihua Shi, Zhichao Xu, Jiaxin Qin, Guoshenghui Zhao, Baijian Yang, Yingjie Victor Chen, Tan Yu, Weijie Zhao
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21605
**TL;DR**: UHR visual agents often answer from common sense; GRPO cannot tell a guess from a read because both earn the same final-answer reward. CrossRPO trains on contrast sets: one question, several 21–120 MP images that differ only in a small evidence region and have different answers. Theory: the set reward does not reinforce a common-sense solution. ViRA-9B/27B trained on ~3,500 items reach 63.3% set accuracy vs 59.3% for GRPO; the 27B beats Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna at max thinking on most benches, including a photographed real-change split.
**Why it matters**: It is a training objective that forces the agent to look, not a bigger zoom tool.

### 3. Differentiable Exact Learning
**Authors**: Hristo Papazov, Francesco D'Angelo, Nicolas Flammarion
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21486
**TL;DR**: Couple a state-tracking neural controller to an unbounded environment and train it on policy-trajectory observations (actions in, hidden states out). Differentiable Finite-State Transducers use multilinear updates, train in log-parallel, and emulate arbitrary finite-state controllers. From tiny datasets, compact DFSTs — and, replicated, RNNs and GRUs — get extreme length generalization on arithmetic and universal simulation. Softmax DFSTs, argmaxed, provably simulate a universal Turing machine and do binary arithmetic. NeurIPS 2026.
**Why it matters**: It turns “state tracker + external tape + expert traces” into a recipe that both length-generalizes and yields a discrete controller you can prove.

### 4. SciResearch-Eval: Evaluating Scientific Judgment in Autonomous Experimental Agents
**Authors**: Brian T. Weston, Alondra Torres Contreras, Aldair E. Gongora
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21541
**TL;DR**: Agents design thermal experiments, fit a heat-conduction simulator that may be under-resolved or physically wrong, and must return each property with a 95% interval scored against exact ground truth. On 93 held-out tasks / 9 models / 2,511 episodes, models handle procedural traps. When physics is missing, most still report 95% confidence while covering the truth 20–62% of the time; GPT-6 Astra covers 94% and Claude Fable 5.2 covers 99%. Fable is the most accurate at default effort (1.9× Astra’s interval score) but spends 6× the tokens; max-effort Claude Opus 5.5 closes the gap only with 13–31× more reasoning.
**Why it matters**: It is the first lab-judgment bench that can tell a calibrated scientist from a confident fitter of a broken model.

### 5. Benchleaking Is Accelerating
**Authors**: Yiwei Qin, Muhang Xie, Xuefeng Li, Mohan Jiang, Dayuan Fu, Tiantian Mi, Qipeng Guo, Pengfei Liu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21315
**TL;DR**: *Benchleaking* is when a benchmark’s own test items enter training, so the score is recall. Over the past month it moved from post-training traces (an agent reciting hidden test requirements it was never given in 23% of attempts) into public *base* models. Few-shot continuation: ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base writes out 96% of HumanEval problems with reference implementations and 85% of OpenBookQA with answers (DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base: 9% and 0%). An Anti-Hack score that marks leaked items wrong costs ERNIE 16.6 points on average vs 1.5 for DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base.
**Why it matters**: Open bases are the substrate of most downstream work; leaked tests now corrupt more than a leaderboard.
