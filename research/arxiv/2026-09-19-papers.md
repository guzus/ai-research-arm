## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Stress-testing Alignment Midtraining
**Authors**: Sid Baines, Jonathan Bostock, Maria Angelica Martinez, Andrew Draganov, David Africa, Daniel Tan
**arXiv**: [2609.20412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20412)
**TL;DR**: Alignment midtraining (AMT) is sold as the way post-training generalizes — keep pretraining on alignment documents so later SFT inherits the right motives. The authors actually run the experiment: up to 110B parameters and 1B midtraining tokens. AMT can steer a model's motivation when post-training is ambiguous. A *tiny* slice of finetune data that suggests a competing motive erases the effect. Rules that never appear in either midtraining or post-training are not robustly learned.
**Why it matters**: Public evidence is not yet enough to treat midtraining as a solution to the core alignment problem — and a sliver of competing SFT is enough to undo it.

### 2. Local Sparsity Enables Unsupervised LLM Safety Detection
**Authors**: Xin Chen, Gil Kur, Alexander Shevchenko, Andreas Krause
**arXiv**: [2609.20129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20129)
**TL;DR**: Deployment-time safety is almost always supervised, so new attacks are out of distribution by construction. Under the linear representation hypothesis, SAE concept space is *locally* sparse: nearby points share a small active support. The authors build a locally masked SAE anomaly detector that models only safe data. With 1% OOD data for calibration it is near-optimal, and it scores on 1–2% of SAE neurons.
**Why it matters**: Safety monitoring that does not need a labeled attack catalog — and does not have to read the whole SAE.

### 3. To Copy or Not to Copy: Controlling Speculative Decoding via Intrinsic Model Signals
**Authors**: Roy Eisenstadt, Ido Cohen, Edo Cohen-Karlik, Lior Wolf, Itamar Zimerman
**arXiv**: [2609.20186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20186)
**TL;DR**: Speculative decoding is stuck between neural drafts (EAGLE-class: robust) and context copy (fast when the model is actually repeating). Copy triggers fire on surface n-gram overlap that is not a structural intent to copy, and those false positives kill throughput. SwitchSD trains lightweight probes on the *target* model's internals; copy-intent AUC > 0.99. It then switches EAGLE-style drafting and context copy. Up to 15% more throughput than EAGLE3 on Llama and Qwen.
**Why it matters**: Copying is a latent control signal, not an n-gram heuristic.

### 4. The First-Order Oracle Complexity of Lipschitz Convex Optimization in Nondual Settings
**Authors**: David Martínez-Rubio, Brian Bullins, Cristóbal Guzmán, Mathieu Molina
**arXiv**: [2609.20687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20687)
**TL;DR**: COLT 2015 asked whether a *smaller* feasible set (\(p < q\)) can improve first-order rates for Lipschitz convex optimization. Yes, on the nonsmooth end, matching prior lower bounds up to logs. Euclidean-Lipschitz objectives over an \(\ell_1\)-ball get \(\widetilde{O}(1/T)\), not the classical \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\). The device is an online game whose comparator is scored by the max of affine losses seen so far, bounded via sequential fat-shattering dimension. The companion [2609.20701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20701) gives polynomial-time algorithms that realize the nearly optimal rates.
**Why it matters**: Geometry of the constraint set is not a constant — it moves the rate.

### 5. Cross-Modal Attention Acts as a Frequency Filter
**Authors**: Farooq Ahmad Wani, Maria Sofia Bucarelli, Mujtaba Hussain Mirza, Oleksandr Pryymak, Aryo Pradipta Gema, Iacopo Masi, Pasquale Minervini, Fabrizio Silvestri
**arXiv**: [2609.20139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20139)
**TL;DR**: VLMs fail under image corruption, but the *question wording* moves robustness both ways. Verbose prompts ("Please look carefully…") make models more robust; fine-grained spatial questions make them more fragile. Cross-modal attention is a spectral filter over image patches: verbosity broadens frequency support, detail concentrates it. Drift is worst when the filter and the corruption share frequencies. Verbose paraphrasing cuts drift variance 70–81% on 8B Qwen3-VL and LLaVA-OneVision.
**Why it matters**: "Pad the prompt" is a frequency intervention, not a politeness trick.
