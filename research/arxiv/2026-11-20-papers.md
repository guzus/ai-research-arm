## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Beam Model Technical Report
**Authors**: Reflection Team
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26358
**TL;DR**: Beam is a 501.3B-parameter sparse MoE (23.4B active) built for coding, reasoning and agents, pretrained on 23.8T tokens with a 1M-token context from a two-stage midtraining pass on verifiable data. Post-training is a fully asynchronous RL campaign on 10.5K GB300 GPUs: 100M rollouts over four weeks, with no plateau as RL compute grew. A 1.93B speculative drafter (DFlash 2) plus FP8/NVFP4 co-design keeps serving cheap; the report claims frontier-level reasoning at 3–4× less inference compute than peers, and 88.9% refuse/pivot on harmful requests vs 81.5% compliance on harmless ones.
**Why it matters**: An open-weight coding/agent MoE whose RL run is large enough that “more RL still helps” is the day’s actual model release, not another 7B ablation.

### 2. RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering
**Authors**: Tuna Han Salih Meral, Kaan Oktay, Alec Helbling, Atakan Kara, Duen Horng Chau, Pinar Yanardag
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627
**TL;DR**: Prompt-dependent skills (text rendering, multi-object composition) look like they need input-dependent transforms, but a *fixed* set of steering vectors on frozen DiT block outputs recovers 90.0–96.3% of the reward gains of six public RL-tuned image models and 89.8% / 111.9% on two video models. On SD3.5-M the vectors are 0.29M parameters vs 18.8M LoRA (65× smaller). Initialized at zero and trained with online RL, the vectors alone beat weight-based RL on OCR and PickScore.
**Why it matters**: Most of diffusion RL is a constant shift of a generator that already knew how — not a new input-dependent policy.

### 3. TideCache: Efficient Hybrid LLM Serving with Transparent Cache Management and Layer-Wise Streaming
**Authors**: Jinbin Luo, Xiaozhe Yao, Shang Yang, Zhijian Liu, Song Han, Qinghao Hu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26619
**TL;DR**: Hybrid open-weight models mix global-attention KV with bounded-state caches whose GPU footprint fights concurrency. TideCache’s transparent page pool, layer-wise prefix streamer (prefetch during bounded-state compute) and request scheduler are implemented in SGLang on GB300 and GH200. Across seven model/platform configs it completes up to 2.04× as many agent turns, cuts overall P50 per-token latency 20–59%, and improves agent P50 TTFT 1.40–3.05× vs SGLang+HiCache.
**Why it matters**: Hybrid architectures only pay off in production if the cache, not the math, is the serving object — Song Han’s group treating HBM as a streamable pool.

### 4. ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step
**Authors**: Kaiwen Zhang, Yiming Gao, Zeqi Xiao, Xintao Wang, Pengfei Wan, Kun Gai, Xingang Pan
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26389
**TL;DR**: Video generators still break physics and logic on multi-step plans because training treats a solution as a fixed-length clip. ViDecide trains the denoiser as a causal decision process that predicts state transitions and when to stop. Corrective Fine-tuning maps failed-decision noise onto the model’s own successful continuations — no policy-gradient RL. Strict success averages 83.8% vs 23.3% for standard fine-tuning and 67.8% for the strongest prior, and it generalizes to larger puzzles and unseen layouts.
**Why it matters**: A video model becomes a planner when the unit of training is a decision, not a clip — and CFT is a stable substitute for diffusion RL.

### 5. Awaken-HI: Scaling Humanoid Intelligence with Whole-Body Human Experience
**Authors**: EngineAI
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26420
**TL;DR**: Awaken-Human pairs ~50,000 hours of egocentric stereo with synchronized whole-body motion across 100+ skills. Awaken-HI jointly predicts the original human trajectory and the retargeted robot action through a shared action expert; human-trajectory supervision transfers loco-manipulation better than robot-only data, and more human pretraining reduces held-out robot action error on an empirical scaling law. After T800 adaptation, mean success is 93.75% on eight tasks, beating GR00T N1.7 and π₀.₅ on both single-skill and long-horizon evals, including objects unseen in robot post-training.
**Why it matters**: Humanoid VLAs still starve on whole-body data; 50k hours plus a shared human/robot action expert is the first scaling-law-shaped answer that beats the public robot baselines.
