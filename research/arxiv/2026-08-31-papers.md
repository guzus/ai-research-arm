# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-31

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. A Formal Limitation on Learning Human Language From Textual Corpora
**Authors**: Emily Cheng, Ryan Cotterell
**arXiv**: [2608.28560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28560)
**TL;DR**: Information-theoretic upper bounds on recovering a speaker's intended meaning from *any* featurizer of an utterance — including LLM hidden states. The leftover uncertainty splits into an irreducible part and a part that only extralinguistic context can resolve; both are intrinsic to language, so no amount of text or supervision can beat them, discrete or continuous meaning space. Artificial languages, Mandarin zero-pronoun resolution, and color reference support the theory.
**Why it matters**: A hard ceiling on what "just scale the corpus" can teach a model about meaning — form is not a sufficient statistic for speaker intent.

### 2. Sliding-window beats linear attention
**Authors**: Alexia Jolicoeur-Martineau, Rhea Sanjay Sukthanker, Pashmina Cameron, Emy Gervais
**arXiv**: [2608.28444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28444)
**TL;DR**: The fashionable retrofit of pretrained LLMs to linear attention is compared against a baseline that most papers skip: sliding-window attention with sinks, no extra post-training. SWA matches or beats post-trained linear models across tasks, and is 2–10× stronger on Needle-in-a-Haystack and BABILong, while staying faster and cheaper. Linear attention likely needs from-scratch training or much heavier adaptation just to tie SWA.
**Why it matters**: A cheap, training-free default that undercuts a large body of linear-attention retrofit work — switch the KV policy before you retrain the mixer.

### 3. How Far Can 5,500 Hours of Driving Take You? A Scaling Law Analysis of Video Diffusion Models
**Authors**: Victor Besnier, Anh-Quan Cao, Elias Ramzi, Spyros Gidaris, Tuan-Hung Vu, Andrei Bursuc, Eloi Zablocki, Matthieu Cord
**arXiv**: [2608.28404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28404)
**TL;DR**: Driving video cannot be scraped at web scale, so the authors map a 1M–9B from-scratch diffusion family on up to 5,500 hours. Validation loss follows power laws in model size and training exposure; loss falls much faster with more steps than with more parameters, but larger models still win asymptotically, so compute-optimal scaling still grows size when the budget allows. A 9B model is claimed as the largest driving video diffuser trained from scratch and sets an open SOTA on nuScenes.
**Why it matters**: The first systematic compute-allocation map for a privacy-bound, data-scarce video domain — longer training beats a bigger model until the budget is large.

### 4. LongPIBench: A Long-Context Benchmark for Prompt Injection
**Authors**: Yupei Liu, Yuqi Jia, Neil Zhenqiang Gong, Jinyuan Jia
**arXiv**: [2608.28411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28411)
**TL;DR**: Existing prompt-injection benches are short-context, which inflates reported defense success. LongPIBench covers peer review, resume screening, code review, and email summary at thousands-to-tens-of-thousands of tokens, with synthetic *and* real-world sets. Even simple heuristic injections get high ASR and routinely bypass current SOTA defenses (EMNLP 2026 Findings).
**Why it matters**: Shows that the field has been grading defenses on the easy exam — long-context applications are the actual threat surface.

### 5. ContextPilot: Teaching Agents for Proactive Context Management via Fine-grained RL
**Authors**: Zhuoshi Pan, Qizhi Pei, Junru Lu, Honglin Lin, H. Vicky Zhao, Di Yin, Xing Sun
**arXiv**: [2608.28476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28476)
**TL;DR**: Long-horizon agents drown in an ever-growing working context. ContextPilot expands the edit toolset (planning, long-term memory, soft offloading), then trains context actions with fine-grained RL that branches on high-impact edits and assigns action-level advantages instead of a single trajectory reward. On long-context QA and deep search it beats prior proactive-memory methods with a *smaller* working context (EMNLP 2026 Main).
**Why it matters**: Treats context management as a first-class learned policy, not a bag of search/delete/summarize tools with a terminal reward.
