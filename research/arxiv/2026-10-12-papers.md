## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. RSI-Anything: Unlocking Recursive Self-Improvement with World Models
**Authors**: Taoyong Cui, Pinxian Zeng, Wanghan Xu, Wanli Ouyang, Zhenfei Yin, Ling Yang et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.13912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13912)
**TL;DR**: Friday's leftover asked whether RSI lives in a rulebook (Memento 3), a multi-agent loop (MASS), more RL compute (MiMo-V2.6), or a discriminator that writes the next bench (GAL). RSI-Anything says the missing piece is a *predictive world model* that picks the next action *and* the next training point. Observed feedback updates the policy and the model; compatibility checks decide which pair to keep. Code repair 26.3→31.2 with a fixed coder; GUI actions −39.4%; lab-planning 21.80→46.01 across five policy versions; reaction optimization 96.15% vs 82.16% Bayesian optimization on the same 40-experiment budget.
**Why it matters**: RSI that cannot forecast which update is worth keeping is just expensive trial-and-error. The world model is the filter.

### 2. AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities
**Authors**: Andis Draguns, Sumeet Ramesh Motwani, Raymond Douglas, Christian Schroeder de Witt
**arXiv**: [2610.13928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928)
**TL;DR**: Friday's Gleave probes assumed hidden capabilities can be elicited. This NeurIPS paper constructs the counterexample: an encrypted neural net is packed into the host weights and decrypts only on a secret trigger. Digital lockers make the hidden circuit cryptographically hard to find *or* interpret, even with full weight access — extending prior string-hiding to arbitrary computation. Fine-tuning, weight noising, gradient trigger search, and latent adversarial training all fail on a chemical-reaction proxy. Models released.
**Why it matters**: Elicitation-based capability audits have a cryptographic ceiling. A model that can hide a circuit can hide a CBRN skill.

### 3. Orthros: Phase-Aware Heterogeneous Attention for Efficient Transformers
**Authors**: Yuhong Chou, Zehao Liu, Yuqi Pan, Qian Liu, Jibin Wu et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.13735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13735)
**TL;DR**: Linear and sliding-window attention cut prefills, then people interleave full layers and the quadratic cost comes back. Orthros switches *by phase*, not by layer: linear-complexity prefill, full attention at decode. A data-efficient Transformer→Orthros conversion can beat the original. 21.9× prefill speedup, near-perfect 128K needle-in-a-haystack, Transformer-level in-context recall. NeurIPS 2026.
**Why it matters**: The quadratic tax is a prefill tax. Pay it only when the KV cache is being read, not when it is being written.

### 4. ReplaySSM: Cache SSM Inputs, Not State
**Authors**: Ze-Wei Liou, Harris Nover, Amey Naik, Tri Dao et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.13778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13778)
**TL;DR**: Hybrid Mamba/DeltaNet serving is I/O-bound on the recurrent state: every step reads and writes it, speculative rollback is expensive, and low-precision state poisons later updates. ReplaySSM stores a checkpoint plus a buffer of recent inputs and updates the checkpoint only periodically. Most steps skip the state write (~½ the traffic); rollback is a buffer pointer; output-only verification never materializes intermediate states. In vLLM at batch 256: 1.20–1.47× standard decode; ~1.8× speculative throughput on two 120B-class models.
**Why it matters**: SSMs were sold as constant-state. The serving bottleneck is that you still touch that state every token. Stop doing that.

### 5. Humanize Olympiad Agents: A Multi-Session Workflow for Olympiad Problem Solving with Solve, Review, Wiki, Verify
**Authors**: Zhengyang Zhang, Jing Xiong, Hongzhou Lin, Song Han, Ligeng Zhu et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.13632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13632)
**TL;DR**: Single-session olympiad agents die on early errors, missing knowledge, and silent weakenings of the theorem. HOA runs three feedback channels across sessions: an independent reviewer, a structured wiki, and a Lean verifier against trusted challenge statements. With GPT-5.6 Sol: perfect IMO / IPhO / IOI 2026, near-full IChO, 672/672 PutnamBench, 251/304 Lean-Eval (1st). With open Kimi-K3: full IMO / IPhO / IOI and 97.1% IChO — matching or beating a closed GPT-5.6 Sol + /Goal baseline on two of four contests.
**Why it matters**: The base model is not the olympiad agent. The multi-session review/wiki/verify loop is, and it transfers to open weights.
