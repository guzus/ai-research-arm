## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets
**Authors**: Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang, Sham M. Kakade, Sadhika Malladi, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Dylan J. Foster
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467
**TL;DR**: REINFORCE — the engine under PPO and GRPO — can drive the probability of optimal responses toward zero even as reward rises, so convergence can take exponentially many steps despite good initial coverage. The failure survives exact gradients and KL regularization to the base model. Repeatedly resetting the KL reference to the latest policy (Approximate Policy Mirror Descent) restores coverage-preserving updates; the paper then proves the first global convergence guarantees for stylized PPO/GRPO and for the squared-loss recipes used to train the Kimi models.
**Why it matters**: It turns a widely observed RLVR stall into a named failure mode with a concrete fix, and it tells post-training which regularizers and resets actually preserve the base model's coverage.

### 2. Emergent Biological Capabilities in a Foundation Model for Molecular Interactions
**Authors**: Adi Mashiach
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14504
**TL;DR**: Bonbon is trained on ~3.2 trillion tokens of paired protein–ligand sequences, with no task labels and no structures. From one representation it zero-shots mechanism of action, binding-site localization (orthosteric vs allosteric), and covalent vs non-covalent engagement, including on out-of-distribution complexes. Across six scales (42M–1.6B) every capability jumps at the largest model while pretraining loss falls smoothly. Frozen embeddings beat co-folding on binding/affinity at 10⁵× speed; wet-lab validation on nine disease targets hits 61.5% dose-response on de novo compounds.
**Why it matters**: It is a biology-side existence proof that loss-based scaling laws miss the jump at which zero-shot capabilities appear, with prospective lab numbers rather than only in-silico scores.

### 3. UGG: Unified Generative Geometry Model
**Authors**: Amani Kiruga, Zhiyi Li, Ruojin Cai, Hansen Lillemark, Xiaoshan Wu, Ayush Tewari, Yilun Du
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14440
**TL;DR**: Feedforward geometry predictors and generative video models have been separate stacks. UGG puts RGB, depth and camera pose in one generative model by assigning different noise levels across modalities and time, so generation and geometric prediction are both conditional completion. Heterogeneous datasets with partial annotations become legal training data. At inference the model searches over scene hypotheses, using geometric evidence from observed RGB to improve depth and pose without ground-truth geometry.
**Why it matters**: It collapses 4D understanding into one noise-level interface, so camera-controlled video, depth and pose no longer need separate architectures.

### 4. Contrastive Discovery: Open-Ended Scientific Discovery over Competing Explanations
**Authors**: Ziang Liu, James J. Kim, Yijia Dai, Jennifer J. Sun
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14514
**TL;DR**: Discovery agents usually test a hypothesis *h* only against ¬*h*. When *h* dies they know what is false, not which alternative the evidence supports. Contrastive Discovery replaces that with a mutually exclusive, exhaustive contrast set and scores each experiment by how it redistributes belief. Across six domains, 89% of the largest pointwise belief shifts are rejections; on the same evidence contrast sets resolve 73% of them to a specific alternative. Guiding search this way resolves 67% more questions under a fixed experiment budget. Blinded experts agreed on 78% of resolved questions.
**Why it matters**: It is a drop-in change of scoring unit that turns “rejected” into “this competing explanation,” which is what an AI co-scientist actually needs.

### 5. ARE-bench: Benchmarking Agents' Research Ability in Real-World Researcher Sessions and Workspaces
**Authors**: Jiahang Lin, Wanying Ren, Wanghan Xu, Huayu Sha, Yang Shu, Qi Zhang, Tao Gui, Xuanjing Huang et al. (27 authors)
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14364
**TL;DR**: Research-agent benches mostly grade standalone tasks in clean workspaces. ARE-bench grades one step inside an ongoing project: 75 tasks from recruited researchers' own sessions, with unfinished files, outdated versions and failed attempts left in place, spanning seven stages, 33 requirements and 1,173 binary checklist items. The strongest of seven frontier models passes every item on only 66.2% of tasks. Checking only delivery contracts scores ~69%; adding historical and workspace requirements drops that to 28.4–40.0%. Design and Execution are the hardest stages for every model.
**Why it matters**: It shows that current research-agent numbers are inflated by ignoring the session and the mess the agent is dropped into.
