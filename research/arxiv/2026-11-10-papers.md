## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. SWAM: Scaling World Action Models with Cross-Modal Alignment
**Authors**: Yu Zhou, Xinyuan Xia, Xuankun Yang, Cewu Lu, Yanwei Li et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12870
**TL;DR**: World action models jointly predict video and actions, but action learning can wreck the language–video alignment that video pretraining just bought. The authors curate ~47,000 hours of general, egocentric, UMI and robot video and ablate which supervision mix keeps that alignment. Under a fixed budget, mixing egocentric video with UMI demos improves control; architecture then decides how well those hours are used. Post-trained only on RoboTwin Clean, SWAM hits 88.10% Easy / 61.56% Hard, and 40.00% SR on three real-world tasks versus 23.33% for the strongest baseline. Data, code and weights are released.
**Why it matters**: It treats language–video–action alignment as a data-and-supervision problem, not just an architecture one, and publishes the recipe.

### 2. World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation
**Authors**: Heng Fang, Zehui Guo, Yuehan Zhang, Mingyang Du, Dingkang Liang, Xiang Bai
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12885
**TL;DR**: Trained action policies store skill in weights; ICL has lagged because a demo from another scene carries no feedback from the world the robot is in. WICL keeps the model frozen: it starts from one demonstration, rehearses in a simulator, and writes the feedback into context. On all 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks it reaches 83.4% mean success across clean and randomized settings with one demo and no gradient updates — more than 20 points above the strongest trained policy — and transfers to a physical dual-arm robot via cross-embodiment memory adaptation.
**Why it matters**: It shows a robot can learn a manipulation task in context as well as in weights, if a world is in the loop.

### 3. Sage-Taiyi: A Metacognitive Architecture for Scientific Discovery
**Authors**: Weida Wang, Benteng Chen, Dahua Lin, Xing Xie, Bowen Zhou, Lei Bai, Wenlong Zhang et al. (90 authors)
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12762
**TL;DR**: A metacognitive loop monitors evidence, assumptions and open questions, then chooses what to investigate next and which reusable reasoning method to apply. On Taiyi-Bench (265 tasks from 50 papers across ten disciplines) it ranks first on all eight metrics, Overall 54.0 vs 47.9 for AI Scientist-v2. Five expert-guided collaborations push proposals into the lab: cobalt-catalyzed carbonylation yield goes from trace to 84%, photocatalytic allylic amination from 13% to 69%.
**Why it matters**: It is an AI co-scientist with wet-lab yield numbers, not only rediscovery scores.

### 4. Is Persona Generalization Predictable?
**Authors**: Olina Mukherjee, Arnav Goel, Aditi Raghunathan, Jacob Mitchell Springer
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12864
**TL;DR**: Fine-tuning in one domain can induce personas in others — the signature of emergent misalignment. In-domain and out-of-domain persona rates fall on an approximately probit-linear “on-the-line” relationship that is shared across Qwen and Llama families and sizes. Learning rate, weight decay and duration mostly slide models along the same line; which layers are tuned changes the slope. For unsafe behavior, later-layer-only fine-tuning keeps in-domain learning while flattening out-of-domain misalignment.
**Why it matters**: Emergent misalignment stops looking like a freak accident and becomes a controllable transfer curve.

### 5. Double Blind Evals: Resolving the Dual Confidentiality Dilemma in AI Safety Auditing
**Authors**: Andrew Trask, Sol Messing, Miles Brundage, Sean McGregor, William Isaac, Kristian Lum et al. (30 authors)
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12604
**TL;DR**: Evaluators want secret prompts; model owners want secret weights. The paper runs both inside an NVIDIA H100 secure enclave (RAM encrypted in hardware) via OpenMined’s PySyft, so neither party sees the other’s artifact. A world-first pilot evaluates a proprietary Google DeepMind model (Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite) against a private slice of the AILuminate corpus on Google Cloud.
**Why it matters**: Closed-model safety audits no longer have to leak either the benchmark or the weights.
