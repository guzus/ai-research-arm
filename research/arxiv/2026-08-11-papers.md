# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-11

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs
**Authors**: Alexander Panfilov, David Schmotz, Ilia Shumailov, Luca Beurer-Kellner, Joachim Schaeffer, Ameya Prabhu, Jonas Geiping, Maksym Andriushchenko
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09867
**TL;DR**: Reveals an architectural vulnerability in how proprietary LLMs return hidden chain-of-thought as interchangeable encrypted blocks: injecting an encrypted reasoning trace from a strong model into a weaker, less-safeguarded sibling model from the same provider forces it to decode and emit the trace verbatim in plaintext. Demonstrates four attack vectors (anti-distillation circumvention, private-data extraction, etc.) across Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google.
**Why it matters**: A provider-agnostic decryption jailbreak that breaks CoT secrecy without ever jailbreaking the target model itself, exposing encrypted reasoning as a security theater.

### 2. Matryoshka Language Model Suites
**Authors**: Nathan Godey, Yoav Artzi
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09703
**TL;DR**: Trains a suite of LMs (500M/1.5B/3B) as nested sub-models inside a single end-to-end-trained architecture instead of training and serving them separately. The suite matches independent baselines in performance while using 36% less training compute, gets free per-step distillation from the largest to smaller sub-models, and speeds up speculative decoding by 14-26% since the draft model lives inside the verifier.
**Why it matters**: Collapses the cost of the standard multi-size model ladder and makes speculative decoding nearly free — a direct efficiency win for inference stacks.

### 3. RynnValue: Scaling Robotic Value Foundation Models with Temporal Distance
**Authors**: Dongchi Huang, Hongyin Zhang, Bohan Hou, Siteng Huang, Zhian Su, Hang Guo, Tong Lu, Zhaofeng Xu, Jiahao Tang, Jianfei Yang, Donglin Wang, Peixi Peng, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09853
**TL;DR**: An open-source robotic manipulation value foundation model that replaces preference/progress supervision with temporal distance (directed cost-to-go to a language goal), scalable to 7,000+ hours and ~3M instruction-conditioned clips without any preference labels. Uses random temporal sampling, order shuffling, and value-isolation attention to suppress shortcuts; beats fully preference-supervised SOTA on RBM-EVAL-OOD.
**Why it matters**: Shows reward-model scaling can drop the expensive preference-annotation bottleneck entirely, a key unlock for scaling robot learning.

### 4. Towards Expert-level Medical AI for Real-time Video Consultations
**Authors**: Mahvish Nagda, Jihyeon Lee, Matthew Thompson, Chunjong Park, Tim Strother, Valentin Liévin, Roma Ruparel, Akshay Goel, Teya Bergamaschi, Suhana Bedi, Meet Shah, Pavel Dubov, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09861
**TL;DR**: First demonstration of expert-level medical AI in real-time clinical video consultations: AMIE (Video), a Gemini-based multi-agent system combining low-latency dialogue, clinical reasoning, and real-time audio-visual perception. In a randomized OSCE study with 30 primary-care physicians, 15 patient actors, and 100 clinical scenarios, clinical evaluators rated AMIE (Video) at clinician level and above its text-only counterpart.
**Why it matters**: Closes the gap between text-only medical AI and the audio-visual reality of clinical practice — the first time a system reaches expert-level performance on real-time video consults.

### 5. SWE-Bench ProMax: Benchmarking Agents on Large-Scale Multilingual Code Refactoring
**Authors**: Yuling Shi, Jinghan Xu, Kelin Fu, Wenhao Zeng, Shilin He, Lei Zhang, Yue Liu, Zelin Zhao, Terry Yue Zhuo, Jialun Cao, Siyu Ye, Tianyu Liu, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09802
**TL;DR**: Introduces an expert-curated, multilingual code-refactoring benchmark of 170 instances across Python, Java, TypeScript, Go, C, C++, and Rust, addressing the audit findings that ~60% of unsolved SWE-bench Verified instances have flawed tests and gold patches can be verbatim memorized. Specifications are rewritten from scratch and test suites manually de-flawed.
**Why it matters**: Refactoring is a harder, behavior-preserving, long-horizon task than bug fixing and this benchmark resists both test contamination and training-data leakage that currently saturate SWE-bench.
