## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Memory Decoder at Scale: A Pretrained, Parametric Long-Term Memory
**Authors**: Rubin Wei, Jiaqi Cao, Jiarui Wang, Junming Zhang, Qipeng Guo, Bowen Zhou, Zhouhan Lin
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27919
**TL;DR**: Treats long-term memory as a *separately pretrained parametric module* rather than a retrieval index or a longer context window. Pairing a 6.9B memory decoder with a 410M Pythia base lifts the score from 29.86 to 37.34 — beating Pythia-12B outright while using 39% fewer total parameters.
**Why it matters**: If memory capacity can be bought as a detachable pretrained component, the scaling budget for the *reasoning* model and the budget for *knowledge* stop being the same budget.

### 2. SpatialCLI: Learning to Reason With Spatial Tools, Then Without Them
**Authors**: Yang Zhou, Zixuan Huang, Sunzhu Li, Zhuo Yang, et al.
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27703
**TL;DR**: Trains a VLM to call explicit spatial tools, then distills the competence back into the weights. Qwen3-VL-8B goes from 29.3% to 84.6% with tools available — past GPT-5.6 Sol's 72.1% — and crucially retains **73.8% with the tools removed**.
**Why it matters**: The tool-free retention number is the result: it argues tool scaffolding is a *training curriculum* for spatial reasoning, not just a permanent inference-time crutch.

### 3. An Empirical Study of Coordination Mode as the First-Class Citizen in From-Scratch Multi-Agent Coding
**Authors**: Yanyu Ren, Yunfeng Bai, Xizheng Wang, Li Chen, Dan Li
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27877
**TL;DR**: Holds the model fixed and varies only the organizational topology of a multi-agent coding system. Topology alone shifts scores by **more than 30 points** and doubles wall-clock time on identical tasks — a swing the authors find rivals the effect of changing model capability.
**Why it matters**: Multi-agent results reported without pinning the coordination topology are under-specified, which makes a large slice of the existing comparison literature hard to read.

### 4. Albilich: Steerable Proof-State Orchestration for LLM-Based Mathematical Research
**Authors**: Ting Gong, Michael Ruofan Zeng, Yong Yang
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27705
**TL;DR**: Orchestrates proof state across an LLM and a computer algebra system. Solves 10/10 RealMath problems with CAS access, and — beyond benchmark scoring — produces a **counterexample to Problem 21.142** and a proof strengthening Problem 20.2 of the Kourovka Notebook.
**Why it matters**: Output that resolves open entries in a standing problem collection is externally checkable in a way benchmark accuracy is not.

### 5. Towards Joint Scaling Laws with Optimal Batch Size Schedules
**Authors**: Jiaxiang Li, Zhiqi Bu, Shiyun Xu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27731
**TL;DR**: Extends scaling-law fitting to jointly cover the batch size *schedule* rather than assuming a static batch size. Dynamic-batch joint laws consistently outperform static-batch baselines, making schedule adaptation a first-class term in the compute-optimal recipe.
**Why it matters**: Standard scaling laws hold batch size fixed by convention; if the schedule is a live variable, published compute-optimal frontiers are optimizing over the wrong space.
