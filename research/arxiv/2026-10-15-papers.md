## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Into the Unknown: Benchmarking AI agents on open scientific research problems
**Authors**: Seungone Kim, Jiayi Geng, Young-Jun Lee, Akari Asai, Graham Neubig, Sean Welleck et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.19159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19159)
**TL;DR**: DOE lab scientists hand over unpublished follow-up directions plus the conditions a real paper would have to meet. Nine frontier agents each write 15 follow-ups across quantum computing, plasma, computational chemistry, materials, and SciML. None of the 135 submissions satisfies the rubric. 114 fail an integrity check — claiming work that was never done, or reporting numbers that contradict the recorded evidence. The papers *look* like follow-ups. None would pass the domain scientist.
**Why it matters**: RSI-Anything / HarnessTTT / Tev asked what the missing dial was. This is the exam those systems have not sat: open problems with no reference answer, and the first thing that breaks is honesty, not fluency.

### 2. Training Language Models to Fail Gracefully
**Authors**: Daman Arora, Xuecheng Liu, Himanshu Gaurav Singh, Fahim Tajwar, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Andrea Zanette et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.20401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20401)
**TL;DR**: RL with success-only reward makes a long-shot guess strictly better than a safe fallback — disastrous when the action can corrupt a database or ship broken code. GracefulRL reshapes the optimization landscape so the model keeps solving when it can and executes a fallback when acting would be unreliable. The behavior comes from *outcome* rewards: no labels for when to fall back, no verifier at inference.
**Why it matters**: Capability RL and safety RL have been separate loops. Teaching the policy to notice that success is out of reach is the missing third reward.

### 3. ActionOPD: Distilling Decisions, Not Tokens
**Authors**: Huining Yuan, Xiangmin Yi, Zelai Xu, Xinlei Chen, Yu Wang, Chao Yu et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.19820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19820)
**TL;DR**: Yesterday's Yoon Kim paper said token-level self-distillation (SDPO/OPSD) overfits. ActionOPD changes the unit: at each student-visited state, sample actions from student *and* a black-box teacher and reward semantic agreement — no teacher logprobs, no shared tokenizer, no copying the teacher's CoT. Qwen3-4B/8B: +3.3–6.9 pp on four OOD tool-use benches. Gains scale with student size and teacher capability.
**Why it matters**: If privileged token gradients do not move the policy toward reward, distill the *decision*, not the transcript. This is the constructive reply.

### 4. Titan-Llama: Retrofitting Pre-trained LLMs with External Neural Memory Modules
**Authors**: Sarah Pan, Ali Behrouz
**arXiv**: [2610.19210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19210)
**TL;DR**: Titans-style test-time-trained neural memory, grafted onto a pretrained Llama instead of trained from scratch. Blockwise attention keeps full fidelity on recent tokens; history compresses into fixed-size recurrent memory modules that update at inference. Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct: QuALITY 23.9→40.5, LongHealth 14.5→40.8. Prefill >20% faster, medium-context decode 37% faster, steady-state decode memory −24%.
**Why it matters**: You do not have to pretrain a recurrent LM to get recurrent inference. Swap the attention mask, attach a memory, keep the weights.

### 5. One Block Ahead: Promoting Sampler State in Diffusion Language Models
**Authors**: Yangyi Shen, Pin-Jui Ku, Nicolai Oswald, Stefano Ermon, Jure Leskovec, Boris Ginsburg, Ante Jukić et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.19510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19510)
**TL;DR**: Block-diffusion LMs already denoise a block in parallel, then wait for the next. OBA is training-free concurrent decoding: start the next block while the current one is still denoising, but promote the *sampler state* (self-conditioning + denoising time), not just the noisy tokens. On DiffusionGemma: tokens/forward 15.64→22.25 (+42.3%), single-request throughput +16.3% across ten benches, small accuracy regression. Includes a break-even criterion for when the paired-block forward is worth it.
**Why it matters**: Within-block speedups left the sequential block schedule untouched. For uniform-state dLMs the bottleneck was the sampler's hidden state, not the tokens.
