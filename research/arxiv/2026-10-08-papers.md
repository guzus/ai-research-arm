## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. RoboJEPA: Scaling Robotic Latent World Models
**Authors**: Artem Zholus, Nicolas Beltran-Velez, Jianhao Yuan, Mahmoud Assran, Jeannette Bohg, Nicolas Ballas et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.10515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10515)
**TL;DR**: Yesterday's JEPA papers asked whether the latent still contains the modes you need to control. This one asks how that latent *scales*. RoboJEPA trains a JEPA world model on real robot data across 12 embodiments. Imagination error — the error of its latent rollouts — follows a second-order power law in compute, good enough to predict quality past the fit range. Planning success tracks that error, so the proxy can stand in for real-robot eval. At 8B it is the largest JEPA predictor to date, and it deploys zero-shot as an agent that plans toward a single goal image on hardware.
**Why it matters**: The first scaling law for multi-embodiment robotic world models trained on real robots, not simulated ones.

### 2. Long-WAM: Scaling the Context of World-Action Models
**Authors**: Wei Huang, Bohan Zhang, Linxi Fan, Song Han, Yukang Chen et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.10528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10528)
**TL;DR**: Access to history is not the same as using it. Lengthening context from 0 to 19.2 seconds lifts RoboCasa GR-1 from 63.3% to 78.7% *only* when the video foundation was pretrained autoregressively; a bidirectional init shows no net gain. Streaming encode + async execution keeps a future-video latent prediction at 107.4 ms on an RTX 5090, and it runs on Jetson AGX Thor. On Unitree G1, dynamic cup stacking hits 95%; π0.5 and Fast-WAM succeed in none of 20 trials.
**Why it matters**: World-action models do not get long-horizon control for free. The pretraining objective has to make history causal, and the system has to keep the loop real-time.

### 3. Decoupling Exploration from Optimization in RLVR
**Authors**: Saif Punjwani, Micah Goldblum
**arXiv**: [2610.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10536)
**TL;DR**: RLVR's promise is new strategies. Novelty bonuses on the same policy keep failing: verifiable rewards cover a thin slice of behavior, and a quality crash is hard to undo. ExpDis trains explorer policies *with* a novelty bonus, filters for correctness, and distills into a student trained *without* one. Repeat. Across seven math benchmarks and two families, it beats DAPO at the same wall-clock, and pass@k scaling improves — more diverse *correct* solutions, not just more samples.
**Why it matters**: Exploration that shares weights with the student is how RLVR eats its own coverage.

### 4. EngramEdit: Decoupled Knowledge Updates in LLMs through Conditional Memory
**Authors**: Hongru Cai, Ran Wei, Wenjie Wang, Yongqi Li, Wenjie Li et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.10533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10533)
**TL;DR**: DeepSeek Engram already stores facts in n-gram lookups so the Transformer can stay a computer. Editing those lookups is the hard part: one fact has many surface forms, and a shared embedding update rewrites neighbors. EngramEdit first builds target memories that make the model say the new fact across expressions, then updates the shared embeddings with a reuse penalty. Near-perfect edit success. Revised facts transfer to unseen phrasings and multi-hop CoT at nearly 3× the strongest baseline, while unrelated knowledge and general capability hold as edits accumulate.
**Why it matters**: Conditional memory stops being a capacity trick and becomes an editable knowledge interface.

### 5. Workhorse: Learning Robust Whole-Body Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from Human Data
**Authors**: Songbo Hu, Qiayuan Liao, Yufeng Chi, Pieter Abbeel, Koushil Sreenath et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.09117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09117)
**TL;DR**: Yesterday QF3 stood a humanoid up with off-policy flow RL. Workhorse plans contact-rich whole-body work from egocentric RGB and proprioception, trained on *robot-free* human video. A visual planner predicts five-link targets (torso, both wrists, both feet); an RL tracker follows them. No retargeting. Each policy is trained on the errors the other will make at deployment. On a Unitree G1 it sorts boxes with a kick, catches a thrown box, and climbs a suitcase. Simulated box-sorting: 77% clean, 64% under 40 N·s pushes; a second body trained from the same demos hits 83%.
**Why it matters**: Human video, not a mocap retarget, is now enough to put hands and feet on the same loco-manipulation job.
