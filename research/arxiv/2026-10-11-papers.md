## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. When Do We Need On-Policy Distillation? Distilling on Offline Student Rollouts Is Often Better
**Authors**: Siyan Zhao, Yonggan Fu, Jindong Jiang, Shih-Yang Liu, Song Bian, Byung-Kwan Lee, Sharath Turuvekere Sreenivas, Wenliang Dai, Hanrong Ye, Aditya Grover, Pavlo Molchanov
**arXiv**: [2610.11291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11291)
**TL;DR**: Friday's leftover OPD cluster treated on-policy student rollouts as the default. This NVIDIA paper asks whether they are. Semi-OPD distills from *offline* rollouts of the *initial* student. Across 17 teacher–student pairs (1.5B–235B), it wins 14, by up to +13.6 points and 11.4× faster. OPD helps only when teacher and student already share a high output-token overlap. For misaligned pairs, student rollouts drift off-policy *w.r.t. the teacher* as context grows; Semi-OPD stays on shorter prefixes and sees more teacher-preferred tokens.
**Why it matters**: On-policyness has to hold for both sides. The default OPD recipe is wasting compute on the pairs labs actually want to distill.

### 2. Emergent Inverse-Depth Scaling From Nonlinearity In Attention
**Authors**: Zirui Peng, Yizhou Liu, Ziming Liu, Jeff Gore
**arXiv**: [2610.11063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11063)
**TL;DR**: Saturday's InfiLoop asked why extra loops start *hurting*. This one asks why depth helps at all. Linear-attention theory ties parameter scaling to a power-law data spectrum — strong directions first. Nonlinear softmax attention instead yields *inverse-depth* loss decay on every spectrum they test: it focuses on relevant tokens, so strong and weak directions learn in parallel. Shared error across layers sets the plateau; layer-specific residuals keep adding, like a CLT.
**Why it matters**: Depth scaling may be a property of attention nonlinearity, not of the covariance of the data. The spectrum story was for the linear special case.

### 3. Generative Adversarial Loops
**Authors**: Kislay Aditya Oj, Nidhi Jain, Sri Surya Varma Datla, Priyanka Jayaswal, Kumar Krishna Agrawal, Aditya Desai
**arXiv**: [2610.11458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11458)
**TL;DR**: Friday's Memento 3 / MASS / MiMo papers asked whether RSI lives in a rulebook, a multi-agent loop, or more RL compute. GAL says the missing process is the *benchmark*. A discriminator agent writes adversarial data that breaks the current method; a generator agent invents the next algorithm. Four tasks — KV compression, sparse video, sparse attention, context extension. CompactorPress on Qwen3-4B at 4× jumps 0.35→0.97 on the discriminator set and still beats RULER-HARD; Dual Chunk Attention 0.20→0.90, with PG-19 / ScienceFiction gains.
**Why it matters**: Automated method search without automated goalposts is half an RSI loop. The discriminator is the other half.

### 4. Mid-Training Language Models on Raw Video
**Authors**: Jaedong Hwang, Xiaoqian Shen, Ernie Chang, Changsheng Zhao, Chong Zhou, Saksham Suri, Qi Qian, Zechun Liu, Lemeng Wu, Qinsi Wang, Raghuraman Krishnamoorthi, Wei Wen
**arXiv**: [2610.11019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11019)
**TL;DR**: Friday's 30k-hour ego-video paper asked what scaling human video still fails to teach a *world model*. This one asks whether raw, uncaptioned web video can mid-train a *language* model. Frames become continuous visual tokens; Qwen3-1.7B predicts the next one, no text loss. Same image-text instruction tune afterward. Mid-training lifts four video benches +2.9 and ten image benches +5.1 (perception, document, chart). Fourteen text benches stay flat (48.9 vs 48.0). Gains appear by 30% of the run and plateau. Predicting captions does *not* beat next-visual-token prediction.
**Why it matters**: Video mid-training does not need captions. The cheap self-supervised objective is enough, and it does not spend the text model.

### 5. NOMOS: Compiling Written Policies into Statically Verified Tool-Call Gates for LLM Agents
**Authors**: Min-Young Yu, Tony Kim, Jang Won Choi
**arXiv**: [2610.11030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11030)
**TL;DR**: Friday's ObligationGuard said agents miss *required* acts more often than they take forbidden ones. NOMOS compiles a natural-language policy into a deterministic tool-call gate. Static schema checks — no prover, no solver, no LLM at decision time — repair or reject 37% (airline) and 13% (retail) of candidate rules; without that, most shipped rules are inoperable. On τ²-bench, state-changing clause violations fall 66.3%→2.6% (airline) and 30.8%→6.9% (retail). AgentDojo banking: 0% ASR. Decisions take microseconds. A 26B on-prem compile is not significantly worse than a frontier compile.
**Why it matters**: Policy text is not a gate. The compiler that makes it one is, and it has to run before the agent does.
