# arXiv AI Research - 2026-08-12

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Long-Horizon AI Research for Grothendieck Constant: A Case Study in Human-AI Mathematical Collaboration
**Authors**: Alan Li, Rahul Saha, Anton Xue, Swarat Chaudhuri, Adam Klivans, Pravesh K Kothari, Raghu Meka
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11195
**TL;DR**: Case study of an AI research system that tightened the best-known bounds on the Grothendieck constant $K_G$ to $\frac{6\pi}{11} \le K_G \le \frac{\pi}{2\log(1+\sqrt2)} - 10^{-4}$, arriving at insights judged novel by domain experts. Documents in detail when and how AI helped, its failure modes, and what conditions enabled breakthrough insights.
**Why it matters**: Real progress on a genuine open problem via an agentic AI research loop — a concrete template for human-AI mathematical collaboration rather than another benchmark-solving demonstration.

### 2. Cracks in the Foundation: Seemingly Minor Architectural Choices Impact Long Context Extension
**Authors**: Amanda Bertsch, Luca Soldaini, Matthew R. Gormley, Graham Neubig, Hannaneh Hajishirzi, Kyle Lo, Dirk Groeneveld
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10296
**TL;DR**: Four "minor" dense-transformer choices (normalization, GQA, pretraining context length, sliding-window attention) compound to cut long-context performance by up to 47%, while being invisible to short-context loss and validation. Releases OlmPool, 26 comparable 7B models with pre/post long-context-extension checkpoints from 170,000+ GPU hours of controlled ablations, several beating Llama 3 on extensibility.
**Why it matters**: A systematic, held-everything-else-fixed answer to why model families differ so dramatically in long-context ability — with released checkpoints and attention-sink analysis.

### 3. Surgical WAM: A World-Action Model for Data-Efficient Surgical Robot Learning
**Authors**: Wenrui Bao, Tianyun Jiang, Zhiben Chen, Ser-Nam Lim, Peter D. Peng, Yuzhang Shang
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11204
**TL;DR**: A unified generative world-action model (built on Cosmos Policy) that first learns surgical visual dynamics from abundant action-free endoscopic video, then fine-tunes on a fixed action-labeled budget and deploys as a closed-loop receding-horizon controller. Video pretraining lifts average success from 63.5% to 77.8% (up to +20pp on PegTransfer), with the largest gains on contact-rich and bimanual tasks.
**Why it matters**: Shows action-free video pretraining is a practical route to scaling surgical robot learning, directly attacking the action-label scarcity bottleneck.

### 4. Once Poisoned, Arbitrarily Controlled: A Programmable Backdoor in VLMs
**Authors**: Tao Lin, Gaojie Jin, Zongxin Liu, Peng Wu, Lijia Yu
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10959
**TL;DR**: A single poisoning phase implants a programmable backdoor that lets an attacker choose previously unseen target-caption semantics at inference time and synthesize corresponding stealthy triggers on demand — an any-to-any caption-control paradigm with no retraining. Combines trigger-as-instruction poisoning with feature-space trigger steganography.
**Why it matters**: Breaks the static one-to-one backdoor assumption, showing dynamically controllable VLM attacks that preserve utility and survive classical defenses.

### 5. Flex-π: A Multi-Stream World-Action Model with Compute Flexibility
**Authors**: Ge Yan, Jinghao Liu, Yuzhi Fan, Lei Cai, Minwen Liao, Jesse Zhang, Dieter Fox
**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10860
**TL;DR**: Discovers a "free lunch": the same frozen video-generation VAE that encodes RGB also encodes 3D pointmaps almost losslessly. Supervises a 6B world-action model on 3D geometry and object-centric DINO semantics at no extra sensors, pretraining, or latency, and uses per-stream dropout so one checkpoint runs anywhere from fast action-only to full joint generation.
**Why it matters**: Demonstration-efficient WAMs beating strongest baselines by 2–7× on precise real-world bimanual manipulation while running faster than π0.5.
