## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Tev: Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic Environments with Dense Progress Rewards
**Authors**: Wenbo Hu, Shraman Pramanick, Krishna Kumar Singh, Nanyun Peng, Kai-Wei Chang, Yong Jae Lee
**arXiv**: [2610.18868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18868)
**TL;DR**: Monday's RSI-Anything said the missing piece was a predictive world model. Tuesday's MagicBeanStalk said it was a calibrated learning signal. Tev says both: first build a *latent world* — a complete hidden spec of the domain, workflow, and expected state after each step — then render a task from it so progress is a number, not a sparse terminal. Progress-potential reward shaping turns that metric into a per-step reward. Tev-Gym: 1,170 verified environments across 7 domains. Tev-27B matches GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 at Pass@5 on Agents' Last Exam computing/math, and transfers to Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0 despite training only on ALE-related data.
**Why it matters**: Long-horizon RSI was stuck on sparse rewards and unverifiable tasks. A hidden spec you can score against is the environment, not a hope.

### 2. Does self-distillation improve reasoning?
**Authors**: Jason Ken Adhinarta, Raina Wu, Ashutosh Sharma, Avinash Balakrishnan, Rameswar Panda, Polina Golland, Yoon Kim, Zhang-Wei Hong
**arXiv**: [2610.18613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18613)
**TL;DR**: SDPO and OPSD were sold as denser, more sample-efficient alternatives to RL: imitate your own predictions when conditioned on the answer or the environment. Across code, science, tool use, and math on Qwen3 1.7B–14B and OLMo-3-7B, the reported gains do not hold on unseen problems. They are overfitting or transferring behavior the model already had — not using the privileged information. Self-distillation gradients align poorly with the policy gradient that actually maximizes expected reward.
**Why it matters**: A week of OPSD/SDPO papers just landed. This is the control experiment. Privileged distillation that does not move the policy toward reward is a more expensive SFT.

### 3. HarnessTTT: Learn to Optimize Harness at Test-Time for Scientific Discovery
**Authors**: Yingzi Ma, Yulong Cao, Nanxi Li, Fang Wu, Yizhe Zhang, James Zou, Yejin Choi, Chaowei Xiao
**arXiv**: [2610.17370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17370)
**TL;DR**: The RSI leftover asked whether the missing dial was a world model, a harness, or more RL. HarnessTTT trains the *proposer* of the harness — system prompt, skills, tools, middleware, execution controls — while the executor stays frozen. A discovery-oriented policy gradient (gap-normalized rewards, RLOO, max-sharpening) updates the proposer from each budgeted rollout. Frozen 9B executor beats OpenEvolve on all 11 benchmark tasks (circle packing 1.17→2.64), holds the best open-weight sub-10B result on nine, and beats the best human reference on Erdős minimum overlap. The learned harnesses transfer: GPT-5.5 plus those harnesses matches or beats the strongest reported system of any backbone on 9/14 tasks.
**Why it matters**: You do not have to train the scientist. Train the lab notebook. The harness is the thing that compounds, and it ports to a stronger model.

### 4. Virtual Head Attention
**Authors**: Xibo Ding, Guoxia Wang, Jinle Zeng, Jiabin Yang, Sirui Tao, Chao Yang, Dianhai Yu
**arXiv**: [2610.17959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17959)
**TL;DR**: GQA is quality-good and cache-heavy; MLA compresses the cache and loses quality; MFA burns training/prefill FLOPs. VHA adds two linear ops — Q Premix recovers virtual-head diversity from halved physical query heads, Linear Postmix fuses inter-head features with a low-rank \(I+AB^\top\) residual — and folds both into the existing Q/O projections at inference, so serving is plain GQA-2. Two KV heads, group size 64, Tensor-Core-friendly decode. 30B-A3B MoE on 500B tokens: +0.70 / +2.60 vs MLA / GQA-4 on a 12-benchmark suite. After long-context mid-training, RULER 8K–128K averages 84.4 vs 73.0 for GQA, on two KV heads instead of four. NeurIPS 2026.
**Why it matters**: The KV/FLOPs/quality triangle is not a law. If the extra ops disappear at serve time, you can keep GQA kernels and still beat MLA.

### 5. TacitVLA: Reasoning Vision-Language-Action Models Without Reasoning Latency
**Authors**: Jack Lu, Yurong You, Boyi Li, Yulong Cao, Thomas Tian, Chaowei Xiao, Xiangyu Chen, Wenjie Luo, Boris Ivanovic, Marco Pavone, Yan Wang
**arXiv**: [2610.17540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17540)
**TL;DR**: Textual reasoning before acting helps VLAs — and then every action waits for the CoT. TacitVLA's action expert never attends to the reasoning generated on the current call. The supervision still improves the backbone; on memory tasks the text is written *during* action, not before. Across RoboMME, RMBench, LIBERO, and PhysicalAI-AV it matches the explicit-reasoning twin at up to 3.4× lower latency, and matches a no-reasoning twin's latency at up to +17.1% success. New published highs: RoboMME Counting 82.7 vs 66.8, RMBench 77.5 vs 56.5; 72.9% LIBERO-Plus; 52.5 scene score on PhysicalAI-AV closed-loop.
**Why it matters**: If the action does not read the CoT, the CoT is training signal, not a serial bottleneck. Keep the reasoning loss; drop the wait.
