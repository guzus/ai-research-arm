# arXiv AI Research - 2026-09-02

## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Mechanism Design for Alignment and Control
**Authors**: Dirk Bergemann, Andrew Koh, Stephen Morris
**arXiv**: [2609.01595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01595)
**TL;DR**: A revelation-principle treatment of AI agents whose preferences *and* capabilities are unknown. Capabilities can be concealed but not counterfeited, which yields nested cyclical monotonicity for implementable policies and conditions under which eliciting higher-order beliefs disciplines multiple agents. Worked cases cover sandbagging (a stronger agent pretending to be weaker), an alignment–interpretability trade-off, peer scoring, competitive reward coupling, and scalable oversight.
**Why it matters**: The first mechanism-design account of honesty *and* obedience for agents that can hide what they can do — the right language for sandbagging and scalable oversight.

### 2. SMELT: Scaling Laws for Compute-Matched MoE Looped Transformers
**Authors**: Shaowen Wang, Ge Zhang, Kairong Luo, … Shen Yan, Jian Li
**arXiv**: [2609.01343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01343)
**TL;DR**: Looping is usually credited with extra FLOPs. SMELT loops the middle half of a sparse MoE twice while matching the unlooped baseline on per-token FLOPs, non-embedding parameters, *and* KV cache, then fits a separate Chinchilla law up to 54B. Loss falls faster with compute (6.8–18.0% fewer training FLOPs on the compute-optimal frontier); the gap is largest on code and grows with sample length and in-context examples. A second pass reduces the attention sink and redirects mass toward content tokens.
**Why it matters**: A budget-matched scaling law that turns depth reuse into a real compute saving, not an apples-to-oranges depth trick.

### 3. Knowledge Distillation During Mid-Training Favors Reasoning over Factual Recall
**Authors**: Jacqueline He, Howard Yen, Shuyue Stella Li, … Pang Wei Koh, Luke Zettlemoyer, Wen-tau Yih
**arXiv**: [2609.01532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01532)
**TL;DR**: Forward-KL distillation from a post-trained teacher helps *both* reasoning and factual recall in pre-training, but during mid-training it keeps lifting reasoning while *slowing* factual acquisition. The cause is an asymmetry: teachers are more confident on procedural data, and students already hold low-entropy facts. Switch Distillation distills only where the teacher is confident (entropy as the router) and otherwise falls back to next-token CE; it beats other KD objectives across teacher sizes.
**Why it matters**: Distillation is stage-dependent — the same KL that builds a student early can starve its knowledge mid-training.

### 4. GlossoGen: Emergent Language in Complex Multi-Agent LLM Interactions
**Authors**: Elias Stengel-Eskin, Newton Sander, Carlos Bonetti, … Hale Sirin, Simon Kirby
**arXiv**: [2609.01491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01491)
**TL;DR**: In SaveVeyru, agents with partial information must talk under pressure. They invent compositional, morphologically productive languages that leave the English prior and become unreadable to humans. Emergence needs efficiency pressure, strong backing models, and a postmortem in which agents ratify conventions; weaker models can *learn* a language once it exists, from usage alone. Transmission to new agents is a different condition than invention.
**Why it matters**: Multi-agent systems can grow private languages that monitors cannot read — a monitorability result, not a linguistics curiosity.

### 5. Harness-of-Harness: Multi-Day Autonomous Software Development with Continual Improvement
**Authors**: Haoyang Yan, Min-le Su, Hangfan Zhang, … Lei Bai, Shuyue Hu
**arXiv**: [2609.01481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01481)
**TL;DR**: HoH wraps existing coding harnesses in iterative plan–code–test loops that balance repair with capability growth, keep evaluation independent of implementation tests, and version the project history. Across GameCraft-Bench, FrontierSWE, and ProgramBench, three harness–model pairs (Codex/GPT-5.5, OpenCode/DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Pi/MiniMax-M3) gain 52.25% on average and 82.86% at peak after three iterations. A 70-iteration multi-day run ships a first-person shooter with a coherent storyline.
**Why it matters**: The first clear demonstration that an outer harness can keep improving software for days without a human in the loop.
