## Highlights (Top 5 Must-Read)

### 1. Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B 24GB: A Hybrid Vision-Language Model for Local AI
**Authors**: NVIDIA (Ganesh Ajjanagadde, Chris Alexiuk, Luke Alonso, Kumar Anshuman et al.)
**arXiv**: [2610.33039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33039)
**TL;DR**: Monday's Kolibri was a 78.1B-A3.46B government-servable EN/DE MoE. Bolt is the other local open drop: a hybrid Mamba–Transformer VLM, 74B total / 9B active, squeezed into 24 GB from Nemotron 3.5 Super VL 120B-A12B via Star Elastic compression and 2-bit quantization-aware distillation. Built for reasoning, tool use, and web/terminal work. A LiLiCorr speculative drafter ships with it. 84 TPS on RTX Spark — enough for long-horizon agents — and it beats same-memory peers on GDPVal v2, BrowseComp, and Terminal-Bench 2.1.
**Why it matters**: The week's other open model was built so a plant can serve it. This one is built so a laptop can run the agent.

### 2. How To Build A Modern Diffusion Language Model
**Authors**: Volodymyr Kuleshov, Marianne Arriola, Yair Schiff, Gilad Turok, Guanghan Wang
**arXiv**: [2610.33391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33391)
**TL;DR**: Monday's HyScale was how you *train* learned sparse attention at a million tokens. This is the recipe for the other non-AR stack that actually shipped. Autoregression leaves the GPU idle at decode and cannot revise a token once it is written. Discrete diffusion denoises the whole sequence in parallel: thousands of tokens per second, a new inference-time compute axis, and built-in error correction. The paper is the Kuleshov lab's survey-plus-build guide — foundations, architecture, and the last two years of scaling to billions of parameters across language and biology.
**Why it matters**: DLMs are no longer a side bet. Someone still had to write down how you actually build one.

### 3. An Empirical Study of Reinforcement Learning Scaling Laws
**Authors**: Zeyu Zheng, Clare Lyle, Khimya Khetarpal, Will Dabney
**arXiv**: [2610.33420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33420)
**TL;DR**: Monday's rewrite laws priced the rewriter against the pretrain. This is the Chinchilla sheet for actor-critic RL — Starpilot and Craftax, procedurally generated, so you cannot memorize the level. The fitted laws predict performance, compute allocation, and hyperparameters two orders of magnitude above the cheap runs. The trick is the metric: a single-agent Elo variant is far more predictable than mean episode return. Applying the laws at larger compute sets a new SOTA on the harder of the two environments.
**Why it matters**: RL has had recipes. It has not had a law you could spend against. This is the first one that holds when you add two zeros.

### 4. RSI Arena: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement with Human Feedback
**Authors**: Yue Huang, Yanbo Wang, Lichi Li, Yuanyuan Chen, Andreas Haupt et al.
**arXiv**: [2610.33624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33624)
**TL;DR**: Monday asked who to blame when the cell is red — model or harness. This one hands the harness to the model and asks it to post-train. Ten frontier agents each start from NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B Base: six days, 1,000 GPU-hours, $300 of API, a shared 64-GPU cluster. They pick data, write the trainer, and choose the checkpoint. All ten ship a working assistant. GPT-6 Astra closes 76% of the base-to-Instruct gap on 12 capability benches; its length-adjusted human preference is close to NVIDIA Instruct. Stage 2 gives each agent its comparison records and three more days — eight of ten rise on style-adjusted rating. Bench gains stay modest and do not track preference.
**Why it matters**: Autonomous post-training is no longer a thought experiment. The gap it closes is real. The gap it does not close is the one that matters for RSI.

### 5. Alignment via Generalization
**Authors**: Shai Shalev-Shwartz, Amnon Shashua
**arXiv**: [2610.34242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34242)
**TL;DR**: Monday's REACT paper steered an early CoT and watched StrongREJECT jump. This one asks what it would even mean to *prove* the model is aligned. Aligned-PAC reduces alignment to generalization: success on the requirements used for training should extend to requirements the system has not seen. Intention is a *collection* of requirements, not one training objective. Independent evaluation can give rigorous evidence across that collection, and they bound how much evaluation you need. An optimizer that combines learned models satisfies the training set under stated assumptions. Choosing the requirements, and knowing when learning will transfer, stay open — now as learning-theory questions.
**Why it matters**: Alignment has had taxonomies and benches. It has not had a PAC reduction. This is one.
