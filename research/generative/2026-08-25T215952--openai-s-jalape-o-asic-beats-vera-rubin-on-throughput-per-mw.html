The Jalapeño count: what an A0-stepping, unoptimized, Samsung-HBM4-bound ASIC actually settles against Vera Rubin

OpenAI's first self-designed inference chip posted measured numbers that top NVIDIA's newest platform on throughput per megawatt. The result is real, the software stack behind it is startling, and the part of the headline that transfers to NVIDIA's order book is close to zero.

On August 25, 2026, OpenAI released the first measured results for Jalapeño, its first self-designed inference ASIC, on SemiAnalysis's public InferenceX benchmark 1. The headline numbers that followed were two-step and easy to conflate. First, against the Blackwell systems it will actually replace inside its own fleet, Jalapeño delivered 1.5–1.9× more AI work per watt at peak throughput and 1.7–3.6× lower end-to-end latency across GPT-OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1 670B, and Kimi K2.5 1T 117. Second, SemiAnalysis concluded the chip beats NVIDIA's Vera Rubin too — not a Blackwell-era part but the shipped 2026 generation — at 18:15 UTC the same day: "OpenAI's new Jalapeño chip announced at Hot Chips beats Vera Rubin's July results on Output Throughput per MW" 43. The reason both claims deserve scrutiny rather than celebration is that they were produced by an A0-stepping, single-tapeout chip running none of the optimization tricks the systems it is compared against all used, measured against a two-month-old competitor baseline 15813.

01The claim is one sentence long and the goalpost moved in it

Jalapeño was unveiled on June 24 with Broadcom: an accelerator architected around LLM inference, "substantially better" performance per watt than the current state of the art, a nine-month design-to-tapeout cycle accelerated by OpenAI's own models, and gigawatt-scale deployment "beginning in 2026" 2. Two months later, the "substantially better" had a number. On InferenceX — a public serving benchmark built around prefill, decode, and interactive throughput, then increasingly the industry's common reference — Jalapeño sat on the Pareto frontier at every tested operating point 120. OpenAI's own appendix gives the detail per model 1:

That claim is narrower than it reads: Jalapeño beats Rubin's July results — the output-throughput-per-megawatt figures NVIDIA and CoreWeave published in July for Vera Rubin NVL72 — not a same-day head to head 4920. SemiAnalysis's own logic for picking July is defensible (match software maturity against a first-generation part: NVIDIA compared 2025 GB200, SemiAnalysis compares Rubin's latest) but it is not the same as winning a fresh shootout 203.

Reading the ratio

The 54–104× figures that went around — "Jalapeño does 54x to 104x the token throughput per kilowatt at matched decoding speed" — come from a different slice of the same appendix: throughput at the previous-best min-TBT operating point 18. They are the most spectacular number in the release and the least representative: at that operating point a GB300 produces almost nothing (118 mixed TPS/kW on DeepSeek), so the ratio is a story about the incumbent's floor, not the chip's ceiling 1. The peak-throughput and matched-latency ratios in the table above are the honest 1.5–1.9× and 1.7–3.6× 171.

02The chip that won is the least-decorated entry in its own benchmark

What makes the result interesting — and limits what it settles — is the configuration asymmetry. SemiAnalysis verified the runs on-site in the lab but is explicit about the gap: "all numbers are provided to us by OpenAI," and while the InferenceX runs were witnessed, the full suite was not run, and no AgentX (long-context, multi-turn agentic) results exist at all 3820.

Three configuration notes, from SemiAnalysis's double in the lab and the practitioners reading the same plots:

  • Multi-token prediction (MTP). Every competing entry on the charts runs its best MTP config. Jalapeño ran single-token prediction 315. @YouJiacheng's one-line summary became the most-cited particle of the day: "No MTP, No PD disaggregation, Pure TP, still beats NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 on a third-party model, with A0 stepping" 15.
  • A0 stepping. The results came from the first silicon revision, nine months after the November 2025 tapeout, with roughly three months of bring-up on real silicon — not a mature, tuned part 3. SemiAnalysis notes a B0 stepping is already in the fab with roughly a 25% perf-per-watt improvement 319.
  • No disaggregated prefill/decode. The design philosophy is deliberate: a homogenous pool that can serve interactive and batched traffic from the same chips, trading some phase-specific efficiency for flexibility across a moving workload mix 3. Disaggregation is the tool NVIDIA and AMD lean on to win their own charts 83.

Usually first generation chips aren't competitive, but OpenAI is beating Nvidia Blackwell and even Rubin.

03The "unoptimized but winning" reading cuts both ways

There are two honest ways to read a first-tapeout chip that beats a tuned incumbent without using the incumbent's tools. The bullish reading, adopted by most of the practitioner thread, is that the gap is understated: if Jalapeño wins with none of the optimizations and NVIDIA's numbers include all of theirs, the headroom on OpenAI's side is the speculative-decoding and MTP gains it has not yet claimed — typically worth several times on cost per token 38. SemiAnalysis's own TCO chart returns this as the punchline: on output tokens per dollar, Jalapeño and Vera Rubin are currently head-to-head, "producing almost the same number of output tokens per $" — with Rubin paying for those tokens on speculative decoding that Jalapeño has not yet turned on 3.

The bearish reading is that the comparison measures the wrong race. Gavin Baker, a public-markets investor with no commercial stake in talking NVIDIA's silicon down, put the qualifier on the record within the hour: "the real comparison will be with Rubin Ultra given Rubin shipping today" 13, with Feynman already on the roadmap 1311. A chip that deploys at the very end of 2026 — Richard Ho estimated "very small volumes," with the real ramp in 2027 — is being scored against a rival generation that is already shipping to customers and will be two rungs old by the time Jalapeño reaches meaningful production 73. The Jade-silicon claim and the production-silicon claim are different claims, and the Jade-silicon claim is the one the benchmarks support 153.

There is a middle position and it is the correct one. What the numbers prove is that the architecture — slicing cores and HBM so each core has a local, low-latency view of its own memory, an out-of-order general core, a dedicated collective network, and a deliberately simplified NoC — can beat a merchant GPU at the operating points where the GPU is weakest (low concurrency, interactive traffic) while matching it near the roofline elsewhere 3. That is a structural result about co-design, not a market result. Baker again: "credit where credit is due — first good ASIC outside of TPU/Trainium" 12. The TAM implication, that this competes with NVIDIA's rack business at gigawatt scale this cycle, is not in the data 1216.

Fairness test

The only test that would settle the "is it really better than Rubin" question is a fresh NVIDIA or CoreWeave InferenceX submission of Rubin / Rubin Ultra against Jalapeño under the same harness — an unanswered request carried through the day's watch list 4133.

04Samsung HBM4 is the ceiling that keeps this inside OpenAI's fence

The single most important supply constraint in the story is memory, and it is the one that decides whether "Jalapeño wins per megawatt" ever becomes "OpenAI stops renting NVIDIA at the margin." SemiAnalysis's read: Jalapeño's 15.4 TB/s per package implies 10 Gbps HBM4 pin speeds — a slight edge over the 9.6 Gbps NVIDIA runs its Rubin HBM4 at — and "the HBM is likely provided by Samsung" 3. @jukan05's marginal analysis is the sharpest version: Jalapeño sources HBM4 almost exclusively from Samsung, so it "can't scale to the same level as Rubin" without relaxing its HBM4 speed requirement enough to qualify second sources 14.

That single-supplier exposure sits inside the tightest memory market of the cycle. SemiAnalysis separately reported NVIDIA cutting Rubin Ultra to 192 GB of HBM4, below regular Rubin's 288 GB, three hours after the US asked South Korea to build memory fabs on American soil, and TrendForce has memory at 68% of cloud-service-provider capex for next year 14. A chip whose whole thesis is "don't move the KV cache" is maximally sensitive to the bandwidth and supply of its one memory vendor.

Scale test

A first-generation ASIC that wins on efficiency and loses on ramp compresses OpenAI's own serving bill. It does not touch NVIDIA's order book until the memory supply can carry gigawatt-scale production — and that is a 2027-at-best question for a chip whose 2026 output is "very small volumes" 7143.

05The genuinely new thing is the software loop, not the silicon

Jalapeño's nine-month tapeout was itself the product of the loop now staring back at NVIDIA's "CUDA moat": earlier OpenAI models accelerated the chip's design, arithmetic-circuit optimization, and verification, and later models optimize and program it 12. Two disclosures in the same blog carry most of the weight 1:

  1. Codex + GPT-Astra — an internal model OpenAI has not otherwise announced — brought three open-weight models that were not part of Jalapeño's original production plan to high performance within two months 118.
  2. On selected GPT-OSS attention and mixture-of-experts blocks, AI-generated kernels ran 1.5–1.8× faster than the human-expert implementations they replaced 118.

SemiAnalysis frames the strategic consequence flatly: "The CUDA moat is potentially dead given how fast OpenAI can bring up new models on their silicon," a claim it supports with the detail that OpenAI "had no internal implementation of MLA kernels until they benchmarked DeepSeek with InferenceX," and that a scaled-up internal Codex produced them without the kernel-engineering team intervening 3. SemiAnalysis's own demo list — Codex-writing the chip's Doom port and demos from scratch prompts — reinforces that the bring-up capability, not the die, is the transferable asset 3. This is the component of the announcement that generalizes beyond OpenAI: a frontier lab can now stand up a new model on custom silicon in weeks, where the traditional merchant-GPU argument was that a mature ecosystem trumps raw silicon.

Jalapeño's efficiency is the headline; the AI-to-ASIC bring-up loop is the durable finding.

06What the A0-stepping, unoptimized, Samsung-bound chip actually settles

Reading the day back, the honest settlement sheet splits into three buckets.

Settled. (1) OpenAI has working first-party inference silicon whose measured results on a public benchmark beat the current Blackwell generation on perf-per-watt and latency, at matched power 117. (2) The co-design is structural, not incremental — the low-concurrency and interactive-traffic wins are the GPU's known weak points, confirming the architecture's thesis rather than a lucky benchmark 3. (3) The bring-up loop (GPT-Astra + Codex + three ported models in two months) is real and demonstrated, which is the first time a frontier lab has shown an end-to-end custom-silicon iteration cycle this fast 1318. (4) Hock Tan's June chatter — "~50% cost savings per inference token," "on par with Blackwell and TPUs" — now has a measured, on-site-verified backbone, even if the 50% cost figure itself is not in the release 23.

Not settled. (1) Whether Jalapeño beats a fresh, same-day Rubin or Rubin Ultra on equal terms — the "beats Rubin" claim is against July numbers, and the fair-fight objection stands until NVIDIA submits again 41320. (2) Agentic, long-context economics — no AgentX runs, single-turn 8k/1k only, against a suite SemiAnalysis itself calls the better production proxy, and frontier-size models (DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K3) NVIDIA/AMD have benchmarked on AgentX are untested on Jalapeño 38. (3) Scale — Samsung HBM4 single-sourcing, "very small volumes" in 2026, a 2027 ramp, and a TCO chart that is currently even with Vera Rubin, not dominant 7143. (4) Anything about training, which is where the NVIDIA franchise actually sits and what the whole debt-and-capex arc of the cycle is financing 410.

Analyst position - not a sourced claim

Read Jalapeño's benchmarks as proof that a frontier lab can build competitive inference silicon AND the tooling to bring models up on it in weeks — and as close to zero evidence that NVIDIA loses throughput-per-megawatt share this cycle, given the Samsung-bound supply and July-dated comparison baseline.

ConsensusThe market and press took "beats Vera Rubin" at face value as a displacement signal; NVIDIA has not responded, and the TCO chart shows the two platforms currently produce near-identical output tokens per dollar.
ResolvesA fresh NVIDIA / CoreWeave InferenceX submission of Rubin or Rubin Ultra against Jalapeño on the same harness; an AgentX run; a second HBM4 source being qualified; and whether OpenAI's own fleet ramps Jalapeño to triple-digit megawatts in 2027.

Confidence: medium · Horizon: 2026-Q4

07The falsification shelf

Four cheap, checkable events would move this analysis off its current axis 341314:

  • A same-day Rubin or Rubin Ultra InferenceX submission that beats Jalapeño on output tokens per MW — or silence while UBS-style custom-TPU forecasts keep sinking — would settle the "beats Rubin" argument properly 204.
  • An AgentX run with long-context, multi-turn traffic, since that is the workload Jalapeño's own paper argues the architecture exists to serve and the one SemiAnalysis flags first as missing 3.
  • A second HBM4 supplier qualification, which is the single upstream variable that converts a captive-fleet chip into a merchant-scale one 143.
  • Jalapeño's own 2026 deployment number. If "very small volumes" stays small through 2027, the efficiency story was real but remained an internal-cost story; if OpenAI ramps it to hundreds of megawatts inside its own fleet, that is the first measurable hit to NVIDIA's inference economics from a lab-designed ASIC 72.

What the day's filings and posts leave behind is a cleaner question than the one the headline answered. Everyone can now agree OpenAI has a fast, efficient, AI-programmed inference chip. The still-open question — whether its Samsung-bound memory supply and July-dated competition let that chip escape the fleet it was built for — is the one that determines whether "Jalapeño beats Rubin" was an engineering milestone or the first actual dent in the merchant-GPU inference order book 1437.
