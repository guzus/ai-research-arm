**Discovery Loop** is a **public benefit corporation** announced on
2026-08-06 by **Jeff Dean** as he left [[google]] after 27 years, together
with **Sanjay Ghemawat**, **Oriol Vinyals** and **Quoc Le**. Its stated first
domain is **automating large-scale experimentation for ML research and
engineering**. Google is a **founding investor and Cloud partner**, so the
departure is a spin-out rather than a defection.

## Why it matters

- **The founding team is the infrastructure lineage, not the product one
  (2026-08-06).** Dean and Ghemawat authored the systems layer modern ML runs
  on; Vinyals and Le are core Google Brain / DeepMind researchers. A company
  assembled from that specific bench, aimed at *running experiments* rather
  than shipping a model, is a bet that the binding constraint on ML progress
  is experiment throughput — not parameters, and not data (TechCrunch,
  @JeffDean, ARA daily digest 2026-08-06).
- **It lands in the same hour as Google's leadership rewiring.** Demis
  Hassabis moved to Chair of Google DeepMind and Alphabet Chief Scientist and
  Koray Kavukcuoglu took operational control minutes before Dean's post — see
  [[google]]. The two announcements are best read as one reorganisation of
  where Alphabet's research seniority sits.
- **Google keeps the relationship, and the compute.** Founding-investor plus
  Cloud-partner status means the spin-out's experiments are expected to run on
  Google infrastructure. That is the cheap version of the [[ai-capex]] pattern
  seen elsewhere this cycle: a hyperscaler funds a lab and books the compute
  spend back as revenue.
- **Automated experimentation is the same claim the verification problem
  keeps breaking.** Machine-run research pipelines produce results faster than
  anyone can independently check them — the failure mode tracked under
  [[verification-bottleneck]]. A company whose product *is* experiment volume
  inherits that problem as its core design question rather than as an
  afterthought.

## Open questions

- **What ships, and for whom?** Nothing published says whether Discovery Loop
  sells a platform, runs research for hire, or publishes its own results.
- **What does the PBC charter actually bind?** The public-benefit form is
  stated; the specific mission lock and governance are not.
- **Does Google hold IP or exclusivity?** Founding investor and sole named
  cloud partner is a tight coupling, and no terms are public.
- **Does anyone else leave with them?** The named four are principals; whether
  the departure widens into a team-scale exit is the signal to watch.
