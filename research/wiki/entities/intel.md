**Intel** entered the wiki on 2026-10-31 as the sharpest example of a company that *pays* for the [[ai-capex]] buildout rather than selling into it. The digest's closing recaps (@DobbinsDownload, @allday_stocks, @WealthCoachMak) tie the sell-off to AI hoarding memory.

## Why it matters

- **Q4 guide and 28.6% drop (2026-10-31).** Intel guided Q4 revenue to **$15–16B** and non-GAAP gross margin to **38.0% versus 44.1%**, attributing the squeeze to AI-driven memory prices. Q4 EPS was guided at **$0.25 against about $0.44 expected**. The stock fell **28.6%** and pulled the chip index down about 7%; [[amd]] fell 10.9% and [[nvidia]] 4.2%.
- **Q3 was strong.** Revenue rose **21% to $16.5B**, and the data-center/AI segment grew **78% to $7.3B**. The market read is that Intel is exposed to the PC supply chain, not to AI demand.
- **PC slowdown into 2027.** The CFO expects the PC slowdown to last at least through **1H 2027**. An explosion at Intel's Israel plant also cost output.
- **Memory pinch quantified.** TrendForce confirmed Q4 PC DRAM contract prices up **13–18% QoQ** after 18–23% in Q3, and Korean suppliers plan to cut PC DRAM shipments more than **40% YoY in 2027** to favor server DRAM. See [[micron]] and [[sk-hynix]].

## Open questions

- Is the margin hit mostly memory cost, or does Intel's own mix and the Israel outage explain part of it? The digest does not separate them.
- The recaps are single-source market commentary; the guide figures should be checked against Intel's filing.
