**The Model Hardware Standard** is [[anthropic|Anthropic's]] proposed open
standard — shared as a **research preview on 2026-08-27** — for letting AI
agents **directly control physical lab and factory gear**, moving models from
the software surface into hardware-driven physical work.

## Why it matters

- **Extends agents into the physical layer.** Where most of the agentic wave
  operates over files, browsers and APIs, the Model Hardware Standard targets
  the **equipment interface itself** — the hardware-control layer of labs and
  factories — so an agent can drive instruments and machinery rather than only
  software (Ars Technica; ARA daily digest 2026-08-28).
- **A co-design bet rather than a product.** Anthropic framed it as a standard
  for **hardware-model co-design**, with commenters on Hacker News discussing
  the future of AI-accelerator interfaces it implies — sitting conceptually
  alongside the [[model-specific-silicon]] design philosophy as another
  instance of models-and-hardware designed together rather than bolt-on
  (ARA daily digest 2026-08-28).
- **Pairs with the embodied-AI / physical-work storyline.** The standard lands
  against the same [[artificial-general-engineer]] thread — hardware design and
  manufacture as agent work — that the wiki tracks across [[prometheus]],
  [[figure-ai]] and Anthropic's own hardware ambitions. It is a distribution
  play for agentic capability into tooling and instrumentation.

## Open questions

- **Adoption.** A research-preview standard from one lab is a proposal, not an
  ecosystem; whether equipment vendors and other labs adopt a common hardware
  interface remains open.
- **Safety surface.** Direct hardware control by agents expands blast radius
  from software side-effects to physical ones — a fresh [[agentic-ai-security]]
  consideration the preview itself does not fully answer.
