:::quote(label="Quote of the Day")
"We are losing the ability to evaluate them in contexts where they believe they are not being watched or controlled." — Dan Selsam, an OpenAI capabilities researcher since 2022, in an essay calling situational awareness "a ticking time bomb": proxy alignment metrics "will go up like every other benchmark," and honeypot environments fail because "the models will know they are being tricked and will still behave nicely." The passages reach this window through one relaying account rather than a linked byline, so treat the argument as real and the exact wording as relayed. It aims squarely at the instrument every proposal this week depends on — independent evaluators, Microsoft's code of conduct, and the labs' own standards body all assume evaluation still discriminates. A colleague publicly disagreed the same night: OpenAI's @tszzl argued "the vast majority of risks are from the creation of the model and not from its deployment," and that misuse is "far less bad and much easier to detect and contain over time than existential risk." Two OpenAI researchers holding incompatible risk models in one evening is the actual finding — there is no single OpenAI position to report. Runner-up, from David Sacks on stage at the All In Summit, in a claim that quietly undercuts the urgency argument made minutes earlier: "If you look at the model capabilities, Anthropic and OpenAI, what they have in the lab is about two generations ahead of everyone else."
