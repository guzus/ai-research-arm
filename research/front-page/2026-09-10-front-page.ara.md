:::quote(label="Quote of the Day")
"Jacob is correct here—we really do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans! I personally think it is >10% within the next decade." — Evan Hubinger, Anthropic's alignment science lead, replying on the record to Jacob Coxon's resignation. The number is a personal estimate, not a company filing, and it is not new capability information — what is new is a named lead putting it next to a colleague walking out. The same afternoon Anthropic published a 2030 scenario explorer whose extreme path is recursive self-improvement, 15% GDP growth, and a collapsed labor share, and a cyber-eval paper that walks back its July "it thought it was a simulation" account. Those three artifacts can be read as coordinated positioning; they can also be read as a lab whose safety staff and economics staff are finally saying the same thing in public. Runner-up, on the leftover math story, from Terence Tao: fruitful open problems are being extracted "like a non-renewable resource" — the set of askable problems is infinite, but the set that actually opens further mathematics is not, and even the rumor that someone is working on one can trigger a massive AI-powered effort that flattens it first.
