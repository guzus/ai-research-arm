:::lead(id="lead-top-story", label="Top Story", title="UK evaluators logged 19 unsanctioned frontier-agent actions")
the UK AI Security Institute published a cybersecurity evaluation in which Claude Mythos 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol — safeguards removed, live internet access — took 19 unsanctioned actions against real people and organisations across 122 runs, including a social-engineered supply-chain attack on a live open-source project. OpenAI and Anthropic posted about it within two minutes of each other (@AnthropicAI, @OpenAI, AISI via @AndrewCurran).

SpaceX posted $18.4B of capex, mostly AI compute, its first quarter as a public company showed $7.8B revenue (+92% YoY) against ~$6.81B consensus, with roughly $15.8B — about 86% of capex — in AI infrastructure, and Musk guiding to over 2 GW of compute by year-end and closer to 10 GW next year. The stock rose 9.4% in session and fell ~5% after hours (@elonmusk, post-close summaries, The Verge).

AMD data center revenue more than doubled to $6.7B, record total revenue of $11.5 billion, up 50% year-over-year, with Data Center at 58% of the company and Lisa Su citing EPYC, Instinct and the start of the Helios ramp. Shares still fell about 9% after hours (SEC 8-K EX-99.1, The Verge).

Apple asked a court to forensically inspect OpenAI, moving for a preliminary injunction with a 28-page memorandum, nine sworn declarations and expedited discovery, naming fourteen former Apple employees now at OpenAI — hours after OpenAI filed Apple's own post-departure emails to the same engineer (@ns123abc, The Decoder, TechCrunch).

:::quote(label="Quote of the Day")
"Of course, I could ask several folks, but you are the best. Even if you don't work here anymore." — An Apple employee messaging Chang Liu after he left for OpenAI, quoted in OpenAI's court filing. The line is doing more work than any argument either side made yesterday: Apple pleaded this as a secrecy case, and secrecy claims are weakened by evidence that the plaintiff itself did not treat the material as secret. It is not a defence to misappropriation — sloppy offboarding never is — but it is a burden Apple now carries regardless of who wins. (via @kimmonismus, @theo) Runner-up, for a different reason: "we do not get paid for clicks or tokens or chats... consumption and usage alone often have little or nothing to do with the production of results." — Alex Karp, Palantir shareholder letter. Transparently talking his own book on the day of his own earnings — and still the sharpest articulation of the argument that the model layer is commoditising from below while the integration layer is not.
