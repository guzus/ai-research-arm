## Breaking News

- **Fired OpenAI safety researchers publish an open letter** (2026-10-08). Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak, and Mikita Balesni deny OpenAI’s claim that they mishandled sensitive information and say the firings will chill internal safety work and third-party evaluation. They deny leaking The Information’s story on less-monitorable architectures and say Korbak’s contact with outside evaluators during the Hugging Face agent-breakout investigation followed norms that were being written in real time. Wang wrote on X that OpenAI cited access to an executive inbox that IT had assigned for recruiting and failed to revoke. An internal memo shared with TechCrunch said the three were not fired for raising safety concerns; a spokesperson called it a “pattern of misconduct” beyond sharing with an outside eval group. The letter warns the firings could be used to end OpenAI’s work with METR. Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/fired-openai-safety-researchers-dispute-misconduct-claims-warn-of-chilling-effect/), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008339/former-openai-safety-researchers-ask-for-more-transparency-about-their-firings).

- **OpenAI bans two “false front” influence operations** (2026-10-08). A Russia-origin cluster (“Dark Clark”) used ChatGPT to draft internal reports, fake leaks, and manage a Latin America “think tank,” the Social Research Center, via a persona named Mia Clark; OpenAI rates it Category 5 on its IO Breakout Scale — the first at that level in 30 operations since early 2024. An Iran-origin cluster ran seven fake journalist personas pitching long-form articles to small and medium outlets and is rated Category 4. Both landed content in mainstream media, not only social posts. OpenAI says the operators used VPN access because Russia is blocked. Source: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-ai-enabled-false-front-operations).

- **USA Today Co. sues OpenAI** (2026-10-08). The publisher and owned local papers including The Tennessean, Indy Star, The Columbus Dispatch, and The Oklahoman allege OpenAI copied “hundreds of thousands” of articles for training and ask for more than $250 million. The complaint joins suits from The New York Times, The Intercept, Ziff Davis, CBC/Radio-Canada, Encyclopaedia Britannica, Merriam-Webster, The Seattle Times, and a coalition of nearly 400 local papers. OpenAI did not immediately comment. Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008198/usa-today-openai-copyright-lawsuit).

- **Anthropic launches the Cyber Mission, CIDP, and free OSS Scanner** (2026-10-08). The Critical Infrastructure Defense Program puts frontier Claude, on-site engineers, and threat research with founding partners Accenture, Booz Allen, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Insane Cyber, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC, and Rockwell Automation. OSS Scanner is an opt-in, no-cost scan of enrolled open-source projects by Anthropic’s strongest models, including Claude Mythos; reports are model-generated without human review, with a claimed true-positive rate above 90%, a proof of concept, and a suggested fix. Anthropic funded the Python Software Foundation, Alpha-Omega/OpenSSF, and the Apache Software Foundation and keeps the scanner free via the Defender Advantage Fund. Sources: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008521/anthropic-open-source-oss-scanner).

- **Google ships a unified Gemini Enterprise agent** (2026-10-08, Google Cloud event). The agent takes objectives rather than step lists, plans work, loads skills, and connects Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, Jira, Confluence, Git, BigQuery, Databricks, Postgres, Snowflake, and any MCP server. It gets its own Workspace identity and email, writes an audit trail, and shows thinking, subagent delegation, and spend in a tasks inbox. Users can override model choice, including Anthropic Claude, with more third-party and open-source options promised. Pichai said Gemini has more than 1 billion monthly users and that nearly 90% of Fortune 100 companies use Gemini Enterprise. Early testers include On, Shopify, and PayPal. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/google-brings-agentic-ai-to-gemini-starting-with-businesses/).

- **White House “Science: A New Golden Age” summit: NVIDIA $1B, Anthropic $150M** (2026-10-08). NVIDIA committed $1 billion over five years for U.S. “super intelligence” research, quantum computing, healthcare, and energy security, plus Genesis Mission phase-2 work in quantum, fusion, accelerator design, and microelectronics. Anthropic committed $150 million over three years to put Claude, Claude Code, and API credits on more than 15 Genesis Mission agencies, including NASA, NIH, and NSF, covering several hundred research projects. Sources: [NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-commits-1-billion-to-advance-us-science-over-the-next-five-years), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/genesis-mission-commitment).

## New Model Releases

- **JetBrains open-weights Mellum2.1** (2026-10-08). `Mellum2.1-12B-A2.5B-Thinking` is a 12B MoE (2.5B active, 64 experts / 8 routed, 131K context) under Apache 2.0. The architecture matches Mellum2; the jump is RL in real repositories with a shell and file tools. JetBrains’ shared-pipeline scores (thinking mode): SWE-bench Verified 2.0 → 47.0; SWE-bench Pro 0.0 → 28.0; Terminal-Bench 2.1 0.6 → 17.4; LiveCodeBench v6 82.0 vs 75.4 for Qwen3.5-9B. Qwen3.5-9B still leads SWE-bench Verified (50.0), SWE-bench Pro (38.0), and GPQA Diamond (77.8 vs 64.6). GGUF builds from 7.0 GB (MXFP4_MOE) to 24.3 GB (BF16). Sources: [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/JetBrains/Mellum2.1-12B-A2.5B-Thinking), [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/08/jetbrains-releases-mellum2-1-a-12b-moe-open-model-for-coding-agents/).

- **Claude Motion and live Dashboards enter beta** (2026-10-08). Motion turns text, diagrams, and images into editable, MP4-exportable animated explainers (about 30 seconds). Dashboards connect BigQuery, Databricks, Snowflake, or Salesforce and expose the underlying query for every number. Decoder: Dashboards is paid; Motion is Team/Enterprise. Docs, Slides, and Design leave beta on every plan, including Free; Anthropic says more than 45 million documents have been created with those tools. Design folds into the main app by 2026-12-14. Sources: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/claude-can-now-generate-animated-explainer-videos-and-live-data-dashboards-from-text-prompts/), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008134/claude-can-now-turn-data-into-animated-explainers).

## Research Papers

Thursday’s 04:00 UTC announcement (IDs through 2610.10539) is still the latest; Friday’s daily build had not posted by ~00:30 UTC. Two papers from that batch were not written up in the 2026-10-08 report:

- **Ledger** ([2610.10538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10538), Chaudhari, Paul, Saria, Chellappa, Bharadhwaj). A persistent 3D object memory from egocentric video: locations, move history, and short descriptions, including untouched objects. HD-EPIC 29.7% → 42.6%; UCS-Bench 33.8% → 38.5%; Ego4D median localization error 0.99 m on returned predictions. Submitted 2026-10-07.

- **CoTrace** ([2610.10426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10426), Chen, Zhang, Ye, Savarese, Wu et al., UCSD / Salesforce). Terminal-agent training that keeps only harness-matched trajectories. On the 102-task Tmax split, Qwen3.5-9B goes 78 → 88 solved under SFT and 90 under online RL. Pooled sibling-harness successes (149–308 trajectories) produced zero accepted model updates; 30–50 matched trajectories did, at 47 GPU-hours per iteration.

- **Goodfire ships “inside-out” activation monitors on Baseten** (2026-10-08). Probes read Kimi K3 internals and only call a second model on a flag. On ~1,500 sessions: about $51 vs $233 for a cheap external monitor and ~$10,000 for a frontier one; 94% of malicious hacking sessions caught, 8.7% of harmless ones escalated; four probes added <2% to time-to-first-token. Customers pick risks (offensive hacking, chem/bio, reward hacking) and the action (log, review, or refuse). Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/goodfire-says-its-new-inside-out-monitors-catch-rogue-ai-agents-at-a-fraction-of-the-cost/).

Decoder: the Association for Human Mathematics formed after 700+ AI manuscripts; three were retracted the next day over a sign error. Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/openais-math-solutions-arent-meeting-the-fields-standards-yet/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/some-mathematicians-call-for-openai-boycott-after-ai-generated-proofs-flood-their-field/).
