## Breaking News

- **SoftBank plans record ¥1 trillion ($6.3B) retail bond sale to fund its OpenAI commitments** — Bloomberg and the Japan Times reported August 24 that SoftBank Group is preparing the biggest retail bond offering by any issuer in Japan's history: 7-year bonds pricing around September 4 with an indicative coupon of 4.3%–4.9%. The raise is part of SoftBank's plan to refinance the $40 billion bridge facility it arranged in March to support its OpenAI investment (SoftBank has pledged more than $60 billion to OpenAI in total) and other corporate needs, alongside asset sales and asset-backed financing. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/softbank-plans-record-1-trillion-retail-bond-offering-in-japan), [Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/08/24/companies/softbank-plan-bond-sale/), [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/08/24/softbank-to-issue-record-63-billion-retail-bonds-to-fund/))
- **Nvidia's Groq 3 LPX inference chip enters full production; SpaceXAI adopts Vera CPUs for Grok** — At Hot Chips 2026 on August 24, Nvidia announced its dedicated Groq 3 LPX inference accelerator (3,400 tokens/sec at 100K context, 256 LP30s per rack) has entered full production, extending Vera Rubin NVL72 with faster token generation for agentic AI; first deployment is alongside Vera CPU and Rubin GPU for cloud provider Nebius, expected live later this year. Separately, SpaceXAI said it will deploy Nvidia Vera CPUs — the first CPU built specifically for AI agents — to accelerate orchestration, tool use, code execution, and simulation behind Grok. ([NVIDIA Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-groq-3-lpx-now-in-full-production-with-world-class-speed-for-agentic-ai), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/24/nvidias-dedicated-inference-accelerator-groq-3-lpx-enters-full-production-to-supercharge-ai-agents/), [StorageReview](https://www.storagereview.com/news/spacexai-adopts-nvidia-vera-cpus-for-grok-with-a-vera-rubin-nvl72-bound-for-orbit-in-starmind))
- **"Instinct" AI personal assistant draws privacy and security backlash from early testers** — TechCrunch reported August 24 that Instinct, a still-private-access AI agent praised by early users as one of the most exciting launches since OpenClaw, is facing criticism over its security model and terms of service. The agent requires standing, always-on access to email, messaging, calendar, and device audio/location/screen; its ToS reportedly grants broad rights to store, modify, and train on user content, and testers report data persisting after disconnecting accounts and actions taken without adequate confirmation. Instinct's team has not publicly responded to the criticism. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/24/instincts-powerful-ai-assistant-is-raising-privacy-and-security-concerns/))
- **Broadcom seeks $60B–$100B in debt via a special-purpose vehicle to bankroll Anthropic's AI chips** — Reported August 24 by Yahoo Finance/247wallst and TheNextWeb: Broadcom is arranging financing for an SPV that would buy custom AI chips and networking gear and lease them to Anthropic, keeping the debt off Broadcom's own balance sheet. The package reportedly pairs a ~$30B junior tranche with a ~$60–70B senior-secured tranche (total potentially reaching $100B), with Broadcom guaranteeing part of the senior tranche. It builds on the $35B AI XPV partnership Broadcom struck with Apollo and Blackstone in June 2026, which the partners say could ultimately finance 20+ gigawatts of AI compute by 2028. Anthropic does not buy the chips directly — investors finance the purchase and lease the hardware to the company. ([247wallst](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/24/broadcoms-60-billion-ai-debt-deal-hides-a-370-billion-question-nobody-on-wall-street-wants-to-answer/), [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/broadcom-60bn-ai-chip-debt-anthropic))

- **OpenAI reinstates 5-hour rolling usage cap on Codex and ChatGPT Work for Plus subscribers** — Effective August 25, 2026, ChatGPT Plus users again face a rolling 5-hour limit on Codex and ChatGPT Work, running independently alongside their existing weekly allowance; reaching either limit pauses included usage until it resets. OpenAI's Codex/ChatGPT engineering lead Thibault Sottiaux said the change is meant to smooth compute load while keeping the weekly plan generous. Pro-tier ($100/$200 per month) subscribers are explicitly exempted from the reinstated cap. ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/24/openai-restores-5-hour-codex-and-work-limits-for-chatgpt-plus-users/))
