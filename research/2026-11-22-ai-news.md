## Breaking News

- **The Lean and Mathlib communities certified Alpöge’s Riemann Hypothesis Lean proof** (Lean community blog 2026-11-21 13:55 UTC, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative). A day after Levent Alpöge released a 17-million-line Lean library, the joint statement says: “We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct.” All four Xena Project criteria are listed as satisfied: Mathlib’s `RiemannHypothesis` statement; the library compiles; `comparator` and `con-leche --compare` match the proved theorem to that statement; and independent kernels including Lean’s official kernel, `con-leche`, and `con-ron` accept an inert export. This is community certification of the **formal artifact**, not a journal paper, not a human-readable write-up, and not a Clay Millennium Prize award (publication, two years, and general acceptance still apply). MIT’s Andrew Sutherland posted a live check log (updated 2026-11-21 12:55 UTC): 175,187 modules compile on Lean/Mathlib v4.33.1; nanoda (Rust kernel) accepts in 27 min 42 s on 80 threads; con-leche accepts 1,468,418 declarations; five headline theorems cover RH plus Dirichlet GRH (not Dedekind/Hecke/automorphic L-functions). Combined with an independent Robin/Lagarias formalization, the export also yields hypothesis-free `robin_inequality` and `lagarias_inequality`. Sources: [Lean community](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), [MIT verification log](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/).

- **Two House committees urged a ban on PRC open-weight models in federal systems; Cursor’s maker told them it is exploring a phase-out of Kimi K2.5** (House Homeland Security / Select Committee on the CCP, press release 2026-11-20; Nov 21 coverage continued). Chairmen Andrew R. Garbarino (R-NY) and John Moolenaar (R-MI) released a joint investigative report after an April 2026 inquiry that corresponded with Airbnb (Alibaba models in customer support), Anysphere (Cursor’s flagship model built on a Moonshot AI base), and DoorDash (multiple PRC model families authorized). The chairs call for extending DHS’s DeepSeek exclusion to **all PRC-developed models** in federal systems, federal contract work, and high-risk critical infrastructure, plus an American open-weight initiative, NIST provenance / AI bill-of-materials rules, and penalties for illicit distillation. Twelve numbered recommendations are in the report PDF. Anysphere confirmed it plans to **explore** phasing out PRC open-weight bases; no replacement timeline is in the press release. Not a statute. Sources: [House Homeland Security](https://homeland.house.gov/2026/11/20/chairmen-garbarino-moolenaar-release-joint-investigative-report-urging-removal-of-prc-open-weight-models-from-federal-systems-high-risk-critical-infrastructure/), [report PDF](https://homeland.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026.11.20-Joint-Report-PRC-AI-Models.pdf).

- **UN talks on autonomous weapons ended without rules** (The Verge, 2026-11-21 20:03 UTC). Geneva diplomats left agreeing only to “start discussing options.” The ICRC, the U.N. secretary-general, and Pope Leo had asked for legally binding rules within a year; the compromise is **two years to propose** regulations, with room for some of them to stay optional. The Verge, citing NPR, names Russia, Israel, and the U.S. as the main blockers (Russia delayed the agenda; the U.S. proposed a five-year window for nonbinding guidelines). Humanitarian groups tie the urgency to civilian deaths from semi-autonomous weapons and drones in Ukraine, Gaza, and Sudan. Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039554/un-regulations-killer-robots).

## New Model Releases

- **Baseten shipped bison-1 and groundhog-1**, open-weight **decision** models on Gemma 4 (MarkTechPost byline 2026-11-20; RSS 2026-11-21 04:01 UTC). Decision models return calibrated probabilities (yes/no, choice, score) in a single prefill pass and generate **no output tokens**. bison-1 is 31B dense; groundhog-1 is a 26B MoE with ~4B active. Both take **128k** tokens and images (8× the 16k context of Baseten’s older Qwen-backed mammoth-1). Vendor/AA numbers: bison-1 scores **76** on Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1 (#1 of 19; Liquid d1.2-ultra 75; mammoth-1 71), Brier **0.14**, When2Call **98**. API: `decision-models/baseten-bison-latest` at **$0.10** per 1M input tokens; groundhog-1 **$0.035**. Training: 192 / 128 B200 GPUs. Groundhog has no official index score yet; every number is vendor-reported. Hugging Face weight URLs 401’d from this runner. Source: [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/20/baseten-bison-1-groundhog-1-gemma-4-decision-models/).

## Industry Moves

- **OpenAI published GPT-6.2 Astra/Sol prompting templates and API behavior changes** (The Decoder 2026-11-21 13:32 UTC; official docs fetched). Astra is more concise by default; templates tell it to make reversible assumptions instead of pausing, skip new tests unless requested, and write a self-contained final answer. Starting with GPT-6.2, the Responses API **drops prior reasoning items** after prefix edits, some model switches, or end-user/org changes — quality can fall and token use can rise as work is repeated. `temperature` and `top_p` are unsupported. Docs recommend reasoning effort `medium` for new integrations (`low` if migrating from `none`/`minimal`). GPT-6.2 itself launched 2026-11-17; this is the Nov 21 docs/recap, not a new SKU. Sources: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-shares-new-prompting-tips-for-gpt-6-2-astra-and-sol-to-fix-brief-answers-and-unnecessary-questions/), [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model).
