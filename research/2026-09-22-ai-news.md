## Research Papers

**NVIDIA researchers (Wang, Zhang, Zhang, et al.; arXiv:2609.24890, submitted 21 September) released OSWorld-Pro, a process-based evaluation benchmark for computer-use agents covering over 300 tasks and 2,800+ human-annotated subgoals, using LLM-judges aligned to human preferences to score subgoal completion rather than only final task outcomes.** Reported result: "top performers like Claude Opus 5" scored 75.7% on OSWorld-Pro versus 83.4% on the original OSWorld benchmark, indicating the process-level evaluation is meaningfully harder than outcome-only scoring. The paper catalogs specific failure modes — subgoal-irrelevant actions and input mistakes — as targets for future agent improvement. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24890))

**Wu, Li, Tafjord, and Kim (arXiv:2609.23939, submitted 20 September, newly surfaced in the 22 September arXiv cs.CL listing) introduce XYEval, a meta-evaluation framework testing whether AI agents recognize and correct users' flawed proposed solutions (the "XY problem") rather than just answering the literal request.** Across five models and six benchmark suites, agents showed performance drops of up to 46.7% (relative) when presented with a misleading user-suggested solution versus a neutral problem statement, worsening further when users demanded justification before accepting a better approach. Basic system-prompt mitigations only partially closed the gap. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23939))

**Researchers from Tsinghua University and collaborating institutions published C2C (Cache-to-Cache), a method letting LLMs exchange information directly through key-value caches instead of generated text, accepted at ICLR 2026 and covered by VentureBeat (Ben Dickson, 22 September).** A trained "cache fuser" maps a Sharer model's KV cache into a Receiver model's representation space and combines it with the Receiver's own cache, avoiding the information loss, ambiguity, and latency of text-based inter-model handoffs. Reported results: receiver accuracy improved 9.6-11.9 percentage points over solo operation and beat text-based communication by 3.1-5.4 points across MMLU-Redux, OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, and C-Eval; latency improved 3.46x-14.41x over text handoffs (one cited case: 90ms vs. 1,312ms to generate 80 communication tokens). VentureBeat's framing notes the technique "only [works] for teams that control their own inference stack," limiting it to same-provider or co-hosted model pairs. ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/text-handoffs-slow-ai-models-down-c2c-lets-them-communicate-through-kv-caches-instead/))

**OpenAI published a proposal calling for international technical standards governing recursive self-improving (RSI) AI, per The Decoder (Matthias Bastian, 22 September).** OpenAI states "fully autonomous RSI is not happening today" and argues it should proceed "until it can be done safely," warning that without safeguards AI could become "more dangerous, less aligned, and, on the whole, a danger to people." OpenAI proposes the US lead a global technical-standards effort building on national AI safety institutes, bodies such as CAISI and ISO, shared measurement methods, incident-reporting systems, and rules for human oversight of automated AI research. What does not survive: the article does not name specific OpenAI officials behind the proposal, attributing it to the organization only.
