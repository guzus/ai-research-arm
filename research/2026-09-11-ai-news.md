## Breaking News

**OpenAI put the Agents API into public beta, exposing the Codex/ChatGPT harness as a token-billed cloud API with no extra platform fee.** The September 10 first-party post says a session is created in one call (`client.beta.agents.sessions.create`) with a model (the sample uses `gpt-6-astra`), tools, environment, and a task. OpenAI hosts the harness; compute can sit in an OpenAI-hosted sandbox or with named partners including Blaxel, Cloudflare, Daytona, DigitalOcean, E2B, Modal, Oracle, Runloop, and Vercel. The harness automatically compacts context across long sessions, searches and loads tool definitions on demand, runs tool calls in parallel in code, and can fan work to concurrent sub-agents (`max_concurrent_subagents` in the sample is 3). It supports MCP, custom functions, and built-in web search, and is built on the open-source Codex harness. Customer quotes in the post are first-party: Ciridae’s CTO says an eval score moved from 0.71 to 0.85 with a 4× latency cut; SafetyKit reports a 60% drop in cost per case; Hypha reports 86% fewer failed agent responses after splitting harness from sandbox. Billing is tokens and tools only. This is the same Agents API yesterday’s pass dropped after the first-party page 403’d; the post loaded today. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-the-agents-api), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-new-agents-api-gives-developers-the-infrastructure-behind-codex-and-chatgpt/))

## Research Papers

Friday’s arXiv daily build (papers submitted 2026-09-10, announced 2026-09-11 04:00 UTC) is distinct from Thursday’s selections in yesterday’s report. Three findings cleared a concrete-result bar.

**Mixture-of-Experts overfit repeated data faster than dense models, and the gap tracks total — not active — parameters.** Jha, Li, Leskovec, Liang and Zettlemoyer (`2609.11917`, submitted 2026-09-10) sweep repetition rates on single- and multi-domain mixes across MoE expert counts and granularity, from 80M to 1B active (8.5B total) parameters. An 80M dense model can repeat data more than 8× with little degradation; matched MoEs start to suffer at 4× and, after 32×, give back the all-unique-data advantage and underperform the dense baseline. The effect grows with sparsity. Dropout and strong masking-based regularization can blunt the overfit. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11917))

**Compiling a game once into static dataflow makes GPU counterfactual regret minimization 30–80× faster than the prior GPU baseline.** Li and Huang (`2609.11923`, submitted 2026-09-10) observe that for a fixed game everything about a CFR iteration except the numbers is known before iteration one. GPU-CFR compiles the tree into flat edge and information-set arrays, folds chance statically, batches by depth, and records a CUDA Graph that replays with a single launch. Static-chance folding plus a dual-lane reach buffer cut framework operations by up to 18.1×. On one A100, across an eight-game suite, the solver is 29.8–80.4× faster than the fastest prior GPU CFR on the same card and 14–258× faster than LiteEFG on the four largest games; the compiled representation on eight CPU threads with no accelerator is already 2.2–51.1× faster than that GPU baseline. The update rule is unchanged; the CPU path reproduces reference iterates bitwise. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11923))

**A multi-signal detector reaches F1 0.915 on HaluEval, and DPO on that signal cuts a small generator’s hallucination rate by more than half.** Chundru and Biswas (`2609.11878`, submitted 2026-09-10) combine fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3, Monte Carlo Dropout, and temperature-scaled calibration. Headline numbers on HaluEval: F1 0.915 and AUROC 0.977 overall, with per-task F1 0.97 (QA), 0.96 (summarization), and 0.82 (dialogue); MC Dropout lifts accuracy to 93.2%. Ablating knowledge context drops summarization F1 24%, which the authors read as entailment rather than surface cues. A 25% data slice already captures 77% of full-data performance. Applying DPO to Qwen2.5-0.5B, scored by this detector, moves the hallucination rate from 85.5% to 37.7% (55.9% relative). Cross-domain transfer to SciFact is poor (F1 0.5 in the truncated abstract). ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11878))

**[later] T1, a 122B-total MoE, is RL-trained on a real shell for 300+ tool-call turns and lifts Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 43.8% to 64.0% resolved.** Yang, Shi, Li, Wang, Li, Mi and Liang (`2609.11042`, submitted 2026-09-10) reward each trajectory by executing the task’s own verifier, warm-start actor-critic with a dense process reward (absolute passing-verifier count), train on exact sampled token IDs with turn-boundary drift repair (TITO), and replay the sampler’s per-token expert choices at every MoE layer. TITO plus routing replay cut the train–infer log-probability gap from 0.021 to 0.013 with zero token drift in the loss region. The corpus is isolated from Terminal-Bench 2.1. On Long-Horizon Terminal Bench, T1 hits 27.9% and the abstract says it surpasses GPT-5.4 and GLM-5.1. Those comparisons are the authors’ numbers, not an independent leaderboard. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11042))

**[later] Speculative decoding’s draft-acceptance rate is a free runtime backdoor detector.** Wen, Salem, Paverd, Russinovich and Li (`2609.11799`, submitted 2026-09-10) observe that when a backdoor fires, the target model shifts toward attacker behavior while a clean draft does not, so draft-token acceptance moves. They formalize when the signal appears and show that suppressing it also weakens the backdoor. Across backdoor types and model families, SpecGuard catches triggered behavior — including stealthy cases input-level filters miss — without an extra generation pass. Mark Russinovich is a listed author; treat the “for free” claim as “no added model-computation cost on a stack that already drafts,” not a production ship. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11799))

**[later] SenseNova-U1.5 is an 8B mixture-of-transformers that understands, reasons about, and generates images in one encoder-free, VAE-free stack at native resolutions up to 4K.** Diao, Wang and colleagues (`2609.11929`, submitted 2026-09-10) train a spatially coherent patch-reconstruction visual interface, then specialize post-training experts for aesthetics, bilingual text, infographics, and editing and fold them with multi-expert on-policy distillation. The abstract claims gains on fidelity, text rendering, composition, multi-reference editing, and interleaved generation, with identity / geometry / unmodified-region preservation. SFT, RL, and OPD training code are to be released. No independent bench table is attached to the abstract. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11929))

**[later] Looped flows train recurrence with local denoising objectives so early steps still help later ones without a long BPTT tape.** Suleymanzade, Lee, Eijkelboom, Boffi, Ceylan and Kim (`2609.11801`, submitted 2026-09-10) decrease shared noise across steps so recurrent state transfers, then integrate the probability-flow velocity of the learned denoiser at inference; harder problems get a finer time grid. The paper’s reported scores include 58.8% on ARC-AGI-1 and 12.2% on ARC-AGI-2, above the authors’ prior looped SOTA. Those are first-party numbers on two of six reasoning benches. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11801))
