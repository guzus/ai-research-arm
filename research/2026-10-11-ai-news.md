## Breaking News

- **(Sunday) WIRED: HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster, and Hachette are using Claude and ChatGPT inside the publishing stack** (Adam Morgan, 2026-10-09 15:28 PDT / 22:28 UTC; not in Friday’s or Saturday’s files). More than two dozen anonymous staff said LLMs now draft publicity and back-cover copy, cover art, marketing materials, emails to literary agents, and — agents suspect — rejection letters. HarperCollins bought Claude licenses “months ago,” “voluntold” staff as AI Champions, and also licensed ChatGPT and Jasper; a spring brainstorming lead called legal, ethical, and environmental objections “the cost of doing business.” Simon & Schuster held an OpenAI workshop that recommended composing emails to literary agents and a May 2024 use-case contest with a $10,000 prize. After an Oct. 6 *New York* Book Gossip item, CEO Greg Greeley told staff the house had explored Skan AI’s Blueprint workflow-analysis tool and that “no decision” had been made. Hachette’s spokesperson said the house supports AI “for operational purposes, when they help our authors’ books reach more readers,” but “do[es] not support creative uses of AI, including communicating with our authors and partners.” Simon & Schuster CMO Wibke Grutjen said employees have “a limited number of enterprise-level AI tools,” use is not mandatory, and unvetted tools are banned. HarperCollins did not comment. Agents told WIRED they have started adding contract clauses that bar unpublished manuscripts from being run through an LLM; HarperCollins legal has told editors not to paste full manuscripts into open-loop models. Source: [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/book-publishers-are-quietly-using-more-ai-staff-are-revolting/).

- **(Sunday) DistroKid confirms it is taking tracks down in response to Universal’s “AI-slop pipeline” suit** (The Verge 2026-10-10 18:52 UTC). Artists said songs vanished with no notice, including non-AI work; DistroKid VP of Artist Services Amanda Ferri told The Verge “a very small number of recordings” were removed “in response to claims made by UMG,” that the company “strongly dispute[s] the allegations,” and that it is “working to minimize that disruption.” UMG’s September complaint alleged DistroKid built an “AI-slop pipeline.” Musician McGwire said six tracks were pulled, including a licensed Stevie Wonder cover and a song DistroKid flagged for sampling a track released two years later. This is a private-copyright enforcement response, not a new statute. Source: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1009309/distrokid-take-down-songs-umg-lawsuit).

- **(Sunday) Apple discloses a reverse acqui-hire of personalized-podcast startup Huxe** (TechCrunch 2026-10-10 19:50 UTC, after MacRumors). Apple told the European Commission it would make employment offers to “certain employees of Huxe AI” and “receive a non-exclusive license to Huxe’s intellectual property rights.” Huxe, founded by people who had worked on NotebookLM’s AI-podcast feature (since renamed Gemini Notebook), posted a shutdown on **2026-05-21** and pulled its apps; Apple notified the Commission on **2026-06-09**. The filing does not name who was offered jobs, whether they accepted, a price, or an Apple Podcasts product date. Reverse acqui-hires are structured to hire staff and license tech without buying the company. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/10/apple-discloses-deal-to-hire-team-and-license-tech-from-personalized-podcast-startup-huxe/).

- **(Sunday) Sakana AI, “Beyond Imitation”** (TMLR; MarkTechPost 2026-10-10 22:02 UTC). Multi-Layered Review is a three-agent Claude reviewer (Haiku 3.5 appendix + Sonnet 4 literature + Sonnet 4 three-pass review) graded on whether it *catches planted errors*, not whether it copies a human review. Contradiction Benchmark: **1,164** Gemini-2.5-Pro / GPT-4.1 insertions across **257** CC-licensed papers (ACL, AISTATS, CVPR, ICML 2025, NeurIPS 2024). With four reviews, MLR caught **73.43%** of distance-0 (core-claim) contradictions versus **14.81%** for the best baseline (AgentReview); a single MLR review still hit 60.79%. On 211 real WithdrarXiv-Check retractions the exact-match rate is **16.11%**. Cost ~**$0.47** per review (189k input tokens; literature agent optional). Hidden prompt injection still swayed every AI reviewer tested. ICLR 2025 score Pearson vs humans: 0.586 (human–human 0.742). Source: [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/10/sakana-ais-llm-peer-review-system-catches-73-of-core-claim-errors/).

- **Semi-OPD** ([2610.11291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11291), Zhao, Fu, Grover, Molchanov et al., NVIDIA). Distilling from *offline* rollouts of the *initial* student beats on-policy distillation in **14 of 17** teacher–student pairs (1.5B–235B), by up to **+13.6** points and **11.4×** faster. OPD helps only when teacher and student already share a high output-token overlap (Pearson *r* = −0.73 between overlap and Semi-OPD’s gain). For misaligned pairs, student rollouts drift off-policy *w.r.t. the teacher* as context grows. Submitted 2026-10-08.

- **NOMOS** ([2610.11030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11030), Yu, Kim, Choi, Corners). Compiles a natural-language policy into a deterministic tool-call gate; static schema checks — no prover, no solver, no LLM at decision time — repair or reject **37%** (airline) and **13%** (retail) of candidate rules. On τ²-bench, state-changing clause violations fall **66.3% → 2.6%** (airline) and **30.8% → 6.9%** (retail). AgentDojo banking: **0% ASR** over 288 pairs. Decisions take microseconds. A 26B on-prem compile is not significantly worse than a frontier compile. Submitted 2026-10-08.

- **Generative Adversarial Loops** ([2610.11458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11458), Oj, Agrawal, Desai et al.). RSI in which a discriminator writes the next benchmark and a generator invents the next method. Four tasks (KV compression, sparse video, sparse attention, context extension). CompactorPress on Qwen3-4B at 4× jumps **0.35 → 0.97** on the discriminator set and still beats RULER-HARD; Dual Chunk Attention **0.20 → 0.90**. Submitted 2026-10-08.

- **Mid-Training Language Models on Raw Video** ([2610.11019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11019), Hwang, Krishnamoorthi, Wen et al., Meta). Qwen3-1.7B predicts the next continuous visual token on uncaptioned YT-Temporal-1B clips, then the same image-text instruction tune as a no-mid-train control. Four video benches **+2.9**; ten image benches **+5.1**; fourteen text benches stay flat (**48.9 vs 48.0**). Gains appear by 30% of the run and plateau. Caption prediction does not beat next-visual-token prediction. Submitted 2026-10-08; dated 2026-10-07 on the abstract.

- **Emergent Inverse-Depth Scaling From Nonlinearity In Attention** ([2610.11063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11063), Peng, Liu, Liu, Gore, Peking / MIT / Tsinghua). Linear-attention theory ties depth scaling to a power-law data spectrum. Nonlinear softmax attention instead yields *inverse-depth* loss decay on every spectrum they test: it focuses on relevant tokens, so strong and weak directions learn in parallel. Shared error across layers sets the plateau; layer-specific residuals keep adding. Submitted 2026-10-08.
