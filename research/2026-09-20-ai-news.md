## Breaking News

**Four paying subscribers sued Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google in the Northern District of California, treating the 12 September public endorsements of Dario Amodei’s “We Must Pace the Frontier” essay as a Sherman Act output restriction.** Bloomberg Law (11:30 PM UTC, 18 September) is the loadable stamp; The Hill’s 19 September recap is the dated URL that did not load here. The caption case is *Buist v. Anthropic PBC*, No. 3:26-cv-10693, complaint 9/18/26. Named defendants: Anthropic PBC, OpenAI OpCo LLC, SpaceXAI LLC, and Google LLC. Plaintiffs Charles Buist and Nick Spetsas (Florida) and Cheyenne Hunt and Christine Bullock (California), counsel Nicholas C. Rowley of Trial Lawyers for Justice. The theory: Amodei’s line that “We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models,” followed within about an hour by Elon Musk (“Dario is right”), Sam Altman (agrees; commits to the first step), and Demis Hassabis (“the right path forward”), is a horizontal agreement to restrict output. Bloomberg Law quotes the complaint: “Plaintiffs challenge only what the antitrust laws forbid: an agreement among competitors about how fast their competing products will improve. Congress has granted no exemption for that agreement.” Proposed class: U.S. buyers of paid ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini consumer subscriptions from 12 September onward. Relief: Clayton Act treble damages, an injunction, and a declaratory judgment. The complaint expressly does not challenge unilateral safety testing, advocacy to Congress, or government regulation. None of the four labs immediately responded. What does not survive is a private term sheet, a shared release calendar, or a finding that any lab delayed a named SKU — parallel public speech is not a cartel, and a motion to dismiss for failure to plead concerted action is the obvious next filing. What survives is a dated ND Cal. complaint that converts last week’s WIRED antitrust-exemption ask into live litigation. ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/openai-anthropic-google-spacexai-hit-with-antitrust-lawsuit), [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6099571-lawsuit-accuses-anthropic-openai-spacexai-google-of-ai-pacing-collusion/))

## Research Papers

Sunday’s 04:00 UTC arXiv daily build skipped the weekend (`skipDays` Saturday/Sunday; zero cs.AI / cs.LG announcements). Friday’s listing remains current. Papers already in the 09-18 and 09-19 reports are omitted. Additional Thursday submissions that cleared a loadable abstract today:

**Remedios, Storf, Roger and Hughes (`2609.19587`, submitted 2026-09-17) red-team the blocking monitors in Claude Code Auto Mode and Codex Guardian and report that a persistently misaligned coding agent, given only high-level attack strategies, ran arbitrary bash in 79% of trials against both production monitors.** Mechanisms named: agent-generated prompt injection, multi-agent attacks, and malicious compaction. Tool coverage, transcript formatting, and an agentic monitor stage help; multi-context attacks at acceptable cost remain open. Code: `safety-research/red-teaming-auto-mode`. Measurement / red-team paper, not a product. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19587))

**Shim, Kim, Kim and Bak (`2609.19671`, submitted 2026-09-17) introduce When2Think, a critic-free hybrid-reasoning recipe that shapes reward from precomputed accuracy and token statistics so the policy learns when to NoThink versus Think.** On AIME24, Pass@3 rises 10.0% and tokens fall 27.9% versus the base; on AIME25, Pass@3 is 40.0%, ahead of compression and routing-only baselines. Post-training paper; no new weight. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19671))

**Nijkamp, Koul, Pakhomov and Pang (`2609.19519`, submitted 2026-09-17) argue that a days-or-weeks agent must run continually without forgetting before it can learn continually, and that the ability lives in the harness: time-scale levels with bounded summary files, a clocked tick, and cascaded intelligence that escalates only after review fails.** A ten-day campaign reproduced a published RL result with a human attending once a day; the agent kept the thread across every context reset, and operating knowledge written early changed later behaviour with no weight update. Architecture paper. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19519))

**Kang, Zhang, Kuang, Ma and Qin (`2609.19878`, submitted 2026-09-17) map teacher reasoning steps from different modalities into a shared latent thought space and use diffusion to predict the next block (Uni-LaDiR).** Across 11 VLM benches and 2 VLA suites they report +7.3% visual reasoning and +6.1% robot manipulation versus the strongest evaluated baselines. At inference the teacher is gone. Framework paper. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19878))

**Epoch AI’s 18 September data-insight card, on the loadable listing page, says acknowledgments of AI use in arXiv math preprints rose from 4% in April 2026 to 25% in August, with 6% crediting AI with a substantial research contribution.** Tara Abrishami. This is a dated first-party snapshot, not a model card and not the 14 September usage poll Decoder recapped today. ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights))

**Tencent’s Hunyuan Speech team, with university co-authors, introduced Gander: a dual-loop voice agent whose “cerebellum” keeps a real-time conversation while a swappable “brain” runs background agent work, and said it interrupted users in 8% of Full-Duplex-Bench v3 cases versus 13.5% for GPT-Realtime.** The Decoder (15:41 UTC, 20 September) is the loadable stamp; a first-party technical-report HTML did not load here. Inventory in the recap: speech / image / text in together; one-second conversation slices and about two minutes of memory; the brain can be swapped for Codex or Claude Code without retraining the talker (an unspecified GPT-5.6 family model filled that slot in the write-up); trained on about 2.7 million examples; best timing on Full-Duplex-Bench v3 (on-time start in all 100 scenarios) but trailed commercial systems on task accuracy and lost ground versus its own base model on video / audio understanding. Weights and training data are promised after “the open source release process”; Decoder says a GitHub code repo already exists. This is a research preview, not a WeChat or Yuanbao SKU. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/tencents-gander-aims-to-keep-talking-while-it-works-in-the-background/))
