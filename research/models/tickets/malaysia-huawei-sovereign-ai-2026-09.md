The interesting question is not whether Malaysia buys Huawei chips. It is what
the answer reveals about the reach of export controls over decisions the US
cannot directly veto.

Controls work on US persons, US technology and re-export. They do not work on a
sovereign government spending its own money on a domestically-operated cluster,
which is precisely the shape of this project: $494 million, Telekom Malaysia as
operator, and an explicitly stated data-sovereignty rationale. The US warning is
diplomatic pressure, not jurisdiction — and Huawei is already inside the
operator's cloud stack, which lowers the switching cost to close to nothing.

Read alongside DeepSeek's reported Ascend inference buildout
([[deepseek-huawei-ascend-950dt-2026-09]]) and Zhipu's confirmed 100k-accelerator
inference fleet ([[zhipu-domestic-chip-datacenter-2026-07]]), the pattern is
Huawei accumulating a customer base that is not primarily Chinese frontier labs.
An Ascend ecosystem with sovereign buyers outside China is a different strategic
object from one confined to domestic substitution.

Single relay of a Bloomberg report, no government confirmation, decision not
made. Everything above is contingent on the report being accurate.
