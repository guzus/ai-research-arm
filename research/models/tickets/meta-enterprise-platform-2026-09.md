Meta spent 2026 shipping a consumer agent that went viral. This is the invoice.

The announcement is short and the product list is the part that matters: Muse,
Meta Business Agent, Muse API, Muse Code. Meta is not building a new enterprise
model — it is putting a sales motion around the one it already ships, the same
sequencing OpenAI and Anthropic ran a year earlier.

What separates it from a routine pillar announcement is who is running it.
Desai left a public-company CEO seat effective immediately, MongoDB had to name
an interim, and the stock took a double-digit hit. Companies do not extract that
kind of hire for a pilot.

The interesting question this ticket will hold open is what Meta actually charges
for. Subscription and usage-based pricing are the obvious answers; the more
consequential one — a cut of transactions closed inside WhatsApp and Instagram
by a Business Agent — is what commentators immediately reached for and what no
Meta statement in-window supports. If that model appears in the pricing sheet,
it is a different business from selling API tokens, and it is the reason the
pillar sits next to advertising rather than inside Meta Superintelligence Labs.

Also worth watching: Meta's enterprise distribution already runs partly through
other people's clouds — Oracle GA and Google Cloud private preview, per the Muse
ticket. Whether Desai's remit absorbs those partnerships or competes with them
is unanswered.
