Two things in this announcement are worth separating, because only one of them
is about capability.

The parameter count is the part that travelled, and it is the part that means
least. 2.5T is vendor-stated, unverifiable from outside, and — as the
loudest skeptic in the same window put it — "very tenuously related to
capability now." xAI's own recent history makes the point: the 2T-class run was
labelled Grok 4.6, then relabelled Grok 4.7 by the same class of source, while
the shipped 4.6 was described as the 1.5T version. Version numbers and
parameter counts have not been tracking each other reliably on this product
line.

The **new C++ software stack** is the more interesting claim and got almost no
attention. Replacing the training stack for a frontier run is a large, risky
engineering commitment, and it is the kind of change that shows up later as
either a cost-per-token advantage or a schedule slip. It is also consistent with
Grok 4.7 having already slipped once for reasons Musk described in capability
terms — RL that "quits hard tasks too early."

The sequencing is the thing to hold onto. xAI is now publicly discussing a model
two releases ahead while the next one has not shipped, which is why
@DavidOndrej1's reply — "before we talk about Grok 4.8 and Grok 5, can we get
Grok 4.7 first..?" — is recorded here as the relevant skepticism rather than
dismissed. The falsifier for this ticket is narrow and near: either pretraining
finishes this week as stated, or it does not.
