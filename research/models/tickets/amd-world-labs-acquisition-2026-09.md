This ticket exists mainly to stop a single line in a crypto-news roundup from
becoming a remembered fact.

The claim is well formed. Named acquirer, named founder, named target, $8.2
billion, and a rationale ("physical-world AI") that fits what World Labs actually
does. Well-formed claims are the ones that propagate, because there is nothing
obviously wrong to catch on. The nine other items in the same list check out
against independent signal in this cycle, which is genuinely relevant evidence
about the compiler's accuracy and is genuinely not evidence about this item.

The reason to be careful rather than credulous is structural. AMD is a US-listed
company; an $8.2B acquisition is a disclosure event, and the reporting ecosystem
around such deals is dense — The Information, Bloomberg, Reuters, plus the
acquirer's own newsroom. A deal of that size that exists only in one aggregated
headline list a day later is anomalous. Either the corroboration is simply outside
this fetch's window and accounts, or the item is wrong.

If it is real, the strategic read is the part worth having ready. A chip vendor
buying a world-model company is not a compute play; it is an attempt to own a
workload. NVIDIA's approach to the same problem has been to invest and license
while keeping the model layer at arm's length, which preserves its position as the
neutral supplier to everyone. AMD buying a model team outright would be the
opposite bet, made from a much weaker share position — plausible precisely because
the neutral-supplier slot is taken.

Resolution should take days, not cycles. Until then: one line, one source.
