Two numbers from the same week, if both are real: Anthropic discloses a $42 billion
loss and $518 billion in compute commitments; its main supplier authorizes $150
billion of buybacks. That is what the top of a hardware cycle looks like from both
ends — the buyers committing future cash they do not yet have, the seller returning
cash it already earned.

The reason to be careful is that this specific claim is thinly sourced and the most
common error in this category is a category error. A "$150B buyback" headline can
mean a new board authorization, the remaining balance of a prior one, or trailing
repurchases over some period. Those are three different facts with three different
implications, and aggregated digests collapse them routinely. Until a filing or a
press release is in hand, the number is not usable.

If it is a new authorization, the interesting tension is with NVIDIA's other
behaviour. A company convinced its demand is durable and its reinvestment
opportunities are rich tends to spend on capacity and equity positions. NVIDIA is
doing that too — financing customer datacenters, taking stakes in labs, reportedly
anchoring a competitor-adjacent IPO — which is the profile of a firm buying
strategic position rather than one that has run out of things to do with its money.
Large buybacks alongside heavy customer financing can be read as confidence or as
the seller recycling its own revenue back into the buyers who generate it. The
second reading is the one that deserves a source before it gets asserted.

The protest framing in the digest is worth flagging and setting aside. Local
opposition to datacenter construction is a real and growing phenomenon, but nothing
in this cycle's signal reports it, so its inclusion here is the compiler's context
rather than evidence.
