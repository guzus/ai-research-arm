Training your own video models in 2026 is an expensive way to enter a market
that already has shipped, priced, commercially-served competitors. So the
interesting claim is not the model — it is the distribution.

Katzenberg's contribution is access to the people who greenlight productions,
and the stated goal is to "win over filmmakers." That is a different product
than Sora: not generation as a consumer toy, but generation as a production
tool, with all the licensing, provenance and union questions that implies. It is
also the half of the video market where the incumbent labs have had the most
trouble, because studios have been suing over training data rather than buying
on it — a pattern visible across this ticket set from
[[midjourney-discovery-motion-2026-07]] onward.

The departure signal deserves its own weight. Whoever ran the Sora app saw
OpenAI's consumer-video numbers from the inside and chose to go compete against
them with a studio-facing thesis. That is information about what they concluded,
even before the new company exists.

What is missing is almost everything: no entity, no raise, no named co-founder,
no date. This ticket is opened to hold a reported intention accountable, and the
first thing that would make it real is a name.
