This is the piece of the pacing story that could outlast the news cycle. An
essay is a position; a standards body is an institution with membership,
protocols and an enforcement question, and it survives changes of mind at
individual labs.

The reported start date matters more than the reporting itself. If these talks
genuinely began in July — before the essay, and roughly alongside the 1,100-plus
employee petition ([[industry-pace-ai-development-petition-2026-07]]) — then the
essay is the public face of work already under way, not its trigger. That would
also explain why the responses from Microsoft, Google DeepMind and OpenAI
arrived within a day and already shared vocabulary.

Two things would move this off `rumored`, and neither has happened. The first is
a name: an entity, a charter, a membership list. The second is the antitrust
question resolving in either direction — Altman's line that OpenAI need not
"wait for an anti-trust exemption" concedes that coordinating release standards
among the three largest labs is the kind of thing that normally requires one.
Cohere's "cartel" framing is the same observation from outside the room.

Until then the honest read is: three companies are talking, one outlet reported
it, one participant's CEO described the activity without naming the structure.
That is a real signal and it is not yet an artifact.
