The evidentiary content here is a company declining to deny something, which is
thinner than the relay's "all but confirmed" framing suggests. CXMT's statement
names no customer and asserts no agreement. Read literally, it is a marketing
line about competitiveness that would be equally true if no Apple conversation
had ever happened.

What gives it weight is the market it lands in. Memory is the binding constraint
on AI infrastructure right now — DRAM up roughly fivefold in a year, Korean
suppliers reportedly under ten days of stockpile, and forecasts putting memory
at over half of hyperscaler infrastructure cost next year. In that environment a
buyer of Apple's volume has a strong incentive to qualify a second source, and
CXMT has a strong incentive to be seen as qualifiable. Both parties benefit from
the rumor whether or not it is true, which is a reason for caution rather than
confidence.

The political dimension is what makes it a roadmap item rather than a
procurement footnote. Apple qualifying a Chinese DRAM supplier while the US is
pressuring third countries away from Chinese silicon
([[malaysia-huawei-sovereign-ai-2026-09]]) would be a considerably louder event
than the supply logic alone implies.

Resolution is cheap and will arrive on its own: a teardown, a named-source
supply-chain report, or either company naming the other.
