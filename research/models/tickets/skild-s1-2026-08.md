**S1** is Skild AI's flagship robotic foundation model, announced 2026-08-25.
Its distinguishing claim is not benchmark score but **learning mechanism**:
show it a single video of a task — up to ten minutes long, and one it has
never seen — and it executes, with **no fine-tuning**.

**The stated thesis.** Skild frames pre-training's purpose for robotics as
enabling the BERT→GPT-3 transition: in-context learning. That is a claim about
where the field is, not just about one model. If it holds up, the bottleneck
in robot manipulation moves from data collection per task to prompt
construction per task — a different economics entirely.

**What Skild reports beyond the headline.** Four emergent behaviours, all from
its own blog:

- **Robustness to perturbation.** Objects slid away mid-approach, swapped, and
  lighting changed — none of it shown in the prompt. S1 completed the task
  regardless. Skild's own framing is that perturbation is the test of whether a
  policy is overfit to training conditions or to the prompt itself.
- **Mistake recovery.** Observed off the shelf, including on out-of-distribution
  tasks like assembling a skateboard wheel, rather than being installed by
  targeted data collection.
- **Common-sense substitution.** Prompted to water a plant with a watering can
  when only a cup is available, it uses the cup; prompted to fill an
  already-full glass, it tops it off.
- **Demonstration correction.** Where the human demonstrator drops an egg and
  makes a mess, S1 performs the step with a controlled motion. Skild: "It
  treats the demonstration as a specification of the goal, not a trajectory to
  reproduce."

**What is not established.** Everything above is **vendor-reported from
curated demonstrations**. No failure rate, no benchmark table, no independent
replication, no disclosed hardware platform, and no availability, weights or
pricing. The relay that carried most of the detail (@AndrewCurran_) is an
enthusiastic reader of the blog, not a second source. Demonstration-correction
in particular — improving on a flawed human demonstration — is the kind of
claim that needs an adversarial evaluation before it means what it sounds
like.
