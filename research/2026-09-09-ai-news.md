## Breaking News

**The NSA, CISA, and FBI accused six China-based AI companies of industrial-scale distillation of U.S. frontier models, and Beijing rejected the charge on the record.** Joint Cybersecurity Advisory [AA26-251A](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-251a), released September 8, names DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI as extracting "billions of tokens across millions of exchanges" from Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok variants since at least late 2024. The agencies say the campaigns are "the core—not merely a supplement—of their AI development strategy," routed through APIs, cloud resellers, and a gray market of "transfer stations" that bypass geographic restrictions, plus shared premium subscriptions. DeepSeek is alleged to have distilled Claude 3.7 / Sonnet 4 / 4.5 / Opus 4.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro and Flash Preview, GPT-4 / 4o / 4 Mini / 4 Nano / 5, and Grok 4 to train R1 and V3; the advisory calls DeepSeek's widely cited $5.6 million training-cost figure "misleading" because it omits that data. Moonshot is accused of pulling Claude Fable 5 into Kimi-K3 and GPT-4o into Kimi-K2; MiniMax of using prompt injection so Claude Code would treat itself as a MiniMax product. Recommended defenses include detecting 24/7 max-throughput new accounts and then "subtly alter[ing] responses" for suspected distillers. On September 9, Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning called the allegations "unfounded accusations" and said China's AI progress is the result of "self-reliance and independent advancement." These are agency allegations, not reconstructed training runs; the named labs also publish architecture papers and train on public data. ([CISA AA26-251A](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-251a), [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202609/09/WS6aa113cfe4b06d4aa055d27d.html))

**Google announced its largest-ever European investment: at least €13 billion of Finnish AI infrastructure, paired with a 22-year nuclear power-purchase agreement.** Google's September 9 Cloud press release commits the spend over 2027–2028 for data centers and supporting infrastructure in Hamina, Kajaani, Muhos, and Vaala, and says the build is expected to add an annual average of €3.6 billion to Finnish GDP and support more than 37,000 jobs during construction. The 22-year life-extension PPA with Fortum is intended to keep the Loviisa nuclear plant — about 580 employees and roughly 10% of Finland's electricity — operating past 2030 through 2050. Google also signed a Fortum MoU on new flexibility and possible additional reactors at the same site, plus onshore wind PPAs that bring Google-supported new-to-grid wind in Finland to 629 MW and a contracted 94 MW battery near Kajaani due late 2027. Alphabet president and CIO Ruth Porat called it "the company's largest single investment in Europe." The €13 billion figure is a two-year capex envelope, not energized megawatts, and the PPA is revenue certainty for a life-extension program rather than a disclosed megawatt offtake. ([Google Cloud](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-09-09-Google-Deepens-Commitment-to-Finland-with-Two-Year-EUR13-Billion-investment-in-AI-Infrastructure))

**An Anthropic pretraining researcher quit the industry over recursive self-improvement, and Anthropic's alignment-science lead put a greater-than-10% extinction estimate on the record.** The Decoder reported September 9 that Jacob Coxon, who spent three years on pretraining at OpenAI and then Anthropic, resigned and accused both labs of "gambling with the survival of the human race," writing that "the people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade." Colleague Evan Hubinger replied that he personally puts the odds of a misaligned superintelligent AI destroying humanity within the next decade at more than 10%. Fellow Anthropic researcher Samuel Marks added that "the more senior the employee, the more concerned they are," and that current methods can only "nudge AIs towards better behavior." Hubinger's number is a personal estimate offered in reply to a departure, not a corporate disclosure; Anthropic is also known for hiring people who take extinction risk seriously. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-scientist-puts-the-odds-of-ai-destroying-humanity-above-ten-percent-this-decade/))

## New Model Releases

**Suno replaced its music-generation stack with Suno v6, which it says was trained on licensed data from Warner Music Group, BMG, and Believe.** TechCrunch reported September 9 (5:05 AM PDT) that the new family is "not trained using the data it used to train previous versions." Three SKUs ship: base Suno v6 (paying users; steerable), Suno v6 wild (paying users; experimental ideation), and Suno v6 mini (faster, available to all). New controls include prompt- or lyric-word edits of part of a song, text/image/video as references, and isolating an instrument from a sample. Older models will be retired. Suno settled with Warner last year and struck a BMG deal last month; it is still being sued by Sony, Universal Music Group, artist Jason Isbell, and users, and it admitted a day earlier to training prior models on YouTube videos. PitchBook puts cumulative funding above $819 million. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/suno-replaces-its-ai-models-with-a-new-one-trained-on-licensed-music-as-copyright-suits-pile-up/))

**OpenAI shipped ChatGPT Images 2.5 as two API models — GPT-Image-2.5 Flare and GPT-Image-2.5 Sunburst — plus a Sketch tool in ChatGPT.** The Decoder's September 9 write-up, citing OpenAI's announcement, says users now generate more than three billion images a week across ChatGPT Images and the API. Flare is the default, claiming higher quality than GPT-Image-2 at up to 50% lower latency; Sunburst is the slower, more controllable editor. Token rates are $8 per million image-input tokens and $30 per million output tokens, with new `xhigh` and `max` quality tiers; a 1024×1024 `max` image is about $0.21. Images 2.5 is live worldwide for ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work, and Codex, including the free tier, with C2PA metadata plus Google DeepMind SynthID watermarking. ChatGPT does not yet expose a Flare/Sunburst picker; The Decoder's tests found Work more edit-stable than consumer Chat. Arena's text-to-image board has Sunburst at 1421 and Flare at 1399 — both labeled preliminary on a few thousand votes. OpenAI's own index page did not load from this environment; the model IDs, prices, and feature list above are taken from The Decoder's dated recap of the first-party announcement. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-images-2-5-faster-more-precise-but-not-the-same-for-everyone/))

## Research Papers

Tuesday's arXiv daily build (papers published 2026-09-08) is distinct from the 2026-09-07 and 2026-09-08 AI-news selections. Four findings cleared a concrete-result bar:

**Two-thirds of material edits to frontier-lab safety frameworks are silent.** Zhu (`2609.08789`) versions every public safety framework from the twelve developers that have one — 710 commitment instances and 244 adjudicated changes — and finds a silent-revision rate of 67% under a strict standard (53% lenient). 77% of material edits weaken or remove a commitment; weakenings are more often silent than strengthenings. The EU and California already treat these documents as accountability instruments and require revision, but not a *legible* one. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08789))

**Bad tests make execution feedback hurt coding agents; splitting test-writing from repair reverses that.** Tao, Peng, Wang, Cheng, Yao, Wu, Ge, Li and Gao (`2609.09133`) introduce ExecCritic: a Test agent writes repository-native tests, a fail-closed harness freezes them, and a Repair agent may change source only. Holding Repair fixed, base-agent tests drop SWE-bench Verified from a no-test 61.2% to 57.3%; GPT-5.6-sol tests raise it to 65.3%. Role-specific RL on Qwen-3.5-35B-A3B lifts Test Base-to-Gold from 22.2% to 62.2%; the two post-trained Qwen agents together reach 72.6% with no stronger-model feedback at eval. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09133))

**Ultra-sparse MoEs need their own hyperparameter laws — activation ratio is a third axis.** Tian, Chen, Liu, Liu, Zhang and Zhou (`2609.08690`) ran 1,800 pre-training jobs across six activated-parameter scales (up to 6B non-embedding parameters, ~20T tokens, 200k H800-hours). Optimal learning rate and batch size shift with activation ratio and cannot be explained by total or activated parameter count alone. At fixed sparsity, optimal batch size is a power of tokens \(D\); optimal LR scales with compute \(C\). Predicted HPs stayed near the observed optimum on a held-out 12B MoE that activates 1/64 of its experts. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08690))

**API benchmark scores do not transfer to the chatbot people actually use.** Wang, Baumann, Ho and Koyejo (`2609.08861`) audited ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini across seven systems and nine benches. API evals score +3.4 percentage points accuracy and +2.1 pp test–retest agreement versus the matching chatbot UI. For ChatGPT, the API–interface gap exceeds the API-only gap between GPT 5.3 and GPT 5.4. Sweeping system prompts, sampling, and reasoning settings moves some cells and does not close the gap. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08861))

## Industry Moves

**Qualcomm said Amazon could buy up to $60 billion of its AI data-center chips and related products, and granted Amazon warrants on 25 million shares.** Reuters reported the September 8 announcement: the partnership covers custom inference silicon plus optical connectivity up to 1.6 terabits per second, and Qualcomm will expand its use of AWS — including Amazon Bedrock — for chip-design workloads. A regulatory filing described a warrant letting Amazon's affiliate buy up to 25 million Qualcomm shares at $161.26 (about $4 billion at strike), vesting with commercial arrangements, binding orders, and actual purchases, with 3.75 million shares vested on issuance against undisclosed initial commitments. The $60 billion figure is a vesting ceiling, not a firm purchase order. Amazon joins Microsoft and Meta as disclosed Qualcomm data-center customers; Qualcomm is targeting $15 billion of data-center revenue by 2029. Shares rose more than 3% on the news. This item was not in the 2026-09-08 report. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/qualcomm-amazon-develop-custom-chips-ai-data-centers-2026-09-08/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/aws-is-using-qualcomm-for-ai-inference-while-qualcomm-uses-aws-bedrock-to-design-the-chips/))

**Instacart launched Clementine, a conversational grocery assistant that turns a chat, list, recipe, or photo of a handwritten list into a ready-to-buy cart.** TechCrunch reported the September 9 (6:00 AM PDT) launch, quoting CEO Chris Rogers: "Every night, millions of families ask the same question: 'What's for dinner?'" Clementine can generate or surface publisher recipes, respect dietary constraints, and suggest cheaper substitutes and promotions. It is live in the U.S. and Canada. Uber Eats and DoorDash already ship similar assistants; the news hook is Instacart trying to keep meal planning inside its own app rather than handing that step to ChatGPT. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/instacart-launches-an-ai-grocery-shopping-assistant-called-clementine/))
