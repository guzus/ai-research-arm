## Breaking News

**Broadcom posts record AI-semiconductor quarter and guides Q4 AI chips to $21.7 billion.** The company's FY26Q3 8-K, accepted September 2 at 16:26 UTC, reports revenue of $29.6 billion (+86% YoY), GAAP operating income $16.0 billion, non-GAAP diluted EPS $3.32, and free cash flow $13.7 billion (46% of revenue). CEO Hock Tan said Q3 AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7 billion, up 221% year-over-year and 54% quarter-over-quarter, and that Q4 AI semiconductor revenue is expected to accelerate to $21.7 billion (+236% YoY). CFO Amie Thuener guided Q4 consolidated revenue to about $34.8 billion (+93%) with non-GAAP operating margin held at 66%. The board declared a $0.65 quarterly dividend payable September 30. ([SEC 8-K EX-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016826000076/avgo-08022026x8kxex99.htm), [filing index](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016826000076/))

**Meta ships Muse Spark 1.3 in Muse Code and the Meta Model API.** Officially posted September 2, Meta says the closed-weights model improves long-horizon agentic work, instruction following, and multitasking, with previously available reasoning modes live now and **max reasoning held for additional safety testing**. Artificial Analysis, dated the same day, scores the public **xhigh** SKU at Intelligence Index 61 (+4 vs 1.2) and a partner-preview **max** at 62 — behind Claude Fable 5.1 (66) and Opus 5 max (63), tied with GPT-5.6 Sol (max) and Grok 4.6 (high). List pricing is unchanged at $1.25 / $4.25 per million input/output tokens ($0.15 cached), or about $0.55 per Intelligence Index task — the cheapest model AA lists at 59+ intelligence. AA attributes most of the gain to agentic benches (Tau3-Bench Banking 35% → 47% xhigh / 52% max; Terminal-Bench 2.1 80% → 85% / 86%; GDPval-AA v2 Elo 1615 → 1709 / 1754) and notes a 1M-token context with text/image/video input. ([Meta AI Research](https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-spark-1-3), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/muse-spark-1-3))

**Hugging Face releases funes, a local memory layer for coding agents.** Posted September 3, funes is a single binary that indexes existing session traces from Claude Code, Codex, pi, and Hermes, then exposes `recall` / `get` tools so a later agent can retrieve raw turns with provenance. Embedding and reranking run on-device (Lance + BM25 + cross-encoder); optional sync publishes a redacted copy to a private Hugging Face dataset the user owns. Hugging Face's own handoff-vs-recall bench says recall was 4–8× cheaper than a written handoff on two tasks that required prior-session knowledge. ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/funes))

**Meta drops token-count and AI-adoption dashboards from performance reviews while staff-test Hatch.** WIRED reported September 2 (9:32 p.m.) that new written guidance this week replaces “usage of AI” / “AI Native” review language with wording that outcomes “can be supported by AI or other means.” Engineers were told the company “will not use AI adoption dashboards or token counts to evaluate impact” (The Information, quoted by WIRED). Spokesperson Tracy Clayton said Meta evaluates contributions and that AI-Native labels were never used for performance evaluation. In parallel, employees have been testing **Hatch**, an internal computer-use agent that can browse and operate other apps, for several weeks ahead of an expected public release; Meta declined to comment on Hatch. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/meta-pushes-its-new-ai-agent-on-employees-but-eases-off-on-tokenmaxxing/))

## Research Papers

**Nemotron-3-Ultra-CC is the first reported AI system to outscore the top human at IOI** (`2609.02849`, submitted September 2). NVIDIA authors train Nemotron-3-Nano-CC (30B-A3B) with SFT+RL and Ultra-CC (550B-A55B) with SFT on 22k curated problems, then apply GenCorrect (iterative generate–evaluate–refine). On IOI 2025, Nano-CC goes 130 → 291 after post-training and 468 with GenCorrect (gold line 438.3); Ultra-CC reaches 502. A competition-specific Ultra-CC system then ran *prospectively* during IOI 2026 under human time, internet, and submission rules and scored **535.4 / 600**, above gold (361.12) and the top official contestant (498.27). The authors note the run was unofficial and unsupervised by IOI. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02849))

**SolarWM: open data and scalable training for long-horizon video world models** (`2609.02886`, September 2). A multi-source engine turns 1.43M clips from 10 datasets into a frame-aligned contract; a backbone-native adapter instantiates four 5B–33B models on Wan2.2, LTX-2.5, and MiniMax-H3. A three-stage recipe (bidirectional adaptation → teacher-forced AR init → distribution-matching distillation) yields causal models that support minute-to-hour interactive rollouts after training on 5-second sequences. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02886))

**Cliff: process rewards from the first mistake** (`2609.02817`, September 2). After a reasoning trace first goes wrong, later tokens are conditioned on an invalid prefix. Cliff uses an off-the-shelf teacher only to mark that first error, then writes token-level advantages (positive on the correct prefix, negative after). Across 12 settings it beats on-policy distillation by 15% and GRPO by 7%, without a specialized process-reward model. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02817))

**oHC: orthogonal Hyper-Connections on SO(4) via quaternions** (`2609.02672`, September 2). Unconstrained residual-stream mixers compound and blow up; mHC projects onto doubly-stochastic matrices, which caps amplification but lets streams collapse. oHC restricts the mixer to SO(*n*); at *n*=4 a pair of unit quaternions parameterizes the group with no extra parameters. The authors report it beats the single-stream residual, mHC, and identity-fixed iHC on a downstream suite. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02672))

**Linguistic illegibility implies CoT monitors cannot be completely sound** (`2609.02852`, September 2). James Mickens argues that once internals are math over activations and language is only a lossy bookend, CoT monitors, constitutional self-critique, and linguistically defined activation probes can never be fully sound. The proposed floor is a sandbox whose guarantees do not read the model's linguistic state: a priori taint-tracking over system state the model must never influence, plus virtualization and third-party audit of the sandbox config. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02852))

## Industry Moves

**Reliance Jio opens JioPC to any internet user in India.** TechCrunch reported September 2 that the cloud-PC service, launched in July 2025 for Jio broadband subscribers, is now a stand-alone subscription: up to 8 vCPUs, 16GB RAM, and 1TB storage streamed to machines as old as eight years. Plans start at ₹1,000 (~$11) for two months; 12-month plans are ₹4,000 (~$42) for 8GB/500GB or ₹5,000 (~$53) for 16GB/1TB. Jio has not disclosed dedicated AI accelerators on the host side. IDC estimates India’s installed PC base at more than 65 million in 2025. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/indias-richest-man-now-wants-to-turn-aging-computers-into-ai-ready-pcs/))

**Amazon's Alexa for Shopping can now verify whether a message actually came from Amazon.** The Verge reported September 2 that customers can ask the assistant about an email, text, or call; it compares the message “against a record of every message Amazon has sent” plus contents, formatting, and sender, and confirms only when “completely certain.” Fake-looking messages are sent to check orders in the app and contact support. Amazon already offered a `verify@amazon.com` forward path earlier this year. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/988518/amazon-alexa-for-shopping-verify-emails))

**Google signs a multi-year MrBeast partnership for Gemini, Google Health, and Fitbit Air.** The Verge reported September 2 that the first video, scheduled September 5, follows Jimmy Donaldson and crew racing through jungle, desert, and Arctic legs using Gemini for survival advice. Donaldson will also use a Fitbit Air in a later challenge and appear in a Gemini campaign. The deal comes after his YouTube channel became the first to hit 500 million subscribers. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/988355/mrbeast-google-partnership-gemini-fitbit))

**Mistral's consumer Vibe product trains on user input and output unless the user opts out.** Mistral's help center (updated this week; the Hacker News thread dated September 2) says conversations, documents, and other user-provided content “may be included in Mistral’s model training programs.” Vibe users are **not** opted out by default and must disable “Allow your interactions to be used to train our models”; Vibe Enterprise customers are opted out by default. Studio/API training is a separate Admin-panel toggle. ([Mistral Help Center](https://help.mistral.ai/en/articles/455207-can-i-opt-out-of-my-input-or-output-data-being-used-for-training), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49535284))

## Regulatory Updates

**Protect Democracy sues four federal agencies to force disclosure of the White House frontier-model safety-review framework.** Ars Technica reported September 2 that the nonpartisan group filed after FOIA requests to the Office of the National Cyber Director, OSTP, Treasury, and Commerce produced no records (ONCD denied expedition). The complaint seeks the unclassified framework text, participation terms, participant identities, and access criteria by September 30, plus an injunction against improper withholding. The group says the August 3 “voluntary” pre-release review and the GOLD EAGLE clearinghouse have been shared only with “trusted partners,” and that Congress is being asked to extend CISA 2015 liability protections that the administration has said sustain GOLD EAGLE without seeing the program's terms. A supporting declaration from California Sen. Josh Becker contrasts the secrecy with the public process around California's SB 813. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/trump-may-be-forced-to-reveal-secret-rules-feds-use-for-ai-safety-testing/))
