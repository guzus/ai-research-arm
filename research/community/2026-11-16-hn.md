- Claude Code / Codex 1994 Jeep ECU: Still the largest AI-by-points story. Overnight jump — 404 points / 196 comments (was 227 / 103 at 2026-11-15 21:03 UTC). youtube.com/watch?v=WPj3aQ-xxpI plus github.com/MZULALI/xj-scanner (author's 1994 Cherokee XJ demo and GPLv3 hobby repo; not an independent eval or a smog-lab study). Pre-OBD2 4.0L, engine computer 56030117; SCI on three wires from the under-hood diagnostic plug (cavities 2 ground / 3 listen / 4 talk) to a $3 Arduino Nano (~$15 parts). Cavity 6 is 12 V with the key on and shares a black/white color with ground. The car was failing California 25 mph NOx; a new O2 sensor blew the previously-passing 15 mph reading (~80 → 900) and 25 mph still failed. Claude Code identified SCI, then live coolant 138 °F / battery 12.38 V, logged 15–25 mph drives, and first reported a 22% extra fuel trim (running rich, which would not explain high NOx). A rusty-port fuel-pressure gauge read 10 PSI; Claude commanded the fuel pump on for ~10 s until the owner stopped it — first write, not just read. A later log said the Chrysler Scanner tool's fuel-trim sign was wrong for this computer (it removes fuel, not adds it) and the mix was fine. Claude dumped the ECU program: a California calibration leans the exact smog-test loads vs a federal table; the live difference came to about a quarter of a percent of fuel. A federal reflash kit failed because the December 1993 board is ~4 months before those computers were programmable. Codex then sent a tiny program over the same three wires (~90 times/s); RAM was overwritten in seconds, so the payload went onto EEPROM. Author says they passed smog: Claude did diagnostics, Codex did memory, owner did wiring and driving. Parameter IDs / scaling / OBD1 sequence from László Dániel's ChryslerScanner (GPLv3); fuel-trim scale from AR1972/jeep_2.5L. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50463399
