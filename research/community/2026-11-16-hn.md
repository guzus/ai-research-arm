The car was failing California 25 mph NOx; a new O2 sensor blew the previously-passing 15 mph reading (~80 → 900) and 25 mph still failed. Claude Code identified SCI, then live coolant 138 °F / battery 12.38 V, logged 15–25 mph drives, and first reported a 22% extra fuel trim (running rich, which would not explain high NOx). A rusty-port fuel-pressure gauge read 10 PSI; Claude commanded the fuel pump on for ~10 s until the owner stopped it — first write, not just read. A later log said the Chrysler Scanner tool's fuel-trim sign was wrong for this computer (it removes fuel, not adds it) and the mix was fine. Claude dumped the ECU program: a California calibration leans the exact smog-test loads vs a federal table; the live difference came to about a quarter of a percent of fuel. A federal reflash kit failed because the December 1993 board is ~4 months before those computers were programmable. Codex then sent a tiny program over the same three wires (~90 times/s); RAM was overwritten in seconds, so the payload went onto EEPROM. Author says they passed smog: Claude did diagnostics, Codex did memory, owner did wiring and driving.
