- [Navier-Stokes – Tristan Buckmaster](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605915) / [On the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613262): still #1/#2 after another overnight climb (1008/439 → 1444/610; 994/799 → 1199/1032). The OpenAI thread is now the most-commented item on the entire front page. Buckmaster's CIMS statement is the independently posted write-up of the Buckmaster–Alpöge AI-assisted work on a friction-free Euler/NS variant (finite-time blow-up). OpenAI's post claims an internal model — "significantly more capable" than GPT-6 Astra — produced an analytical proof plus Lean formalization that an initially smooth fluid at rest can develop a finite-time singularity under a smooth force with finite energy (Clay statements C and D); ~10,000 concurrent agents, ~88 hours to the resolution plus 17 hours of Lean via GPT-6 Astra, ~130B output tokens on the NS path. OpenAI says it will not claim the Millennium Prize; Clay's evaluation has not started. OpenAI also says it did not see the Buckmaster–Alpöge work before public release and that the Euler results differ (forced vs unforced). The pair now has a same-day commentary thread from Terence Tao and a same-morning lab-resignation thread sitting underneath them.

- [Tao: Open math problems being non-renewably mined by AI](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49616968): new since 2026-09-08 22:42 — Terence Tao on Mathstodon (257/185, posted ~21:00 UTC). Fruitful open problems are being extracted like a non-renewable resource: the set of *askable* problems is infinite, but the set of problems that actually open further mathematics is not (ocean vs drinking water). AI has flattened the difficulty landscape in some areas without drawing a stable AI-feasible / AI-hard frontier, in part because labs do not publish negative results or the process that produced a solution. Identifying a promising problem is now the scarce resource; even the *rumor* that someone is working on one can trigger a massive AI-powered effort that "flattens it" before the original project matures — a direct commentary on today's Navier–Stokes cluster. Newest research-process / formal-math-policy thread.
