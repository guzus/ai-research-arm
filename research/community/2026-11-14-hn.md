- Generative AI energy / non-English text: Still on the page. 168 points / 94 comments (was 104 / 70 at 18:08 UTC). news.umich.edu/generative-ai-uses-more-energy-on-non-english-text/ (Kate McAlpine / U-M Engineering, published 2026-11-05; university write-up of Deng / Mihalcea et al., EMNLP 2026, DOI 10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.2113; authors' measurements, not an independent replication). Three open-weight models — Alibaba Qwen 3, Google Gemma 3, Meta Llama 3.1 — answering Belebele reading-comprehension items in 122 languages, energy via Michigan's ML.Energy framework; also grade-school math and open-ended prompts translated into seven other languages with Meta NLLB. Headline range: 1.4× to 180× the energy of an equivalent English prompt for non-English generation; most Western European languages ≤2×; Hindi 7.9× and Bengali 15.3×. Authors estimate ~5.2 billion people (Ethnologue) face more than 2× energy to generate in their first language vs English. Mechanism they name: more tokens per script (Devanagari / Bengali characters combining into large unique sets), which fills memory, shrinks batch size, and raises energy per token; companies that price by token also charge more. English used the least energy on average; the gap persisted at larger sizes and across processor types. Recommendations on that page: language-specific models or a reliable translate-to-English pipeline, plus crowdsourced native-speaker training data. First author Naihao Deng; corresponding Rada Mihalcea. Partial NSF / DARPA funding. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50441444

Premise: Musk claimed "the lowest-cost place to put AI will be space […] within two years, maybe three." Named programs: Google Project Suncatcher (TPU satellites in dawn-dusk SSO); SpaceX FCC filing for up to one million data-center satellites; Blue Origin, Ramon.Space, Starcloud, Aetherflux; China's Three-Body Computing Constellation (12 operational satellites, Alibaba Qwen3 in orbit); NVIDIA Space-1 Vera Rubin Module at GTC March 2026. Authors' take: ODCs unlikely to be a meaningful share of compute before 2030, cost-competitive with present-day terrestrial DCs in 3–5 years if Starship stays on track; then possibly much or most of 2030s buildout. Launch is the lever: present ~$1,500/kg; space solar reaches parity with present-day off-grid terrestrial power at ~$250/kg and cheaper than any current terrestrial source at ~$50/kg ($25–45/MWh with Starlink-like arrays). Per-GW 5-year cycle (IT hardware held at $25.6B/GW): Earth grid $37.6B, conservative microgrid $42.5B, bullish solar+gas $36.8B; ODC conservative $48.2B ($250/kg), median $35.0B ($100/kg, 17% mass cut), bullish $30.3B ($50/kg, 54% mass cut). Starship cost table on that page: expendable ~$475/kg; booster reuse ~$228; full reuse early ~$93; Falcon-like 25/25 lives ~$51; floor ~$13/kg propellant+ops at 150 t, ~$10/kg if Block 4 hits 200 t. Cooling: ISS-era ~13 W/kg; authors' carbon-fibre selective-coating panels ~163–346 W/kg (13–28×); NASA Marshall Hi-K carbon-composite ground tests imply ~90 W/kg; coolant 25–45°C vs ISS ammonia ~-40°C; GPU throttle ~83–85°C. Conservative radiator ~14% mass / ~5% cost; median ~8% / ~2%, less than terrestrial cooling over a comparable lifecycle. Chip bleed: Meta 16,384-GPU H100 cluster, 419 hardware failures in 54 days (~9%/year annualised); in orbit that is a ~38% compute loss over a 5-year lifecycle, modelled as ~38% extra non-compute hardware. Bandwidth: commercial ISL 1–100 Gbps over thousands of km; Suncatcher proposes 1–2 Tbps per transceiver pair at km/sub-km spacing; constellation vs Starcloud modular station (~40 MW docked modules). Inference is the early fit (LEO latency a few ms); frontier training still wants terrestrial bisection bandwidth. Radiation: Google Trillium TPU no hard faults to 15 krad, ~20× the expected 5-year shielded dose at 550 km; uncorrectable bit flips ~0.01–0.1 per GPU per year. Starlink: zero confirmed debris-strike failures across ~27,000 satellite-years; >300,000 collision-avoidance manoeuvres/year. FAA Starship cap 145 launches/year (~0.5 GW/yr). Slow scenario: ~4 GW orbital by 2031 vs ~100 GW terrestrial by 2030. Fast scenario: rapid reuse by 2027, 10,000+ launches, up to 100 GW orbital by EOY 2030. Governance aside they flag: SpaceX is the only near-term heavy-lift path and now operates a frontier lab via xAI. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50498945
