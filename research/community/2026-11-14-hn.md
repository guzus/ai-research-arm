HN title copies TechPowerUp's "engineered scarcity" framing. The WSJ account of the August meeting, as relayed by VideoCardz and Sulat, has Huang describing too much committed supply, not a shortage: wafers and memory from TSMC coming at him like an "avalanche" while major cloud providers planned alternative chips. Nvidia was in talks to guarantee billions of dollars of borrowing for companies that could not afford its GPUs, including deals led by VP Nico Caprez (Huang's son-in-law) for upstart cloud providers and data-center developers, with Nvidia proposing a revenue share in exchange for credit support. Kress, then CFO and now interim CEO, pushed to rein those deals in; Huang called her a "deer in the headlights." One proposal was a ~$250B guarantee around an Ohio OpenAI data-center project (~$300B of Nvidia chips); Kress cut it roughly in half (TechCrunch later put the signed commitment at $105B; Reuters sources at $120B; signed 17 Aug). By late August Nvidia paused some credit-support deals and added senior/board review. On the 26 Aug earnings call Kress said Nvidia had invested or committed nearly $50B in frontier AI labs, expected those labs to be ~a quarter of next year's business, and acknowledged "some will call this circular financing." She also said ~70% revenue growth through Jan 2028 is limited by supply, not demand. The scarcity motive is TechPowerUp/Wccftech's reading, not a quoted WSJ claim from the meeting. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50493251
- Jonathan Blow / AI-solved-coding talk: New, and now the largest AI comment thread on the page. 314 points / 395 comments. youtube.com/watch?v=IRK8h67ol7Q (interview with Wolf Tivy; Blow's opinions, not a benchmark). Blow's claim: if LLMs did what the hype says, that would be the most valuable company in the world, but he does not see high-quality code or new software capabilities. He treats current models as a fast/cheap search that can one-shot ~30-line throwaway tasks (his Excel/VBA example: ~5 h from first principles vs ~1 h with the model) while failing at judgment, tradeoffs, and the constraint-graph a large program has to keep true. He does not buy 10× / 100× productivity claims because, as an external observer, consumer software has not advanced a corresponding number of years; the extra output looks like more lines and more technical debt (proofs and generated programs both "10×–100× longer" than a human's). He would rather pair an AlphaZero-style rule-operating system for code with an LLM for talk. HN's top thread agrees the bottleneck is deciding what is worth building and modelling it, not typing; dissenters point at more/better indie games and at software that would not have existed without agents. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50484658

- JEPA / LeWM experiments: Still on the page. 264 points / 44 comments (was 208 / 35 at 2026-11-14 00:53 UTC). haddyalchaer.com/jepa (Haddy Alchaer; author's 600-experiment writeup, not an independent eval). LeWM (Maes / Le Lidec / Scieur / LeCun / Balestriero, arXiv 2603.19312) on Push-T. Author's numbers: vanilla LeWM 78% vs exact-physics predictor 77% on 250 long-horizon (75-step) tasks with expert subgoals every 25 steps; as published, 22% planning all 75 steps at once vs 80% with 25-step subgoals (exact physics all-at-once 42%). A latent memory graph of ~460,000 demonstration embeddings plus a cost on inter-demo jumps reached 85%, matching expert subgoals. Standard Push-T goals sit on paths demonstrations already travel (84% already had a near-identical stored state); on 250 fresh standard episodes at 50 steps, 44% vs 68% on the official 50. Harder test (start from one demo, goal from another; block on target for last 10 steps): memory-graph 75% standard / 45% harder. Deleting stored states near the goal dropped success 93% → 32%; filling the gap with shifted / rotated / mirrored demo segments recovered 85% (harder test 45% → 56%). Scoring the pusher in the goal image: exact-physics planner 9/50 with pusher vs 39/50 block-only; searching 16 pusher placements doubled LeWM on the harder test 11% → 23%. No weight updates. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50487185
