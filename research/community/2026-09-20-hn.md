Method note, not a tool launch. Two rules that keep the model a copyeditor rather than a ghostwriter: **(1) you may not use a single word an LLM suggests** — frontier models write "magazine headlines," and you will not reliably spot the Velveeta; **(2) forbid encouragement** — the default "that's gold, Jerry" freezes first-draft structure, which *is* the voice. What he will take: mechanical flags (passive, nominalizations, repeated turns, sawdust adverbs, two or three movable paragraphs). Workflow: write the piece yourself → ask the model to spot problems → rewrite → present original vs rewrite to a *fresh* context that does not know you just edited. Book he names: Williams / *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*. Closing corollary of rule two: he asked GPT-5 whether this post was 20% too long, it said yes, and he left it. Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown) guest post on Terence Tao's blog, dated 18 September 2026 (posted ~06:28 UTC yesterday). Pedagogy note, not a model release — and the first named reply this cycle to the "proof-generating machines make mathematicians obsolete" line that sat under last week's now-off-page Gowers Fields-letter note. Load-bearing claim: proofs have been a *proxy* for human understanding; once models emit proofs without that understanding, the proxy fails, so the field should give **motivated explanations** academic credit comparable to new proofs of open problems. Definition he draws: in a proof, definitions sit at the start; in a motivated explanation they sit in the middle, and it is allowed — often desirable — to start with an idea that is not quite right. Genre he names: Michael Nielsen's "discovery fiction." Named miss: there will never be Lean for motivated explanations, because human understanding is squishier than a kernel check. Exemplars: Princeton Companion Part IV (Gowers spent ~half his time for five years editing it, and says the Fields Medal is what made that feel allowed); Thurston's *On Proof and Progress in Mathematics*; Timothy Chow's "open exposition problem"; and the 2026 chain on **Erdős Problem 1196** — Liam Price + **GPT-5.4 Pro** produced a solution, Nat Sothanaphan and Jared Lichtman cleaned it into human-readable form, then an eight-author paper including Tao expanded the key idea into a cleaner account of primitive sets. Practical asks: treat small problems as mini-defenses whose deliverable is a talk; enumerate Hilbert-style *open exposition* problems; start journals in the Mathematical Discourse vein; count textbooks in hiring.
