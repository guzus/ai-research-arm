- [More questions about whether researchers can trust OpenAI with unpublished math](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49639408): jumped hard (521/535 → 754/690) and remains both the #1 AI story and the most-commented item on the entire front page. Andreas Thom / TU Dresden. Three-part Mathstodon thread addressed to Tristan Buckmaster: after OpenAI's August non-sofic-group announcement (one of the ten problems Astra settled on 2026-08-01), Thom emailed Mark Sellke and Sébastien Bubeck because he and a Dresden colleague had spent months discussing the expander matching problem and Kun–Thom extensions inside ChatGPT. He asked two things — whether those chats entered training data, and whether they were accessible to the solving process. Sellke's complete answer: "Regarding your conversations with ChatGPT: that did not happen." Thom now reads that as answering only direct access, and as "plainly dishonest" once OpenAI's Navier–Stokes statement admitted it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models." He opted out of "Improve the model for everyone" on June 29; the toggle is unauditable from outside and does not cover earlier chats or already-extracted derivatives. Remaining provenance / unpublished-math-trust thread — John D. Cook's same-evening Lean-formalization piece dropped overnight, so this is now the only NS-cluster item left on the front page.
- [Cognition launches SWE-2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49645443): kept climbing (324/133 → 374/160). First time the lab scaled RL into the multi-trillion-parameter regime: SWE-2 is post-trained from Kimi K3 (2.8T / 104B active) with an RL recipe that trains all reasoning-effort levels in one run. Self-reported table: FrontierCode 1.1 Main 50.0 vs Fable 5.1 50.9 / GPT-6 Astra 53.3 / Kimi K3 44.2 / SWE-1.7 42.0 (64% cheaper than Fable; a quarter of Astra); DeepSWE 1.1 73.0 vs Astra 74.1 / Sol 72.7; Terminal-Bench 2.1 92.8 (highest in the published table) vs Fable 91.4 / Astra 89.9; Terminal-Bench 4.0 27.3 vs Fable 55.8 / Astra 57.9 — long-horizon agentic work is where the gap still lives. On FrontierCode, SWE-2 medium beats SWE-1.7 with 58% fewer turns and 81% lower average cost; first real edit at a median of step 18 vs 48. Mean steps per run: 53 / 80 / 98 (medium / high / max) vs 127 for SWE-1.7. Method: cost-penalized reward \(R = S - \lambda_e C\) with \(\lambda_e\) matched to the local slope of the base model's Pareto frontier; length-weighted group baseline \(\sum R_i L_i / \sum L_i\); NVFP4/FP8 MoE inference plus a SpecForge-retrained draft model (15% longer accept lengths) and a prefill delayer (+10–20% TPM/TPS). Proprietary weights; Devin Desktop and CLI today, Web/Fusion rolling out; no per-token API. Remaining coding-agent / Pareto-RL thread — the Tokenstead companion card dropped overnight, and the OpenAI Agents API docs have now climbed past it as the other managed-harness story.
