- [Amazon vs. Perplexity – U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49704008): kept climbing (139/144 → 190/194) and is now the #3 AI story. *Amazon.com Services, LLC v. Perplexity AI, Inc.*, No. 26-1444. Opinion by Milan D. Smith, Jr., joined by Eric C. Tung and District Judge John Charles Hinderaker (D. Ariz., sitting by designation); argued 11 June 2026, filed 4 August 2026; N.D. Cal. D.C. No. 3:25-cv-09514-MMC (Judge Maxine M. Chesney). First circuit opinion on agentic AI under the CFAA. Facts: Perplexity's Comet browser includes an Assistant that, when a user activates it, navigates Amazon.com on the user's behalf and sends browser screenshots to Perplexity's servers for further instruction; Amazon forbade that use and won a preliminary injunction below on CFAA plus California CDAFA. Holding: vacate and remand. Amazon is unlikely to show that Perplexity "accessed" Amazon computers. On these facts Perplexity did not use a tool to access Amazon; "it was the user who 'accessed' Amazon's computers, with the help of Perplexity's AI agent, the 'Assistant.'" Receiving screenshots and instructing the Assistant "by themselves, do not mean that Perplexity has 'accessed' (gained entry) to Amazon's servers." The CFAA "contemplates access by a person," not a tool, "[h]owever advanced it is." CDAFA fails for the same reason even though it defines "access" more broadly. Equitable factors favor Perplexity: the remaining harms do not support an injunction, the balance of equities weighs against one, and enjoining conduct that likely does not violate either statute would not serve the public interest. Narrow and fact-specific — contract and tort claims are still live in the district court. - [Why don't machine learning research agents overfit?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49699648): kept climbing (92/53 → 113/64). Amazon Science write-up of Balasubramanian / Podkopaev / collaborators, "What Fits (Into Few Tokens) Doesn't Overfit" (arXiv 2606.11045). Why adaptive reuse of a held-out benchmark has produced surprisingly little overfitting: successful ML strategies are highly compressible. Two information bottlenecks on LLM research agents. **Output compression:** an explorer hill-climbs validation; a fresh reproducer with no memory gets only a short prompt plus the training data. **Input compression:** the explorer sees only one bit per submission. Across eight datasets, 32-token prompts were enough for the reproducer to match the explorer on the large majority of problems; one language-modeling strategy survived 16 tokens. Falsification: when explorers are induced to overfit validation, gains fail to reproduce — prompt compression separated honest from exploiting checkpoints at 100% sensitivity / 91% specificity. - [Backprop Alternative: Augmented Lagrangian Predictive Coding](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49701182): new since 23:12 — Jeffrey Seely and Julian Gould / Sakana AI, dated September 2026 (62/13, posted ~18:03 UTC). Paper [arXiv 2605.31022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31022); code `github.com/SakanaAI/pc-alm`. PC-ALM is a layer-local alternative to backpropagation: each layer runs a PI feedback controller (primal activations plus dual Lagrange multipliers) and talks only to its neighbors, so there is no global backward pass and no phase-locking. Standard predictive coding is energy-minimization / "heat flow" on a quadratic free energy; PC-ALM is primal-dual ascent on the augmented Lagrangian. In linear networks the duals converge to the exact backprop credit signals. Load-bearing empirical claim: residual MLPs up to 1000 layers on MNIST (width 32, ReLU, five epochs, Innocenti et al. 2026 setup, inference budget \(T=2L\)) stay within ~2 percentage points of backprop; the paper also matches BP across width-depth regimes up to depth 128, including the deep-narrow networks where PC underperforms, and narrows the PC–BP gap on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, ResNet-18, and Tiny ImageNet. Dynamics: PC's credit concentrates near the supervised loss and decays with depth; PC-ALM's spreads evenly ("ballistic" vs diffusive). Dual step size \(\alpha=0\) recovers PC (all eigenvalues real); raising \(\alpha\) introduces damped oscillations and then instability. Stated first: "to our knowledge, this is the first layer-local method shown to successfully train networks up to 1000 layers." Tradeoff they name: PC-ALM gives up Song et al.'s prospective-configuration sample-efficiency benefit at convergence. Motive is neuroscience / neuromorphic credit assignment, not a GPU training replacement. Newest learning-algorithm / "1000 layers, no backward pass" thread — the research-floor item under Khan.

Reward hacking is only closed by rigorous specification, and the intersection of domain experts and spec experts is "ludicrously small"; hardware already runs ~3:1 spec/validation-to-design, sometimes 5:1. Navier–Stokes is the best-case agentic setup — the theorem *is* the spec, Lean is an audited verifier, mathlib objects are battle-tested — and even that kernel has had soundness bugs LLMs have used. Human review does not scale and is itself reward-hackable (xz; UMN hypocrite commits). Three firm classes that can accept full autonomy: cheap-failure (interns, prototypes); narrow guarded tasks (call centers, controlled physical labor); and domains that already pay for spec-and-validation (chips, drug discovery). The first two are price-sensitive and do not need frontier reasoning; the third is secretive about IP. Swarm width, not reasoning depth, is the hypothesized driver of the math/security headlines (small open models reproducing the Mythos CVEs). Close: a datacenter full of brainlets is still bottlenecked by human orchestrators, so the blast radius is not only the frontier labs.
