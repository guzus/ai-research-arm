### Notable Discussions

- **Mea Culpa – Dark Hours**: The snapshot's top story and by far its largest
  AI thread (470 points, 216 comments). Terry Godier (maker of the Current
  RSS reader) launched a web app, Dark Hours, built "using Claude" — and
  discovered it was strikingly similar to an existing open-source project
  called DarkHours.app, down to reproducing a bug the original author had
  already fixed. His response: he killed his iOS-app plans, redirected his
  domain to the original project, and wrote this mea culpa vowing not to use
  AI "in this way to create any more web stuff." The discussion splits
  between readers crediting the accountability move and a harder critique —
  that this is what uninspected model-generated output looks like at scale,
  and that provenance tools (cf. the *Human vs. AI* diff-provenance post
  lower in the table) exist precisely because of this failure mode —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49231154)

- **How I use LLMs to learn complex topics**: A personal workflow writeup on
  using LLMs as a learning scaffold — decomposing unfamiliar subjects,
  generating checkable practice problems, and treating the model as a tutor
  rather than an oracle. The thread's useful counterpoint: LLMs as
  self-verification traps unless the learner cross-checks against primary
  sources, with several commenters arguing the technique works best for
  breadth ("map the territory") and worst for depth —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49234675)

- **Everything you do is being recorded**: The Atlantic's survey of
  countermeasures against AI-wearable surveillance — from detect-and-evade
  clothing to jammers and anti-wearable norms. 114 comments, mostly on the
  arms-race framing: recording is already too cheap to stop, so the realistic
  interventions are social and legal rather than technical. Included as
  AI-adjacent commentary (wearable-AI surveillance policy), not model news —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49230477)

- **John C. Lilly on solid state intelligence (1978)**: The 1978 essay on
  non-biological ("solid state") intelligence superseding humanity, resurfacing
  to 99 points. Climbed from 35 points at the 16:56 fetch. The comment mix is
  historical framing — what Lilly's pre-AGI argument got right about
  computation, what reads as era-specific mysticism — with a recurring note
  that it reads as an early draft of today's alignment/AGI discourse —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49231397)

- **OpenChamber: An Agentic Development Environment**: A new agentic coding
  environment hitting 64 points with healthy engagement (41 comments). The
  thread is a live field report on the agent-IDE category: how it compares to
  existing harnesses, multi-agent orchestration ergonomics, and where a
  newcomer slots into an already-crowded space —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49233448)

- **Human vs. AI – Diff-based line-level provenance**: Crossed the 10-point
  floor (from 3 points at 16:56 to 36 now). A GitHub project that attributes
  each line of a text diff to human vs. AI authorship under agentic editing —
  mechanical provenance for exactly the failure mode the *Mea Culpa* story
  illustrates. Thread is small (8 comments) but directly relevant to the
  day's dominant AI story —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49232300)
