### Notable Discussions

- **Mea Culpa – Dark Hours**: Still the front page's dominant AI thread
  (565 points, 257 comments), and still growing. Terry Godier's mea culpa over
  building a Claude-generated web app that turned out to reproduce an existing
  open-source project — down to a bug the original author had already fixed —
  keeps drawing the two camps the digest flagged yesterday: credit for the
  accountability move (domain redirected, iOS plans killed, a vow not to build
  web stuff this way again) vs. a harder read that uninspected model output is
  exactly the failure mode provenance tooling exists to catch —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49231154)

- **How I use LLMs to learn complex topics**: Jumped from 134 points at the
  20:53 fetch to 445 now, making it the snapshot's top *pure* LLM story. The
  workflow writeup — decompose unfamiliar subjects, generate checkable
  practice problems, treat the model as a tutor rather than an oracle —
  continues to draw the strongest counterpoint: LLMs as self-verification
  traps unless the learner cross-checks against primary sources, and the
  technique working best for breadth, worst for depth —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49234675)

- **Everything you do is being recorded**: The Atlantic's survey of
  countermeasures against AI-wearable surveillance grew 121→220 points and
  now carries 173 comments, mostly on the arms-race framing — recording is
  already too cheap to stop, so realistic interventions are social and legal,
  not technical. Included as AI-adjacent commentary (wearable-AI surveillance
  policy), not model news —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49230477)

- **John C. Lilly on solid state intelligence (1978)**: Climbed again,
  99→127 points. The 1978 essay on non-biological ("solid state")
  intelligence superseding humanity keeps surfacing as an early draft of
  today's alignment/AGI discourse; the thread mixes historical framing with
  what Lilly's pre-AGI argument got right about computation —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49231397)

- **OpenChamber: An Agentic Development Environment**: Grew 64→111 points
  with 57 comments. A live field report on the agent-IDE category — how it
  compares to existing harnesses, multi-agent orchestration ergonomics, and
  whether a newcomer can slot into an already-crowded space —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49233448)

- **The tragedy of the commons, AI edition**: Crossed the 10-point floor
  decisively, from 8 points at the 20:53 fetch to 78 now. The Economist piece
  treats frontier-lab compute as a shared-resource problem — finite high-end
  silicon and energy pitted against racing model developers — framing compute
  allocation as a commons governance question rather than a market one —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49235011)

- **Human vs. AI – Diff-based line-level provenance**: Grew 36→44 points.
  The GitHub project attributing each line of a text diff to human vs. AI
  authorship under agentic editing is mechanical provenance for exactly the
  failure mode the *Mea Culpa* story illustrates —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49232300)
