### Notable Discussions

- **Muse Glimmer – 30B-parameter open agentic model** (814 pts, the snapshot's
  top AI thread): Meta Research's open-weights model "optimized for always-on
  local agent workflows." The 455-comment discussion argues over the 30B
  agentic framing, realistic local/inference costs at that size, and whether a
  "always-on" local agent model actually dethrones API-hosted coding agents in
  practice — plus heavy skepticism about marketing the model against much
  larger frontier systems — [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49241679)

- **Docker Sandboxes – Disposable, isolated sandboxes for AI agents** (539):
  Docker's entry into agentic compute: ephemeral, throwaway execution
  environments purpose-built for agents. The 317-comment thread's core
  contention is whether per-task disposable sandboxes are the right substrate
  for agents or a regression from persistent VMs/containers; cost, cold-start,
  and multi-agent isolation dominate — [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49239751)

- **Mistral Patent for "Code implemented tool calls"** (148): A USPTO-gazetted
  Mistral patent on tool-calling implemented in code. The 127-comment
  discussion is a split take: some read it as a defensive patent in the
  agentic-tool-use rush; others flag how broad "implementing tool calls in
  code" reads, and what it means for open tool-calling implementations —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49243397)

- **Kinney Drugs pulls back AI phone assistant** (102): A pharmacy chain's
  AI phone assistant logged "hundreds of customer complaints" and was pulled.
  The 114-comment thread treats it as another data point on the AI-voice-agent
  failure mode: fine for scripted intake, brittle the moment the caller's need
  leaves the script — and the reputation cost of shipping it untested —
  [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49244569)

- **Zuckerberg attacks 'closed' AI rivals as Meta returns to open models**
  (102): Meta re-committing to open-weights (Muse line) while hitting OpenAI
  and Google as "closed." The 113-comment thread splits on whether this is
  strategy (regulatory framing) or substance, given Meta's own track record of
  restricting its largest models — [link](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49243880)
