- [Getting 50 GB/S Back from the Apple Neural Engine](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49636479): new since 22:20 — Eileen Yoon / eiln (107/21, posted 2026-09-10 ~00:16 UTC; dated 10 August 2026), the DMA-throughput companion to yesterday's M1 ANE retrospective (`ane.html`), which has now left the front page. RTL performance erratum on the M3 Neural Engine: DRAM weight-streaming collapses from a nominal 45–60 GB/s to 17–19 GB/s whenever the total weight size is an integer multiple of 1 MiB — currently 7 of ANEMLL's 15 models. Discovery was a single-token decode sweep \(X[1,D] \times W[D,N] = Y[1,N]\) at \(N=4096\): \(D=1536\) ran nearly 3× faster than Llama 3.2's default \(D=2048\); on an M3 Air, 40-run median, \(D=2048\) was 16.93 GB/s vs 44.5 GB/s at \(D=2016\). An FFT of throughput against \(D\) showed a dip-harmonic at wavelength 2048; every multiple of 2048 sits on the same 17–19 GB/s floor and recovers ~256 lines away. Not a correctness bug — KernelDMA still completes — but a credit-starved issue regime. Core-count and address-scramble ablations ruled out bank aliasing (latency flat from 1–16 cores; random IOVA scramble recovered ~1 GB/s, not the missing ~28–43). Load-bearing hardware claim: at \(N=4096\), every \(D += 2048\) adds 1 MiB/core (\(256\) kernels × \(4\) KiB); 1 MiB ÷ 64 B/line = `0x4000` lines, which wraps a 14-bit prefetch ring sized at one 16 KiB Apple-Silicon page, with a 256-line lookahead. Software fix: do not request 1 MiB KernelDMA transfers — split into two 512 KiB tasks (or pad ±8 KiB). Control: splitting a transfer that was already off-notch gives no speedup; splitting a 1 MiB-multiple gives 2.6× (one `0x4000`-line task 17.25 GB/s → two `0x2000` 45.52 GB/s). Chunked 512k splits climb to ~60 GB/s at larger \(D\) (8 MiB: 19.1 → 60.5 GB/s, 3.16×). Compiler emits two partial reductions so the graph cannot fuse them back. LLM numbers: Llama 3.2 1B 10.0 → 24.3 tok/s (DRAM 24.7 → 60.0 GB/s); Qwen3-8B 1.36 → 2.97 tok/s (22.4 → 48.7 GB/s). Newest ML-hardware / "the notch is a prefetch wrap" thread — the serving-side sequel to yesterday's CNN-dataflow retrospective.
