Load-bearing stamps from the post: they do **not** endorse testing advanced mathematical problems on proprietary models and ask labs to stop; human understanding of mathematics remains "of paramount importance." Three principles: release significant results responsibly and as soon as possible; labs that drop output without immediate human understanding must fund the understanding that follows; that understanding must stay organic and community-led, not lab-directed. Two tracks: (2.A) a mathematician who fully understands the argument follows ordinary preprint / journal / seminar norms; (2.B) not-yet-understood output gets a cleaned traditional write-up, literature-scouring citations, deposit in a lab-independent repository with a persistent id, the model name / prompts / summarized chain of thought / time / estimated cost, formalization artifacts (`formalization.yaml`, challenge file) where possible, and a companion document on how many comparable problems the model tried and failed. Step II: labs fund conferences, workshops, postdocs, or expository books through existing nonprofit grant processes, without that funding conferring legitimacy on the labs' practice. Access close: proprietary internal models risk a two-tier field; publicly available models should be granted "broad, equitable access."
