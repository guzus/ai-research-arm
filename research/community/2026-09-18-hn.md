- [Infinite-Parameter LLMs: Generating and Adapting Weights from Live Data](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49743483): kept climbing (90/26 → 126/38) and is now the #5 AI story. Jinli Hu, Ross M. Clarke, Yichuan Zhang, and José Miguel Hernández-Lobato, [arXiv 2609.18842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18842) (submitted 16 September 2026 15:49 UTC; posted ~16:55 UTC yesterday). Architecture proposal, not a trained frontier checkpoint. Bind they name: MoE rode scaling laws by activating a slice of a **static** pretrained bank; live facts and corrections still go in the prompt and die with the request. **Infinite-Parameter LLM**: a compact hypernetwork turns run-time data into a low-rank modulation of a shared base, so feed-forward weights are *compiled* from the session rather than stored. Prior weight generators read the context once and freeze; they keep a **Bayesian belief over the generator's latent code** and re-derive the effective weight as the session proceeds. Stored footprint stays fixed; the weights the model can compile are "effectively infinite." Claimed properties vs putting the same material in the prompt: amortized compute, freed context, persistence across turns, and (they say) better generalisation. Evaluation protocol they specify is exactly that comparison against in-context learning and retrieval. Remaining architecture / "write the session into the weights" thread — now sitting over Hacktron after Skillsync / Canto left.
