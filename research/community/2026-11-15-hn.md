- Generative AI energy / non-English text: Still on the page. 198 points / 104 comments (was 168 / 94). news.umich.edu/generative-ai-uses-more-energy-on-non-english-text/ (Kate McAlpine / U-M Engineering, published 2026-11-05; university write-up of Deng / Mihalcea et al., EMNLP 2026, DOI 10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.2113; authors' measurements, not an independent replication). Three open-weight models — Alibaba Qwen 3, Google Gemma 3, Meta Llama 3.1 — answering Belebele reading-comprehension items in 122 languages, energy via Michigan's ML.Energy framework; also grade-school math and open-ended prompts translated into seven other languages with Meta NLLB. Headline range: 1.4× to 180× the energy of an equivalent English prompt for non-English generation; most Western European languages ≤2×; Hindi 7.9× and Bengali 15.3×. Authors estimate ~5.2 billion people (Ethnologue) face more than 2× energy to generate in their first language vs English. Mechanism they name: more tokens per script (Devanagari / Bengali characters combining into large unique sets), which fills memory, shrinks batch size, and raises energy per token; companies that price by token also charge more. English used the least energy on average; the gap persisted at larger sizes and across processor types. Recommendations on that page: language-specific models or a reliable translate-to-English pipeline, plus crowdsourced native-speaker training data. First author Naihao Deng; corresponding Rada Mihalcea. Partial NSF / DARPA funding. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50441444
