- Positional embeddings in modern LLMs: New. 74 points / 0 comments. magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-positional-embeddings (Sebastian Raschka, 2026-11-15; tutorial with a small PyTorch RoPE walkthrough and the author's LLM Architecture Gallery counts, not a paper or independent eval). Walks GPT-2's learned absolute table (added before the first block; GPT-2 has 1,024 learned position vectors; GPT-2 Medium's positional table is 0.30% of weights at 1,024 tokens and would be >22% at a 100k context) vs the original transformer's fixed sinusoids vs RoPE (Su et al. 2021 RoFormer; rotates queries and keys, not values). Modern examples on that page: MiniMax-M3 injects RoPE inside attention after QK-Norm and uses partial RoPE — 64 rotary dimensions of each 128-dimensional head; for a 1M-token context that halves the precomputed cosine/sine tables, saving 0.134 GB (128 MiB) in BF16 or 0.038 GB (36 MiB) in NVFP4 including block scales (KV cache still stores full keys/values). The Fractional Rotation, Full Potential? paper is cited for 1B–8B runs where rotating ~10% of each head's features often matched full-RoPE training loss; Raschka's take is that partial RoPE is "hardly worth it." SmolLM3 (3B) applies RoPE in three of every four layers across 36 blocks; the authors of that model said the skip improved long-context performance. Thinky's Inkling adds a T5-style learned relative position bias to sliding-window and global attention scores before softmax. Gallery trend he reports: RoPE in every softmax attention layer is still the most common choice, but its share fell from 2025 to 2026 and again in H2 2026; among RoPE models, partial RoPE went from a 2025 minority to a majority in both 2026 groups. NoPE-only gallery entries were recurrent hybrids; "Other" is xLSTM 7B (2025, no softmax attention) and Inkling (2026). He expects RoPE to remain predominant into 2027. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50506376
- Qwen conversation state-space atlas: New. 49 points / 15 comments. operator0x.com/field-notes/qwen-conversation-state-space/ (Operator_0x, 2026-11-13; author's visualization of frozen Qwen3-1.7B residual states, not an independent eval or a claim about other models). Residual stream is 2,048-d; input embedding table is 151,936 vocabulary rows. Dataset: 100 topics × 3 question styles × 3 examples = 900 Q&A per language across English, Chinese, German, and Croatian, plus a separate 90-example Python coding set; thinking off, greedy decoding, 256-token answer cap. Capture: exact question + two newlines + exact answer as raw text; final RMSNorm token vectors L2-normalized; conversation averages from those rows. English 3D projection: a best-fitting plane held ~99.3% of English token-state variation in that view. Full-space checks on 264,571 final token states: the displayed 3D sheet held 99.7% of the *visible* 3D variation but the three Global PCA axes captured 17.6% of original 2,048-d variation (a uniform split across 2,048 directions would be ~0.15%; the observed share is ~120× that); the best 2D plane in the full space captured 28.7% (28.4% on held-out topics); 684 PCA directions were needed for 90%. Conversation PCA (fit on the 3,690 conversation averages, not the vocab table) captured 80.1% of average-vector variation vs 42.4% for the three Global PCA directions. Chinese states sat in a neighboring, visibly offset region; German and Croatian overlapped English's Latin-script neighborhood; the 90 coding examples sat in that same broad neighborhood, though a distance comparison of projected conversation averages put coding closer to Croatian and German than to English. Method notes on that page: not real multi-turn chats; German/Croatian catalogues were coordinator-translated without independent native-speaker certification; one German and ten Croatian answers hit the token limit and were kept; coding outputs were not executed; Global PCA fitting data is ~98% vocabulary embeddings. - https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50481321
