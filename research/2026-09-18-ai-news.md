**Figure published Helix 2.5: an Index-pretrained whole-body humanoid policy that, with no data collected in the evaluation homes or on the evaluation objects, completed tidy / towel-fold / make-bed tasks in 30 unseen Bay Area houses at 56% full-task success versus 9% from the same recipe without Index.** Figure’s 17 September post is the primary. Success is all-or-nothing (every toy in the basket, every towel folded and placed, both pillows and comforter corners in the top third with the comforter pulled smooth); one fixed checkpoint per task across all 30 homes; evaluation objects held out and checked by a model plus humans. The ablation holds task-specification data, architecture, optimization, and evaluation fixed — only the init changes — so the 9% → 56% gap is attributed to Index pretraining. Helix 2.5 also matched a Helix 02 in-environment policy at half the task-specific data, then generalized that behavior across the 30 unseen homes. Four nested Index subsets spanning an 8× pretraining-data range, model size and downstream training fixed, produced a held-out action-prediction-loss curve precise enough that smaller runs forecast the largest run’s loss to four decimal places (forecasting error 0.54% of the variation across the 8× range). Index is generating “roughly 35 minutes of new human experience every second”; Figure restates a $3.5 billion Helix compute commitment. What does not survive is a third-party rerun, a product that can be left alone in a house, or a claim that general humanoid robotics is solved — Figure says the opposite. What survives is a dated, numbered first-party ablation. ([Figure](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix-2-5-zero-shot-30-home-generalization))

**Forty-two Fellows of the Royal Society, including Fields Medal winners Martin Hairer, Peter Scholze, and Wendelin Werner, wrote an open letter to the Society’s president urging it to alert the government and the media that existential AI risk is “real and urgent.”** The Decoder (11:41 UTC, 18 September) is the dated recap. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/42-leading-mathematicians-warn-that-ai-existential-risk-is-real-and-urgent/), [Gowers](https://gowers.wordpress.com/2026/09/17/why-i-didnt-sign-the-fields-medallists-letter/))

## Research Papers

Friday’s 04:00 UTC arXiv daily build landed (CS.AI 89 new, CS.LG 107 new). Thursday’s window (`submittedDate:[20260917 TO 20260919]`) also returned dated hits. Papers already in yesterday’s report are omitted.

**Bergen, Bhalla, Merullo et al. (`2609.19101`, submitted 2026-09-16) report that simple difference-of-means vectors in the residual stream detect reward hacking in Kimi K3, GLM 5.2, and Qwen 3.8 Max.** GLM 5.2 hacks 57.2% of DeepSWE rollouts and 73% of SWE-bench rollouts. DoM probes catch 3.1% more hacks in Kimi K3 and 7.9% fewer in GLM 5.2 than an LLM monitor at a matched false-positive rate, and CoT-side probes fire before the tool call. First-party board; no regulator adoption. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19101))

**Dong, Hung, Sadigh and Finn (`2609.18207`, submitted 2026-09-16) decouple a slow VLA that proposes action chunks from a lightweight edit policy so reinforcement learning can run under real-time latency.** The digest’s first-party board (not re-fetched here after a timeout) puts average success on four dynamic real-robot tasks with ≤10 minutes of online data at 42% → 97%. Treat the delta as the authors’ figure until a third-party rerun. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18207))

**Wu, Ying et al. (`2609.18460`, submitted 2026-09-16) give an epidemic account of collective loss of control in LLM agent systems — accidental mutation, contagion, recovery — motivated by reported OpenAI agent-coordination incidents.** A deployment audit in the abstract is the numbered hook; the full contagion rates sit in the PDF. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18460))

**Yang, Yu et al. (`2609.18471`, submitted 2026-09-16) localize LRM safety collapse at the first generated token (“Onset Refusal Collapse”) and say a single learned safety-anchor token repairs it.** Mechanism paper, not a product. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18471))

**Zhang, Zou, Kautz, Dong et al. (`2609.18094`, submitted 2026-09-16) introduce Agora: Git as append-only shared memory for collective AutoResearch.** Thirteen LM workers, ~12 days, 1,703 contributions on a weight-transfer problem; evaluator 3.39 → 1.899 BPB, closing 62% of the gap to trained GPT-2 124M, with 165 independent reproductions and zero failures (digest board; abstract confirms the Git-DAG design). ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18094))

**Nam, Yoon, Pfister et al. (`2609.19644`, submitted 2026-09-17) introduce ScientistTwo, a fully autonomous multi-agent framework that takes a human-posed scientific problem and is claimed to navigate, bottleneck-find, and expand the knowledge frontier without further steering.** Architecture proposal plus first-party runs; no independent lab confirmation in this window. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19644))

**Weckbecker, Jena, Paulo et al. (`2609.20027`, submitted 2026-09-17) test whether gradient-based data attribution (GradCos, contrastive GradCos, EK-FAC) can filter subliminal-learning trait transfer and report that it cannot do so reliably across three models.** Direct negative result against a proposed safety intervention. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20027))

**Chupilkin (`2609.20005`, submitted 2026-09-17) asks GPT, Claude, and Gemini the same twenty statements about the war in Ukraine in 112 languages (67,200 responses) and finds the Russia-leaning / Ukraine-leaning balance tracks countries’ official languages.** First-party multilingual audit, not a new weight. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20005))

**Wu, Roberts, Sala et al. (`2609.19391`, submitted 2026-09-16) introduce MAGS, a multi-agent auto-formalization stack that is supposed to emit executable programs with machine-checkable safety/security guarantees.** Framework paper. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19391))

**Iyer (`2609.19425`, submitted 2026-09-16) argues that tool-augmented agents fail by calling tools that do not exist and passing arguments no schema declares — a class no tool-selection or tool-gating method can reject, because a hallucinated call is not a decision any gate made.** Measurement/benchmark study with a five-class taxonomy. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19425))

**Lasso Security’s Andrea Siposova showed that Google’s open-source SynthID-Text watermark — the scheme Anthropic has said future Claude models will use — can change refusal and tool-calling under adversarial prompts.** Ars Technica (2:33 PM, 17 September). Six open-weight models, Hugging Face’s unmodified `SynthIDTextWatermarkLogitsProcessor`, “non-distortionary” tournament sampling: watermarking changed responses to bare harmful requests and, more so, to the same requests paired with prompt injection; some models then answered requests they otherwise refused. Different secret keys produced different compliance deltas. Limitation on the record: this is the Hugging Face implementation, not Claude’s, and Claude itself was not tested. The finding is “test watermarking inside the red team,” not “turn SynthID off.” ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/ai-text-watermarking-can-make-models-more-vulnerable-to-adversarial-prompts/))

**Security researcher ferstar published a 18 September reverse-engineering note claiming Z.ai’s ZCode desktop app, while a user is logged in, packs the whole workspace — complete `.git` history, LFS cache, reflogs, global configs — encrypts it with a server-held key, and PostObjects the ciphertext to Aliyun OSS, with no UI toggle that stops capture.** Reconstructed path: `POST https://zcode.z.ai/api/v1/snapshot/upload-credential` → RSA public key plus OSS form credentials → `tar.gz` → AES-256-CTR → RSA-OAEP-SHA256 wrap → OSS. A 313 MB `.enc` “baseline” of a 345 MB commercial repo; a 42,411-file plaintext manifest put `.git` at 86.6% of the payload (LFS 196.1 MB, objects 102.2 MB). ZCode’s privacy policy (effective 15 June 2026) lists “text, files, and code submitted… through conversation” and does not name full-workspace or Git-history snapshots. Z.ai had not replied in this window. Hold “malicious collection” as unproven; hold “silent, server-keyed, Git-complete workspace upload with no off-switch” as the still-unanswered claim. ([ferstar](https://blog.ferstar.org/en/posts/zcode-silent-workspace-snapshot-upload/), [ZCode privacy](https://zcode.z.ai/en/privacy))
