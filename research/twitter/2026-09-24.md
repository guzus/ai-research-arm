  <p class="twitter-story-lead">Detailed provisions of the Sanders–Casar bill put a number on its central mechanism: an immediate pause on training and deploying "advanced AI," defined as any system trained with at least 10^25 operations, until a new Department of Artificial Intelligence writes safety rules and model-review processes. The prohibited "superintelligence precursor" list includes unauthorized hacking.</p>

    <div class="twitter-story-body">
      <p><strong>Evidence:</strong> The provision breakdown at 22:50 UTC is the first to carry the compute threshold: pause training and deployment of "advanced AI," defined as systems trained with at least 10^25 operations, until the new department stands up safety rules and model-review processes. It also prohibits "superintelligence precursor" capabilities — automating AI research, resisting shutdown, self-modifying, evading human oversight, unauthorized hacking, or assisting with nuclear, chemical or biological weapons — gives the department power to force flagged models offline and requires confirmed superintelligence to be destroyed immediately, and requires federal charters granting government access to developers' systems, processes, staff and physical infrastructure (<a href="https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2102893393189949910">@rohanpaul_ai</a>). Penalties are asset and IP forfeiture for entities and up to 20 years for individuals.</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The number is what converts the bill from a headline into a testable object, and the test it fails is scope. 10^25 operations is roughly the GPT-4-era training scale and is the same figure the EU AI Act uses as its systemic-risk presumption — but the EU uses it to trigger obligations, while this bill uses it to trigger a halt. Applied that way it does not pause frontier development; it pauses essentially all of it, including models already deployed, for an indefinite period bounded only by how fast a department that does not exist can write rules. The counter-hypothesis that this is a messaging bill therefore survives intact: no Republican co-sponsor has surfaced, and the threshold choice is not one a drafter picks when trying to pass something. What it does establish is a first concrete anchor — every future US compute-threshold proposal will now be read against 10^25.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> Yesterday's 16:00 cycle covered the bill's introduction, the corporate death penalty and the collision with the White House directive renaming the field "super intelligence." The threshold and the precursor list are new. One item on that list is worth holding next to this cycle's lead story: the bill would prohibit systems capable of unauthorized hacking, filed on the same day a national government said an AI agent reached its files.</p>
