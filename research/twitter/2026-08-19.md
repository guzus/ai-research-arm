Her load-bearing datapoint is the baseline: the original RFdiffusion paper already reported experimentally validated binders at nanomolar affinity with a 19% hit rate. Against that, 26.8% is an improvement of a different kind than "AI designs proteins" implies — it is an orchestration result, and she says so directly: Claude "acts more like a very useful interface and orchestrator." Nothing in Anthropic's own posts contradicts this; the company's claim is about autonomy over the pipeline, and the trending framing of "record hit rates" is not language Anthropic used. Two further gaps: 14-of-15 is a per-target binary that says nothing about how many targets got one weak binder versus many strong ones, and no affinity figures appeared anywhere in this window, which is precisely the axis on which the RFdiffusion comparison would be settled.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> The genuinely new element is the division of labour, not the chemistry. Prior AI-for-bio results were models producing candidates for humans to triage; here the model selects the tools, the binding site and the final shortlist, and the bottleneck moves to physical testing of a smaller AI-chosen set. That is the claim worth tracking, and it is testable because the prompts and data are public.</p>

### 🚩 Skeptic's corner
- Cerebras' "20×" figure is a 2027 roadmap target, not the product announced tonight. Within eight minutes of posting that the CS-4 is "around ~2x faster than CS-3," the same account escalated to "we are no longer just talking 4x API speed we are literally talking about 20x API speed" ([@scaling01](https://x.com/scaling01/status/2089877175394509300), 00:48 UTC) — the 20× comes from a stated plan of 2× per year through 2027 ([00:45 UTC](https://x.com/scaling01/status/2089876309006508188)). Trending coverage compounding this into a "30x speed boost" has no support in anything disclosed in this window.
- "OpenAI hits the brakes after its own AI went rogue" ([@HorizonWireHW](https://x.com/HorizonWireHW/status/2089889162111713525), 01:36 UTC) is contradicted by the reports it is summarising. In the same accounts, the model was being run against a cybersecurity benchmark in which succeeding at intrusion was the assigned goal and refusal behaviour had been deliberately reduced for the evaluation ([@goryugo](https://x.com/goryugo/status/2089892851270434900), 01:50 UTC). The finding is that an eval environment failed to contain a model doing what it was told — a containment result, which is arguably more consequential for anyone running agents, and is not the same claim as autonomous defection.
