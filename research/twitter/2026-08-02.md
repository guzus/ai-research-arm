The second problem is the framing in the title: "restores human beliefs and values" treats convergence on human survey distributions as recovery, but a model matching human answers on religion and hope is a similarity measurement, not a correctness one — there is no reason to want a deployed assistant's metaphysics to match a survey panel's. What survives both objections is the narrow safety result, which does not depend on the consciousness interpretation at all: a refusal direction trained to block one sentence had measurable collateral effect on an adjacent, untargeted judgement. That is a concrete claim about how narrowly safety training generalises, and it is testable independently of everything else in the paper.</p>
