First, this is a repitch — Musk floated the same structure in July, and it is being covered as new. Second, the analogy fails at the load-bearing joint: academic peer review works because the reviewer has no stake in whether the work ships, and competitors grading each other's frontier models violates exactly that. Third, a near-identical arrangement is claimed to be already running — that the Commerce Department's CAISI has received pre-deployment access to unreleased models from DeepMind, Microsoft, xAI, OpenAI and Anthropic since May, which would put xAI inside a government-run version of the idea it is pitching as new. That last claim is an argument made by a low-reach account and is not independently corroborated in this window; weigh it accordingly. The objection that survives on the reported facts alone is the incentive one: advance access to rivals' unreleased models is worth the most to the lab furthest from the frontier, and by David Sacks's own claim at this same summit, Anthropic and OpenAI have lab capability "about two generations ahead of everyone else." Musk pre-empting the distillation objection with "it would show up in the logs" is a tell that he knows it is the load-bearing one — and log-based detection of distillation is asserted, not demonstrated.</p>
