# Twitter/X AI Pulse — 2026-10-18

## 04:00 UTC

**Cycle summary**: Three unannounced OpenAI API model IDs, aurora-red/green/blue-alpha, were spotted by two separate watchers. SemiAnalysis reports that an NVIDIA vLLM decode optimization speeds up Kimi K3 (2.8T) by 1.68×, to 423 tok/s per user.

### Quick hits
- **Three unannounced model IDs, `aurora-red-alpha`, `aurora-green-alpha` and `aurora-blue-alpha`, have appeared in the OpenAI API model list.** @scaling01 and @AndrewCurran_ reported them independently, about 15 minutes apart. OpenAI has not commented, and nothing is known about their capabilities. @scaling01 points out that an earlier OpenRouter stealth model called "aurora-alpha" was likely GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark, so these could be fast coding variants. That is an inference, not a confirmation. [@AndrewCurran_](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2111656899338973391) · [@scaling01](https://x.com/scaling01/status/2111653358322729401)
- **SemiAnalysis: an upstream vLLM optimization by NVIDIA lifts Kimi K3 (2.8T parameters) to 423 tok/s per user, 1.68× faster on the same hardware.** The work uses decode-specialized Cute DSL kernels written by NVIDIA's CentML team, and SemiAnalysis says DeepSeek V4.1 support is next. The speedup is single-source and the hardware isn't named. PyTorch's @marksaroufim pushed back on the "12,000 LoC Megakernel" framing, describing it as a well-written CUDA-graph engine with persistent kernels and PDL. He notes the author never called it a megakernel. [@SemiAnalysis_](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2111627094878790023) · [@marksaroufim](https://x.com/marksaroufim/status/2111649118380777868)

### Watch list (next 24h)
- Whether the aurora-*-alpha IDs show up in OpenAI docs, release notes or as an OpenRouter stealth model, and whether early API testers report what they are.
- A merged vLLM PR or NVIDIA benchmark naming the hardware behind the 423 tok/s Kimi K3 figure.
