      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The level is the weakest part of the finding and the framing is why. "Near where you live" is a proximity question, and proximity questions poll badly for essentially every industrial facility — landfills, prisons, transmission lines, wind farms. 75% opposing a data center next door is not 75% opposing data centers, and nothing in the thread asks the abstract question, so the headline number cannot be quoted as a verdict on the buildout. A single pollster's series also carries its own house effects and panel drift, and Heatmap is an energy-and-climate outlet whose editorial interest runs toward this question being salient. What survives all of that is the part the objection cannot touch: the wording is held constant across three waves by the outlet's own account, so the delta — 42% → a bare majority → 75% — is the measurement, and NIMBY framing that was stable a year ago does not produce a 33-point move on its own. The strongest remaining doubt is not that the shift is fake but that it is a salience effect: opposition may be rising because the question is now familiar rather than because attitudes hardened, and only a second pollster settles that.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> This brief carried the National Republican Senatorial Committee memo at 03:00 UTC yesterday on one reporter's characterisation of a private document, with the watch item set as whether a named outlet would publish it. It still has not. The scale framing is the sharper corrective: the same report sets Japan's tens of trillions of won against the hundreds of trillions going into Yongin and Honam, roughly a tenth, and explicitly calls it "a relatively smaller-scale production base" and "an additional overseas manufacturing site." So the financial story is marginal. What is not marginal is positional, and it is the reason this is worth carrying: the report notes the United States has been pressing the Korean government and Korean companies to build memory fabs on US soil, and that a Japanese investment would intensify those demands — plus domestic Korean political sensitivity about siting strategically important semiconductor capacity in Japan specifically. Those are constraints no capex figure resolves, and they are why a tenth-scale fab is a bigger decision than its cost.</p>

A company retiring that share of its cash while opening a new overseas manufacturing front is not obviously contradicting itself, but it is a claim that the capacity plan is already funded, and this report is the first thing that tests it.</p>
