  <h3 class="twitter-story-title">Anthropic says an open-weights model has crossed the cyber-offense threshold: GLM-5.3 built working browser exploits in 50 of 410 attempts, against Mythos Preview's 56</h3>
  <p class="twitter-story-lead">Anthropic published research finding that Z.ai's freely downloadable GLM-5.3 approaches its own frontier model on exploit development while shipping without meaningful safeguards. On ExploitBench, GLM-5.3 produced working browser exploits in 50 of 410 attempts versus Claude Mythos Preview's 56, and achieved full control-flow hijacks in 4% of trials against Mythos Preview's 6% — where Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 succeed at neither. In a separate controlled experiment, researchers used GLM-5.3 to find previously unknown browser vulnerabilities and chain them into a webpage that read files off the test machine.</p>

  <h3 class="twitter-story-title">The "Super Intelligence" order is signed — and it is narrower than yesterday's reporting: terminology only, with existing contracts and regulations exempt</h3>
  <p class="twitter-story-lead">Trump signed the executive order "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," directing the executive branch to use "Super Intelligence (SI)" instead of "artificial intelligence" in official communications, websites, reports and policy documents. The operative scope is narrower than reported yesterday: existing regulations, contracts and historical documents explicitly do not have to be rewritten, and SI continues to mean exactly what AI already means in federal law. The one forward hook is a 60-day deadline for the White House science office to propose a new official definition and any statutory changes it requires.</p>

Factually, OpenAI's own launch post says Ultrafast is available today for GPT-6 <em>Astra</em>, with GPT-6.1 Sol "coming soon" — so the product SemiAnalysis names as running on GPUs may not be generally available yet, and whatever was probed is at most a preview path. Inferentially, "not on Cerebras today" is a much weaker statement than "Cerebras cannot do this," and SemiAnalysis itself poses the future as a question rather than answering it; a launch-week serving stack is the least likely moment to see a novel accelerator carrying production traffic. The contrast that makes it interesting is timing rather than judgement: earlier the same day General Compute announced it was deploying "the world's fastest inference" with Cerebras, and Cerebras itself was promoting ~1,500 tokens/sec on a Qwen model. The gap between that positioning and the fastest tier at the largest lab running on GPUs is the actual story, and it is a question about design wins, not about the silicon's capability.</p>
