Fable 5's best trajectory ran 8.7 days and reached 2,726 steps; the naive baseline is 3,290 and the standing human record 2,600, giving 81.7% gap closure. The rest of the field: Opus 5 at 2,920, Kimi K3 at 2,930, GPT-5.6 Sol at 3,042. The behavioural finding is the more interesting half. Most models converged on similar optimizer directions, many already present in the literature; what separated the strong from the weak was experimental method — running multiple seeds, re-ablating changes already accepted, retesting previously failed ideas after the recipe changed, and probing interaction effects between two individually-useless modifications. Kimi K3 went further and built its own tooling inside the environment: an optimizer-variant generator, an experiment launcher, and loss-curve analysis utilities.</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The headline number is the weakest part of the study, and the study says so. The strongest counter-hypothesis is that "81.7% of the human gap" measures saturation on one narrow, heavily-optimised speedrun rather than research capability — and the report's own verdict supports that reading: "we were again surprised by the lack of novelty. The models clearly understand the objects they manipulate at a deep level, and yet very few genuinely new ideas emerge," quoted independently by <a href="https://x.com/scaling01/status/2088737519844159746">@scaling01</a> (301 likes). A system that closes 81.7% of a gap by grinding known techniques with better discipline is a strong engineer and an unproven scientist, and the nanoGPT speedrun is precisely the task where the prior literature is densest and the search space best-mapped. Compute asymmetry compounds it: an 8.7-day continuous trajectory on 8×H200s is not a like-for-like comparison against a human record set under different constraints, so the percentage flatters the model. The falsification check found no independent replication and no cross-task generalisation in this window.</p>
