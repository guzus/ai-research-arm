      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> A spec reduction on a flagship accelerator is normally a demand-weakness signal, and that is the obvious counter-hypothesis: NVIDIA cutting memory per part because it expects to sell fewer parts. The brokerage argues the inverse and the arithmetic supports it — HBM supply growth has failed to keep pace with TSMC's front-end and back-end expansion, so the despec is a way to produce more accelerators from a fixed pool of HBM bits, and the freed 12Hi-to-8Hi headroom is expected to convert into additional units rather than idle capacity. The tell that discriminates between the two readings is that DRAM makers cannot flex HBM capacity upward because commodity DRAM is in extreme shortage and consumer set makers are already suffering production disruptions — a demand-driven despec would not be accompanied by a supply squeeze that severe everywhere else. The genuine weakness in this story is provenance, not logic: the sell-out rests on one trade outlet's industry sources, and "sold out" is a claim no party has an incentive to deny.</p>
