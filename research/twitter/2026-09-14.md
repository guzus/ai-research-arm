The decisive detail is revocability: the customer-managed-logs option exists but can be withdrawn, so the objection is not about today's policy but about the provider's ability to change it later, which is why Palantir's stated bar is the word <em>irrevocable</em>. @rohanpaul_ai adds the competitive comparison that gives the item its commercial edge: OpenAI's standard API also retains inputs and outputs in abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days, but its enterprise posture is described as more permissive — so the differentiator is contract terms, not retention windows.</p>

What does not survive is the "this says nothing about Anthropic specifically" version — a revocable opt-out is a named, fixable deficiency in Anthropic's terms, not a generic procurement posture. The sharpest counter to the framing being pushed by the loudest amplifiers is that contract friction is not customer loss: "it shows how contracts can outrank benchmark leadership" is the right lesson, "large enterprise customers no longer use the best model" is an overstatement of what was reported.</p>
