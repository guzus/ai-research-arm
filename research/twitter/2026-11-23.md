The proof of the Riemann Hypothesis makes the atomic bomb look like a “homework problem,” and it was done with an “alien mind” we do not understand. o3’s December 2024 AIME results were an earlier “the world is changing” moment. Since then, closing non-intelligence gaps (domain knowledge, context, plumbing) has left coding “nearly solved,” produced “extremely powerful cyber capabilities,” and made agents general assistants. The remaining barriers to superhuman performance are domain expertise, engineering, and experimentation, not intelligence. AI research is easier than the Riemann Hypothesis, so models will be able to automate it and accelerate biology, weapons, and economics faster than humans can understand or monitor. His prescription: “It is hard to imagine a world where humanity can maintain control over fully unconstrained recursive self improvement.” Progress on safety, alignment, and security is necessary but not sufficient.       <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The strongest counter-reading is that this is positioning, not policy. The excerpts contain no mechanism, no metric, no Meta commitment, and no word on Meta’s own open-weight releases. That contrasts with Pachocki’s essay yesterday, which at least proposed a cross-lab perplexity cap. The essay also builds on a premise that is still contested. The “proof of the Riemann Hypothesis” is the Alpöge Lean certificate: machine-checked, but with no human-readable paper. The Association for Human Mathematics and Daniel Litt, in a thread Andrew Curran retweeted this cycle, have argued it is being over-read. The step from “hard competition math” to “intelligence is no longer the bottleneck everywhere” is an analogy, not evidence. No Meta statement, reply, or external fact in this snapshot contradicts the quoted wording. The essay survives as an accurate report of what Wang said, not as support for its capability claims.</p>
