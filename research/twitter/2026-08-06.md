      <p><strong>Evidence:</strong> The structural point is that US datacenter siting fights have run through county boards and city councils, and New York is the first state to preempt that with a blanket instrument. EO 62 is a pause-and-study: it holds discretionary DEC permits at 50MW and above while the state completes a generic environmental review, and directs the Department of Public Service to finish its large-load interconnection study. The Responsible Data Center Development Act would bite at 20MW rather than 50, and writes durable rules into statute: dedicated electric and water rate classes above 20MW, public hearings before new permits, efficiency targets above 5MW, a host community benefit program and prevailing wage requirements. SemiAnalysis expects a veto with the executive order held as leverage while a revised bill is negotiated, which leaves the operative constraint as something the Governor can withdraw unilaterally (<a href="https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2085154080037429715">@SemiAnalysis_</a>).</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> Read narrowly this is less dramatic than "moratorium" suggests — it touches only discretionary DEC permits, exempts facilities already built, and is time-bounded by a review the state controls the pace of. A developer with as-of-right siting and an existing interconnection position is not stopped. The forward-looking risk is not EO 62 but the 20MW threshold in the bill, which would capture a much larger share of projects and, unlike an executive order, would survive a change of governor. The forecast that the bill dies is also the analytically convenient outcome for a research firm whose subscribers are long the buildout; it deserves to be tracked against the actual signature rather than assumed.</p>
