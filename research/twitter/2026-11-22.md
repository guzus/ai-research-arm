      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The strongest counter-reading is that this is positioning, not a constraint. A perplexity number depends on the tokenizer and the eval corpus, so it is only comparable across labs if everyone agrees on a held-out set. The excerpts name neither that set nor a numeric first limit, so nothing binds yet. A lab that leads on the metric also gains from making it the yardstick. As a regulatory unit it can be gamed: data contamination, or doing more of the capability work in post-training and tool use, could move capabilities without moving perplexity. The falsification check was whether the quoted passages differ between OpenAI insiders and outside readers. Pachocki, Altman, Curran and swyx quote consistent wording, so the essay and its claims are reported accurately. The claims themselves remain unverified first-party assertions.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> The “~0.76 → groundbreaking math” line lands two days into this week’s AI-mathematics thread (the Alpöge Lean RH certificate, the GPT-6 Astra Deligne write-up). It is OpenAI’s first attempt to tie those results to a single pretraining number.</p>
