    <div class="twitter-story-body">
      <p><strong>Evidence:</strong> The mechanism is now specific. Investors in these platforms are lending against hardware whose resale value depends on how fast Nvidia's next generation arrives — the one variable they cannot underwrite and Nvidia controls. A residual-value guarantee covers exactly that risk, which is why it is the term that gets the money moving. The reason the structure exists at all is a financing gap: hyperscalers can buy chips with cash, while the smaller labs, neoclouds and enterprises behind the rest of the demand have to borrow, and their cost of credit has risen as investment-grade issuers absorb available capacity. CoreWeave's above-9% yields this month are the market price of that constraint. Routing capital through outside asset managers replaces Nvidia's own balance sheet — previously deployed as customer investments and debt guarantees — with pension, insurance and sovereign money, and moves the credit decision outside Nvidia.</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The strongest counter is that residual-value support is ordinary, unremarkable vendor finance — aircraft and equipment manufacturers have written residual guarantees for decades, and it is the standard way to make long-dated hardware financeable. Three things weaken the alarm and one strengthens it. Weakening: the backstop is optional, per-project, and 25% of a project's cost is not 25% of $500B; no aggregate exposure figure has been disclosed. Strengthening: a residual-value floor pays out precisely when accelerator resale prices collapse, which is the same scenario in which every other AI credit in the book deteriorates — the exposure is correlated, not diversifying. The "circularity lite" reading survives this cycle because moving the money to outside asset managers does not move the residual risk anywhere; Nvidia retains it, just contingently and off the reported balance sheet until drawn.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> This resolves the open term question logged yesterday. The Information reported that Nvidia held an option to backstop up to 25% of a project inside the ~$500B partnership signed with BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, Apollo and KKR, but one sentence could not distinguish first-loss, offtake or residual-value support — and those imply very different amounts of transferred risk. It is residual-value.</p>

      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The strongest counter is that memory spot markets are thin, volatile and routinely trade far above contract during any shortage, so a 2× premium on a small float is a weak signal about the real cost of supply — most volume moves on contract, and the contract number is the one that hits income statements. First, it carries its own bearish note — an explicit call that price increases will moderate — which is not what a pure hype relay contains. Second, the internal structure is not the shape a fabricated survey would take: a simple bullish fake would show scarcity worst at the leading edge, and this shows the opposite, consistent with wafer starts being pulled toward high-density and HBM output while the commodity tier is starved.
