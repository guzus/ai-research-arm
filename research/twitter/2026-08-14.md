The objection set is specific enough to argue with: at very high utilization prices detach from production cost and lab margins go "from ~80%+ to something even more ridiculous"; cheap overnight tokens raise demand and flatten the curve, ending in near-100% utilization with high prices around the clock; large corporates hedge by reserving fixed-price capacity, pushing volatility onto everyone else; model-release days become price spikes that make insider information valuable; token derivatives make it profitable to drive demand with agent swarms; and — the sharpest one — "attacks on competitors infrastructure would be encouraged, since you would make gigantic windfall profits from the demand spikes" (<a href="https://x.com/scaling01/status/2088076927173083381">@scaling01</a>, 01:35 UTC). The follow-up adds the concentration case: if one lab pulls ahead, spot prices explode and every listed failure amplifies (<a href="https://x.com/scaling01/status/2088082397665354002">@scaling01</a>, 01:56 UTC).</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> This is two people arguing, not an event, which is why it ranks third. The weak link is on the proposing side and nobody tested it: the day/night curve premise assumes load concentrates in local waking hours, but a global API is served across every time zone at once, and DeepSeek's own peak window is defined in China time rather than by measured global utilization. No lab has published a utilization curve, so the empirical basis for time-of-day pricing is currently one vendor's rate card. The rebuttal has its own gap in the other direction — most of its failure modes require a liquid secondary market and derivatives that do not exist, whereas what DeepSeek actually shipped is a two-tier published price, closer to off-peak electricity billing than to a spot exchange. What makes the exchange worth carrying is that an OpenAI researcher is publicly endorsing the mechanism a competitor deployed this week, which is a signal about where rate cards are heading regardless of who wins the argument.</p>
