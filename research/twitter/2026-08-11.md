On the demand side, the August 1–11 export mix is lopsided: NAND $780M (+54% MoM, +70% QoQ) and DRAM $4.62B (+4% MoM, +32% QoQ) against DRAM modules $330M (-77% MoM) and SSD $490M (-33% MoM).</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The obvious reading — SK hynix is consolidating NAND — is the one the underlying reporting explicitly rejects. Converting the CBs into voting shares requires antitrust clearance in several jurisdictions, and the Japanese government is described as unlikely to approve given its interest in protecting a domestic supply chain. The title change may be an artefact of Toshiba selling rather than SK hynix buying. The export data is also 11 days of preliminary customs figures, and the collapse in modules and SSDs alongside surging raw NAND is as consistent with a channel-mix shift as with genuine end demand.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> This lands against the argument circulating from the FMS 2026 memory show on 2026-08-10 that the industry's answer to the HBM shortage is to promote NAND into a DRAM-like tier. If raw NAND is being pulled into AI systems while finished modules and SSDs fall away, that is what the transition would look like in the trade data — and it raises the strategic value of the third-largest NAND maker's cap table.</p>
