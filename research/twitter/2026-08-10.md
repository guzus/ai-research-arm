      <p><strong>Evidence:</strong> The framing is that FMS tracked the workload — 2024 training, 2025 inference, 2026 agents — and that agentic scaling runs straight into an HBM and local-DRAM shortage that is now the bottleneck (<a href="https://x.com/statementdog/status/2086673702855557550">@statementdog</a>, 04:39 UTC). The technical argument is a chain of three sequential bottlenecks, each with a named fix. First, host DRAM: in CPU-centric architectures SSD data must transit host DRAM before reaching GPU HBM, so Nvidia is open-sourcing cuFile — the key GPUDirect Storage API — to push adoption beyond its own stack. Second, CPU I/O control: KV-cache records are mostly under 512B, so hundreds of thousands of GPU threads issue small reads while the CPU still arbitrates, which GPUDirect Storage does not fix; Nvidia's SCADA framework moves I/O control to the GPU. Third, drive IOPS: once the CPU clears, small-block IOPS binds, which inverts SSD design from capacity and bandwidth toward random-access IOPS. The numbers are concrete — against a Storage Next target of 100M IOPS at 512B, Micron has demonstrated 44 of its 9650 PCIe 6.0 SSDs behind three Broadcom PEX90000 switches to reach 230M 512B random-read IOPS, while Kioxia's XL-FLASH-based GP1 is due in H2 at 10M and the company targets a single 100M-IOPS drive in 2027. Separately, SK hynix and SanDisk released the first version of the HBF standard, UCIe-interconnected to the XPU — a read-oriented, bandwidth-advantaged part whose limited write endurance points it at model weights and MoE parameters rather than at KV cache.</p>
      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The obvious counter is that this is vendor roadmap laundered as analysis — every exhibitor at a memory show has an incentive to claim its part solves the shortage, and "positioning NAND as a DRAM-like resource" is exactly what NAND vendors would say in a year when DRAM is scarce and NAND is not. That objection has real force on timing: the author's own commercialisation estimate puts SCADA plus high-IOPS SSDs at 2027 for KV-cache offload and says HBF "still needs time," so none of this relieves the 2026 shortage that is driving current pricing. Where it survives is that the claims are falsifiable in a way marketing usually is not — a specific Micron configuration with a measured figure, a named Kioxia part with an H2 ship date and a stated IOPS, and an Nvidia API whose open-sourcing is verifiable. The read also resists the easy bullish conclusion, noting that DRAM and NAND demand now overlap and substitute, so the two should be analysed as one market rather than as independent cycles.</p>
