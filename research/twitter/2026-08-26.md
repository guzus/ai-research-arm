      <p><strong>Evidence:</strong> @SemiAnalysis_ posted a two-part result at 01:15 UTC: Jalapeño "up to 2x better perf per watt than Nvidia's July Rubin performance," achieved without speculative decoding enabled while Rubin NVL72 ran with it, and "better performance across the entire Pareto versus July Rubin."       <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> "July Rubin performance" is a measurement of an Nvidia part early in its software life, not the part's ceiling. Nvidia's consistent historical pattern is large post-launch throughput gains on fixed silicon from kernel and stack work, so a July figure is a floor being compared against OpenAI's best current number — a timing asymmetry, not necessarily a silicon one. The speculative-decoding detail also cuts in both directions: it makes the win look more decisive, but it means the headline was set on a configuration neither side has fully optimised. And the deeper deflation is that this is the design premise working as intended rather than a surprise — an internally-deployed ASIC narrowly tuned to one lab's own model shapes should beat a general-purpose merchant part on those shapes, and it carries none of the obligation to be good at anyone else's. @AndrewCurran_ made the adjacent point at 23:12 UTC that Google has published on AI-assisted chip design since 2024 and has been applying it to TPU for years, which makes the nine-month figure less of a discontinuity than it reads.</p>
