That reading survives partly: nothing here names a publisher, quantifies how many titles are contested, or shows an administrator ruling either way. What makes it more than friction is the structure of the settlement itself. A per-title award with a default split and a contract-dependent carve-out for reverted rights puts the burden on individual authors to prove a reversion against a counterparty holding the paperwork, and the $3,000 figure is small enough that contesting a claim can cost more than the claim is worth. That asymmetry is a design feature, not a rumor.</p>
      <p><strong>Context:</strong> A federal judge granted final approval to this settlement on July 21 — the largest copyright settlement in U.S. history. The court had already granted Anthropic summary judgment that training on the named authors' books was fair use, leaving piracy as the separate damages issue the money resolves. That framing is why the distribution rules matter beyond this case: the split precedent set here is the template every future AI training-data settlement will be argued against.</p>

      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The internal tension is the story. Turning bullish on a sector while forecasting its price growth to fall by roughly half is not incoherent — a decelerating but still-positive price curve with rising bit volumes can expand margins — but it is a much weaker call than "turning positive" implies, and the deceleration is the number a reader is likelier to act on. The bull case also rests on hyperscaler DRAM inventory levels, which are not publicly observable and are exactly the variable the same desk got wrong badly enough in July to require this reversal. Meritz's supporting argument is weaker still: it leans on Astra's 99.9% ARC-AGI-3 score as evidence of a step-change in agentic capability, but OpenAI's own launch materials put that benchmark at 63% under a standard harness, and an embargoed draft carried 98.6% against a live 99.99%. A demand thesis anchored to the most contested number on the scorecard is the part to discount.</p>
