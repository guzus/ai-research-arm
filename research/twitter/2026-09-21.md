      <p><strong>Counter / contradicting:</strong> The sharpest reading is that the remit is shaped precisely around the complaint. Ten days ago Terence Tao's objection was about speed — "the AIs are steamrolling through math everywhere… we have to slow down" — and the body OpenAI has created is empowered on communication, standards and tooling while pace is explicitly excluded (<a href="https://x.com/ns123abc/status/2102123811378229375">@ns123abc</a>). Read charitably, that boundary is the price of independence: an outside group with a veto over internal research cadence is a governance body, not an advisory one, and no lab would grant it. Read uncharitably, OpenAI has bought external legitimacy on the axis where it was cheap and retained control of the axis that was contested. Both readings are consistent with the published text, and the text is the only evidence available — no member has spoken independently yet. The second, smaller caution: "resolved more than 100 long-standing open problems" is OpenAI's own characterisation and is exactly the kind of claim the new group exists to adjudicate, so it should not be treated as settled by the announcement that cites it.</p>
