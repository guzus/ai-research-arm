## Breaking News

**In her 16 September State of the Union address, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said frontier models will enable “hacking on a level we never thought possible,” called AI-agent escape incidents “a mere glimpse,” and invited the main frontier labs to talks on pacing.** The official multilingual address (dated 16 September 2026) is the primary: “AI is fast becoming a foundational layer for our economy and security.” After the Hugging Face incident, “developers are ringing the alarm. And CEOs of the most advanced companies tell us that it is time to slow down on the self-recursive models. To pace the frontier.” She said the AI Act “is of course a crucial piece to putting guardrails in place,” pledged to team up with Canada, the United Kingdom and others on “model evaluation, verification, early warning, AI security,” and added: “I will invite the main frontier labs for a discussion on how we can support ongoing industry efforts to pace the frontier.” She also said Europe “need[s] to massively boost our computing capacity” and stay in the race “for our independence.” The Decoder’s same-day recap matches those lines. What does not survive is a new statute or a signed lab pact. What survives is the Commission president, on the record, adopting the industry’s pacing vocabulary and naming an invitation. ([European Commission — SOTEU 2026 address](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/0e4622b7-6970-49c5-a4d8-0f803e1717f5_en?filename=State%20of%20the%20Union%202026%20-%20Multilingual%20address.pdf), [SOTEU hub](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/state-union-2026_en), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/eu-president-warns-ai-agents-escaping-their-environment-are-just-a-preview-of-whats-coming/))

**Third-party evaluators told TechCrunch that Amodei’s and Altman’s weekend pledge to embed them will only be independent if they get checkpoint access, interview rights, and publish-without-edits — none of which the labs have specified.** TechCrunch (2:07 PM PDT, September 16) is a same-day follow, not a re-cut of Lehane’s 15 September confirmation. Apollo’s Alexander Meinke: companies should be able to answer whether a model “ever actively try to undermine its own alignment training,” and “right now we are completely relying on AI companies to both carefully check this themselves and then truthfully report this.” FAR.AI’s Adam Gleave said evaluators want intermediate checkpoints, post-training reward environments, and transcripts; he has turned down frontier contracts that left too much publication control with the lab. Palisade’s John Steidley compared benchmark-trained “good behavior” to Dieselgate. Concrete track-record numbers in the piece: OpenAI gave METR and Redwood “roughly a week” on the Hugging Face incident and both said they could not draw confident conclusions; Apollo got three days on GPT-6 Astra and wrote that “low rates of misbehavior here do not provide substantial evidence.” Meta, xAI, and DeepMind have not committed to embedding evaluators. California SB 813, signed this month, creates state-recognized “independent verification organizations”; the EU AI Act already requires documented evaluations. Neither company named which evaluators, when, or what they can disclose. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/anthropic-and-openai-want-to-embed-safety-evaluators-will-they-really-be-independent/))

## Research Papers

**Chain-of-Self-Questioning (CoSQ) cut unsupported wrong answers on TruthfulQA without extra training: Grounded-CoSQ at τ=0.90 moved the mean unconditional wrong-commitment rate from 13.1% under chain-of-thought to 8.9% (a 32.1% relative drop) and answered accuracy from 86.9% to 89.7% while still answering 87.6% of questions.** `2609.17516`, submitted 2026-09-15: prompt-only abstention on the 817-item TruthfulQA multiple-choice validation set across eleven open-weight and hosted model families and seventeen conditions. Both improvements held for all eleven models at every evaluated threshold. Neighboring variants (Critical-CoSQ, Adaptive-CoSQ) sit at 88.6% and 86.5% coverage. First-party board; no independent reproduction is in this window. ScienceBuddy (`2609.17523`, same day) still carries no extractable benchmark numbers in the abstract and is omitted. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17516))

**Salesforce’s DarwinX evolved an agent harness — not the model — and reported Monet’s WebArena-Infinity score moving from 43.5% to 93% on frozen GPT-5.5, after an audit that screened invalid or exploit-style trajectories.** VentureBeat (7:53 AM PT, September 16) is the dated recap of Salesforce AI Research / Agentforce work: preserve-and-extend selection across an archive of harness variants, with confirmation reruns before a change is trusted. Other first-party numbers in the piece: Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 75.5% to 83.2% on frozen GPT-5.5 (84.7% on a stronger base); TerminalWorld held-out 25/41 (61%) to 28/41 (68.3%) on Opus 4.8; SWE-bench Verified transfer 80.8% → 84.2% with no SWE-bench feedback during evolution. Beagle, the experiment infrastructure, is on GitHub under Apache 2.0; Monet itself stays proprietary. Treat the 49.5-point WebArena jump as the authors’ audited figure, not an independent third-party rerun. ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/salesforce-researchers-took-an-ai-agent-from-finishing-43-5-of-browser-tasks-to-93-without-touching-the-model))
