## New Model Releases

**ElevenLabs shipped v4 and v4 Turbo speech models, per TechCrunch (Ivan Mehta, 7:00 AM PDT, 28 September).** The models clone a voice from 10 seconds of audio, support stacked inline expression tags for context-aware delivery over long passages, and start generating audio as an LLM streams its answer to cut latency for voice agents. Language support expands from 70 to 90, with quality gains called out in Japanese, Brazilian Portuguese, Mandarin, and Cantonese. TechCrunch reports ElevenLabs' annualized revenue rose from roughly $330 million to over $600 million, enterprise customers are 55% of the business, the company is valued at $22 billion after a $500 million Sequoia round, headcount exceeds 800, and the CEO said an IPO is planned "in the next years" with no firm date. What does not survive: no pricing for v4/v4 Turbo was given in the article.

**Hcompany released Holo4, a family of open-weight computer-use agent models, per its Hugging Face blog post (28 September).** The lineup — Holo4-27B (dense), Holo4-35B-A3B (MoE), and Holotron4 Nano (30B-A3B) — is a single model family that clicks and types on a screen, writes and runs its own code, and calls MCP/API tools across desktop, web, Android, and code-sandbox targets without separate variants. On OSWorld 2.0, Holo4-27B scores 61.7% against Claude Opus 5.5's 81.8%; Hcompany's pitch is competitive AutomationBench performance at a fraction of the parameters and cost of frontier closed models. Weights are on Hugging Face in BF16/FP8/NVFP4/4-bit-GGUF, with training trajectories and datasets also released; the model is also served via H Company's own API. What does not survive: no explicit license string was visible on the fetched page, and the OSWorld/AutomationBench comparisons are Hcompany's own reported numbers, not an independent bakeoff.

## Funding & Acquisitions

**Instinct raised a $1 billion Series C at a $10 billion valuation, per TechCrunch (Sarah Perez, 6:38 AM PDT, 28 September).** Investors were Sequoia Capital, Benchmark, and Coatue. Instinct is a consumer AI agent, invite-only since its August 2026 launch, that texts with users via SMS to book travel and restaurants, make purchases, pay bills, cancel subscriptions, order groceries, place phone calls, and do research on their behalf. The round follows a $350 million raise in August 2026 at a $2.5 billion valuation — a 4x valuation jump in about a month. Founder Noah Shinn: "We're building Instinct to be the best personal agent that can handle the deeply personal nuances of everyday life." TechCrunch frames Meta's Muse, which has already reached millions of downloads with deeper social-product integration, as Instinct's main competitive threat.

**Modulate raised $25 million for its voice-intelligence platform, per TechCrunch (Ivan Mehta, 7:05 AM PDT, 28 September).** Future Ventures led, with Hyperplane and Lakestar participating; PitchBook had the Boston company (founded 2017 by Mike Pappas and Carter Huffman) previously valued at $170 million after a $41 million raise. Modulate runs more than 100 small, specialized models to transcribe calls, detect deepfake/AI-generated audio, flag fraud and cyberattacks, and monitor AI voice-agent quality and policy compliance for regulated industries, positioning smaller on-device-friendly models as an advantage over large general-purpose ones for this workload.

**Insurance-automation startup Outmarket raised a $34.5 million Series B at a $335 million valuation, per TechCrunch (28 September).** SignalFire led, joined by Fika Ventures, Permanent Capital Ventures, TTV Capital, and Dash Fund, just four months after a $17 million Series A. Outmarket uses AI to automate commercial-insurance paperwork — evaluating policies and processing applications across 250+ coverage types — and says it has signed more than 300 agencies, including a quarter of the top 100, in the 14 months since launch.

## Industry Moves

**Harvard psychologist Steven Pinker published an open letter on Quillette pushing back on AI-extinction rhetoric, per The Decoder (Manuel Uth, 28 September).** Responding to tech blogger/psychiatrist Scott Alexander, who has put a 20% probability on AI wiping out humanity, Pinker argues that "paperclip maximizer"-style doom scenarios conflate intelligence with dominance and unrealistic single-goal fixation. He concedes he underestimated both current LLM capability and "how recklessly AI companies would release their products," and says he takes bioterrorism, cyberattacks, and unchecked AI agents seriously — but argues the response should be "sober safety engineering built on independent oversight, liability, and human control," not doomsday framing. Pinker reportedly declined a public debate with Alexander, calling such exchanges a "spectator sport."
